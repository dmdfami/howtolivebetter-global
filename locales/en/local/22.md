### 1. Before entering KTV, a bar or an escape room, check where the emergency exits are; if they are locked or blocked, go somewhere else
- In the United States: The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) requires employers to keep exit routes free and unobstructed. No materials or equipment may be placed within the exit route. You cannot lock exit doors from the inside.
- Official sources: 29 CFR § 1910.37, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-XVII/part-1910/subpart-E/section-1910.37
- Checked on: 2026-11-02
- Reliability: verified

### 2. Ask for the price list before ordering: venues must display prices clearly and may not charge you after the fact
- In the United States: The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) Act prohibits "unfair or deceptive acts or practices," which includes deceptive pricing and hidden charges. State consumer protection agencies also enforce laws against hidden charges. You can report fraud to the FTC or your state attorney general.
- Official sources: 15 U.S.C. 45, https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/USCODE-2022-title15/USCODE-2022-title15-chap2-subchapI-sec45
- Checked on: 2026-11-02
- Reliability: verified

### 3. If someone offers you "something" at the venue, leave at once; supplying drugs or letting others use them in your room is not "helping a friend"
- In the United States: Under the federal Controlled Substances Act, it is a crime to knowingly open, lease, rent, use, or maintain any place for the purpose of manufacturing, distributing, or using any controlled substance. This is sometimes known as the "crack house statute."
- Official sources: 21 U.S.C. 856, https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/USCODE-2022-title21/USCODE-2022-title21-chap13-subchapI-partD-sec856
- Checked on: 2026-11-02
- Reliability: verified

### 4. Do not eat sweets or snacks from strangers, do not drink from a glass that has been out of sight, and do not take vape cartridges from others
- In the United States: Federal law makes it a crime to distribute a controlled substance to anyone without a legitimate medical purpose. Tricking someone into consuming drugs can also be prosecuted under state laws as poisoning, battery, or distributing a controlled substance.
- Official sources: 21 U.S.C. 841, https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/USCODE-2022-title21/USCODE-2022-title21-chap13-subchapI-partD-sec841
