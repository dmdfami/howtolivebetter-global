### 3. Get every vaccine in the national immunization program on schedule. They are free, and if one is missed, catch up only the doses not yet given
- In the United States: The federally funded Vaccines for Children (VFC) program provides vaccines at no cost to eligible children through enrolled healthcare providers. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) provides a catch-up schedule for children who start late or fall behind, instructing providers to determine the recommended interval without restarting the full series.
- Official sources: <https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/programs/vfc/about/index.html> (CDC, Vaccines for Children Program); <https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/schedules/hcp/imz/child-adolescent.html> (CDC, Child and Adolescent Immunization Schedule)

- Reliability: verified

### 7. The vitamin K injection at birth must be given
- In the United States: The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) states that Vitamin K deficiency bleeding is easily prevented by giving babies a vitamin K shot into a muscle in the thigh just after birth. Without this shot, infants can develop life-threatening bleeding into their intestines or brain at any time up to 6 months of age.
- Official sources: <https://www.cdc.gov/vitamin-k-deficiency/about/index.html> (CDC, About Vitamin K Deficiency Bleeding)
- Checked on: 2026-11-02
- Reliability: verified

### 10. Choose diapers by fit, how often you change them, and whether they have been named in a safety spot check, not by brand
- In the United States: The Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) regulates consumer products such as diapers. If a safety issue is found, the CPSC issues recalls. Parents can search the CPSC recalls list rather than relying solely on brand reputation.
- Official sources: <https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls> (U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission Recalls)
