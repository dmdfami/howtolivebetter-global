### 1. First, count what you can get: a national childcare subsidy of 3,600 yuan per child per year, paid until age 3; and a special income tax deduction of 2,000 yuan per child per month
- In the United States: You may qualify for the Child Tax Credit, worth up to $2,200 per qualifying child under 17, if your annual income is not more than $200,000 (or $400,000 if filing jointly). If you have little or no federal income tax liability, you may qualify for the refundable Additional Child Tax Credit, up to $1,700 per child, if you have earned income of at least $2,500. A partial credit is available for higher incomes.
- Official sources: Internal Revenue Service, Child Tax Credit, <https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/individuals/child-tax-credit>

### 2. Maternity leave is 98 days, and maternity benefits are paid from the maternity insurance fund at the employer's average monthly wage of the previous year; paternity leave for fathers and parental leave for both parents follow the provincial regulations
- In the United States: The federal Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) gives eligible employees up to 12 workweeks of unpaid, job-protected leave in a 12-month period for the birth of a child. To be eligible, you must have worked for a covered employer (usually one with 50 or more employees within 75 miles) for at least 12 months and have at least 1,250 hours of service during the 12 months before your leave starts. The United States does not have a federal paid maternity leave program, though some states offer paid family leave.
- Official sources: Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA), Fact Sheet #28, <https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/28-fmla>; <https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fmla>

### 3. Know this: an employer may not cut your pay or dismiss you because of pregnancy, childbirth or breastfeeding
- In the United States: Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, amended by the Pregnancy Discrimination Act, forbids employment discrimination based on current, past, or potential pregnancy. The PUMP for Nursing Mothers Act (under the Fair Labor Standards Act) requires most employers to provide reasonable break time and a private, non-bathroom space to express breast milk for a nursing child for one year after birth. If the employer already provides paid breaks, you must be paid for that time; otherwise, the time does not need to be compensated as long as you are completely relieved from duty.
- Official sources: Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, <https://www.eeoc.gov/pregnancy-discrimination>; PUMP for Nursing Mothers Act, Fact Sheet #73, <https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/73-flsa-break-time-nursing-mothers>; <https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/pump-at-work>
