# 19. Employment, resignation and work injury

This section is about money: how pay is calculated, what to watch for when you sign, and how deadlines work. The first three items cover pay you should already be receiving while you are still employed. The next five cover leaving a job. After them, seven items cover occupational disease and work injury. Work-injury compensation is an order of magnitude larger than layoff pay, and its deadlines are stricter. Health lost to occupational disease cannot be won back. The section ends with nine more items. The first four cover what to do about being bullied at work, how to get a separation certificate, how individual income tax applies to severance pay, and how to transfer your social insurance when you move to another city. The middle three cover double pay for not signing a contract, employers that do not pay social insurance, and demands for a penalty when you leave. The last two cover how to respond when your employer wants to reassign you or cut your pay, and who to turn to about pay and compensation as a dispatched worker.

The entries are grouped by theme below. The numbers in brackets are item numbers.

**Contracts and pay while employed**: overtime pay (item 1), annual leave (item 2), probation (item 3), double pay for not signing a written contract (item 20), not paying or underpaying social insurance (item 21), putting in writing that you do not accept a reassignment or pay cut (item 23), equal pay for dispatched workers (item 24), long-term bullying at work (item 16).

**Leaving and layoffs**: work out your N first (item 4), no 30 days' notice (item 5), compensation for unlawful dismissal (item 6), do not sign "resignation for personal reasons" (item 7), keep evidence before you leave (item 8), separation certificate (item 17), income tax on severance pay (item 18), being asked for a penalty when you leave (item 22), transferring social insurance to the new insurance location (item 19).

**Occupational disease and work injury**: three occupational health checks (item 9), employers must provide protective equipment (item 10), applying for work-injury recognition (item 11), call 120 (China's ambulance number) first if you suddenly feel unwell (item 12), no work-injury insurance (item 13), work-capacity assessment (item 14), the three payments after a work-related death (item 15).

### 1. Overtime is paid at 1.5x, 2x or 3x; if it is not paid, complain to labor inspection, and late payers owe 50% to 100% more
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Nothing. As you go, keep copies of your clock-in records, shift schedules and overtime approval forms. Screenshot any work-chat messages where managers assign you work. The hard part is remembering to do it every month.
- Plain language: Normal overtime pays 1.5 times your wage. Rest-day overtime pays 2 times if no time off is given. Public holidays pay 3 times, and time off cannot replace that. If your employer refuses, complain to the labor inspection bureau. If it still does not pay after the deadline, it must add 50% to 100% more.
- Benefit: Under the law, normal working time is no more than 8 hours a day and an average of 44 hours a week. Ordinary overtime generally may not exceed 1 hour a day. Where there are special reasons for longer hours, overtime may not exceed 3 hours a day, and no more than 36 hours in a month. Overtime pay has three tiers. When your employer extends your working hours on a normal day, it must pay at least 150%. When it has you work on a rest day and cannot arrange time off in lieu, it must pay at least 200%. When it has you work on a statutory public holiday, it must pay at least 300%. For this top tier, the law gives no option of replacing the pay with time off. If an employer schedules overtime but does not pay it, the labor administrative department orders it to pay within a set period. If it still does not pay after that period, the department orders it to pay the worker, on top of the amount owed, additional compensation of 50% or more but no more than 100% of the amount payable (nationwide). Statutory public holidays are set by Article 2 of the State Council's Measures on National Holidays and Memorial Days: New Year's Day (1 day), Spring Festival (4 days), Qingming Festival (1 day), Labour Day (2 days), Dragon Boat Festival (1 day), Mid-Autumn Festival (1 day) and National Day (3 days). Article 7 of the same measures allows "unified holidays with adjusted working days", which join these days to weekends to make longer breaks.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2018). 劳动法（2018 年第二次修正，第三十六、四十一、四十三、四十四条）. <https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/bgt/art/2023/art_d9aa750028b14b99a776cb93726a360d.html>（国家市场监督管理总局刊登）；全国人大常委会 (2007). 劳动合同法（第八十五条第三项）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2007/content_711013.htm>；国务院 (2024). 全国年节及纪念日放假办法（国务院令第 795 号修订，2025 年 1 月 1 日起施行，第二条、第七条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2024/issue_11726/202411/content_6989774.html>
- Notes: China only: Rest days and statutory holidays must be calculated separately. The whole-year statutory holidays are only New Year's Day, Spring Festival, Labour Day, National Day and the other listed days. Within a long break made by adjusting working days, the days that are not themselves listed holidays count at the rest-day rate: time off where it is arranged, and 200% where it is not. This is inferred from Article 2 of the Holiday Measures and Article 44 of the Labor Law. "Company rules require approval for overtime" and "I stayed voluntarily" do not mean the employer can skip paying, but they make it harder for you to prove your case, so keep the assignment and approval records. Employers that want to use the special working-hour systems of comprehensive calculation or flexible working hours must first get approval from the labor administrative department; they cannot decide this alone. For the arbitration time limit on disputes, see section 8, item 19.

### 2. Annual leave is 5, 10 or 15 days by cumulative years of service; unused days are paid at 300% of the daily wage
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Nothing. Add up the years you have worked at every employer; your social insurance contribution records prove them. The hard part is refusing to sign a "voluntary waiver of annual leave" form when an employer asks you to.
- Plain language: Cumulative service of 1 to under 10 years gives 5 days of annual leave a year. 10 to under 20 years gives 10 days, and 20 years or more gives 15 days. Service at every employer counts. If the employer cannot give the leave and you agree not to take it, unused days are paid at 3 times your daily wage. Signing a "voluntary waiver" form leaves you with only your normal pay.
- Benefit: You earn annual leave after 12 consecutive months of work. The number of days is based on cumulative working time, counting years at all employers: 1 to under 10 years gives 5 days, 10 to under 20 years gives 10 days, and 20 years or more gives 15 days. Public holidays and rest days do not count toward annual leave days. If the employer cannot arrange leave because of work needs, and you agree not to take it, the unused days must be paid at "300% of the worker's daily wage." That 300% already includes the pay you would get for working that day, so the extra you actually receive is 200%. Daily wage = your monthly wage ÷ 21.75. The monthly wage is based on the 12 months before the payment, with overtime pay removed, averaged. If you joined this employer in the current year, leave is pro-rated over the remaining calendar days at that employer. When you leave, leave is pro-rated by how much of the year you have worked and settled in cash; days you have already taken beyond your entitlement are not deducted back. If the employer neither arranges leave nor pays for it, it is ordered to correct this within a set period. If it still does not correct this after that period, it must pay, on top of the annual leave pay, compensation of the same amount (nationwide, effective 1 January 2008).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2007). 职工带薪年休假条例（国务院令第 514 号，第二、三、四、五、七条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2008/content_859865.htm>；人力资源和社会保障部 (2008). 企业职工带薪年休假实施办法（人社部令第 1 号，第三、四、五、十、十一、十二、十五条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2009/content_1265995.htm>
- Notes: China only: Cumulative working time must be added up across employers; do not count only your current employer's service. Only if the worker requests in writing, for personal reasons, not to take the leave can the employer pay only normal wages, so do not sign the "voluntary waiver" form. If you take 20 days or more of personal leave in total and the employer does not deduct pay for it, you get no annual leave that year. Sick leave beyond the set number of months also removes it: under 10 years of service, 2 months; 10 to 20 years, 3 months; 20 years or more, 4 months. Home-visit leave, marriage and funeral leave, maternity leave and the paid work-injury sick leave period do not count toward annual leave.

### 3. Probation has a legal maximum, pay must be at least 80% of the agreed wage, and it can only be agreed once
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Nothing. Spend a few minutes before signing checking that the contract term and the probation length match.
- Plain language: A contract of 3 months to under 1 year allows up to 1 month of probation. 1 to under 3 years allows up to 2 months. 3 years or more allows up to 6 months. An employer can only put you on probation once. Probation pay must be at least 80% of the agreed wage or of the lowest grade for the same job, and not below the local minimum wage.
- Benefit: A contract of 3 months or more but under 1 year may have a probation of no more than 1 month. A contract of 1 year or more but under 3 years may have a probation of no more than 2 months. A contract of 3 years or more, and an open-ended contract with no end date, may have a probation of no more than 6 months. A contract for a single task, or a contract totaling less than 3 months, may not include probation at all. The same employer and the same worker may agree on probation only once. If the contract states a probation period that is not valid, that period is simply part of the contract term. Probation pay "must not be lower than 80% of the lowest grade for the same post at the employer or the wage agreed in the labor contract, and must not be below the local minimum wage standard where the employer is located." If the agreed probation is longer than the statutory limit and you actually work it, the employer must pay compensation for the excess, calculated on your monthly wage after probation for the excess period. During probation, the employer may terminate the contract only on the grounds the law lists, and it must explain its reasons to the worker (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2007). 劳动合同法（第十九、二十、二十一、八十三条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2007/content_711013.htm>
- Notes: China only: The three most common unlawful practices are: first, signing a one-year contract with a three-month probation; second, putting a worker on probation again after a transfer following confirmation; third, not paying social insurance during probation. Social insurance obligations start on your first day of work, whether or not you are on probation; the employer's duties are in section 12, item 16. Being dismissed during probation does not mean you get nothing: it depends on whether the employer's reason is one the law allows. If you were unlawfully dismissed, calculate it as 2N under item 6 of this section.

### 4. Work out your N first: one month's pay for each year of service, and under six months counts as half a month
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Nothing. Do the sum on a calculator once; it takes ten minutes.
- Plain language: Your employer owes one month's wage for each full year worked. Six months or more but under a year counts as a full year, and under six months counts as half a month. The monthly wage is your average over the last 12 months, with bonuses and allowances included, capped at 3 times the local average. Years of service are capped at 12.
- Benefit: The economic compensation set by the Labor Contract Law, which people often call N, is what the employer owes when it lays you off. The formula is: "one month's wage for each full year... six months or more but under one year counts as a full year; under six months, half a month's wage is paid to the worker." If the monthly wage is above 3 times the local average monthly wage of workers in the previous year, it is capped at 3 times, and years of service are counted up to a maximum of 12.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2007). 中华人民共和国劳动合同法（2008 年 1 月 1 日施行）（第四十七条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2007/content_711013.htm>
- Notes: China only: Monthly wage means the average wage for the 12 months before the contract is ended. Bonuses, allowances and subsidies are included, not just base pay. This is the item employers most often underpay. For whether this payment is subject to income tax, see item 18 of this section (income tax on compensation).

### 5. If the employer dismisses you for illness, incapacity or major change without 30 days' notice, it must also pay one more month's wage
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Nothing.
- Plain language: If your employer dismisses you because you cannot work after an illness, cannot do the job, or circumstances have changed, it must give you 30 days' written notice. If it does not, it must pay one extra month's wage on top of N. This is often called N+1. When you negotiate, list this month separately so the employer cannot count it as part of N.
- Benefit: The Labor Contract Law gives the employer two options when it ends a contract under this article. One is to give the worker written notice 30 days in advance. The other is to pay the worker one extra month's wage and end the contract at once. If the employer wants you out immediately, it must pay the extra month. This extra month, often called pay in lieu of notice, is calculated separately from N. The extra month is based on your wage for the month before you leave, while N is based on your average wage over the 12 months before you leave, so the two bases differ.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2007). 中华人民共和国劳动合同法（2008 年 1 月 1 日施行）（第三十六、三十九、四十、四十一、四十六、四十七、八十七条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2007/content_711013.htm>；国务院 (2008). 劳动合同法实施条例（国务院令第 535 号）第二十条：「用人单位依照劳动合同法第四十条的规定，选择额外支付劳动者一个月工资解除劳动合同的，其额外支付的工资应当按照该劳动者上一个月的工资标准确定」. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2008/content_1107604.htm>
- Notes: China only: This extra month appears only in the three situations under Article 40 of the Labor Contract Law. (a) Illness or non-work injury: after the medical treatment period ends, the worker cannot do the original job or the other job the employer arranges. (b) The worker still cannot do the job after training or a transfer. (c) A major change in the objective circumstances that existed when the contract was signed, with no agreement reached after consultation. No other situation gives this extra month. Economic redundancy (Article 41) only requires the employer to explain the situation to the trade union or all staff 30 days in advance. If the employer proposes and both sides agree to end the contract, the payment is N. If you are dismissed for serious misconduct, there is no compensation. If the employer dismisses you unlawfully, the payment is 2N. For the three situations under Article 40, calculate N and the extra month separately, so the employer cannot count the extra month into N.

### 6. If the employer terminates your contract unlawfully, compensation is twice the statutory economic compensation
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Nothing. Arbitration takes several months. The hard part is staying with the process for those months and not giving up halfway.
- Plain language: If the employer unlawfully ends your contract, the compensation is double the usual N amount. This is often called 2N. Whether a termination is unlawful depends on the facts. Get advice from legal aid or a lawyer first, then decide whether to negotiate with the employer or go to arbitration.
- Benefit: The Labor Contract Law says that when an employer terminates or ends a labor contract in breach of this law, it "shall pay the worker compensation equal to twice the economic compensation standard in Article 47 of this law." The method is to first calculate the amount under the economic compensation rules, then double it.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2007). 中华人民共和国劳动合同法（2008 年 1 月 1 日施行）（第八十七条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2007/content_711013.htm>
- Notes: China only: This is what is commonly called 2N. Whether a termination counts as unlawful depends on the specific circumstances. Consult legal aid or a lawyer first, then decide whether to negotiate or go to arbitration; the routes are set out in section 7.

### 7. Do not sign "voluntary resignation for personal reasons"; that signature wipes out N
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=high gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Nothing. The hard part is resisting the pressure to sign on the spot.
- Plain language: Signing "voluntary resignation for personal reasons" usually means you lose both economic compensation and unemployment insurance benefits. If you quit on your own, you are generally not entitled to compensation, unless the employer broke the law, for example by not paying wages or social insurance. You do not have to sign the same day. Take a night to think it over.
- Benefit: The Labor Contract Law limits the cases where economic compensation is owed to the situations the law lists. A worker who quits on their own is not in that list, unless the reason is a statutory fault by the employer, such as unpaid wages or unpaid social insurance. If you sign a "voluntary resignation," economic compensation and unemployment insurance benefits usually both fall away.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2007). 中华人民共和国劳动合同法（2008 年 1 月 1 日施行）（第三十八条、第四十六条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2007/content_711013.htm>
- Notes: China only: A common line from employers is "sign first so the process can go through; compensation will be paid separately." If you want compensation, write it into the agreement on the spot; if it is not written, do not sign. Not signing that day has no legal consequence, so take a night and think it over.

### 8. Before you leave, save your payslips, attendance records, contract, social insurance records and chat logs
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Nothing. Half an hour is enough to save everything. The hard part is remembering to do it before you leave.
- Plain language: For overtime, you must prove you actually worked it. Employers only have to keep pay records for two years. Once you hand back your computer and accounts, you lose access to these records. Keep payslips, attendance records, social insurance records and original chat logs, so you have something to show if the employer denies what happened.
- Benefit: Once you hand back your computer and accounts, these materials are gone for good. Under the rule that the party making a claim must prove it, you must produce evidence for what you assert. Where evidence is held by the employer, the employer must produce it, and if it does not, it bears the adverse consequences. At arbitration, if you cannot get materials held by the employer, the tribunal may order the employer to hand them over within a set time. For disputes over dismissal, termination, reduction of pay and calculation of years of service, the employer bears the burden of proof. Overtime is different: you must first prove that you actually worked overtime. Only once you show that the overtime records are held by the employer and it refuses to produce them does the employer bear the adverse consequences. Employers are only required to keep wage payment records for at least two years. When paying wages, the employer must also give you an individual wage statement. If there is no written contract, wage payment records, social insurance contribution records and work ID can all be used to prove an employment relationship. Employee registration forms, attendance records and testimony from other employees can also be used. Chat records count as electronic data under law and can serve as evidence. When submitting evidence, you must provide originals; a printout made directly from the original record also counts as an original. The Shanghai procuratorate handled one case. A worker sought the shortfall in minimum wages over ten years. The first-instance and second-instance courts only accepted the last two years before he left. The court's reason was that the employer only had to keep records for two years, so the worker had to prove the earlier eight years himself. He had ten years of payslips and bank statements, but the employer did not accept the payslips as genuine. In August 2024 he applied for procuratorial supervision. After the procuratorate protested, the employer paid RMB 19,000 in compensation under mediation. He had sued in 2021, when the company cleared out staff, and by the time he applied for supervision about three years had passed (nationwide).
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2007). 劳动争议调解仲裁法（第六、三十九条）. <https://rsj.ankang.gov.cn/Content-2150407.html>（安康市人力资源和社会保障局转载）；最高人民法院 (2020). 关于审理劳动争议案件适用法律问题的解释（一）（法释〔2020〕26 号，第四十二、四十四条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/282121.html>；最高人民法院 (2019). 关于民事诉讼证据的若干规定（法释〔2019〕19 号，第十四、十五条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/212721.html>；劳动部 (1994). 工资支付暂行规定（第六条），黑龙江省人民政府「政策帮你问」引述. <https://www.hlj.gov.cn/hlj/c116413/202402/c00_31712524.shtml>；劳动和社会保障部 (2005). 关于确立劳动关系有关事项的通知（劳社部发〔2005〕12 号），黑龙江省人民政府「政策帮你问」引述. <https://hlj.gov.cn/hlj/c116413/202408/c00_31757488.shtml>；郭勇辉, 边琬茹 (2025). 两年之外的工资支付情况举证责任应由谁承担？检察日报. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/llyj/202508/t20250806_703164.shtml>（最高人民检察院网站刊载）；维权路径见第 7 节
- Notes: China only: Keep records of your own employment relationship. Do not take the company's source code, customer lists or technical documents with you; the risks of doing so are in section 11. Do not just screenshot chat records; keep the original records on your phone and do not delete them. Save payslips and bank statements monthly if you can, because years after you leave, the employer no longer has to keep records from two years earlier. The Shanghai case was reversed only because the procuratorate protested, and most people never reach that step. When you leave, also ask the employer for a separation certificate; see item 17 of this section (separation certificate).

### 9. Before you take a job with dust, noise or chemicals, check whether the contract states the hazards; the employer arranges and pays for three occupational health checks
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. When you sign the contract, read the occupational hazard disclosure section line by line. Keep your own copy of every health check report. The hard part is asking face to face and getting the documents in your hand.
- Plain language: When you sign a contract, the employer must state in writing what occupational disease hazards the job carries, how to protect yourself, and what benefits apply. If they hid this from you, you can refuse the work, and the employer cannot fire you for refusing. The employer arranges and pays for three occupational health checks: before you start, during the job, and when you leave. If you did not get the exit check, the employer cannot terminate your contract.
- Benefit: The Occupational Disease Prevention Law requires the employer to explain four things when you sign a labour contract: which occupational disease hazards the work may involve, what health consequences they can cause, how to protect yourself, and what benefits apply. The statute says: "truthfully inform the worker, and state it in the labour contract; concealment or deception is not permitted." If the employer later moves you to a hazardous task not covered by the contract, it must inform you again and negotiate changes to the contract terms with you. If the employer fails to do this, "the worker has the right to refuse work that involves occupational disease hazards, and the employer may not terminate the labour contract for that reason." There are three health checks: before the job, during the job, and when leaving. "The employer bears the cost of occupational health checks," and the results must be given to you in writing. A person who has not had the pre-job check may not be assigned to work with occupational disease hazards. A person whose body is unsuited to such work (an occupational contraindication) may not be assigned to the contraindicated work. If a check finds health damage linked to the job, the employer must move the person off that post and resettle them properly. "The employer may not terminate or end the labour contract of a worker who has not had the exit occupational health check." When you leave, you "have the right to request a photocopy of your own occupational health surveillance file, which the employer must provide truthfully and free of charge, and stamp the copy." (National.)
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2018). 职业病防治法（2018 年修正，第三十三、三十五、三十六条）. <https://www.beijing.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengcefagui/qtwj/201711/t20171104_779851.html>（北京市人民政府转载）
- Notes: China only: Ask three questions at the interview and you will get a rough sense of whether the employer is legitimate: Does this job carry occupational disease hazards? Does the contract say so? Who pays for the pre-job health check? If the answer is "we'll sort it out once you're working", weigh the risk yourself. The exit check is the easiest to overlook: without it, the employer cannot terminate or end your contract. The report is also key evidence if you later apply for an occupational disease diagnosis, and the same goes for the file photocopy. Get both before you leave. If you do get an occupational disease, it is handled as a work injury; benefits are in item 11 onward.

### 10. Many injuries from dust, noise and chemical poisons are irreversible (such as pneumoconiosis and noise-induced deafness): the employer must supply protective equipment, and you may refuse work that has no protective measures
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=high gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. The difficulty is wearing a particulate respirator, earplugs and goggles all the time, and turning down unprotected work, which may get you labelled as "difficult" by colleagues.
- Plain language: Pneumoconiosis, noise-induced deafness and chemical eye burns are all officially recognised occupational diseases. Once your lungs or hearing are damaged, they do not recover. You have the right to know what hazards the job carries and to demand protective facilities and equipment. You can refuse unprotected work. If the employer cuts your pay or fires you for this, that action is void.
- Benefit: From 1 August 2025, the officially recognised occupational diseases are 135 conditions in 12 categories, including 4 open-ended items. Category 1 is occupational pneumoconiosis. It includes silicosis, coal worker's pneumoconiosis, graphite pneumoconiosis, carbon black pneumoconiosis, asbestosis, talc pneumoconiosis, cement pneumoconiosis, mica pneumoconiosis, potter's pneumoconiosis, aluminium pneumoconiosis, welder's pneumoconiosis and foundry worker's pneumoconiosis, plus one open-ended item. Under occupational ENT and oral diseases are noise-induced deafness and blast deafness. Under occupational eye diseases are chemical eye burns and electro-ophthalmia. The damage from pneumoconiosis and noise-induced deafness is permanent. The US National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (NIOSH) says silicosis is "permanent lung damage called silicosis. It is a progressive, debilitating, and sometimes fatal disease" and "There is no known cure for silicosis." A Lancet review says lung function keeps declining as the disease progresses, even after the patient is no longer exposed to dust. The US Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) says noise-induced hearing loss is "permanent hearing loss that cannot be corrected through surgery or with medicine." The law gives you four rights you can invoke on the spot. First, to know what hazards the workplace contains, what harm they can cause, and what protective measures to take. Second, to require the employer to provide qualified protective facilities and personal protective equipment and to improve working conditions. Third, to criticise, report and bring complaints against practices that violate occupational disease prevention laws or that endanger life or health. Fourth, to "refuse illegal orders and forced work without occupational disease protective measures." Work that is non-compliant or unprotected can be refused. And where a worker is paid less or loses benefits, or is dismissed or has the labour contract terminated, for exercising these rights lawfully, "such conduct is void." (National.)
- Evidence level: A

### 12. Do not believe "if you make it to your desk, it counts as a work injury": for a sudden illness, call 120 first, not your time clock
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free.
- Plain language: Rushing to the office when you suddenly feel unwell will not earn you extra money. A deemed work injury requires that you fall ill during working hours at your work post. Delaying medical care in order to meet these two conditions costs you your chance of survival, and the compensation you would receive is not guaranteed either.

### 16. Long-term bullying, abuse or harassment at work: do not just endure it. Record it first to preserve evidence, then complain, call the police or go to arbitration, depending on the nature of the conduct

### 18. Check the tax on severance pay first: the part up to 3 times last year's local average wage is tax-free, and any excess is taxed separately, not added to this year's wages
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: No cash cost. Look up last year's local average wage, then spend a few minutes checking how your employer withheld the tax. The Individual Income Tax app, run by China's tax authority, shows the declared details of each income.
- Plain language: Severance pay is tax-free up to 3 times last year's local average wage. Any excess is taxed separately, not added to this year's wages. If your employer merged the whole amount into a month's wages, ask it to explain first, then call 12366 (the national tax hotline).
- Benefit: A Ministry of Finance and State Taxation Administration notice governs one-off payments received when an individual and an employer end their labour relationship. These payments include economic compensation, living subsidies and other subsidies. The part up to 3 times last year's local average wage is exempt from individual income tax. Any part above 3 times is not merged into the year's comprehensive income. It is taxed on its own, using the comprehensive income tax table. That table taxes the part of the annual amount up to RMB 36,000 at 3%, the part from RMB 36,000 to RMB 144,000 at 10%, and rises in steps to 45%. For example, if last year's local average wage was RMB 100,000, then 3 times is RMB 300,000. On RMB 360,000 in compensation, tax applies only to the RMB 60,000 excess, which is RMB 3,480. ### 24. If a staffing agency dispatches you to work for another company, your pay must follow the same pay system as regular staff in similar posts at that company, and if you are harmed there, both companies pay jointly
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: No cash cost. Get your labour contract out and check which company's seal is on it. This takes a few minutes. Then ask the dispatch agency about the pay and social insurance in the dispatch agreement; it must tell you. If there is a real dispute, go to labour arbitration. It is free, the case is settled within 45 days of acceptance, extendable by up to 15 days. If you disagree, go to court. First-instance ordinary proceedings should finish within 6 months under the rules, extendable, and you pay your own lawyer's fees.
- Plain language: Your contract is with the dispatch agency, but the company where you actually work also has legal duties. Your pay should be calculated with the same method as similar posts there, and overtime and performance bonuses come from it. If you are harmed at that company, both companies are jointly liable, and you can claim from either one. In months when you are not sent out to work, the agency must still pay at least the local minimum wage.
- Benefit: The Labour Contract Law says a dispatch agency is your employer. It must sign a fixed-term contract of at least 2 years with you and pay wages monthly. For periods when you are not dispatched to work, it must pay at least the local minimum wage each month. The agency must tell you what the dispatch agreement says, and it may not withhold the wages that the host company (legally, the "user unit") pays you. Neither company may charge you fees. Dispatched workers have the right to equal pay for equal work with regular staff at the host company. The host company must use the same pay distribution method for similar posts. Where no similar posts exist, pay is set by reference to the same or similar posts locally. The host company must pay overtime and performance bonuses, provide welfare related to the post, and may not discriminate against dispatched workers. For continuous employment, pay must be adjusted in the normal way. Dispatch may be used only for temporary, auxiliary or substitute posts, and a temporary post is one lasting no more than 6 months. Dispatched workers may not exceed 10% of the host company's total workforce. If the host company harms a dispatched worker, the agency and the host company bear joint liability for compensation. Joint liability means you can claim the full amount from either one, or from both. If you have a labour dispute with either one, both are joint parties in the arbitration. If you are injured at work at the host company, the dispatch agency applies for work-injury recognition and bears the work-injury insurance liability, and the host company must help with the investigation. The host company may send you back only in the situations the law defines. Arrangements labelled as contracting or outsourcing, but in practice used as dispatch, are treated under the dispatch rules (national; the interim provisions took effect 1 March 2014).
