# 1. Do not die early

This section covers three things: accidents and injuries that cause death, such as car crashes, fires and poisoning; a small number of vaccines and screening tests with the strongest evidence; and body signals that are worth checking. The numbers in the benefit column come from randomized trials, long-term studies that follow large groups of people for years, or official statistics. The cost column is the author's rough estimate based on market prices. It is only for comparing options and should not be cited.

The entries in this section are grouped by theme below. Numbers in brackets are item numbers.

**On the road**: wear a seatbelt (item 1), wear a helmet (item 2), do not speed or drive drunk (item 9), use a child car seat (item 10).

**Fire, gas and electricity at home**: install smoke alarms (item 3), replace gas hoses when they expire (item 4), do not wheel e-bikes into stairwells (item 6), keep fire extinguishers and a first-aid kit ready (item 26), fit a guard before using an angle grinder (item 42), and ventilate, test and set up protection and supervision before entering a septic tank (item 46).

**Accidents involving children, older adults and the outdoors**: fit window restrictors (item 11), keep children within sight near water (item 12), people over 60 should practise balance (item 13), do not eat wild mushrooms (item 5), do not pick up metal objects of unknown origin (item 36), measure the ice thickness before going onto ice outdoors (item 41), and finish soaked wood ear fungus in one meal (item 48).

**Vaccines**: check your hepatitis B serology panel (item 14), tetanus (item 15), HPV vaccine (item 16), flu vaccine (item 20), shingles vaccine (item 21), pneumococcal vaccine (item 22).

**Screening and checkups**: check your blood pressure (item 7), fasting blood glucose (item 8), breast cancer screening (item 17), cervical cancer screening (item 18), colorectal cancer screening (item 19), H. pylori test (item 23), low-dose chest CT (item 24), bone density test (item 39), osteoporosis drugs (item 40), anticoagulants if you are found to have atrial fibrillation (item 44), one hepatitis C antibody test (item 45), gastroscopy screening (item 47).

**Body warning signs and sexual health**: visible blood in the urine (item 27), erectile problems: check your cardiovascular health first (item 28), weight loss and quitting smoking can improve erectile function (item 29), condoms and never sharing needles (item 30), HIV test (item 31), pre-exposure prophylaxis (item 38).

**Mental health crises**: if you have suicidal thoughts, call 12356 (item 25); tell one person about suicidal thoughts first (item 32); gambling until you borrow money to keep gambling (item 37); the costs that follow being rescued after swallowing pesticide or inhaling coal gas (item 33); the costs of a fall from height (item 34); the costs of living with one kidney (item 35); and asking the people around you directly whether they have considered suicide (item 43).

### 1. Wear your seatbelt, in the front and the back seats
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. Buckling up adds 2 seconds each time you get in.
- Plain language: Buckling up in the front seat of a car cuts your chance of dying in a crash by more than four in ten. In a light truck's front seat, the cut is six in ten. In the US in 2022, among people killed in cars whose seatbelt use was recorded, half were not wearing one.
- Benefit: The US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) estimates that seatbelts reduce the risk of a fatal injury for front-seat passengers in cars by 45%. For front-seat occupants of light trucks, the reduction is 60%. Among people killed in passenger vehicles (cars carrying people) in the US in 2022 whose seatbelt use was known, 50% were not wearing one. The World Health Organization (WHO) says seatbelts can reduce the risk of death for people in vehicles by up to 50%. WHO estimates that 248,099 people in China died in road traffic crashes in 2021, equal to 17.4 deaths per 100,000 people. China's National Bureau of Statistics yearbook records 62,218 road traffic deaths in 2021.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: NHTSA (2024). Occupant Protection in Passenger Vehicles: 2022 Data (DOT HS 813 573)：「Among killed passenger vehicle occupants with known restraint use, 48 percent of those in the front row and 60 percent of those in the second row were unrestrained.」<https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813573> ; WHO (2025). Road traffic injuries fact sheet. <https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/road-traffic-injuries> ; WHO GHO RS_196/RS_198 (China, 2021). <https://ghoapi.azureedge.net/api/RS_196?$filter=SpatialDim%20eq%20%27CHN%27> ; 国家统计局 (2022). 中国统计年鉴 2022，表 24-5 道路交通事故情况（2021 年）：死亡人数 62218 人. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/ndsj/2022/html/C24-05.jpg>
- Notes: Wear the rear seatbelt too. In the same report, 60% of second-row deaths with known restraint status were unbelted. The 45% and 60% figures are estimates the NHTSA calculated from crash databases (citing Kahane 2015). They are not randomized trials. The WHO figure for deaths in China is an estimate, about four times the 62,218 in the statistical yearbook. The two sources count different things, so this entry uses only the WHO basis.

### 2. Wear a helmet on a motorcycle or e-bike, and fasten the strap
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: A helmet costs 100 to 300 yuan. Putting it on takes 5 seconds each time.
- Plain language: Wearing a helmet with the strap fastened cuts a motorcyclist's risk of dying in a crash by 42%. It cuts the risk of head injury by 69%. The strap has to be done up. A helmet hanging loose on the head does not count.
- Benefit: Cochrane pooled several studies. For motorcycle riders, wearing a helmet reduces the risk of death by 42%, odds ratio (OR) 0.58, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.50 to 0.68. Head injury falls by 69% (OR 0.31, 95% CI 0.25 to 0.38).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Liu BC 等 (2008). Helmets for preventing injury in motorcycle riders. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004333.pub3>
- Notes: The helmet strap has to be fastened; a helmet hung on the handlebars does not count. The figures above come from motorcycles. There is no direct study on e-bikes; the figures are carried over because the way the head is injured in a crash is the same. For helmets when skiing, snowboarding and cycling, see section 22, item 12 (helmets for skiing and mountain biking). For the age limit for children riding e-bikes on the road, see section 30, item 20 (riding e-bikes on the road under age 16).

### 3. Install a smoke alarm; if you burn coal or use gas for heat indoors in winter, also install a carbon monoxide alarm
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: A smoke alarm costs 30 to 100 yuan. A carbon monoxide alarm costs 50 to 150 yuan. Replace the batteries once a year after installing.
- Plain language: A working smoke alarm cuts the risk of dying in a house fire by about 60%. In US fatal house fires, 41% started while people were asleep. If you burn coal or use gas for heat indoors in winter, add a carbon monoxide alarm. In China in 2018, 11,523 people died of carbon monoxide poisoning, and about seven in ten of those deaths in the winter months happened at home.
- Benefit: Researchers in North Carolina, US, looked back at a group of residential fires and compared households where a fatal fire occurred with those where it did not. A household with a working smoke alarm had about a 60% lower risk of death, odds ratio (OR) 0.39, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.18 to 0.83. Of fatal fires in occupied homes in the US in 2018 to 2020, 24% of homes had no smoke alarm, and 41% of fires started while people were asleep. China reported 11,523 deaths from carbon monoxide poisoning in 2018. Of these, 72.59% in December, 67.42% in January and 66.48% in February occurred at home.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Marshall SW 等 (1998). Fatal residential fires: who dies and who survives? JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.279.20.1633> ; USFA (2022). Fatal Fires in Residential Buildings (2018-2020), Topical Fire Report Series 22(2). <https://www.usfa.fema.gov/downloads/pdf/statistics/v22i2.pdf> ; You J 等 (2020). Number of Deaths due to Carbon Monoxide Poisoning by Month and by Place of Death — China, 2018. China CDC Weekly. <https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2020.008>
- Notes: The reason for grade B: the smoke alarm evidence rests on a single study that looked back at households, not a randomized trial. There are no studies showing that carbon monoxide alarms themselves reduce death rates. The benefit here is inferred from the fact that carbon monoxide poisoning deaths cluster in winter and in the home.

### 4. Replace gas hoses and stoves when they expire; do not alter the pipes yourself; you can refuse door-to-door gas company sales
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: A compliant gas hose costs a few dozen yuan. Stoves need replacing every few years. Check the expiry date when you replace them.
- Plain language: Replacing hoses and stoves when they expire is a duty of the user under China's Urban Gas Management Regulations. Altering pipes or removing gas meters yourself is banned. Gas companies may not require you to buy their designated products, so you can refuse a sales visit that insists you must replace something.
- Benefit: The regulations make it the user's duty to replace gas burners and connecting hoses that have been phased out by the state or have reached the end of their service life. If a stove or hose has reached its age limit or been phased out, you must replace it. The regulations also list installing, modifying or removing indoor gas facilities and gas meters without authorization as prohibited acts, which includes altering pipes or removing gas meters yourself. The regulations also state that gas operators may not require users to buy products they designate or accept services they provide.
- Evidence level: C
- Sources: 国务院 (2010). 城镇燃气管理条例（国务院令第 583 号）第二十七条：「燃气用户应当遵守安全用气规则，使用合格的燃气燃烧器具和气瓶，及时更换国家明令淘汰或者使用年限已届满的燃气燃烧器具、连接管等」；第二十八条禁止行为含「（一）擅自操作公用燃气阀门」「（二）将燃气管道作为负重支架或者接地引线」「（四）擅自安装、改装、拆除户内燃气设施和燃气计量装置」；第二十条燃气经营者不得「（六）要求燃气用户购买其指定的产品或者接受其提供的服务」；第二十九条用户可就收费、服务向燃气管理等部门投诉，「有关部门应当自收到投诉之日起15个工作日内予以处理」. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2010/content_1758214.htm>；深圳市住房和建设局 (2024). 安全用气小知识（二）：「立即关闭家里的燃气总阀门」「迅速开门、开窗进行通风」「一定不要开、关家里的电灯、抽油烟机等电器」「人员应该迅速撤离，转移到安全的地方，并且及时拨打燃气公司的报警电话」. <https://zjj.sz.gov.cn/zcjzts/content/post_11882410.html>；张家港市应急管理局 (2024). 燃气安全使用知识：「不要情急之下打开排气扇进行通风」「应远离现场之后才能打电话求助」. <https://www.zjg.gov.cn/zjgszwz/yjaqzs/202411/72b4fad7399c48378302460c6ce2d55b.shtml>；市场监管总局 (2023). 燃气器具产品质量安全风险提示（鄂州市城建委转载）：「鼓励用户安装使用燃气智能表、燃气泄漏安全保护装置、自闭阀、独立式感烟火灾探测器、可燃气体探测报警器等保护装置」. <https://cjw.ezhou.gov.cn/zxzx/gzdt/202308/t20230821_569108.html>
- Notes: China only: The regulation text can be checked word for word, but it only establishes that replacing expired parts is your duty. It does not show how many accidents replacement would prevent. No official figure was found for how much replacing gas parts by age reduces gas accidents, so this entry is only grade C. If a home inspector asks you to buy a specific shut-off valve or alarm, you can refuse. You can also complain, because Article 20(6) of the regulations bars gas companies from making such demands. The State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) encourages shut-off valves and alarms, so if you install one, choose a certified product yourself. If you smell gas, first close the main gas valve; for bottled gas, close the cylinder valve. Then open doors and windows to ventilate, and do not use an exhaust fan. Do not switch electric lights, range hoods or other appliances on or off, and do not touch a lighter. Leave the area, and only after you are clear of the scene call the gas company's emergency line. Carbon monoxide poisoning is a separate matter; see section 13.

### 5. Do not pick, buy or eat wild mushrooms; no folk test can tell whether a mushroom is safe
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is giving up a seasonal wild food.
- Plain language: In 2025 alone, China's CDC investigated 828 mushroom poisoning incidents, with 2,165 people poisoned and 13 deaths. That year, 34 poisonous species were newly recorded. Folk tests, such as silver-needle tests, insect-eaten mushrooms being safe, or bright colours signalling toxicity, are all false.
- Benefit: The China CDC investigated 828 mushroom poisoning incidents in 2025, involving 2,165 people and causing 13 deaths. The case fatality rate was 0.6%, meaning 0.6% of poisoned people died. From 2019 to 2024, the number of incidents per year ranged from 276 to 676, with case fatality rates between 0.87% and 2.86%. In 2025 alone, 138 poisonous mushroom species were identified, 34 of them recorded in China for the first time as causing poisoning. The China CDC's public guidance rebuts folk identification methods one by one. Bright colours signal toxicity: wrong; toxicity has nothing to do with colour. Mushrooms eaten by insects or with maggots are non-toxic: wrong; many deadly mushrooms are also eaten by insects once mature. Mushrooms that turn silver or change colour when cooked with silver, garlic or rice are toxic: wrong; mushroom toxins do not contain sulphur, and silver blackens because of sulphur. Deep-frying at high heat removes toxins: also unreliable. The conclusion is that "there is currently no simple, easy and fast method to tell whether a mushroom is poisonous." Beijing's market regulator's 2026 notice says that if you feel unwell after eating, "seek medical care immediately and do not handle it yourself," and keep leftover mushrooms and food residue so doctors can identify the toxin.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Mushroom Poisoning Outbreaks — China, 2025. China CDC Weekly (2026)：「In 2025, China CDC investigated 828 mushroom poisoning incidents across 27 provincial-level administrative divisions (PLADs), affecting 2,165 individuals and causing 13 deaths - a case fatality rate of 0.6%, the lowest of the past six years. In total, 138 poisonous mushroom species were identified, including 34 newly recorded in poisoning incidents in China.」「From 2019 to 2024, the annual number of incidents ranged from 276 to 676, and the case fatality rate ranged from 0.87% to 2.86%.」<https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2026.120>；同刊 2024 年度报告 <https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2025.106>；中国疾病预防控制中心职业卫生所 尹萸、李海蛟 (2019). 防控蘑菇中毒，任重而道远：「方法4：虫食或生蛆的蘑菇无毒。错！」「方法6：与银器、大葱、大蒜、大米同煮可使其变色的蘑菇有毒。错！」「因此，目前没有简单易行、快速有效的识别蘑菇是否有毒的方法」. <https://www.chinacdc.cn/jkkp/zyjk/zdzs/201908/t20190802_301109.html>；北京市疾病预防控制中心 (2023). 远离毒蘑菇，不做「菇勇者」：「民间流传辨别毒蘑菇的方法，如看颜色和形状、看生长环境、用银器辨毒、看分泌物、看有无生蛆生虫等均不可靠」. <https://www.bjcdc.org/cdcmodule/jkfw/yyyspjk/jkyf/swzdyf/2023/35518.shtml>；北京市市场监督管理局 (2026). 关于防范误食野生蘑菇中毒的风险提示：「请立即就医，切勿自行处理，以免延误诊疗时机。妥善保存剩余野生蘑菇、食物残渣等样本」. <https://scjgj.beijing.gov.cn/zwxx/scjgdt/202606/t20260625_4715961.html>
- Notes: If you think you have eaten a poisonous mushroom, go to hospital at once; do not treat it at home. Bring the leftover mushrooms, food residue or photos. Mushroom poisoning comes in several types, and identifying the species lets doctors treat it correctly. New species that can poison people are recorded every year, so the idea that locals can tell which ones are safe does not hold up. Silver-needle tests, cooking with garlic, the claim that insect-eaten means safe, and the claim that bright colours mean toxic are all false. Overly soaked wood ear and white fungus, and wet rice noodles left too long, carry a different toxin; see item 48 (soaked wood ear).

### 6. Do not wheel e-bikes into stairwells or lifts, and do not charge them indoors
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is walking a few dozen metres to a shared charging point every time.
- Plain language: Parking or charging e-bikes in the lobby, corridors, stairwells or exits of a high-rise is banned by an emergency-management rule. Those who refuse to correct it can be fined 500 to 1,000 yuan. The bigger problem is that a fire there breaks out on your only escape route.
- Benefit: An Emergency Management Ministry rule expressly prohibits "parking electric bicycles or charging them in the public lobbies, evacuation corridors, stairwells and safety exits of high-rise civil buildings." Those four spaces are ruled out for both parking and charging. Individuals and organisations not doing business who refuse to correct it are fined 500 to 1,000 yuan; businesses are fined 2,000 to 10,000 yuan. The rule does not cover lifts or the inside of homes. The Ministry of Emergency Management, replying to National People's Congress deputies, said that storing and charging e-bikes and batteries indoors in breach of rules is a key target for correcting major fire-safety hazards. It warns the public of the danger of "bikes brought into buildings, wires taken out of homes, and self-dismantling or modification." Lifts are governed by local rules: from 1 May 2025, Beijing bans bringing e-bikes and charging batteries into lift cars, and individuals who refuse to correct this face fines of 500 to 1,000 yuan.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 应急管理部 (2021). 高层民用建筑消防安全管理规定（应急管理部令第 5 号，第三十七条、第四十七条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-08/01/content_5628815.htm>；应急管理部 (2023). 关于政协第十三届全国委员会第五次会议第00969号提案答复的函：「2021年，全国共接报电动自行车火灾1.8万起，造成65人死亡、222人受伤，直接财产损失达2.21亿元，其中发生在建筑物内3782起，造成60人死亡、175人受伤」. <https://www.mem.gov.cn/gk/jytabljggk/zxwytadfzy/2022zx/202301/t20230119_440918.shtml>；应急管理部 (2025). 2025年1月15日新闻发布会：「每百万辆电动自行车火灾数量由2023年的5.6起下降至3起，火灾起数同比下降42.2%，伤亡人数下降超过90%」. <https://www.mem.gov.cn/xw/xwfbh/2025n01y15xwfbh_6337/>；应急管理部 (2024). 对十四届全国人大一次会议第7187号建议的答复：「将电动车（蓄电池）违规在室内停放或充电、未按要求设置防火分区等问题作为消防安全重大风险隐患专项排查整治2023行动整治重点」「向社会宣传提示"车入楼、线出户、自拆改"的火灾危险危害」. <https://www.mem.gov.cn/gk/jytabljggk/rddbjydfzy/2023_5984/202401/t20240130_476731.shtml>；北京市东城区人民政府转载北京消防 (2025). 北京消防条例：电动自行车、电池进电梯，个人最高可罚1000元：「禁止携带电动自行车、充电电池进入电梯轿厢。违规拒不改正的，可对单位处2000元以上1万元以下罚款，对个人处500元以上1000元以下罚款」. <https://www.bjdch.gov.cn/ztzl/aqsczbgjsnxd/zdgz/202504/t20250410_4061604.html>
- Notes: China only: Do not buy modified batteries, do not use off-brand chargers, and do not leave a battery charging overnight unattended. Once a lithium battery catches fire, the whole pack is engulfed within seconds and gives off highly toxic smoke. A bike parked in a stairwell that catches fire burns your only escape route. Figures from the Ministry of Emergency Management: in 2021 there were 18,000 e-bike fires nationally, killing 65 people. Of these, 3,782 fires happened inside buildings and killed 60 people, almost all of them indoors. After the 2024 crackdown, fires fell 42.2% from the year before, and casualties fell by more than 90%. Fires per million e-bikes dropped from 5.6 to 3. The press conference did not give absolute numbers for the full year.

### 7. Measure your blood pressure; if it is high, take medication to bring it to target
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: An electronic blood pressure monitor costs 100 to 200 yuan. A measurement takes 1 minute. Most blood pressure medicines cost a few to a few dozen yuan a month. The hard part is continuing to take them when you feel fine.
- Plain language: Each 10 mmHg drop in systolic pressure cuts the chance of a major heart and blood vessel problem by about 20%, and of stroke by 27%. Heart failure falls by 28%, and the chance of death falls by 13%. In China, 44.7% of adults aged 35 to 75 have high blood pressure, but only 7.2% have it under control.
- Benefit: Combining 123 trials involving more than 610,000 people, each 10 mmHg reduction in systolic blood pressure lowers major cardiovascular events by about 20%, relative risk (RR) 0.80, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.77 to 0.83. Systolic pressure is the higher of the two numbers in a blood pressure reading. Stroke falls by about 27% (RR 0.73). Heart failure falls by about 28% (RR 0.72). All-cause mortality falls by 13% (RR 0.87, 95% CI 0.84 to 0.91). A screening of 1.7 million people aged 35 to 75 in China found 44.7% had high blood pressure. Of these people, 44.7% knew they had it, 30.1% were being treated, and only 7.2% had it under control.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Ettehad D 等 (2016). Blood pressure lowering for prevention of cardiovascular disease and death: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)01225-8> ; Lu J 等 (2017). Prevalence, awareness, treatment, and control of hypertension in China (China PEACE Million Persons Project). Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(17)32478-9>
- Notes: Get your blood pressure measured first. Fewer than one in ten Chinese adults with high blood pressure has it under control, so this single measurement is one of the highest-value steps in this book. What counts as on target (130 or 140) is still debated, but knowing you have high blood pressure and bringing it down is not in dispute.

### 8. Once you are over 35 and overweight, get a fasting blood glucose test; if it is normal, repeat it every three years
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no gain=mid scope=health who=35plus -->
- Cost: A fasting blood glucose test costs a dozen-odd yuan. HbA1c costs a few dozen yuan. One blood draw at a clinic is all it takes.
- Plain language: A blood draw costing a few dozen yuan can detect prediabetes before you have any symptoms. US guidance says adults aged 35 to 70 who are overweight or obese should be screened. If the result is normal, repeat the test every 3 years.
- Benefit: The US Preventive Services Task Force, the official US body that issues screening recommendations, now recommends screening adults aged 35 to 70 who are overweight or obese for prediabetes and type 2 diabetes, even if they have no symptoms. Overweight means a body mass index (BMI) of 25 or more; obese means 30 or more. For Asian Americans, it recommends lowering the cut-off to a BMI of 23 or more, because diabetes risk is higher at the same BMI. China's industry standard defines overweight as a BMI of 24.0 to 28.0 and obesity as 28.0 or more. The recommendation grade is B. It also states: "For adults with normal blood glucose, screening every 3 years is a reasonable approach."
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: US Preventive Services Task Force (2021). Screening for Prediabetes and Type 2 Diabetes. <https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/screening-for-prediabetes-and-type-2-diabetes>：「at a lower BMI (≥23) if the patient is Asian American」；国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2024). 居民体重管理核心知识（2024 年版）释义：「根据《成年人体重判定》（WS/T 428-2013）行业标准……24.0≤BMI＜28.0 为超重，BMI≥28.0 为肥胖」. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202407/P020240703481519509521.pdf>；Wang L 等 (2021). Prevalence and Treatment of Diabetes in China, 2013-2018. JAMA：「In 2018, among adults with diabetes, 36.7% (95% CI, 34.7%-38.6%) reported being aware of their condition」「These rates did not change significantly from 2013.」<https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2021.22208>
- Notes: Finding prediabetes does not mean you must take medication. Eating less, moving more and losing some weight can cut the risk of diabetes by 30% to 60%. In the 2018 national survey, only 36.7% of Chinese adults with diabetes knew they had it, with no clear improvement since 2013. For how much the risk falls, how to change your habits, and how often to recheck after a finding, see section 2, item 45 (prediabetes findings).

### 9. Do not speed or drive drunk, do not look at your phone, and stop to rest when you are drowsy
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. Driving more slowly adds a few minutes per trip. The hard parts are drinking less at dinners and resisting the urge to check messages. On long drives, stop for at least 20 minutes every 4 hours.
- Plain language: Each 1% rise in average speed raises the chance of a fatal crash by about 4%. There is no safe amount of alcohol for driving. Handling a phone while driving makes a crash 3.6 times as likely, and dialling 12.2 times. Drowsiness that others can see makes a crash about 3.4 times as likely.
- Benefit: The World Health Organization (WHO) says that each 1% rise in average speed increases the risk of a fatal crash by 4%. The risk from drink driving starts rising from a very low blood alcohol level. WHO also says drivers using a mobile phone are about 4 times as likely to be in a crash as drivers not using one, and that hands-free phones are not much safer than hand-held ones. ### 10. Use a car seat for children under 4 instead of holding them on your lap
<!-- tags: money=high time=low will=no gain=high scope=health who=infant,child,pregnancy -->
- Cost: A seat costs 300 to 2,000 yuan (RMB) and lasts several years. Buckling a child in adds about 1 minute to each trip.
- Plain language: For babies under 1 year old, a car seat lowers the chance of a fatal injury in a crash by about 70%. For children aged 1 to 4, the drop is about half. Holding a child on your lap gives no such protection.
- Benefit: The US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) estimates that car seats cut the risk of a fatal injury for babies under 1 year old in passenger cars by 71%. For toddlers aged 1 to 4, the cut is 54%. The World Health Organization (WHO) puts the drop in infant deaths at 71%.
- Evidence level: A

- Notes: Install the seat as the manual says. A wrong installation is no installation. Babies under 1 must ride rear-facing, facing the back of the car. The figures above were estimated by US officials from crash data.

### 11. If there are children at home, fit window restrictors and balcony guards; a window screen is not protection

- Cost: A window restrictor or child safety lock costs a few dozen yuan (RMB). Fitting one takes about ten minutes.
- Plain language: A restrictor costs a few dozen yuan. New York City gave high-risk households free window guards and home visits. In one borough, reported child falls dropped by half between 1973 and 1975. New York later passed a law requiring landlords to install guards in homes with children aged 10 and under. A window screen is not protection.

In the Bronx, reported falls fell 50% from 1973 to 1975. New York City then amended its health code to require landlords to install window guards in apartments where children aged 10 and under live.

### 12. Keep children within sight near water; wear life jackets for boating and open-water swimming

### 13. Adults over 60: practice balance and leg strength, and adapt the bathroom and stairs at home
<!-- tags: money=low time=high will=high gain=high scope=health who=elderly,60plus -->

### 14. Test for hepatitis B markers, check your vaccination record, and decide whether you need a catch-up dose
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: A test costs a few dozen yuan. An adult who needs vaccination: three hepatitis B shots cost about 100 to 300 yuan in total, depending on the local clinic. The usual schedule is at months 0, 1 and 6.
- Plain language: In Qidong, China, babies vaccinated against hepatitis B at birth later had an 84% lower risk of liver cancer. Adults cannot expect that same drop. Low antibody levels alone are not a reason for three shots. If you finished the full course and have normal immunity, you usually need no booster. If you are unsure of your record, see a vaccination clinic.
- Benefit: The Qidong trial randomized communities rather than individuals, and followed people for 30 years. Neonatal hepatitis B vaccination reduced primary liver cancer by 84% (95% CI 23% to 97%). It reduced the HBsAg positivity rate by 72% (95% CI 68% to 75%). HBsAg positivity is the share of people carrying the virus in their blood. In the control group, 67.8% of people received a catch-up dose at ages 10 to 14. Catch-up vaccination was only 21% effective at reducing HBsAg positivity in early adulthood (95% CI 10% to 30%), far weaker than vaccination at birth. Across China's whole population, HBsAg positivity fell 52% between 1992 and 2014, and 97% among children under 5. These figures cannot be applied directly to adult catch-up vaccination. People with normal immunity who completed the course and once had protective antibodies keep immune memory after the antibodies fade, so they usually need no booster. Immune memory means the body can mobilize its defenses quickly when it meets the virus again. Whether to vaccinate should be judged from vaccination records, surface antigen, surface antibody and core antibody results. If the vaccination history is unclear, see a vaccination clinic.
- Evidence level: A

- Notes: Adults who were not vaccinated, did not finish the course, or are unsure of their history should bring their vaccination record and test results to a vaccination clinic. ### 15. If a nail or splinter pierces your skin, or a wound gets dirt in it, get it treated that day and ask whether you need a tetanus shot
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Registration and wound treatment cost a few dozen yuan. If the doctor says you need a shot, the vaccine or immunoglobulin costs a few dozen to a few hundred yuan.
- Plain language: In the US, about 1 in 10 people who develop tetanus die from it. Deep wounds and wounds contaminated with soil carry the highest risk. If the wound is dirty and your last shot was five or more years ago, you need one booster. Treating the wound the same day costs only a few dozen yuan, so do not delay.
- Benefit: In US tetanus cases, about 1 in 10 is fatal. Deep wounds and wounds contaminated with soil carry a higher risk. China's National Health Commission 2024 tetanus guideline (non-neonatal) splits wounds into two risk groups. Low risk: a shallow wound treated within 6 hours and not contaminated with soil, feces or animal saliva. High risk: a wound treated more than 6 hours after the injury, or contaminated with those substances, or a puncture, avulsion, crush, burn or frostbite wound, or a wound with foreign matter left inside. If you have had three or more doses and the last one was less than 5 years ago, you need nothing. If the last one was 5 to 10 years ago, a high-risk wound needs one booster. If it was 10 years or more ago, a booster is needed for either risk group. If you did not complete the three doses or cannot remember, complete the vaccine series. High-risk wounds also need passive immunization with immunoglobulin or antitoxin, which gives antibodies directly.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Tetanus: "Tetanus bacteria can get into someone's body through broken skin, usually through injuries." "Tetanus can lead to death (1 in 10 cases in the United States are fatal)." "People who didn't complete the primary series or who aren't up to date with their 10-year tetanus booster shots are also at increased risk." "Vaccination also helps prevent tetanus in people with wounds, depending on their tetanus vaccination history." <https://www.cdc.gov/tetanus/about/index.html>；国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2024). 关于印发非新生儿破伤风诊疗规范（2024 年版）的通知（国卫办医急函〔2024〕381 号，风险分级见附件第 16 页，表 4 见第 20 页）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202410/content_6982262.htm>；附件 PDF（扫描件）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202410/P020241023483425528555.pdf>
- Notes: Let a doctor judge at the hospital. Do not decide on your own, and do not skip asking because it seems like a hassle. Not every wound needs a shot. The doctor will check which risk group the wound falls into, then ask when you last had a shot, and decide. "A shot in childhood protects you for life" is wrong. For a dirty wound, a booster is needed once five years have passed since the last shot. So before you go, think about which year you last had a tetanus shot.

### 16. Girls and women: get the HPV vaccine as early as possible
<!-- tags: money=high time=low will=no gain=high scope=health who=women,child -->
- Cost: A domestic bivalent vaccine costs over 300 yuan per dose, and an imported nonavalent vaccine costs about 1,300 yuan per dose. There are 2 to 3 doses in total. Each visit takes half a day.
- Plain language: A Swedish study followed 1.67 million women. Women vaccinated against HPV before age 17 were about one-eighth as likely to develop invasive cervical cancer as unvaccinated women. Those vaccinated between ages 17 and 30 had roughly half the risk. The earlier the better.
- Benefit: A Swedish study tracked 1.67 million women. Among vaccinated women, the rate of invasive cervical cancer was lower than among unvaccinated women. Vaccination before age 17 reduced it by about 88% (raw IRR 0.12, 95% CI 0.00 to 0.34). Vaccination between ages 17 and 30 reduced it by about 53% (IRR 0.47, 95% CI 0.27 to 0.75). ### 18. Women over 30: get cervical cancer screening, with an HPV test first

A single HPV screening test lowered the risk of dying from cervical cancer by about 48% (HR 0.52, 95% CI 0.33 to 0.83). It lowered the risk of advanced cervical cancer by about 53% (HR 0.47, 95% CI 0.32 to 0.69). The interval follows the 2018 US Preventive Services Task Force recommendation: for women aged 30 to 65, HPV testing for high-risk types alone every 5 years.

### 19. From age 45 to 50, get colorectal cancer screening: a fecal immunochemical test (FIT) for hidden blood, or a colonoscopy
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no gain=mid scope=health who=35plus -->
- Cost: A stool test costs a few dozen yuan and is done every 1 to 2 years. A colonoscopy costs a few hundred to over 1,000 yuan; if the result is normal, one every 10 years is enough. Set aside a day for bowel preparation before a colonoscopy.
- Plain language: A stool blood test every one or two years, costing a few dozen yuan, lowers the chance of dying from colorectal cancer by about 16%. For people who have had at least one round, the reduction is about 25%. For people invited to have a colonoscopy, the 10-year chance of developing colorectal cancer fell from 1.20% to 0.98%.
- Benefit: Cochrane pooled several studies. People screened with stool blood tests had about a 16% lower risk of dying from colorectal cancer (RR 0.84, 95% CI 0.78 to 0.90). People who took part in at least one round had about a 25% lower risk (RR 0.75). Looking at the NordICC randomized trial: among people invited to have a colonoscopy, colorectal cancer incidence over 10 years fell from 1.20% to 0.98% (RR 0.82, 95% CI 0.70 to 0.93).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Hewitson P et al. (2007). Screening for colorectal cancer using the faecal occult blood test, Hemoccult. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001216.pub2> ; Bretthauer M et al. (2022). - Notes: Disputed. NordICC counts people who were invited to have a colonoscopy. Colorectal cancer deaths were 0.28% versus 0.31% (RR 0.90, 95% CI 0.64 to 1.16), and the difference is within the margin of error. Guidelines start screening between ages 45 and 50, depending on the country. People with a family history of this disease should start earlier. ### 20. Adults with heart disease and older adults should get a flu shot every year
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Each shot costs RMB 50 to 150. Get one every autumn.
- Plain language: In a trial of people after a heart attack, the share who died within a year fell from 4.9% to 2.9% after a flu shot. If you have heart disease or are older, get a shot each autumn for RMB 50 to 150.
- Benefit: One trial was run after myocardial infarction (heart attack) and randomised 2,571 people. Neither the doctors nor the patients knew which injection was given. Over 12 months, total mortality was 2.9% in the flu-vaccine group and 4.9% in the placebo group. A placebo is an inactive control shot with no active ingredient. The reported HR was 0.59 (95% CI 0.39 to 0.89). The HR for cardiovascular death was 0.59. A second study pooled several trials. Major cardiovascular events occurred in 3.6% of vaccinated people and 5.4% of controls. The reported RR was 0.66 (95% CI 0.53 to 0.83). The RR for cardiovascular death was 0.74 (95% CI 0.42 to 1.30). That difference falls within the margin of error, so it is not statistically significant.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Fröbert O 等 (2021). Influenza Vaccination After Myocardial Infarction: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled, Multicenter Trial. - Notes: Disputed. Grade A applies only to people with heart disease. For otherwise healthy older adults, the Cochrane review reaches only one conclusion: flu cases in a season fell from 6% to 2.4%. For pregnant women, see section 27, item 18 (inactivated flu vaccine during pregnancy). For people with COPD, see section 16, item 12 (inhaled medicines for COPD).

### 21. Get the shingles vaccine after age 50
<!-- tags: money=high time=low will=no gain=mid scope=health who=elderly,35plus -->
- Cost: The recombinant shingles vaccine takes two doses, about RMB 3,000 to 4,000 in total. You pay all of it yourself.
- Plain language: In a phase 3 trial, this vaccine prevented 97% of shingles cases, and it worked about equally well in every age group. The downside is the price: two shots you pay for entirely yourself, about RMB 3,000 to 4,000. What that money buys is a 97% lower rate of shingles.
- Benefit: A phase 3 trial spanned 18 countries and enrolled 15,400 people. Participants were randomly assigned to a vaccine group or a control-shot group, and followed for an average of 3.2 years. The vaccine group had 6 cases of shingles and the placebo group had 210. Per 1,000 person-years, that is 0.3 versus 9.1. A person-year is one person observed for one year, so per 1,000 person-years means 1,000 people each observed for one year. The paper reports: "The overall efficacy of the vaccine against herpes zoster was 97.2% (95% CI 93.7–99.0, P<0.001)." Efficacy across the age groups ranged from 96.6% to 97.9%. In the 7 days after the shot, injection-site and whole-body reactions were more common in the vaccine group. Grade 3 symptoms (the most severe category) were reported by 17.0% of the vaccine group and 3.2% of the placebo group. The shares with serious adverse events or deaths were similar in the two groups.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Lal H, Cunningham AL, Godeaux O, et al. (2015). Efficacy of an adjuvanted herpes zoster subunit vaccine in older adults. New England Journal of Medicine, 372(22), 2087-2096：「There were solicited or unsolicited reports of grade 3 symptoms in 17.0% of vaccine recipients and 3.2% of placebo recipients. The proportions of participants who had serious adverse events or potential immune-mediated diseases or who died were similar in the two groups.」<https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1501184>
- Notes: Its value for money is not high, mainly because it is expensive. Shingles itself rarely kills. The real problem is postherpetic neuralgia, nerve pain that can last months to years. In the week after the shot, injection-site and whole-body reactions were more common than after placebo. Grade 3 symptoms were reported by 17.0% versus 3.2%, and serious adverse events were similar in both groups.

### 22. Get a pneumococcal vaccine after age 65
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no gain=mid scope=health who=elderly,60plus -->
- Cost: A few hundred RMB. Some areas offer it free to older adults.
- Plain language: In a trial of 84,500 older adults, a 13-valent pneumococcal vaccine prevented about 45.6% of pneumonia caused by the strains it covers. It prevented 75% of the most dangerous invasive infections. It showed no effect on pneumonia from all causes, so do not expect it to prevent all pneumonia.
- Benefit: In the Netherlands, 84,500 people aged 65 and over were randomly assigned to two groups for comparison. The vaccine was a 13-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine. Its efficacy against vaccine-type pneumonia was 45.6% (95.2% CI 21.8–62.5). Vaccine types are the strains this vaccine covers. Its efficacy against invasive pneumococcal disease was 75.0% (95% CI 41.4–90.8). This is the most dangerous category of infection. For pneumonia from all causes combined, the vaccine had no effect.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Bonten MJ, Huijts SM, Bolkenbaas M, et al. (2015). Polysaccharide conjugate vaccine against pneumococcal pneumonia in adults. New England Journal of Medicine, 372(12), 1114-1125. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1408544>
- Notes: It only protects against the vaccine's covered strains, not against all pneumonia. The 23-valent polysaccharide vaccine that older adults in China often get is not the same as the 13-valent conjugate vaccine used in this trial. ### 23. Test for Helicobacter pylori, and eradicate it if positive
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: A carbon-13 or carbon-14 breath test costs RMB 100 to 200. Eradication means taking four drugs together for two weeks, costing RMB 200 to 500 in medicine. The hard part is taking every dose on schedule for the full two weeks.
- Plain language: In a trial in Linqu, Shandong, followed for 22 years, people who cleared H. pylori later had about half the risk of stomach cancer. Their risk of dying from stomach cancer was about 38% lower. A test costs RMB 100 to 200, and clearing the infection takes two weeks of medicine.
- Benefit: The randomised trial in Linqu, Shandong, followed people for 22 years. People who received H. pylori eradication treatment had about half the risk of developing stomach cancer. The OR was 0.48 (95% CI 0.32 to 0.71). Their risk of dying from stomach cancer was about 38% lower (HR 0.62, 95% CI 0.39 to 0.99).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Li WQ 等 (2019). Effects of Helicobacter pylori treatment and vitamin and garlic supplementation on gastric cancer incidence and mortality: follow-up of a randomized intervention trial. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.l5016>
- Notes: After eradication, reinfection is still possible, so family members should be tested and treated together. This trial enrolled residents of an area with high stomach cancer rates. People who live in low-incidence areas would likely gain much less. Eradication does not replace endoscopy. If you are over 45 and at high risk, see item 47 (get a gastroscopy screening).

### 24. Heavy smokers should get a low-dose chest CT once a year
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no gain=high scope=health who=35plus -->
- Cost: RMB 200 to 400 per scan, and it takes about 10 minutes. Also factor in follow-up checks after a false alarm, and the anxiety of waiting for them.
- Plain language: For heavy smokers who qualify, a yearly low-dose CT lowers the chance of dying from lung cancer by 20% compared with a chest X-ray. The overall chance of death falls by 6.7%. The cost is follow-up checks after a suspicious result, and the anxious wait during that time.
- Benefit: The US National Lung Screening Trial (NLST) enrolled 53,454 people aged 55 to 74 with more than 30 pack-years of smoking who had quit no more than 15 years earlier. A pack-year measures cumulative smoking: one pack a day for one year equals one pack-year. Compared with chest X-ray, low-dose CT produced a relative reduction of 20.0% in lung cancer mortality (95% CI 6.8 to 26.7). All-cause mortality fell by a relative 6.7% (95% CI 1.2 to 13.6). Each person was screened for three years. Across the three rounds, 24.2% of low-dose CT results were suspicious, and 96.4% of those suspicious results were false positives.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: National Lung Screening Trial Research Team (2011). Reduced Lung-Cancer Mortality with Low-Dose Computed Tomographic Screening. New England Journal of Medicine：「The rate of positive screening tests was 24.2% with low-dose CT and 6.9% with radiography over all three rounds. A total of 96.4% of the positive screening results in the low-dose CT group and 94.5% in the radiography group were false positive results.」<https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1102873>
- Notes: This applies only to high-risk smokers. In the same trial, 24.2% of low-dose CT results were suspicious across three rounds, and 96.4% of those were false positives. Non-smokers were not in the trial, so it cannot show whether screening helps them.

### 25. Call 12356 when depressed or suicidal, and do not stockpile sleeping pills or pesticides at home
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. It takes one phone call. Also lock up medicines, and do not keep pesticides at home.
- Plain language: Taking dangerous means out of reach has increasingly strong evidence behind it. Controlling painkillers cut related suicides by 43%, and protective barriers at places where people often jump cut them by 86%. So keep sleeping pills and pesticides out of the house. If you feel like ending your life, call 12356, which runs at least 18 hours a day.
- Benefit: A systematic review of the past 10 years of research found growing evidence that restricting access to lethal means works. After controls on painkillers, painkiller-related suicides fell by 43%. At sites where people often jump, adding protective barriers cut suicides by 86% (confidence interval 79% to 91%). A school-based cognitive program reduced suicide attempts by about 55% (OR 0.45, 95% CI 0.24 to 0.85). Antidepressant medicines and psychotherapy are an important part of suicide prevention. From 1 May 2025, China's 12356 national mental health hotline has been reachable nationwide, with service at least 18 hours a day.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Zalsman G 等 (2016). Suicide prevention strategies revisited: 10-year systematic review. Lancet Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(16)30030-X> ; 国家卫生健康委 (2024). 关于应用"12356"全国统一心理援助热线电话号码的通知（国卫医政函〔2024〕259 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202412/content_6994470.htm>
- Notes: China only: Rated B because most of the evidence on restricting means comes from ecological studies, which compare figures before and after a policy took effect. For data on this timescale and long-term outcomes after an attempt, see item 32 of this section (tell one person around you as soon as suicidal thoughts appear). For what happens after someone is rescued, see item 33 of this section.

### 26. Keep a fire extinguisher, fire blanket, escape mask and first-aid kit at home, and check them once a year

### 27. Get checked for visible blood in your urine, even if it does not hurt or clears up the next day
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no gain=low scope=health -->
- Cost: A urine test costs a few dozen RMB, and a urinary ultrasound costs RMB 100 to 200. If the doctor thinks it is needed, a cystoscopy adds about RMB 1,000.
- Plain language: In UK primary-care records, about 2.8% of people over 60 with visible blood in their urine were later found to have bladder cancer. For people aged 40 to 59 the figure was about 1.2%. Even a rate this size justifies a check. Get checked even if it happened once, in a small amount, and without pain.
- Benefit: The UK study used electronic records from community GP (primary care) practices, comparing 4,915 bladder cancer cases with 21,718 controls. Among people with visible blood in urine, this is the proportion who were actually diagnosed with bladder cancer. Over 60, it was 2.8% (95% CI 2.5 to 3.1). Aged 40 to 59, it was 1.2% (95% CI 0.6 to 2.3). This proportion is called the positive predictive value. Blood visible only under a microscope also matters: the odds ratio was 20 (95% CI 12 to 33). For people over 60 with blood seen only under a microscope, the proportion was 1.6% (95% CI 1.2 to 2.1). The UK's National Health Service (NHS) says blood in urine can be a sign of cancer and must be checked. Book a doctor's appointment promptly even with no other symptoms, if it is the first time, or if the amount is small.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Price SJ, Shephard EA, Stapley SA, Barraclough K, Hamilton WT (2014). Non-visible versus visible haematuria and bladder cancer risk: a study of electronic records in primary care. British Journal of General Practice, 64(626), e584-e589. <https://doi.org/10.3399/bjgp14X681409>；NHS. - Notes: Pain is not a reason to wait. The NHS says blood in urine must be checked promptly, even with no other symptoms, if it is the first time, if the amount is small, or if you are not sure it is blood. Painful urination, frequent urination or fever may point to a urinary tract infection. Severe pain that comes in waves from the side to the groin, with restlessness, may be a stone. The NHS also says not to self-diagnose; let a doctor look. Red urine may not be blood if it follows eating beetroot, starting a new medicine, or happens during a period. For people on anticoagulants, do not automatically assume the drug is the cause. This is the author's advice; the studies above did not analyse this group separately. Benefit is rated "small" because the study gives only a detection rate, not an effect size for how much earlier checks reduce deaths.

### 28. Erectile problems warrant a check of your heart and blood vessels first, not just the sexual side

- Cost: Registration costs a few dozen RMB. A blood pressure check and blood tests for glucose and lipids cost RMB 100 to 200. The visit takes a trip to the hospital and several days to get results.
- Plain language: Men with erectile dysfunction later had about 44% more cardiovascular events than men without it, including about 62% more heart attacks. The gap was larger in younger men. So check your blood pressure, fasting blood sugar and blood lipids first, instead of trying to fix the problem with self-bought pills.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis pooled 14 cohort studies with 92,757 men, followed for an average of 6.1 years. Compared with men without erectile dysfunction, men with it had these later risks. For cardiovascular death, the gap did not reach significance (RR 1.19, 0.97 to 1.46). The ratios were higher in younger men. Men at moderate baseline cardiovascular risk had higher ratios than both high-risk and low-risk men. Studies that defined erectile dysfunction by questionnaire found a ratio of 1.61 (1.38 to 1.86). Studies that asked a single question found 1.27 (1.18 to 1.37).

### 29. Losing weight, quitting smoking and controlling blood pressure and blood sugar can improve erectile function
<!-- tags: money=0 time=high will=high gain=low scope=health -->

### 30. Use a condom for the whole of every sex act, and never share needles
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: One to two yuan (RMB) per condom. The hard part is not the money. It is using one every single time, and putting it on from the start.
- Plain language: Wear one all the way through every time, and between men and women the chance of passing on HIV falls by more than about 70%. Using one "most of the time" works not much better than not using one at all. Condoms also protect against other STIs, but they do not cover syphilis or genital herpes sores on skin the condom does not reach.
- Benefit: One study pooled 25 studies covering 10,676 heterosexual couples. In each couple, one partner had HIV and the other did not; the technical term is serodiscordant couples. Compared with people who never used condoms, people who used them every time had about 71% lower risk of HIV transmission (RR 0.29, 95% CI 0.20 to 0.43). Compared with people who used them on and off, the risk was about 77% lower (RR 0.23, 95% CI 0.13 to 0.40). The authors concluded that condoms are not perfect, but "consistent use can reduce HIV transmission by more than 70%." The World Health Organization says that correct and consistent condom use is one of the most effective ways to prevent sexually transmitted infections, including HIV. However, condoms do not protect against ulcers in places they do not cover, such as syphilis and genital herpes.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Giannou FK, Tsiara CG, Nikolopoulos GK, et al. (2016). Condom effectiveness in reducing heterosexual HIV transmission: a systematic review and meta-analysis of studies on HIV serodiscordant couples. Expert Review of Pharmacoeconomics & Outcomes Research, 16(4), 489-499. <https://doi.org/10.1586/14737167.2016.1102635>；Fonner VA 等 (2016). Effectiveness and safety of oral HIV preexposure prophylaxis for all populations. AIDS. <https://doi.org/10.1097/QAD.0000000000001145>（暴露前预防 PrEP）；WHO (2025). Sexually transmitted infections (STIs) fact sheet：「When used correctly and consistently, condoms offer one of the most effective methods of protection against STIs, including HIV. Although highly effective, condoms do not offer protection for STIs that cause extra-genital ulcers (i.e., syphilis or genital herpes).」<https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/sexually-transmitted-infections-(stis)>
- Notes: "Irregular use" and "no use" differ only slightly, so condoms must be used every time, for the whole act, to work. Sharing needles is a separate high-risk route that has nothing to do with sex, including injecting drugs, tattooing and piercing. High-risk people can also use pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP), taking drugs in advance to block infection. Pooling 18 studies, in trials where more than 70% of people took their pills on schedule, people taking PrEP had about 70% lower risk of HIV infection than those on placebo (risk ratio 0.30, 95% CI 0.21 to 0.45). In trials where few people took the pills on time, no protective effect was seen. PrEP is a prescription drug. Ask at an infectious diseases clinic or your local disease control centre, and do not buy it online yourself. For what to do after an exposure, see section 13.

### 31. After any high-risk behaviour, get an HIV test once: the disease control centre tests for free and keeps results confidential
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. Go to a disease control centre or a designated medical facility and have a blood sample taken. The hard part is walking in and asking for the test.
- Plain language: Testing for HIV at a disease control centre costs nothing. No one may disclose your name, address, workplace or medical history without your consent. ### 36. Do not pick up, take apart or sell metal parts of unknown origin, and keep well away from anything bearing the trefoil warning sign

- Plain language: Radiation is invisible, and a radioactive source is a solid piece sealed inside a small metal case, so "it looks harmless" is no test. - Benefit: The National Nuclear Safety Administration (NNSA) explains that a radioactive source is solid radioactive material permanently sealed in a container or enclosed in a tight casing. ### 41. Before going onto natural ice, drill and measure its thickness. Do not go on if it is under 15 cm, and stay off river ice and away from ice-fishing holes.

### 43. Ask someone who seems upset, "Have you ever thought about suicide?" Asking does not make them more likely to do it.
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=low scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. You ask one question, then take time to listen. The hard part is starting the conversation, since many people fear that asking will bring the thought on.
- Plain language: Research has found no sign that asking about suicide plants the idea. In a survey given to high school students, including a suicide question did not make them more distressed, and it did not increase suicidal thoughts. Students who already had depressive symptoms were actually less distressed after being asked, and students who had attempted suicide before had fewer suicidal thoughts afterwards.
- Benefit: Six high schools in New York State randomly divided 2,342 students into two groups by class. One group's survey included a suicide question; the other's did not. Immediately after and two days later, distress levels did not differ between the groups (P = .66 and P = .41; a P value above 0.05 means no detectable difference). Two days later, 4.7% of students who had been asked reported having thought about suicide, versus 3.9% of those not asked; no difference (P = .49). Students who already had depressive symptoms were less distressed when asked (P = .01). Students with a past suicide attempt had fewer suicidal thoughts when asked (P = .02). A 2022 meta-analysis reviewed 17 studies and pooled 8 that compared "asked" with "not asked". After being asked, there was no significant increase in suicidal thoughts or behaviour, self-harm not intended as suicide, or psychological distress, either immediately or later. The strongest evidence comes from 8 randomised controlled trials with low or unclear risk of bias. A 2014 literature review also wrote that no study had found a significant increase in suicidal thoughts after asking.

### 44. Get an ECG if you have palpitations or an irregular heartbeat. If atrial fibrillation is found, ask a doctor about anticoagulants to prevent stroke. Do not use aspirin as a substitute.
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: An ECG costs a few tens of yuan. Warfarin is cheap but requires regular blood tests to adjust the dose. Newer anticoagulants do not need frequent blood tests but cost more. The hard part is taking them every day even with no symptoms, and telling your doctor before tooth extractions or surgery that you take an anticoagulant.
- Plain language: In atrial fibrillation the heartbeat is chaotic, blood can pool and clot in the heart, and a clot that reaches the brain causes a stroke. Taking anticoagulants as your doctor advises cuts strokes by about 60% and deaths by about 30%. Aspirin-type drugs cut strokes by only about 20%, so they cannot replace them. In a 2014 to 2016 survey in China, only 6% of people at high risk of atrial fibrillation were taking anticoagulants.
- Benefit: In atrial fibrillation the atria quiver, blood pools in them, and clots can form. A clot that reaches the brain's blood vessels causes a stroke. The most common symptoms are palpitations, tiredness on exertion and chest discomfort, but about 25% of people report no symptoms. A single-lead ECG of 30 seconds or more, or a standard 12-lead ECG of 10 seconds or more, confirms the diagnosis. A 2007 meta-analysis pooled 29 randomised trials with 28,044 people. Compared with no drug, dose-adjusted warfarin cut strokes by about 60% (64%, 95% CI 49% to 74%). Antiplatelet drugs such as aspirin cut them by about 20% (22%, 95% CI 6% to 35%). Compared head to head, warfarin cut strokes by a further 39% relative to antiplatelet drugs. Absolute increases in major bleeding outside the brain were no more than 0.3% per year. A Cochrane review looked only at 2,313 people who had never had a stroke. Warfarin lowered death by about 30% (OR 0.69, 95% CI 0.50 to 0.94). Four large trials, 71,683 people in total, compared newer oral anticoagulants with warfarin. The new drugs cut stroke and systemic embolism by a further 19% (RR 0.81) and death by 10% (RR 0.90). Intracranial bleeding fell by 52% (RR 0.48), but gastrointestinal bleeding rose by 25% (RR 1.25). Aspirin cannot replace anticoagulants. In 5,599 people who could not take warfarin, apixaban cut strokes and embolisms by 55% compared with aspirin (HR 0.45). Major bleeding was 1.4% versus 1.2% per year, with no detectable difference (HR 1.13, 95% CI 0.74 to 1.75). Another trial enrolled only 973 people over 75. Combining fatal or disabling stroke, intracranial bleeding and arterial embolism, warfarin was 52% lower than aspirin (RR 0.48). Bleeding outside the brain was 1.4% versus 1.6% per year. The guideline does not recommend antiplatelet drugs to prevent stroke caused by atrial fibrillation. The 2023 US guideline states that the decision to take anticoagulants depends on stroke risk, not on whether atrial fibrillation comes and goes or is continuous. ### 47. If you are over 45 and live in an area with high rates of stomach or oesophageal cancer, have a family history of these cancers, or test positive for H. pylori, get a gastroscopy screening.
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no gain=mid scope=health who=35plus -->
- Cost: An ordinary gastroscopy costs a few hundred yuan, and a painless gastroscopy adds a few hundred more; prices vary by region. You must go on an empty stomach, which takes half a day including preparation. If you choose painless gastroscopy, have someone come with you. The hard part is the few minutes when the tube passes the throat.
- Plain language: For people over 45 at high risk of stomach or oesophageal cancer, those who have had a gastroscopy were about half as likely to die from either cancer. But people who choose to be screened are already more health-conscious, so this figure is probably overstated. Using the strictest comparison, oesophageal cancer deaths fell by only about 20%, within the margin of error. The guideline does not recommend screening for people outside the high-risk groups.
- Benefit: The National Health Commission's 2024 programme defines the high-risk groups. First, you must be at least 45 and meet at least one of the following. One: you live in a county with high rates of stomach or oesophageal cancer; the threshold is an age-standardised incidence above 20 cases per 100,000 people for stomach cancer and above 15 for oesophageal cancer. Two: a parent, child or sibling has had one of these cancers. Three: a positive H. pylori test, which counts only for stomach cancer. Four: smoking, heavy drinking, or eating salty or pickled food; for oesophageal cancer, also eating very hot food. Five: a history of atrophic gastritis, gastric ulcer, gastric polyps, Barrett's oesophagus or reflux oesophagitis. Screening ages are generally 45 to 74; gastroscopy is the preferred method, normally repeated every 5 years. The 2022 national guideline explicitly does not recommend stomach cancer screening for people outside the high-risk group. A study in six high-incidence regions in China followed about 637,500 people aged 40 to 69. People who had one gastroscopy had a 57% lower risk of dying from upper gastrointestinal cancer than people who were not invited (RR 0.43, 95% CI 0.40 to 0.47). Incidence of upper gastrointestinal cancer was about 23% lower (RR 0.77). Only 33.53% of those invited actually attended. Counting all invited people, mortality was about 31% lower (RR 0.69, 95% CI 0.66 to 0.72). Another study, in a village with high oesophageal cancer rates, was not randomised and followed people for 10 years. Cumulative oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma mortality was 3.35% in the screened group and 5.05% in the control group. In Korea's national screening programme, people who had gastroscopy were about 47% less likely to die of stomach cancer (OR 0.53, 95% CI 0.51 to 0.56). Barium meal screening showed no difference (OR 0.98). More gastroscopies were associated with lower risk. Pooling 10 studies from Asia, people who had gastroscopy had about 40% lower stomach cancer mortality (RR 0.60, 95% CI 0.49 to 0.73). The number of stomach cancers did not change. A village-randomised trial in northern China enrolled 33,800 people aged 45 to 69 and followed them for 9 years. In the screening group, oesophageal cancer mortality was about 18% lower (HR 0.82, 95% CI 0.53 to 1.26), within the margin of error. Incidence was about 19% lower (HR 0.81, 95% CI 0.60 to 1.09), also not significant. A 2023 meta-analysis adjusted for the bias that people who volunteer for screening are already healthier. Among those who actually had gastroscopy, stomach cancer mortality was about 48% lower (RR 0.52, 95% CI 0.39 to 0.79). Counting the whole screening programme, only RR 0.94 remained (95% CI 0.71 to 1.28), showing no effect. (China / Korea / Asia)
- Evidence level: A

### 48. Eat soaked wood ear and white fungus the same day and do not keep them overnight; buy wet rice noodles such as ho fun or rice rolls for same-day eating; do not make your own fermented corn flour
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. Soak only as much wood ear and white fungus as you will eat, and throw away any leftovers. Buy only as much wet rice noodles as you can finish that day. The hard part is hating to throw away soaked food, and some families are used to making their own sour-soup corn-flour dumplings (suantangzi).
- Plain language: Over-soaked wood ear and white fungus, old wet rice noodles, and home-fermented corn flour can grow a toxin called bongkrekic acid. Boiling does not destroy it, and there is no antidote. Over the 11 years from 2010 to 2020, about 30% of people who fell ill from this poisoning in China died.
- Benefit: In China's national foodborne disease outbreak surveillance, 19 bongkrekic acid poisoning outbreaks were reported from 2010 to 2020. There were 146 cases and 43 deaths, a case-fatality rate of 29.5% (29.5% of those who fell ill died). By food: home-fermented corn flour products caused 9 outbreaks and 33 deaths. Wet rice noodles caused 5 outbreaks and 7 deaths. Wood ear fungus caused 3 outbreaks, 5 people ill and 3 deaths. In 2020, nationwide food poisoning deaths totalled 143, of which 12 were from bongkrekic acid. Ranked above it were poisonous mushrooms (80 deaths) and methanol (14 deaths). The Beijing Market Supervision Administration warns that bongkrekic acid is not destroyed by boiling at 100°C or by pressure cooking, and there is currently no specific antidote. Guangdong began requiring small sealed packs of wet rice noodles with clear shelf-life labels in 2018. Before and after, the province's average annual number of cases dropped from 6.25 to 0.5.
