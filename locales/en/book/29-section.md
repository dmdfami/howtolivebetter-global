# 29. After a Major Blow

Death of a loved one, a serious illness diagnosis, losing a job, divorce. Research finds one thing these events share: it is not only your mood that suffers, but also your body. The risk is highest in the first few weeks to first year. This section does not explain how to "get over" these events. It covers which things someone must keep an eye on during this period, which decisions must be postponed, and when to see a doctor. Most items calculate the probability of death. The four items about money and benefits calculate money: do not start paid grief counselling right away (item 9 of this section); call 12356 (China's national mental health hotline) and see a psychiatric outpatient clinic (item 11 of this section); postpone irreversible big decisions (item 12 of this section); and do not use death as a way to pay off debts (item 13 of this section). The two kinds of numbers are not converted into each other. If you have no relatives or friends to lean on, how to make "someone keeps an eye on you" work in practice is in item 6 of this section. If you have been through a car accident, an assault or a disaster and keep having nightmares or flashbacks, see item 14 of this section (trauma-focused psychotherapy).

Paperwork, the body and money to collect after a relative's death: see section 25. Guardianship and wills for an older person before they die: see section 17. What you can claim after losing a job: see section 7. Exercise and light exposure for low mood: see sections 3 and 22. If you have suicidal thoughts, call 12356 first: see section 1, item 25. How long such thoughts last: see section 1, item 32. Aftereffects after being rescued: see section 1, item 33. This section does not repeat these.

### 1. In the first days after a loved one dies, do not stay alone; keep taking heart, blood pressure or diabetes medicine as usual; and call 120 (China's ambulance number) for chest pressure
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. Do not stay alone in these first days, and keep taking your medicine as usual.
- Plain language: In the first day after a loved one dies, the risk of a heart attack is 21.1 times normal, and it falls each day after that. For people aged 60 to 89, heart attack or stroke risk roughly doubles in the 30 days after a spouse dies. If you have heart disease, do not stop your medicine. Call 120 for chest pressure, breathlessness or one-sided weakness.
- Benefit: The study covered 1,985 people who had a heart attack. Each person was compared with their own earlier time periods. Of them, 270 people (13.6%) had lost an important person in the 6 months before the attack, and 19 had lost one in the day before it. Within 24 hours of losing an important person, the rate of acute heart attack was 21.1 times higher (95% CI 13.1–34.1, so the true multiple is roughly between 13 and 34). After that it fell each day. In real numbers: among people with a 5% ten-year heart attack risk, 1 extra case occurs for every 1,394 people who have just lost a loved one. Among people with a 20% risk, 1 extra case occurs for every 320. A separate UK study compared 30,447 people aged 60 to 89 who were widowed with 83,588 matched controls. Within 30 days of being widowed, 50 people (0.16%) in the widowed group had a heart attack or stroke, against 67 people (0.08%) in the control group. IRR 2.20 (1.52–3.15), meaning the chance of such an event was about 2.2 times that of the controls. For heart attack alone, 2.14 (1.20–3.81), about 2.1 times. For stroke, 2.40 (1.22–4.71), about 2.4 times. The effect weakened after 30 days. The study also looked at two other conditions within 90 days. One was acute coronary syndrome that is not a heart attack (a sudden problem with the heart's blood supply that has not yet reached heart attack level), IRR 2.20 (1.12–4.29). The other was pulmonary embolism (a blood clot blocking the blood vessels in the lungs), IRR 2.37 (1.18–4.75). For recognising these conditions, the UK National Health Service (NHS) lists signs that need an emergency call straight away. A heart attack feels like chest pressure or squeezing, with pain that may spread to the arm, neck or jaw, and breathlessness. A stroke causes one side of the face to droop, one arm or one side of the body to become weak or numb, and speech to become slurred.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Mostofsky E, et al. (2012). Risk of acute myocardial infarction after the death of a significant person in one's life: the Determinants of Myocardial Infarction Onset Study. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.111.061770>；Carey IM, et al. (2014). Increased risk of acute cardiovascular events after partner bereavement: a matched cohort study. JAMA Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2013.14558>；NHS. Heart attack: symptoms. <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heart-attack/symptoms/>；NHS. Symptoms of a stroke. <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/stroke/symptoms/>
- Notes: China only: 120 is China's ambulance number. Give your pill box to someone else to manage rather than relying on your memory. Do not drive long distances in these days. Do not get through it by drinking; for the cost of alcohol, see section 2. For how to recognise a heart attack or stroke and how to call an ambulance, see section 13. Both studies looked back at population data, so it is not possible to tell how many of those people were going to have an attack anyway. But the direction and timing of the two studies agree. For people who have no one to hand the pill box to, and who will be alone at home these days, see item 6 of this section.

### 2. In the week after a serious illness diagnosis, do not collect the report alone, and postpone decisions that are not about treatment
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. Ask someone to come with you to collect the report. Postpone big decisions that are not about treatment by a few days.
- Plain language: In the first week after a cancer diagnosis, the suicide rate is 12.6 times that of people without one, and cardiovascular death is 5.6 times. Bring someone when you collect the report, and do not stay alone afterwards. Leave decisions like quitting a job or selling a home until your next outpatient visit.
- Benefit: Swedish national registers followed 6,073,240 people from 1991 to 2006. Compared with people without a cancer diagnosis, the relative risk of suicide in the first week after diagnosis was 12.6 (95% CI 8.6–17.8), about 12.6 times. The true multiple is roughly between 8.6 and 17.8. The rate in that week was 2.50 per 1,000 person-years, meaning 2.5 cases per 1,000 people per year. By the first year the figure fell to 3.1 (2.7–3.5; 0.60 per 1,000 person-years), about 3.1 times. Cardiovascular death in the first week was 5.6 (5.2–5.9; 116.80 per 1,000 person-years), about 5.6 times. In the first 4 weeks the figure was 3.3 (3.1–3.4; 65.81 per 1,000 person-years). The raised risk fell quickly within the first year after diagnosis. The rise was greater for cancers that are harder to treat and have lower survival. Comparing the same patients across different time periods gave the same conclusion.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Fang F, et al. (2012). Suicide and cardiovascular death after a cancer diagnosis. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1110307>
- Notes: This is data on cancer patients. There is no equally strong source for other major diagnoses. Relatively few people are affected: the first-week suicide rate is 2.50 per 1,000 person-years, meaning 2.5 cases per 1,000 people per year. Writing this out is not meant to frighten; it shows why people need someone beside them in the first days after learning the news. For long-term management, reimbursement and check-ups after diagnosis, see section 16 and section 24. If you cannot find someone to go with you, see item 6 of this section (replace "that person" with a neighbour and a community list).

### 3. After losing your job, set a routine for your medical insurance and job hunting, and do not stay at home all day
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. First register as unemployed and claim unemployment insurance benefits, then schedule your job search at fixed times. The hard part is getting up at set times when no one is pushing you.
- Plain language: Unemployed people have about 60% higher risk of death than people in work, most in the first ten years. Part of this comes from smoking and drinking, which you can control; allowing for them, the increase is about a quarter smaller. First, register as unemployed and claim unemployment insurance.
- Benefit: The review combined 42 studies, 235 death risk estimates and over 20 million people. After adjusting for age and other factors, the average hazard ratio (HR) for death from all causes among unemployed people was 1.63, about 60% higher than for people in work. The risk was higher for men than for women. It was clearer for people in the early and middle parts of their careers, and smaller later on. Risk was highest in the first 10 years of follow-up and fell after that. In the studies that also counted health behaviours such as smoking and drinking, the average HR was 24% lower, meaning the calculated increase was about a quarter smaller. This gives a practical direction you can act on.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Roelfs DJ, et al. (2011). Losing life and livelihood: a systematic review and meta-analysis of unemployment and all-cause mortality. Social Science & Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2011.01.005>
- Notes: China only: This review looked back at population data, so it cannot tell which way cause and effect run. People in poor health are also more likely to lose their jobs. For unemployment benefits, where to claim them and how to keep social insurance going, see section 7. If you were dismissed and want to know how much compensation to expect, or are considering a voluntary resignation, see section 19.

### 4. If a loved one died by suicide, in an accident or by homicide, do not try to cope alone; actively seek professional help
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Calling 12356 (China's national mental health hotline) is free. Seeing a doctor and visiting a psychiatric outpatient clinic costs the same as a regular outpatient visit. The hard part is reaching out to someone.
- Plain language: Within five years, people widowed by suicide are about 80% (men) or 70% (women) more likely to get a mental disorder diagnosis than people in general, and 70% to 100% more likely than those widowed by other causes. They often feel shunned and ashamed, so seek help actively rather than wait.
- Benefit: The Danish national registers followed 6.7 million adults from 1980 to 2014, adding up to over 136 million person-years (the total years everyone was followed, added together). For people widowed by a spouse's suicide, the incidence rate ratio (IRR) for a new mental disorder diagnosis within 5 years, compared with the general population, was 1.8 for men (95% CI 1.6–2.0), about 80% higher. For women it was 1.7 (1.6–1.8), about 70% higher. Compared with people widowed by other causes of death, the IRR was 1.7 (1.5–1.9) for men and 2.0 (1.9–2.2) for women, meaning 70% to double higher. This group also had higher rates of suicide attempts, death, and need for municipal social assistance. Physical illnesses such as cirrhosis and sleep disorders were also more common. A further 57 studies were reviewed one by one, without combining their numbers. Bereavement by suicide was linked to four things. First, the surviving partner's suicide risk rises. Second, when a child dies by suicide, the parents' risk of psychiatric admission rises. Third, when an adult child dies by suicide, the mother's suicide risk rises. Fourth, when parents die by suicide, their children's risk of depression rises. Compared with people bereaved by other violent deaths, people bereaved by suicide more often say they feel shunned and ashamed.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Erlangsen A, et al. (2017). Association Between Spousal Suicide and Mental, Physical, and Social Health Outcomes: A Longitudinal and Nationwide Register-Based Study. JAMA Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2017.0226>；Pitman A, et al. (2014). Effects of suicide bereavement on mental health and suicide risk. The Lancet Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(14)70224-X>；Kristensen P 等 (2012). Bereavement and mental health after sudden and violent losses: a review. Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1521/psyc.2012.75.1.76>（备注里那份综述）
- Notes: China only: 12356 is China's national mental health hotline. "Sudden and violent deaths" means accidents, homicides and disasters. After such deaths, PTSD, depression and stuck grief are more common than after deaths from illness, and recovery is slower. If you are still having nightmares, flashbacks, or avoiding things a month later, see item 14 of this section (trauma-focused psychotherapy). For what to do if you have suicidal thoughts, see section 1, item 25.

### 5. For the first six months after being widowed, have one specific person keep track of your meals, sleep, chronic-disease medicine and check-ups
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. Choose one specific person, such as a child, sibling or friend, and ask them to visit or phone you regularly. The hard part is asking them to take this on.
- Plain language: In the first six months after being widowed, the risk of death is about 40% higher than for people who are not widowed, falling to about 14% after six months. For men it is about 23% higher; for women the difference may be chance. So do not rely on memory: give your medicine, meals and check-ups to one specific person.
- Benefit: Fifteen studies that registered people first and then followed them, pooling 2,263,888 people. For death within less than 6 months of widowhood, RR 1.41 (95% CI 1.26–1.57), meaning the probability of dying in that period is about 40% higher than for people who are not widowed. After 6 months it was 1.14 (1.10–1.18), about 14% higher. For men, RR 1.23 (1.18–1.28), about 23% higher. For women, 1.04 (1.00–1.08), not statistically significant, so this small gap may be chance. No clear difference was seen between people under 65 and over 65. A second analysis pooled 123 papers, 1,377 risk estimates and over half a billion people. After adjusting for differences in conditions, HR 1.23 (1.19–1.28), meaning about 23% higher overall. Men were still higher than women: men 1.27 (1.19–1.35), 27% higher; women 1.15 (1.08–1.22), 15% higher.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Moon JR, et al. (2011). Widowhood and mortality: a meta-analysis. PLoS ONE. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0023465>；Shor E, et al. (2012). Widowhood and mortality: a meta-analysis and meta-regression. Demography. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s13524-012-0096-x>
- Notes: These studies looked back at population data, so other factors cannot be fully removed. Couples share lifestyles and illnesses to begin with. The finding that the first six months carry the highest risk comes from the first summary, which split results by time since widowhood. The second summary did not split by time since widowhood; it split by how long the studies followed people. Studies that followed people for only 6 months had a pooled HR of 1.58 (1.32–1.88), and those following for 16 to 20 years had 1.22 (1.02–1.47). The authors concluded that risk is highest in the first few years after widowhood, but also noted that most studies did not start following people right after they were widowed. The two summaries point in the same direction, so it is worth treating the first six months as a window for close attention. Men have higher risk and are less likely to ask for help. If there is an older widowed person at home, sort out their accounts, guardianship and will as set out in section 17. For people without anyone like this (living alone, having lost their only child, or with no children left), hand this task to the community and to your phone; see item 6 of this section.

### 6. If you have no relatives or friends, replace "the person who keeps an eye on you" with three things: a neighbour who can get into your home, the community visiting list, and an emergency contact on your phone
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is speaking up: telling a neighbour and the neighbourhood committee, "I live alone, and something bad has just happened at home."
- Plain language: With no one close, you need someone to notice if something goes wrong, and a routine for medicine and meals. Write a list and set an alarm rather than rely on memory. Leave a spare key with a neighbour, ask the neighbourhood committee to add you to the visiting list, and set an emergency contact on your phone.
- Benefit: The international REACH registry followed 44,573 outpatients who were at risk of, or had, atherothrombosis (plaque building up in the arteries, which can be blocked by clots). Doctors in many countries enrolled them between December 2003 and December 2004, and they were followed for 4 years. Of these, 8,594 people (19%) lived alone. Over 4 years, all-cause death was 14.1% for those living alone and 11.1% for those living with others. Cardiovascular death was 8.6% against 6.8%. For both measures the log-rank P was below 0.01, so the difference is unlikely to be chance. The gap differed by age (interaction P=0.03). Aged 45 to 65, death was 7.7% for those living alone against 5.7% for those living with others; adjusted HR 1.24 (95% CI 1.01–1.51), meaning about 24% higher. Aged 66 to 80, it was 13.2% against 12.3%, HR 1.12 (1.01–1.26), about 10% higher. Over 80, it was 24.6% against 28.4%, HR 0.92 (0.79–1.06), so no increase was seen. China's visiting-care guidance, issued in 2022 by ten agencies including the Ministry of Civil Affairs (China's social welfare ministry), requires visiting-care services. The target group includes older people who live alone, have empty-nest arrangements, are left behind, disabled, severely disabled, or in special family-planning households. Service methods are regular home visits, phone or video calls, and remote monitoring. Township or street offices carry out initial surveys, and village or neighbourhood residents' committees assist. They record basic information and each person's "willingness to accept visiting-care services", and include them in grid-based management, where each area is assigned a specific person to follow up. By the end of 2025 the aim was to "ensure a monthly visiting rate of 100%" for especially vulnerable older people, and "for disabled vulnerable older people, at least one visit per month". The document also encourages families to install smart call systems, smart electricity and water meters, health monitoring products and elderly guardianship devices, which "when monitoring detects an abnormality, can give early warning and simultaneously send prompts to emergency contacts". Staff who find an urgent problem "should immediately help call the emergency help line".
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Udell JA, et al. (2012). Living alone and cardiovascular risk in outpatients at risk of or with atherothrombosis. Archives of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archinternmed.2012.2782>；民政部等十部门 (2022). 关于开展特殊困难老年人探访关爱服务的指导意见（民发〔2022〕73 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-10/13/content_5718017.htm>
- Notes: China only: the visiting-care guidance and the neighbourhood committee are specific to China. Here is how to do the three things. One: give a spare key, or a temporary door code, to a neighbour or your property manager, and say "I am on my own these days." You do not need a close relationship; you only need someone to know you are at home. Two: go to your neighbourhood committee, say you live alone, and ask whether you can join the visiting-care list. Older people who live alone, have empty-nest arrangements or have lost an only child are officially covered. Also ask whether free smart call buttons or smart water meters are available locally. Three: set an emergency contact and a medical emergency card on your phone, and keep it unmuted and switched on these days. This item is rated B for two reasons. First, the link between living alone and death comes from studies that looked back at population data. They cannot rule out the reverse explanation, that people in poorer health or circumstances are more likely to live alone, and the source itself says the result needs confirming. Second, the visiting-care guidance only covers older people. There is no equivalent dedicated system for people under 60. What you can rely on is one line in another document, which asks grid workers and social workers to "promptly identify risks of family crisis, unemployment, school dropout and other psychological crises" (see item 11 of this section: call 12356, the national mental health hotline, and talk to someone). Why having someone in the house matters: see section 13, item 1 (chest compressions). Out-of-hospital cardiac arrest happens at home in 79.2% of cases. When bystanders do chest compressions, 16.1% survive to leave hospital; with no one doing them, 3.9% do. No one at home puts you in the lower group. Two other practical steps. Funeral arrangements already involve several offices: the funeral home, the community health service centre, the police, and the housing provident fund (a Chinese housing savings scheme). See section 25. Making it a fixed outing once a day gives you a ready schedule for going out during these days. Hotlines can be called repeatedly, not just once (see item 11 of this section). Living alone is itself a long-term risk factor, with an odds ratio for death of 1.32, about 30% higher; see section 22, item 9 (treating regular contact with people as a health expense). This item only covers the next few days; the long-term picture is considered separately.

### 7. After a parent dies, tell the child what happened honestly and let them take part in saying goodbye
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is that you are grieving too and still have to explain things to your child clearly.
- Plain language: Children who lose a parent before 18 have about 50% higher risk of death later than other children. If the parent died by accident or suicide, the risk is about 80% higher; from illness, about 30% higher. The American Academy of Pediatrics advises telling the child honestly what happened and letting them attend the funeral.
- Benefit: The study covered everyone born in Denmark from 1968 to 2008 and in Sweden from 1973 to 2006, plus the 89.3% of people born in Finland from 1987 to 2007 who were selected for the study. Together, over 7.3 million people. Of these, 189,094 lost a parent before age 18. Overall mortality for these children was 50% higher than for children who had not lost a parent (mortality rate ratio 1.50, 95% CI 1.43–1.58, about 1.5 times). When the parent died of a non-natural cause such as an accident or suicide, the ratio was 1.84 (1.71–2.00), about 80% higher. When the parent died of a natural cause such as illness, it was 1.33 (1.24–1.41), about 30% higher. The risk was highest when the child's cause of death was the same type as the parent's. In 2016 the American Academy of Pediatrics issued a clinical report for paediatricians. It asks them to explain clearly what happened and what it means when talking with a child who has lost a parent, and to correct any wrong information or misunderstandings the child has picked up. The report also gives advice on supporting children to attend funerals and other farewell ceremonies.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Li J, et al. (2014). Mortality after parental death in childhood: a nationwide cohort study from three Nordic countries. PLoS Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001679>；Schonfeld DJ, Demaria T, Committee on Psychosocial Aspects of Child and Family Health, Disaster Preparedness Advisory Council (2016). Supporting the Grieving Child and Family. Pediatrics, 138(3), e20162147. <https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2016-2147>
- Notes: When talking to the child, do not make up a story such as "they have gone away on a long trip". Put guardianship and care arrangements in writing. Whether to change schools or send the child to live elsewhere: no direct study or guideline text was found on this. The authors' view is that fewer changes to the child's life are better. This is rated B not because the numbers are weak: it is a whole-population follow-up across three countries, and the numbers alone would merit A. It is because there is no direct trial evidence linking those numbers to specific practices, such as how to talk to children or whether to attend the funeral. The authors also note that data on family relationships and children's lifestyles after a bereavement are missing. The increase may partly reflect inherited constitution, and partly the long-term effects of the loss. Appointing a guardian and arranging property: see section 17. Child support and schooling: see section 18.

### 8. If grief has stayed stuck for over six months and daily life is not manageable, register at a psychiatry or clinical psychology clinic
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Registration fee plus treatment fees. This kind of treatment runs as a course of sessions; in the trial, 16 sessions over an average of 19 weeks. The hard part is taking the step yourself to book the appointment.
- Plain language: About one in ten bereaved adults gets stuck. Six months or more after the death, they still miss the person so intensely that they cannot eat, cannot work, or cannot look after their children. They feel numb and feel that life has no meaning. This is not a failure of willpower. It has diagnostic criteria and specific treatment. Targeted treatment worked for 51% of people, compared with 28% for general psychotherapy, and it worked faster.
- Benefit: Pooled across 14 studies of adults whose relative did not die violently and who had not themselves been seen in psychiatric care, the prevalence of prolonged grief disorder was 9.8% (95% CI 6.8–14.0). That is about 10 in every 100 bereaved adults. The proportion rises with age. Another study tested whether the diagnostic criteria are accurate. It followed 291 bereaved people through three interviews, at 0–6, 6–12 and 12–24 months after the loss. The core feature of the diagnosis is intense longing for the person who died. You also need at least five of these nine symptoms. The first five are emotional numbness, shock, feeling that life has no meaning, distrust of others, and suffering because of this loss. The last four are difficulty accepting the death, not knowing who you are anymore, avoiding reality, and being unable to carry on living. These symptoms must occur every day, or be severe enough to stop you functioning. They must also persist at least six months after the death and affect daily life. One trial randomly assigned 95 people to two groups and gave 16 sessions over an average of 19 weeks. Treatment for complicated grief had a response rate of 51%. General interpersonal psychotherapy had 28% (P=0.02, meaning the difference is unlikely to be due to chance). The former also worked faster. The number needed to treat was 4.3, meaning that on average one extra person improved for every 4.3 people treated.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Lundorff M, et al. (2017). Prevalence of prolonged grief disorder in adult bereavement: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Affective Disorders. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2017.01.030>；Prigerson HG, et al. (2009). Prolonged grief disorder: Psychometric validation of criteria proposed for DSM-V and ICD-11. PLoS Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000121>；Shear MK, et al. (2005). Treatment of complicated grief: a randomized controlled trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.293.21.2601>
- Notes: China only: Do not decide by counting months yourself. The real test is whether it affects eating, sleeping, working or looking after your children. If it does, book an appointment. The department may be called psychiatry, psychology or clinical psychology, and public specialist psychiatric hospitals all have them. This condition is called complicated grief in some sources and prolonged grief disorder in others. Different criteria require different durations: some say 6 months, others 12 months. The 9.8% figure comes from 14 studies with quite different methods, and the authors warn it may not carry over directly to other populations.

### 9. Do not start paid grief counselling straight away. First check whether your grief is really stuck (the symptoms in item 8)
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. It can also save you the cost of counselling.
- Plain language: Giving counselling to every bereaved person offers little benefit, and the benefit fades after a while, because most people would recover on their own. But for people who are clearly struggling to adjust, treatment works just as well as it does for other psychological problems. So first check against item 8, which covers grief that has stuck and calls for a psychiatrist. If it matches, then spend the money.
- Benefit: Pooled across 61 controlled studies, broad psychological intervention for bereaved people gave little benefit when treatment ended, and by a later follow-up no benefit was visible. Interventions that enrolled only bereaved people who were clearly struggling worked as well as psychotherapy for other psychological problems. Studies that did not screen participants in advance had poor results, because the comparison group would also have recovered over time. Another 14 randomised trials, pooled, found that treatment for people already in difficulty was clearly better than control at the end of treatment and at later follow-up, and the effect grew over time. Preventive intervention offered broadly to people not yet in difficulty had no effect. One study followed 205 people from before the death of a spouse to 6 and 18 months afterwards. It identified five trajectories: common grief, chronic grief, chronic depression, improvement during bereavement, and resilience. Resilience was the most common, and common grief was relatively rare.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Currier JM, et al. (2008). The effectiveness of psychotherapeutic interventions for bereaved persons: a comprehensive quantitative review. Psychological Bulletin. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.134.5.648>；Wittouck C, et al. (2011). The prevention and treatment of complicated grief: a meta-analysis. Clinical Psychology Review. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2010.09.005>；Bonanno GA, et al. (2002). Resilience to loss and chronic grief: a prospective study from preloss to 18-months postloss. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.83.5.1150>；Stroebe M 等 (2007). Health outcomes of bereavement. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(07)61816-9>（备注里那份综述）
- Notes: This means not everyone needs it, not that nobody should have it. Bereaved people at high risk, such as those whose relative died by suicide, accident or homicide (item 4), are the people for whom the evidence supports intervention. So are people whose grief has already become complicated (item 8), and those with depression or stress disorder after bereavement. A Lancet review reached the same conclusion: grief is not an illness, and most people do not need professional intervention. Put the effort where the risk is highest.

### 10. After separation or divorce, do not let health insurance lapse and do not drink to cope
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. Keep your health insurance current and stop drinking. The difficulty is that during this period it is easy to lean on alcohol.
- Plain language: People who are separated or divorced have a clearly higher chance of dying early than people who are still married. The increase is larger for men and for younger people. This does not necessarily mean divorce caused it, since divorced people may already differ in health or income. Two things you can control are: do not let your health insurance lapse, and do not rely on smoking or alcohol to cope.
- Benefit: Pooled across 32 prospective studies covering more than 6.5 million people, 160,000 deaths, more than 755,000 divorces, and 11 countries, separated or divorced adults had a clearly higher risk of early death than adults who were still married. The increase was clearly larger for men and younger people than for women and older people. The authors discussed four possible pathways. First, social selection: people who were already less healthy or less well placed were more likely to end up divorced. Second, loss of resources: income and security are cut off. Third, changes in health behaviour: smoking, drinking and sleep get worse. Fourth, chronic psychological stress (prolonged strain).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Sbarra DA, et al. (2011). Divorce and Death: A Meta-Analysis and Research Agenda for Clinical, Social, and Health Psychology. Perspectives on Psychological Science. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691611414724>
- Notes: China only: The source states only "clearly higher" and group comparisons, with no citable multiplier, so no specific figure is given here. The benefit is rated medium, based on knowing the direction but having no reduction figure that can be applied as a threshold. Property, debts and bride price on divorce are covered in section 8 and section 10. For child arrangements, see item 23 of section 10 (the divorce agreement should set out child support and visitation).

- Plain language: If you want someone to talk to, call 12356. This is China's national psychological support hotline, and it is free. ### 12. For the first three months after a loss, postpone every major irreversible decision
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. It only means putting decisions off, which may cause you to miss some opportunities. The difficulty is that at the time, it feels as if opportunities will not wait.
- Plain language: During this period your judgment about "opportunities" is at its worst. In one experiment, sad people accepted roughly a little over 10% to a little over 30% less money to avoid waiting three months. Any decision involving more than one month's income, or with a high cost if reversed, should be postponed by three months. First talk it through with someone who has no stake in the matter, rather than deciding on the spot.
- Benefit: This avoids irreversible asset decisions made in the period when judgment is weakest, right after a blow. Such decisions include selling a house, quitting a job, investing a pension or compensation payment in one lump sum, remarrying in haste, and guaranteeing someone else's debt. These decisions typically involve amounts from tens of thousands of yuan (RMB) up to the value of a house, so the benefit is rated medium. Official documents likewise list "family crises, unemployment, and dropping out of school" as psychological crisis risks that communities should proactively screen for, so a person in this period is already treated as one who needs outside help. The US National Institute on Aging's grief guide lists "difficulty making decisions" as a common feature of grief. It advises that, if possible, major life decisions be postponed until you feel better. While grieving and possibly unable to think clearly, it is best not to make major changes such as selling a house or quitting a job. One study ran three experiments. Researchers randomly assigned some participants to a sad state and then asked them to choose between money now and money in three months. Compared with calm participants, sad participants accepted 13% to 34% less money to avoid waiting three months. This is sadness induced in an experiment, not actual bereavement. The US Federal Trade Commission (FTC) warned in 2023 about a scam in which someone posing as a funeral home tells family members that the funeral will be cancelled unless they pay immediately. Its first advice is to resist the pressure to act right away: legitimate businesses give you time to decide.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: 国家卫生健康委等 25 部门 (2026). 健全社会心理服务体系和危机干预机制实施方案（国卫医政发〔2026〕8 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065035.htm>；National Institute on Aging. Coping With Grief and Loss（「Postpone major decisions if possible」一段，页面 2026-01-26 更新）. <https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/grief-and-mourning/coping-grief-and-loss>；Lerner JS, Li Y, Weber EU (2013). Imposters prey on families preparing for the funeral of a loved one. <https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/06/imposters-prey-families-preparing-funeral-loved-one>
- Notes: China only: If you have no one like this to talk to, call 12356 and go through it there (see item 11 of this section). You can also write it down and reread it three days later. But no study has directly compared bereaved people who postponed decisions with those who did not, in terms of financial outcomes. The NIA also says only "when you feel better", with no timeframe. "Three months" and "more than one month's income" are thresholds the author set. ### 13. Do not treat death as a way to clear debts: life insurance does not pay within two years, work injury is not recognised, and debts are still deducted from the estate first
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. It only requires working out this sum clearly.
- Plain language: Under a life insurance contract, if the policyholder dies by suicide within two years, the insurer will not pay. It will only refund what the policy can return as cash value, not the full sum insured. Suicide and self-harm are not recognised as work injuries, so none of the three work-death payments are available. Debts you owe are deducted from your estate first, and your family receives only what is left. They also bear the health cost described in item 4 of this section, the death of a relative by suicide or violence.
- Benefit: Insurance Law Article 44 governs contracts where payment is conditional on the insured's death. If the insured dies by suicide within two years from the date the contract takes effect or is reinstated, the insurer is not liable to pay the insurance benefit. This exception does not apply if the insured lacked civil capacity when they died by suicide (a person who cannot be held legally responsible for their own actions). In that case the insurer must refund the cash value of the policy as stated in the contract, meaning only what the policy can refund, not the sum insured. Life insurance sums insured are typically in the hundreds of thousands of yuan (RMB), so the benefit is rated large. Work Injury Insurance Regulations Article 16, item 3, provides that self-harm or suicide cannot be recognised as a work injury or treated as equivalent to one. The three work-death payments under Article 39 then all fall away. They are: first, a funeral subsidy of six months of the local average monthly wage of employees in the previous year; second, a survivor's pension for dependent relatives; third, a one-time work-death subsidy equal to 20 times the national per-capita disposable income of urban residents in the previous year. Civil Code Article 1159 provides that when an estate is divided, the decedent's legally owed taxes and debts must be paid first, and only the remainder is divided. Article 1161 provides that heirs pay only up to the actual value of the estate they inherit, so they do not need to pay from their own money, and heirs who renounce the inheritance are not liable for repayment.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2015 修正). 中华人民共和国保险法第四十四条. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf7c4060811>；国务院 (2010 修订). 工伤保险条例（国务院令第 586 号）第十六条、第三十九条. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1778064.htm>；全国人大 (2020). 中华人民共和国民法典第一千一百五十九条、第一千一百六十一条. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff808081729d1efe01729d50b5c500bf>
- Notes: China only: This note covers only the money side. The other items in this section, and in section 1, are about not getting to this point. Insurance Law Article 44 covers only the first two years; it is quoted to give the full provision, not as a timetable. ### 14. After a car accident, assault or disaster, do not rely on a one-off psychological debriefing straight after the event. If nightmares, flashbacks and fear are still severe after a month, see a psychiatrist for trauma-focused psychotherapy
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Registration fee plus treatment fees, usually 8 to 12 sessions. The difficulty is that treatment means going back to the experience you most fear recalling.
- Plain language: After a major event, nightmares and being easily startled in the first few weeks are common, and most people recover on their own. If it is still severe after a month, it may be post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), and psychotherapy aimed specifically at trauma helps clearly. Being pulled in to recount what happened, in a one-off session right after the event, does not prevent the condition. In one trial, people who had this session were actually worse off three years later.
- Benefit: The National Health Commission's Standards for Diagnosis and Treatment of Mental Disorders (2020 edition) states that about 8% of people who experience psychological trauma go on to develop PTSD. Its core symptoms fall into three groups. First, nightmares and flashbacks, in which the event keeps intruding. Second, avoiding things, places and people linked to the event. Third, constant hypervigilance, being easily startled, and insomnia. The diagnosis is made only when symptoms last more than one month and seriously affect family life, study or work. The standards note, in the section on acute stress disorder, that most people return to normal through self-adjustment or crisis intervention in the acute phase. Treatment effectiveness is shown by a 2020 review of 114 randomised controlled trials involving 8,171 people. Trauma-focused cognitive behavioural therapy versus waitlist or usual care gave SMD −1.32 (95% CI −1.57 to −1.08, credible range), with moderate-quality evidence. Eye movement desensitisation and reprocessing (EMDR) gave −1.23 (−1.69 to −0.76), with low-quality evidence. SMD and d are the same yardstick; 0.8 and above is considered large, and both exceed that line. General supportive counselling versus waitlist showed no difference (−0.43, −0.90 to 0.04). A 2013 Cochrane review by the same group, of 70 trials and 4,761 people, pointed in the same direction. Trauma-focused cognitive behavioural therapy gave −1.62 (−2.03 to −1.21), and EMDR gave −1.17 (−2.04 to −0.30). However, the evidence in that review was rated very low quality. The UK NICE 2018 guideline sets out the approach as follows. For adults with severe symptoms within a month of the event, offer individual trauma-focused cognitive behavioural therapy. Offer the same to those diagnosed, or with clear symptoms, after a month, usually 8 to 12 sessions. For trauma not caused by war, if symptoms persist at 3 months, EMDR is also offered. For people with mild symptoms who do not meet the diagnostic threshold, monitor during the first month and book a review within that month. The psychological treatments listed in China's standards are also these: prolonged exposure, cognitive processing therapy and EMDR. For one-off debriefing right after an event, three sources are relevant. A 2002 Cochrane review of 11 trials found that a single individual debriefing session did not reduce distress and did not prevent PTSD. At 3 to 5 months, OR 1.22 (0.60–2.46), no difference. One trial followed people to 1 year and found that those who had debriefing had a higher risk of developing the condition: OR 2.88 (1.11–7.53), about 2.9 times. The authors concluded that people affected by trauma should no longer be compelled to undergo debriefing. Another randomised trial enrolled people hospitalised after road traffic accidents. At 3 years, the debriefing group had significantly worse psychiatric symptoms, anxiety when travelling in vehicles, pain, physical problems, overall functioning and financial problems. People who had severe flashbacks and avoidance at the start still had symptoms three years after debriefing, while those who did not have debriefing recovered. The NICE guideline explicitly states not to offer psychologically focused debriefing to prevent or treat PTSD. The reason is that neither individual nor group debriefing showed benefit, and there are signs it can be worse than doing nothing.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Lewis C, et al. (2020). Psychological therapies for post-traumatic stress disorder in adults: systematic review and meta-analysis. European Journal of Psychotraumatology. <https://doi.org/10.1080/20008198.2020.1729633>；Bisson JI, et al. (2013). Psychological therapies for chronic post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) in adults. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, CD003388. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD003388.pub4>；Rose S, et al. (2002). Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, CD000560. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD000560>；Mayou RA, Ehlers A, Hobbs M (2000). Psychological debriefing for road traffic accident victims. Post-traumatic stress disorder (NG116), recommendations 1.6.4, 1.6.5, 1.6.15–1.6.19. <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng116/chapter/Recommendations>；国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2020). 关于印发精神障碍诊疗规范（2020 年版）的通知（国卫办医函〔2020〕945 号），第八章创伤及应激相关障碍. <https://www.nhc.gov.cn/yzygj/c100068/202012/b4305ace9e14440792eb76d29602c88a.shtml>
