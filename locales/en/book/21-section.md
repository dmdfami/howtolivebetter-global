# 21. Going Abroad, Travel and Safety Overseas

This section covers money and personal freedom. A few of the items also affect personal safety. Once you are abroad, the law that applies to you is the law of the country you are in, not China's law. What Chinese embassies and consulates can do for you has clear limits.

### 1. Check the Ministry of Foreign Affairs safety advisory before you leave, and skip anywhere marked "do not travel"
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. Open the China Consular Service Network, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs consular website, and read through it. It takes a few minutes.
- Plain language: The Ministry of Foreign Affairs rates overseas risks in four levels. The highest is "do not travel." Since 15 September 2026, administrative regulations also tell citizens to avoid high-risk places. Staff who handle your documents or exit checks may warn you and advise against going. Checking the page takes minutes.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Foreign Affairs publishes overseas safety advisories by risk level. From lowest to highest, the four levels are "pay attention to safety," "strengthen safety precautions," "exercise caution," and "do not travel." The new rules, in force from 15 September 2026, also write the individual's duty into administrative regulations. The original text reads: "Chinese citizens should pay attention to overseas safety advisories and travel destination safety risk warnings, and avoid going to or staying in high-risk countries or regions." The new rules also require immigration authorities (the agencies that issue entry and exit documents and run border inspection) to remind people who are going to high-risk areas. The original text reads: "For Chinese citizens preparing to go to countries or regions rated at the highest risk level, or where serious personal-safety incidents occur suddenly and frequently, officials should, when necessary, advise them not to go."
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2026). 关于出境入境管理的规定（国令第 841 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202607/content_7077173.htm>（第二条）；国务院 (2023). 领事保护与协助条例（国令第 763 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm>（第十九、二十一条）；外交部领事司. 安全提醒. <https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/zggmzhw/lsbh/aqtx/>；全国人大常委会 (2018). 旅游法（2018 年修正，第五十八、六十二条）. <https://zwgk.mct.gov.cn/zfxxgkml/zcfg/fl/202105/t20210526_924763.html>（文化和旅游部政府信息公开转载）
- Notes: China only: Warnings change with events, so this book does not copy the list. Check the page yourself before you travel. As of 7 September 2026, the page still listed these "do not travel" type warnings: Eswatini (25 August 2026), Palestine (10 October 2023), and Sudan (17 April 2023). The other four were Afghanistan (17 February 2023), Syria (4 January 2023), Peru (29 December 2022), and Somalia (24 November 2022). There are also specific warnings for northern Myanmar and some provinces of the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Travel agencies also have a duty to inform you. Under the Tourism Law, when you sign a package tour contract, the agency must tell you about safety precautions and the destination's laws, customs and religious taboos. Package tour contracts must be in writing. Before joining a tour, you can ask the operator to write down the destination's risks. This is the author's suggestion.

### 2. Save 12308 and your local embassy or consulate's consular protection number in your phone, copy them onto paper in your wallet, and do not wait until something goes wrong to look for them
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Saving two numbers and writing them down takes a few minutes.
- Plain language: If something goes wrong, call the Ministry of Foreign Affairs' global consular hotline first. From inside China, dial 12308. From abroad, dial +86-10-12308. Since it opened, the hotline has answered over 3 million calls. In the same period, the ministry and its overseas posts handled over 500,000 consular protection cases.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Foreign Affairs runs a global consular protection and service emergency hotline, staffed 24 hours a day. People inside China dial 12308. People abroad dial +86-10-12308 or +86-10-65612308. Since the hotline opened in 2014, it has answered over 3 million calls in total. In the same period, the ministry and its overseas embassies and consulates handled over 500,000 consular protection cases.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 外交部、中国驻缅甸使领馆 (2023). 关于当前缅北地区安全局势的领事提醒. <https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/zggmzhw/lsbh/aqtx/202311/t20231109_11176682.shtml>；国务院新闻办 (2023). 国务院政策例行吹风会介绍领事保护与协助有关情况. <https://www.gov.cn/xinwen/2023zccfh/11/index.htm>
- Notes: China only: Your local embassy or consulate also has its own consular protection number, which is usually faster than the hotline. Look it up by country on the China Consular Service Network before you leave, and save it next to the hotline number. If your phone is lost or out of battery, saved numbers are of no use, so also copy them by hand onto paper and keep it in your wallet, or write them down for family at home.

### 3. Know what consular protection can and cannot do: it can visit you, but it cannot get you out of detention, and you still pay the costs
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. A few minutes to read this boundary carefully.
- Plain language: If you are detained abroad, consular staff can check your case with local authorities, visit you and sit in on your trial. They cannot overturn a verdict or get you released. Costs that others advance for you are still yours to pay. So prepare money and insurance before you go, and do not count on the state to cover you.
- Benefit: If you are detained, the embassy or consulate can do several things. It can check the situation with the relevant authorities of the country you are in, and ask them to handle the case fairly and according to law. The regulation says: "visit or contact the Chinese citizen under the host country's laws and any international treaties that China and the host country have concluded or jointly joined; understand their needs; and ask the host country's relevant authorities to give the Chinese citizen humane and fair treatment." In plain terms, consular staff visit you and keep in touch under local law and treaties, ask what you need, and ask local authorities to treat you humanely and fairly. Staff can sit in on your court hearings and ask that your right to a legal defense be protected. If you are injured or caught in a disaster, staff can push for local emergency rescue and help your family with aftermath arrangements. The regulation is equally clear on costs: "Chinese citizens, legal persons and unincorporated organizations that receive food, accommodation, transport, medical or other goods and services from third parties during consular protection and assistance should pay the costs that are their own responsibility." So costs others advanced for food, transport and medical care are still yours to pay.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2023). 领事保护与协助条例（国令第 763 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm>（第九、十、十四、二十六条）
- Notes: China only: Embassies and consulates do not pay your fines, medical bills or air tickets, and they cannot change a court's ruling in the country. What they can give you is information on local lawyers, interpreters, medical and funeral services, and help contacting family and friends and finding ways to seek remedy. So prepare money and insurance yourself before you leave, and do not treat "the state will cover me" as a plan.

### 4. Buy insurance that covers overseas medical care and medical evacuation, not just flight-delay cover
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: A short-term overseas travel policy costs from tens to hundreds of RMB.
- Plain language: You pay for medical care abroad yourself. Medical insurance in China mostly does not pay out abroad, and consular staff will not advance the money. The real risk to a family is a long hospital stay after serious injury, plus transport home. Check two limits: overseas medical costs, and emergency medical transport and repatriation.
- Benefit: You pay for medical care abroad yourself. Medical insurance in China basically does not reimburse costs abroad. Consular protection does not advance this money either; see item 3 (what consular protection can and cannot do). The basis is Article 26 of the Consular Protection and Assistance Regulations. What can really ruin a family is not outpatient fees but hospital stays after serious injury, and medical transport. Moving someone from a foreign hospital back to China needs a chartered plane or a medical escort, which is one of the most expensive self-paid items. The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention says that emergency transport from a remote area to a good hospital could cost more than US$100,000 if you pay yourself. When you buy insurance, check two limits first. One is the overseas medical expense limit. The other is the emergency medical transport and repatriation limit, which covers how much is paid for transfer to another hospital and for returning you home after an incident.
- Evidence level: C
- Sources: 国务院 (2023). 领事保护与协助条例（国令第 763 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm>（第二十六条）；美国疾病控制与预防中心. Travelers' Health: Travel Insurance：「Medical evacuation insurance covers emergency transportation from a remote area to a high-quality hospital, which could otherwise cost more than $100,000.」. <https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/page/insurance>；其余为作者经验，无直接文献
- Notes: China only: The US$100,000 figure is a lower-bound statement, not a cost range. I did not find an official or industry statistic on cost ranges, so this book does not give a specific range. Before you buy, check three things. First, whether the country you are going to is covered. Second, whether high-risk activities such as diving, skiing and riding motorbikes are excluded. Third, whether the insurer pays the hospital directly, or whether you pay first and claim the money back later. Buy cover even for countries where you do not need a visa.

### 5. Treat any "high-paying job abroad" offer as a scam, because if you are tricked into online fraud work overseas, you may be barred from leaving China when you return
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is turning down a job that looks good.
- Plain language: Watch for these signs: pay far above the market rate, no questions about education or experience, no proper contract, a gathering point in a border city, and someone holding your passport. Once you go, you may lose your freedom and your passport. If your papers are false, border inspection will stop you from leaving.
- Benefit: Official sources describe these cases directly. In recent years, some people were tricked into going abroad, or invented a reason to leave illegally, and then took part in illegal activities such as cross-border gambling and telecom and online fraud. These activities seriously endanger people's lives and property. The new rules tighten both ends. On the exit side, the reasons for applying to leave or enter, and for staying or residing, must be genuine and lawful. If you submit false materials or tell lies, immigration authorities may refuse to issue your documents or let you leave. On the return side, a Chinese citizen who engages in illegal or criminal activity abroad that endangers national security or interests may be barred from leaving China for 6 months to 3 years, starting from the day they return. Being administratively detained for obtaining documents by deception, or for illegal exit or entry, carries the same 6-month to 3-year ban.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2026). 关于出境入境管理的规定（国令第 841 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202607/content_7077173.htm>（第三、四条）；司法部、公安部、国家移民局 (2026). 就《国务院关于出境入境管理的规定》答记者问. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202608/content_7077270.htm>
- Notes: China only: The signs are fairly consistent: a salary far above the same job's market rate, no education or experience required, no formal contract, an order to gather in a border city, and an agent who arranges your visa and keeps your passport. There are already specific consular warnings for northern Myanmar. If you go, your personal freedom and passport are no longer in your own hands, so do not assume you can leave whenever you want.

### 6. Overseas cash withdrawals are capped at RMB 100,000 a year across all your cards in your name, you may carry at most RMB 20,000 per entry or exit, and foreign cash over US$5,000 must be declared to customs
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. Before you leave, estimate how much cash you need and pay for the rest by card. The hard part is breaking the habit: if you can pay by card, do not withdraw cash. Taking a large amount of foreign banknotes out of China requires a bank-issued certificate first, which means one extra trip.
- Plain language: Cash withdrawals abroad count across all cards in your name, up to RMB 100,000 a year. Going over blocks withdrawals this year and next, but card payments are not affected. You may carry at most RMB 20,000 in renminbi each time you enter or leave China. Foreign cash above US$5,000 must be declared, and hidden cash can be confiscated.
- Benefit: The basis is the notice on regulating large cash withdrawals abroad by bank card issued by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE, the government agency that manages China's foreign currency), Huifa [2017] No. 29, in force from 1 January 2018. Article 1 says: "An individual holding a domestic bank card may withdraw cash abroad with a total equivalent of up to RMB 100,000 per calendar year across all bank cards in their name, including supplementary cards. If the annual limit is exceeded, withdrawals abroad with domestic bank cards will be suspended for the current year and the following year." Paragraph 2 says: "Individuals may not evade, or help others evade, the overseas cash withdrawal controls by borrowing someone else's bank card or lending their own." Article 2 says SAFE sends the suspension list to card-issuing banks every day, and banks must stop listed people from withdrawing cash abroad with their cards from 5 pm Beijing time on the same day at the latest. Article 3 says people on the list may bring a valid ID to a SAFE branch to check their own overseas withdrawal records. Article 4 raises the daily limit per foreign-currency card from the equivalent of US$1,000 to the equivalent of RMB 10,000; renminbi cards stay capped at the equivalent of RMB 10,000 per day. Article 6 says banks, domestic renminbi card clearing organizations and individuals who violate the notice are punished under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations. SAFE's Q&A also clarifies several points. The notice does not affect card spending abroad. Food, lodging, transport and shopping for tourism, business or study are current-account transactions, and all of them can be paid by bank card. These purchases also do not use up the individual's annual US$50,000 foreign-exchange purchase quota. SAFE calculates the annual total from data reported by each card-issuing institution, because real-time control would slow transactions down, so it uses delayed control (it does not block withdrawals on the spot). Nationwide, 81% of cash withdrawals abroad on domestic bank cards in 2016 were under RMB 30,000. Carrying cash in and out follows separate rules. From 1 April 2025, customs uses a new declaration form. Its back page says: "Each person may carry no more than RMB 20,000 in renminbi each time they enter or leave the country." Foreign banknotes brought into China must be declared to customs in writing if they exceed the equivalent of US$5,000. Taking out between the equivalent of US$5,000 and US$10,000 requires an outbound foreign currency carrying permit from a bank first, then a customs declaration. Above US$10,000, the permit must come from SAFE. SAFE's measures say that people leaving China "in principle may not carry foreign banknotes exceeding the equivalent of US$10,000 out of the country." Customs baggage rules say that carrying currency or banknotes above the prescribed amount in or out of China must be declared to customs in writing. If a customs inspection starts before the written declaration is finished, the cash counts as undeclared. Undeclared cash that is not hidden draws a warning, and a fine of up to 20% of the item's value. Cash that is hidden, disguised or concealed counts as smuggling: it is confiscated, and a fine of up to the equivalent amount may also apply.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国家外汇管理局 (2017). 关于规范银行卡境外大额提取现金交易的通知（汇发〔2017〕29 号，第一、二、三、四、六、七条，2018 年 1 月 1 日起实施）. <https://www.safe.gov.cn/safe/2017/1230/21873.html>；国家外汇管理局 (2017). 外汇局有关负责人就规范银行卡境外大额提取现金交易有关问题答记者问. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/2017-12/31/content_5251958.htm>；中国银联国际. 境外ATM取款. <https://m.unionpayintl.com/wap/cn/serviceCenter/cardUsingInstructions/805.shtml>；海关总署 (2025). 关于进出境人员行李物品申报有关事宜的公告（海关总署公告 2025 年第 43 号，附件申报单背面「重要提示」第二部分）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202504/content_7017195.htm>；海关总署 (2025). 中华人民共和国海关进出境行李物品监管办法（海关总署令第 276 号，第九、十、十一条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202502/content_7005697.htm>；国家外汇管理局、海关总署 (2003). 关于印发《携带外币现钞出入境管理暂行办法》的通知（汇发〔2003〕102 号，第三、五、七条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2004/content_63085.htm>；中国人民银行 (2004). 中国人民银行公告〔2004〕第 18 号（人民币出入境限额调整为 20000 元，2005 年 1 月 1 日起施行）. <https://www.pbc.gov.cn/tiaofasi/144941/3581332/3582861/2018072309595152720.pdf>；国务院 (2022 年修订). 海关行政处罚实施条例（第七、九、十九条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff80818180e0a61301811e43f41f1fa2>；U.S. Customs and Border Protection. Money and Other Monetary Instruments：「Federal law mandates that when entering or leaving the United States you must report amounts exceeding $10,000」「the $10,000 threshold applies to the total amount they are carrying or sending collectively, not per individual」. <https://www.cbp.gov/travel/international-visitors/kbyg/money>
- Notes: China only: Limits reset on 1 January each year. The suspension applies only to cash withdrawals abroad; card spending abroad is not affected. If you really need large amounts of cash overseas, first buy foreign currency under the Measures for the Administration of Individual Foreign Exchange, then take the foreign banknotes out of China. Above US$5,000, get the carrying permit first and declare at the customs red channel. Destination countries have their own declaration thresholds, so check before you leave. For example, US Customs and Border Protection says that entering or leaving the United States with more than US$10,000 must be reported, and the threshold applies to the combined amount for a group, not per person. The cash rules were checked against the official websites on 14 October 2026. Cash withdrawals have two more traps, as described on the UnionPay International "ATM cash withdrawal abroad" page. UnionPay is China's bank card network. First, dual-branded cards may not be able to use the UnionPay channel at some ATMs in some countries. Second, some overseas ATMs show an extra surcharge on screen. The local acquiring institution collects it, and it "has nothing to do with UnionPay." The same page advises that because credit card cash withdrawals carry overdraft interest, cardholders should use a UnionPay debit card for withdrawals abroad. If a card is lost, swallowed or fraudulently used, see item 5 of section 14 (first report the loss and freeze the card, then call the police). Cash and carrying limits are as of October 2026; before you leave, check the latest notices from SAFE and customs.

### 7. Photograph your passport, visa and ID card and keep a copy in the cloud; if the passport is lost, report it to the police first, then apply for a travel document
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. Take a few photos before you leave.
- Plain language: If your passport is lost, your embassy or consulate can issue a travel document for going home, but you must prove who you are. Photos of your passport and visa pages and entry records speed this up. Report the loss to local police first and keep the receipt. Phones get lost too, so keep photos off your phone alone.
- Benefit: If your passport is lost, your embassy or consulate abroad can issue a travel document for returning home, but you must first prove who you are. A clear photo of the passport data page, a photo of the visa page and your entry and exit records make a big difference to how quickly it is issued. You must also report the loss to local police and obtain a police report receipt. Many countries require this document for exit checks and insurance claims. To reach the embassy or consulate, use the two consular protection hotlines you saved in item 2.
- Evidence level: C
- Sources: 国务院 (2023). 领事保护与协助条例（国令第 763 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm>（第十六条）；作者经验，无直接文献
- Notes: China only: Store copies by photographing the pages and placing them in two separate places, for example one copy in a cloud drive and one sent to a family member. Also print a paper photocopy and keep it apart from the original. Photograph your bank cards and the prescriptions for any regular medicines too. Phones are usually lost together with passports, so a copy stored only on your phone is no copy at all. Cloud drives often require an SMS verification code to log in, so if your phone is lost you may be locked out. Before you leave, try logging in on another device to confirm you can open the files without this phone. ID photos are sensitive information, so an encrypted store is safer. But if you forget the password to an encrypted store, nobody can open it, so do not skip the copy for your family.

### 8. Before driving abroad, check whether the destination accepts a Chinese license; most "international driving permits" sold online are worthless
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Check the destination's rules once before you go.
- Plain language: An "international driving permit" bought through an agency has no legal force in mainland China. It is a translation recognized only among 1949 Geneva Convention states, and mainland China is not one of them. Whether your Chinese license works depends on the destination's rules, so check first.
- Benefit: The so-called "international driving permit" (IDP) is a translation recognized between states party to the 1949 Convention on Road Traffic. This convention was concluded in Geneva on 19 September 1949 and entered into force on 26 March 1952. It currently has 103 contracting parties (countries and territories that have signed it). Mainland China is not on this list, so China does not issue such international permits. Any agency in China that "arranges an international driving permit" produces no legal effect. Whether you can drive on a Chinese license depends on the destination's own rules. Some accept a Chinese license with a notarized translation, some require you to obtain a local license, and some do not accept it at all.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: United Nations Treaty Collection. Convention on Road Traffic, Geneva, 19 September 1949 — status of treaties. <https://treaties.un.org/pages/ViewDetailsV.aspx?src=TREATY&mtdsg_no=XI-B-1&chapter=11&clang=_en>
- Notes: China only: Driving on a license the destination does not accept can get your insurance claim refused, and that is the real cost. This point reflects the author's experience; check your policy wording and local law. There are two ways to check: look up the destination's "Notice for Chinese Citizens" on China's consular services website (run by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs), or ask the car rental company for written confirmation. Hong Kong, Macau, Taiwan and some countries have separate arrangements.

### 9. When using an agency for visas, study abroad or immigration, first ask whether it has filed with the immigration authority
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Ask one more question about its filing status, and keep the paperwork in writing.
- Plain language: From 15 September 2026, agencies that handle visas, study abroad and immigration must file with the immigration authority. Exaggerated advertising, and providing or helping with false materials, are expressly banned. Be careful of anyone who says "guaranteed to pass", "guaranteed visa" or "we will prepare your materials".
- Benefit: In 2018 the approval requirement for private exit-entry agencies was cancelled, so no permit was needed before opening one. The number of agencies then grew quickly, and problems appeared: the basic situation of agencies was unclear, some agencies handled business illegally, and the lawful rights of people leaving and entering the country were harmed. The authorities could not even count how many there were. From 15 September 2026, these agencies move to a filing system. An agency must file with the immigration authority where it is located within 15 days of being set up. Agencies already operating must complete filing within 90 days of the rules taking effect. The prohibited conduct falls into four types. First, "publishing false information, or attracting clients through exaggerated or misleading advertising". Second, "providing or helping to provide false materials, or helping others handle visas, residence permits, passports or other entry-exit documents or procedures in violation of the rules". Third, "leaking, selling or illegally providing personal privacy or personal information". Fourth, "organizing or helping others engage in cross-border illegal criminal activity". Overseas companies and institutions may not provide this kind of agency service within China.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2026). 关于出境入境管理的规定（国令第 841 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202607/content_7077173.htm>（第七、八、十、十二、十三条）；United States Code, Title 8, §1182(a)(6)(C)(i) Misrepresentation. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/8/1182>（康奈尔大学法律信息研究所转载）
- Notes: China only: Agencies that routinely promise "guaranteed pass", "guaranteed visa" and "we will prepare your materials" are doing things that are expressly prohibited. If something goes wrong, the applicant bears the consequences. An honest mistake in filling in a form usually means the application is refused this time; this is the author's experience. Using false materials, which is judged as fraud, has far more serious consequences. Taking the United States as an example, immigration law provides that a foreigner who obtains a visa through fraud, or by willfully misrepresenting a material fact, may not enter. Policy consultation and information lookups that are not done for profit do not count as agency services.

### 10. Do not issue invitation letters for strangers, and do not let anyone use your name for entry-exit procedures
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is turning down a favor in person.
- Plain language: If you issue a false invitation or application for someone, you pay a fine of 5,000 to 10,000 yuan (RMB), plus confiscation of any illegal gains. A company pays 10,000 to 50,000 yuan, and its managers and handlers pay another 5,000 to 10,000. A single favor can cost you this money.
- Benefit: The person who issues an invitation letter must answer for whether its content is true. The original text reads: "An individual who issues a false invitation letter or other application materials for another person's entry or exit, stay or residence shall be fined 5,000 yuan or more but no more than 10,000 yuan by the immigration authority; any illegal gains shall be confiscated." If a unit issues false materials, it is fined 10,000 yuan to 50,000 yuan. In addition, the directly responsible supervisors and other directly responsible persons (the leaders in charge and the people who handled the matter) are each fined a further 5,000 yuan to 10,000 yuan.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2026). 关于出境入境管理的规定（国令第 841 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202607/content_7077173.htm>（第三、十一条）
- Notes: China only: This is the same kind of risk as item 29 of section 8 ("do not carry things for strangers"): the material is issued in your name, so the consequences fall on you. Public officials and military personnel who try to use an agency to illegally obtain foreign nationality or overseas residence status will not be helped. The agency must also report them to the supervisory authority, China's anti-corruption watchdog.

### 11. Before leaving the country, give one family member your itinerary, accommodation and travel companions, and agree how often you will check in
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. Send one message before you leave.
- Plain language: If you lose contact abroad, family can ask the embassy or consulate for help, and it will explain how to report to local police. This only works if someone at home knows where you should be and when you should check in. Send one family member your flight, address, companions' phone numbers and check-in schedule.
- Benefit: When a Chinese citizen goes missing abroad, relatives can ask overseas diplomatic missions for help. The regulation states: "Overseas diplomatic missions shall provide local police reporting methods and other information on how to obtain help." Once local police open a case, the embassy or consulate will urge them to act promptly. Whether this help can be triggered depends on whether someone at home knows where you are supposed to be and when you are supposed to make contact.
- Evidence level: C

- Notes: China only: Four things are enough: your flight number, your accommodation address, your companions' names and phone numbers, and how often you agreed to check in. ### 12. Check the destination's medicine rules before you travel: cold and cough remedies with pseudoephedrine or codeine, sleeping pills, psychotropic drugs and strong painkillers need original packaging, a prescription or doctor's certificate, and only your own quantity
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Free to check the destination's official website, which takes a few minutes. If you need to carry controlled drugs, you also have to visit a hospital for a certificate, and apply about two weeks ahead.
- Plain language: Japan requires advance permission for cold and cough medicines with over 10% pseudoephedrine or over 1% codeine. Sleeping pills over one month's supply also need a Japanese permit, and Singapore requires advance approval for controlled medicines. Carry medicine in original packaging with a prescription or doctor's letter, and only your own amount. Otherwise it may be confiscated and fined.
- Benefit: Japan sorts medicines by ingredient. Medicines containing more than 10% pseudoephedrine count as stimulant raw materials, and those containing more than 1% codeine count as narcotics. Both groups need permission from the Narcotics Control Department of Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare before you enter, applied for at least 14 days ahead. You must carry the medicine yourself. It cannot be mailed, and you cannot ask family to carry it. Psychotropic drugs such as diazepam and triazolam need no permit for up to one month's supply. Ordinary medicines also have limits: up to one month's supply for prescription drugs and up to two months' supply for other drugs. Beyond that, you must first obtain an "import confirmation certificate". The Chinese Embassy in Japan warns that bringing medicines into Japan in violation of the rules "will result in fines, detention or even prison, and the prohibited items will be confiscated". Singapore's Health Sciences Authority (HSA) allows medicines without controlled ingredients up to 3 months' supply. Carry a photocopy of your prescription or doctor's letter. Medicine should stay in its original packaging with the pharmacy label. For medicines with controlled ingredients, apply for approval at least 2 weeks in advance. Anything over 3 months' supply is not allowed in, and medicine in chewing-gum form is not allowed either. Carrying medicine for someone else is limited to a close relative's own prescription medicine. Non-compliant medicine will be confiscated, and enforcement action may be taken. On the Chinese side, the Customs Law requires items carried in and out by individuals to be "for personal use and of reasonable quantity". Narcotics and Class I psychotropic drugs can be carried up to the maximum quantity of a single prescription, with a hospital diagnosis and proof of identity. Customs releases them on a personal-use and reasonable basis.

### 13. On returning to China, do not bring meat products (vacuum-packed ham and dried meat included), cheese, fresh fruit or seeds; if you carry any animal or plant products, go through the red channel and declare them
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Avoid these categories when shopping abroad, and check your luggage before you return. If you have items you are not sure about, go through the red channel and queue a little longer.
- Plain language: When you return to China, meat cannot be brought in, raw or cooked, and that includes vacuum-packed ham, dried meat and sausage. Cheese, butter, fresh eggs, fresh fruit and vegetables, and seeds are also banned, and they will be sent back or destroyed. Undeclared animal or plant products can also bring a fine of up to 5,000 yuan.
- Benefit: In 2021 the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs and the General Administration of Customs published a list of prohibited items. It covers goods carried in your luggage, mailed, couriered, or bought directly through cross-border e-commerce. The categories that matter most to ordinary travelers are these. The first is meat, in the original wording "(raw or cooked) meat, including offal and meat products"; the list makes no exception for packaging. The second is milk and eggs, including cream, butter, cheese and condensed milk, as well as preserved duck eggs, salted eggs and mayonnaise. The third is fresh fruit and vegetables, and fresh-cut flowers. The fourth is seeds, seedlings and other plant material that can grow. The fifth is live animals, except cats and dogs. Bird's nest is also banned, except canned bird's nest that has undergone commercial sterilization. Article 29 of the Law on Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine states that items on the list brought in "shall be returned or destroyed". Items not on the list may be carried, but must still be declared. Customs' luggage supervision measures state that anyone carrying animals, plants or their products must declare them to customs in writing. If the written declaration is not complete when customs begins inspection, the item counts as undeclared. The measures also say: "If you do not understand the customs rules or do not know how to choose a channel, you should choose the red channel." Failure to declare is penalized under Article 39 of the quarantine law, which fines "failure to submit for inspection". Article 59 of the implementing regulations sets this fine at up to 5,000 yuan.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 农业农村部、海关总署 (2021). 农业农村部 海关总署公告第 470 号（中华人民共和国禁止携带、寄递进境的动植物及其产品和其他检疫物名录）. <https://www.moa.gov.cn/nybgb/2022/202202/202203/t20220324_6393815.htm>；全国人大常委会 (2009 年修正). 进出境动植物检疫法（第二十九、三十、三十九条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf6ab3604e7>；国务院 (1996). 进出境动植物检疫法实施条例（国务院令第 206 号，第四十一、五十九条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff8080816f3cbb3c016f4086afae03c1>；海关总署 (2025). 中华人民共和国海关进出境行李物品监管办法（海关总署令第 276 号，第九、十一、十二、二十五、二十七条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202502/content_7005697.htm>；海关总署 (2025). 关于进出境人员行李物品申报有关事宜的公告（海关总署公告 2025 年第 43 号，第一、三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202504/content_7017195.htm>

Dried or cooked aquatic products, such as squid and dried shrimp, are not on the prohibited list but must still be declared.
