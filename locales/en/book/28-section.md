# 28. Do Not Damage Your Body for Your Looks

This section is about several ways people harm their bodies to change how they look: starving themselves, taking drugs, getting injections, and having surgery. These decisions are often made because of comparing oneself with others, the costs fall entirely on the person, and a good share of the damage cannot be undone.

This section does not judge what anyone wants to look like. That is each person's own choice. For the same goal, you can go through a route with a prescription, a licensed provider and follow-up checks, or through online shopping, a beauty salon, or dosing yourself. This section only compares how much risk the two routes carry. The main measure is what actually happens to the body, such as blindness, hospital admission or death. Two of the items below also carry criminal liability for illegal medical practice.

### 1. Do not control your weight with extreme dieting, fasting or purging; if you want to lose weight, do it through exercise
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Nothing. You also save the money you would spend on weight-loss products.
- Plain language: People with anorexia nervosa are about 5.9 times as likely to die as their peers. Out of every 1,000 people followed for a year, 5.1 die, one in five of them by suicide. Girls who dieted severely were 18 times as likely to develop an eating disorder. To control weight, exercise more rather than eat too little.
- Benefit: Pooled across 36 studies. The anorexia nervosa group was followed for a combined 166,642 person-years (the years each person was followed, added up). Their standardized mortality ratio was 5.86, meaning their risk of death was about 5.9 times that of people their age. This is 5.1 deaths per 1,000 person-years, that is, 5.1 deaths among 1,000 people followed for one year. About one in five of the deaths were suicides. The standardized mortality ratio was 1.93 for bulimia and 1.92 for other eating disorders, each about 1.9 times the rate for people their age. Another study followed 14–15-year-old students for three years at 44 middle schools in Victoria, Australia, checking the same students six times. New eating disorders among girls were 21.8 per 1,000 person-years, that is, 21.8 new cases per 1,000 girls followed for one year. Girls who dieted severely were 18 times as likely to develop one as girls who did not diet, and moderate dieters were 5 times as likely. The researchers then adjusted for past dieting and existing mental symptoms, removing the effect of "dieted before" and "already had mental symptoms." After that adjustment, BMI (a body-size measure calculated as weight divided by height squared), physical activity and sex no longer predicted new eating disorders. The authors concluded that "using exercise rather than food restriction to control weight among adolescents may be associated with a lower risk of eating disorders."
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Arcelus J et al. (2011). Mortality rates in patients with anorexia nervosa and other eating disorders. A meta-analysis of 36 studies. Arch Gen Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archgenpsychiatry.2011.74>；Patton GC et al. (1999). Onset of adolescent eating disorders: population based cohort study over 3 years. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.318.7186.765>
- Notes: "Severe dieting" here means keeping food intake far below what the body needs over a long period. It does not mean skipping one meal or a late-night snack. If you are vomiting, binge eating and blaming yourself afterwards, or weighing yourself repeatedly and cannot stop, see a psychiatrist or the clinical psychology department of a general hospital. This is a condition with established treatment, not a matter of willpower. For the link between BMI and mortality, see item 32 of section 2, which covers long-term weight ranges, not weight-loss methods.

### 2. Before any injection, thread lift or surgery, check two things: whether the clinic's license covers medical aesthetics, and whether the person doing it is the registered lead doctor
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Nothing. Checking these two things takes a few minutes. The clinic should display its Medical Institution Practice License in the lobby. You can verify a doctor's qualifications in the health authority's registered-practitioner search.
- Plain language: Anything that breaks the skin or goes into the body counts as medical treatment. Only a clinic licensed for medical aesthetics may do it, and the person must be a registered doctor. Salons, studios and hotel rooms do not qualify. Illegal practice that seriously harms someone can mean 3 to 10 years in prison, and a death 10 years or more.
- Benefit: Article 2 of the Medical Aesthetic Services Management Measures defines medical aesthetics. The original text reads: "repair and reshaping of a person's appearance and the form of body parts using surgery, drugs, medical devices, or other medical techniques that are traumatic or invasive." In plain terms, if a procedure breaks the skin, goes into the body or uses needles, it counts as medical treatment. Articles 8 and 24 require a clinic to first obtain a Medical Institution Practice License and have the registration authority approve a medical aesthetics department before it can operate. If "medical aesthetics" is not listed on the license, the clinic cannot perform these procedures. Article 11 requires the lead doctor, the one who performs the procedure, to hold a physician's license and be registered. Aesthetic surgery requires at least 6 years of relevant clinical experience, aesthetic dentistry 5 years or more, and aesthetic traditional Chinese medicine and aesthetic dermatology at least 3 years each. Article 20 requires written disclosure before the procedure of the indications, contraindications (when the procedure is suitable and when it is not), medical risks and precautions, and a signed consent. Article 336 of the Criminal Law covers illegal medical practice, meaning a person without a doctor's license who gives injections, performs surgery or treats patients. For serious cases, the penalty is up to 3 years in prison, criminal detention or public surveillance, with or without a fine. Seriously harming a patient's health carries 3 to 10 years in prison plus a fine. Causing a patient's death carries 10 years or more in prison plus a fine.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 卫生部 (2002). 医疗美容服务管理办法（卫生部令第 19 号，第二条、第八条、第十一条、第十六条、第二十条、第二十四条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2003/content_62198.htm>；全国人大 (1997). 刑法（第三百三十六条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>
- Notes: China only: The same Measures in Article 16 also require medical aesthetic procedures to be done only in medical aesthetic institutions, or in hospitals or clinics that have a medical aesthetics department. So home injections or private work in a studio breaks the rule on location alone. Article 20 also says that minors and others who cannot decide for themselves need guardian consent, with a parent's signature. Facials, massage and manicures count as everyday beauty services and fall outside these rules. But once needles or knives are involved, it counts as medical aesthetics.

### 3. Avoid filler in the bridge of the nose, between the brows and on the forehead, and do not seek unlicensed injectors to save money
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Nothing. You only change where the filler goes, or skip it.
- Plain language: More than half of the vision losses after filler were in the nose, then between the brows and on the forehead. Over 80% of the fillers were hyaluronic acid. About one in five people fully recovered their sight, and nearly one in five had brain complications. No rescue treatment reliably works, so avoid these areas.
- Benefit: Pooled from case reports published in journals worldwide. From January 2015 to September 2018, 48 new cases of partial or complete vision loss after filler injection were reported. The most affected site was the nose, at 56.3%. Next were between the brows at 27.1%, the forehead at 18.8%, and the nasolabial fold (the line running from the side of the nose to the mouth corner) at 14.6%. Hyaluronic acid was the filler in 81.3% of cases. Skin changes occurred in 43.8%. Central nervous system complications (problems in the brain) occurred in 18.8%. Vision fully recovered in 10 cases (20.8%), partly recovered in 8 (16.7%), and the rest did not recover. The authors concluded that treatment approaches vary widely and that no single treatment has been shown to work consistently.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Beleznay K et al. (2019). Update on Avoiding and Treating Blindness From Fillers: A Recent Review of the World Literature. Aesthet Surg J. <https://doi.org/10.1093/asj/sjz053>
- Notes: These numbers come from counting published cases one by one. They include only the people who had problems, not the total number of people injected. So they cannot show the chance of going blind from one injection. They only show which areas have more problems and that the damage is usually hard to reverse afterwards. This is also why the rating is B. Standard safety steps at legitimate clinics are already routine. First, map the blood vessels under the injection site. Use a blunt cannula rather than a sharp needle. Inject small amounts while withdrawing, and have hyaluronidase (an enzyme that dissolves hyaluronic acid) ready in advance. Each of these steps requires a trained doctor doing the procedure, which is another reason not to use unofficial channels. Before any procedure, check item 2 of this section (the clinic's license and the lead doctor).

### 4. Do not buy diet pills, coffees, candies or "enzyme plums" that promise quick weight loss
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Nothing. You also save the money you would spend on these products. The hard part is accepting that there is no such thing as losing several jin (a jin is half a kilo) in a week.
- Plain language: Some of these products are secretly laced with sibutramine, a former weight-loss drug that China withdrew in 2010. Over more than three years, people over 55 with heart disease or diabetes who were overweight had about 30% higher risk of heart attack and 36% higher risk of stroke. Their extra weight loss was only 1.7 kg. Tests may catch the drug only after you have taken it.
- Benefit: The SCOUT trial enrolled 9,804 people aged 55 or older who were overweight or obese and had cardiovascular disease or type 2 diabetes. Participants were randomly assigned to take the drug or not, for an average of 3.4 years. The main endpoint was a set of predefined adverse events: non-fatal heart attack, non-fatal stroke, cardiac arrest with resuscitation, and cardiovascular death. These events occurred in 11.4% versus 10.0%. HR 1.16 (95% CI 1.03–1.31, P=0.02) means the people taking the drug had about 16% higher risk of these events. The true value is probably between 3% and 31% higher. Non-fatal heart attack: 4.1% versus 3.2%, HR 1.28 (1.04–1.57), about 30% higher. Non-fatal stroke: 2.6% versus 1.9%, HR 1.36 (1.04–1.77), about 36% higher. Cardiovascular death and all-cause death were not increased; all-cause means deaths from any cause combined. After randomization, the sibutramine group lost an average of 1.7 kg more. The former State Food and Drug Administration concluded, based on a safety assessment, that "the risks of weight-loss treatment outweigh the benefits," and in October 2010 it notified that production, sale and use of sibutramine preparations and raw materials must stop. Sibutramine and phenolphthalein are banned from being added to food, including health foods.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: James WPT et al. (2010). Effect of sibutramine on cardiovascular outcomes in overweight and obese subjects. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1003114>；天津市市场监督管理委员会. 警惕部分食品中违法添加「盐酸西布曲明」和「酚酞」. <https://scjg.tj.gov.cn/tjsscjdglwyh_52651/hdpt/cjwtyxfts/202007/t20200720_2972549.html>
- Notes: China only: The SCOUT trial enrolled people who already had heart disease or diabetes and were therefore at high risk. The trial does not show that healthy younger people face the same harm. But because it was impossible to tell who was high-risk, regulators withdrew the drug altogether. Phenolphthalein is another ingredient often added in secret. It is a laxative that, taken long-term in excess, can disturb the body's electrolytes and trigger heart rhythm problems. To judge a product: genuine weight-loss drugs are prescription medicines, and you can check their approval number at pharmacies and hospitals. Products sold as "coffee", "candy", "plums" or "meal replacements" that promise several jin of weight loss in a week should, by definition, have no effect. If they do, something has been added. The original withdrawal notice is document Guo Shi Yao Jian Ban [2010] No. 432, published on the National Medical Products Administration website. This section cites a summary page from the Tianjin Market Supervision Administration instead; the two statements "the risks outweigh the benefits" and "production, sale and use stopped in October 2010" can be checked word for word there.

### 5. Do not use anabolic steroids ("muscle-building shots" or "oral pills") to build muscle
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Nothing, and you save on drug costs. The hard part is accepting slower muscle growth.
- Plain language: In a Danish study, men caught with steroids in gym tests had about three times the death risk of their peers and visited hospital more than twice as often each year. Acne, breast enlargement and erectile dysfunction each affected over 1 in 10 users. These drugs are prescription-only in China, so selling them without a prescription is illegal.
- Benefit: Researchers in Denmark looked back through records and matched each user with age-matched controls. The users were 545 men who tested positive for androgenic anabolic steroids in gym tests between 3 January 2006 and 1 March 2018, matched with 5,450 controls. A second check used 644 men who refused testing and 6,440 controls, and its results were consistent. Result: users' death rate was 3 times that of controls, HR 3.0 (95% CI 1.3–7.0), meaning the true multiple is roughly between 1.3 and 7. The median number of hospital admissions or visits per year was 0.81 versus 0.36 (P<0.0001). The median is the number that half of people are above and half are below. This P value means the difference is very unlikely to be chance. Acne, male breast enlargement and erectile dysfunction each affected more than 10% of users, meaning at least one in ten users had each, and all were clearly higher than in controls (P<0.0001). The second check gave consistent results. Article 7 of the Anti-Doping Regulations: the state strictly controls prohibited substances on the doping list, and no unit or individual may illegally produce, sell, or import or export them. The same regulation puts anabolic agents and peptide hormones under prescription-only control, meaning they can be bought only with a doctor's prescription. "Protein anabolic preparations" is the regulatory term for this type of drug.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Horwitz H et al. (2019). Health consequences of androgenic anabolic steroid use. J Intern Med. <https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.12850>；国务院 (2004). 反兴奋剂条例（国务院令第 398 号，第七条、第九条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2004/content_63129.htm>
- Notes: China only: Disputed: This is an observational study following people over time, not a randomized trial. People who use these drugs may also take other risks, so the 3-fold figure should not be attributed entirely to the drugs. The true multiple could be as low as 1.3 or as high as 7.0, a very wide range. But the side effects of acne, male breast enlargement and erectile dysfunction, each seen in more than 1 in 10 users, are visible and less affected by this kind of bias. The regulatory term "protein anabolic preparations" refers to these drugs; if you see that term in regulations, it means them. Ordinary protein powder and creatine are not covered by this item. The problem is that products marketed as "fast muscle" may secretly contain ingredients. The test is the same as for weight-loss products (item 4 of this section, do not buy diet products that promise quick weight loss): real drugs are prescription-only and have a checkable approval number. Products sold as "muscle powder" or "nutritional supplements" that promise results within weeks have something added if they work.

### 6. If you take weight-loss pills or injections such as semaglutide, get a prescription from a hospital, follow the doctor's dosing and check-ups, and do not buy from online shops that ship without a prescription or get injections at beauty salons
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: One clinic registration plus the trip there and back, several tens of RMB and half a day. People on weight-loss injections also need to return for check-ups on the doctor's schedule. Drug costs are extra, and after stopping the injections weight usually returns, so budget for long-term use.
- Plain language: Prescription drugs need a real prescription, even online, and a verified buyer. Shops that ship without a consultation, and salons that inject at home, often sell drugs of unknown origin with no one checking dose or safety. After a year off weight-loss injections, about two-thirds of the weight returned, so plan for long-term use.
- Benefit: The Measures on Quality Supervision of Drug Operation and Use require retail drug companies to follow the classification system that separates prescription drugs from non-prescription drugs. Under this system, one class must be bought with a doctor's prescription and the other can be bought without one. Pharmacies must sell prescription drugs against a prescription and keep prescriptions for at least five years. When the pharmacist or other pharmaceutical staff are not on duty, a sign must say so, and no prescription drug may be sold without their review. Prescription drugs may not be sold in open-shelf format, meaning customers cannot pick them off shelves. Article 9 of the Measures on Online Sales of Drugs requires that online sales of prescription drugs to individuals ensure the prescription is genuine and reliable and that buyers register under their real names. Article 10 requires prominent risk warnings on every product page. Before a prescription is reviewed, the product insert may not be displayed and purchase services may not be offered. If a prescription has not been reviewed, the page should not show a buy button at all. Article 8 says drugs under special state control may not be sold online. These include vaccines, blood products, narcotic drugs, psychotropic drugs, toxic drugs for medical use, radioactive drugs, and drug precursor chemicals. The National Health Commission's Obesity Diagnosis and Treatment Guideline (2024 Edition) states that five drugs approved by the National Medical Products Administration are used for weight loss in adults with primary obesity: orlistat, liraglutide, beinaglutide, semaglutide and tirzepatide. The same guideline defines overweight as BMI 24 to under 28, and obesity as 28 or above. Drugs are considered only for people who are overweight with related conditions such as high blood sugar, high blood pressure, abnormal blood lipids or fatty liver, or who are already obese. This applies when lifestyle changes have not worked; drugs are added on top of that. The guideline requires a full assessment of the condition and contraindications before use, and regular follow-up with a specialist during treatment. Semaglutide starts at 0.25 mg per week, with a maintenance dose of 2.4 mg or 1.7 mg. Tirzepatide starts at 2.5 mg weekly, up to 15 mg. For people who lose less than 5% of their weight, the guideline recommends stopping. What happens after stopping is shown by three trials. The STEP 1 extension followed 327 people. After 68 weeks of semaglutide, average weight loss was 17.3%. A year after stopping, weight rose by 11.6 percentage points, leaving a net weight loss of only 5.6%. The authors concluded that about two-thirds of the previously lost weight returned, and most cardiometabolic measures went back close to their starting values. In STEP 4, 803 people who had taken semaglutide for 20 weeks and lost an average of 10.6% were randomly assigned. Those who continued lost a further 7.9% over the next 48 weeks, while those switched to placebo regained 6.9%. In SURMOUNT-4, 670 people first took tirzepatide for 36 weeks and averaged 20.9% weight loss. Those who continued lost a further 5.5%, while those switched to placebo regained 14.0%. In July 2026, the Yinchuan Municipal Market Supervision Administration issued a risk warning. It said that viral "miracle weight-loss shots" and "slimming pens" online are mostly repackaged, smuggled or counterfeit drugs of unknown origin. Buying them online or through a proxy and self-injecting can cause severe allergic reactions, acute pancreatitis, organ damage and wound infection. Aesthetic injection procedures may only be carried out by institutions with medical aesthetic qualifications. Home visits or private injections at beauty salons are not compliant; see item 2 of this section (checking the clinic license and lead doctor before injections, thread lifts or surgery).
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: 国家市场监督管理总局 (2023). 药品经营和使用质量监督管理办法（总局令第 84 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2023/issue_10846/202311/content_6917322.html>；国家市场监督管理总局 (2022). 药品网络销售监督管理办法（总局令第 58 号，第八条至第十条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2022/content_5717002.htm>；国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2024). 关于印发肥胖症诊疗指南（2024年版）的通知（附件第三部分「十、药物治疗」）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202410/content_6981734.htm>；Wilding JPH et al. (2022). Weight regain and cardiometabolic effects after withdrawal of semaglutide: The STEP 1 trial extension. Diabetes Obes Metab. <https://doi.org/10.1111/dom.14725>；Rubino D et al. (2021). Effect of Continued Weekly Subcutaneous Semaglutide vs Placebo on Weight Loss Maintenance in Adults With Overweight or Obesity: The STEP 4 Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2021.3224>；Aronne LJ et al. (2024). Continued Treatment With Tirzepatide for Maintenance of Weight Reduction in Adults With Obesity: The SURMOUNT-4 Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2023.24945>；银川市市场监督管理局 (2026). 市场监管部门医美消费及「减肥神药」风险提示. <https://scjg.yinchuan.gov.cn/zxdt/tzgg/202607/t20260703_5281032.html>
- Notes: China only: The legal provisions can be checked word for word, and the weight-regain figures also come from randomized trials. However, no quantitative study has measured how much less harm prescription use causes compared with self-purchase, so the rating is B, "hard to quantify." The STEP 1 extension stopped not only the drug but also diet and exercise guidance, and it followed only part of the original trial's participants. The steroids and sex hormones in items 5 and 7 of this section follow the same rules. When weight loss by medication is truly needed, a doctor first checks BMI, other conditions and any reasons not to use drugs, then decides whether to take one and which. That judgment cannot be bought.

### 7. Use sex hormone drugs only with a doctor's prescription and regular check-ups; do not buy them online or adjust the dose yourself
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: Regular clinic visits plus blood tests. Several hundred RMB a year and several half-days of your time.
- Plain language: This is about how to use them, not whether. Transgender women taking estrogen had more venous blood clots than their peers: about 4 extra per 1,000 over two years and about 17 over eight years. Regular check-ups watch for this risk. Buying online or changing your own dose means no one is checking.
- Benefit: Researchers followed people using electronic health records from Kaiser Permanente, a US health care group. The study included 2,842 transgender women and 2,118 transgender men, followed for an average of 4.0 and 3.6 years. Each person was matched with about 10 cisgender controls by birth year, race, clinic and enrollment time, giving 48,686 cisgender men and 48,775 cisgender women. Transgender women had more venous thromboembolism (blood clots blocking veins) than controls. Compared with cisgender men, the rate at 2 years was 4.1 per 1,000 people (95% CI 1.6–6.7), and at 8 years it was 16.7 (6.4–27.5). In other words, per 1,000 people there were 4.1 extra cases over two years and 16.7 over eight years. Compared with cisgender women, these figures were 3.4 (1.1–5.6) and 13.7 (4.1–22.7). Ischemic stroke (a stroke caused by a blocked blood vessel) and overall myocardial infarction (heart attack) rates were similar across groups. However, among transgender women who started hormone therapy during follow-up, the differences in venous clots and ischemic stroke were more pronounced. The evidence for transgender men is not sufficient to draw conclusions. The authors concluded that "these results may suggest the need for long-term vigilance regarding the vascular side effects of estrogen in transgender hormone therapy."
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Getahun D et al. (2018). Cross-sex Hormones and Acute Cardiovascular Events in Transgender Persons: A Cohort Study. Ann Intern Med. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M17-2785>；国家市场监督管理总局 (2022). 药品网络销售监督管理办法（总局令第 58 号，第九条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2022/content_5717002.htm>
- Notes: China only: The researchers themselves noted a weakness: they could not see how many people bought drugs outside the health system. So the line between "used the drug" and "did not" was never clean. This actually shows that drug use should be built into regular follow-up. The study only looked at whether blood vessels had problems. It does not answer whether hormone therapy should be used or what benefits it brings; that is a separate body of evidence. The same applies to every use of sex hormones, including contraceptive pills, menopausal hormone therapy and testosterone replacement. For people who buy online, follow forum posts to adjust doses, and never test for clots or liver and kidney markers, the risk comes from the lack of monitoring, not from the reason for taking the drug. The proper route in China is a regular medical institution's endocrinology department or the relevant specialty clinic for assessment, prescription and follow-up.

### 8. If feeling ugly has become repeated mirror checking and repeated urges to change, get a body image assessment before considering surgery
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=some gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: One psychiatry or clinical psychology appointment, a few dozen RMB. The hard part is going to this appointment before booking surgery.
- Plain language: About 15% of people seen at plastic surgery clinics have body dysmorphic disorder, a condition in which barely noticeable flaws are magnified enough to disrupt life. Among dermatology patients it is about 13%. In both groups, over 70% are women. Get a mental health check before deciding on surgery.
- Benefit: Pooled from 33 studies found through a systematic search. Among people seen at plastic surgery clinics, the prevalence of body dysmorphic disorder (magnifying barely visible flaws to the point of disrupting life) was 15.04%, with individual studies ranging from 2.21% to 56.67%. In other words, about 15 out of every 100 people seeking plastic surgery met the criteria, and single studies ranged from 2 to 57 per 100. The average age of these people was 34.54 ± 12.41 years, where the second figure is the spread around the average, and 74.38% were women. Among dermatology patients, the figure was 12.65%, with studies ranging from 4.52% to 35.16%, an average age of 27.79 ± 9.03 years, and 76.09% women. The authors recommended that plastic surgeons and dermatologists actively screen patients, identify those at high risk, and arrange joint management across several specialties. Another study asked 200 people with body dysmorphic disorder: 71.0% had sought non-psychiatric treatment such as dermatology or plastic surgery, and 64.0% had received it. The authors concluded that these treatments "rarely improve" body dysmorphic disorder and appear not to work for it.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Ribeiro RVE (2017). Prevalence of Body Dysmorphic Disorder in Plastic Surgery and Dermatology Patients: A Systematic Review with Meta-Analysis. Aesthetic Plast Surg. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s00266-017-0869-0>；Crerand CE, Phillips KA, Menard W, Fay C (2005). Nonpsychiatric medical treatment of body dysmorphic disorder. Psychosomatics, 46(6), 549-555. <https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.psy.46.6.549>；NHS. Body dysmorphic disorder (BDD). <https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/body-dysmorphia/>
- Notes: Study-level rates ranged from 2.21% to 56.67%, a very wide spread, because the questionnaires and populations differed. So the data only show that the rate is not low and that screening first is worthwhile. Do not treat 15.04% as a precise figure, which is also why the rating is B. At this rate, most people seeking plastic surgery do not have body dysmorphic disorder. This item targets the smaller group whose concerns are recurrent, persistent and already affecting work and relationships. For them, surgery and skin treatment rarely help. The UK NHS lists these signs: spending much time comparing one's looks with others, checking mirrors constantly or avoiding them altogether, making great efforts to cover a body part, and picking at the skin to make it "smooth." It affects work, social life and relationships.

### 9. For teeth straightening, see an orthodontist in person for an examination and keep follow-up appointments; do not buy aligners online and wear them at home
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=some gain=low scope=health -->
- Cost: More than buying online, because of registration and follow-up fees and regular trips to the clinic. The hard part is that treatment often lasts one to two years, so you need to keep attending follow-ups.
- Plain language: In China, clear aligners are Class II medical devices, and their instructions require use under an orthodontist's guidance with regular check-ups. The US FDA has not approved aligners sold directly to consumers without a dentist's supervision. Without in-person checks, your bite may shift, teeth may move in the wrong direction, and you may have pain or lose teeth.
- Benefit: The Technical Review Guideline for Registration of Bracketless Orthodontic Appliances (2022), issued by the Center for Medical Device Evaluation of the National Medical Products Administration, states that these aligners are "Class II." It requires the instructions to state that "during treatment, wearing must be carried out under the guidance of a physician with orthodontic professional qualifications," and to include "requirements for eating, brushing and regular follow-up visits." Product names should not contain vague, hypothetical concept names such as "invisible." The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) states: "The FDA has not authorized clear aligners that are sold directly to consumers without a prescription and supervision by a dentist or orthodontist," meaning it has not approved any clear aligners sold directly to individuals without a dentist's prescription and supervision. The FDA also says: "In-person examination by a dentist or orthodontist can help lower the risks of uneven bite, unintended tooth movement, pain, and tooth loss," meaning in-person checks by a dentist can reduce those risks. Regular in-person follow-ups after starting treatment are needed to confirm that teeth are moving safely as planned. Both documents are regulatory texts and do not give the proportion of harmful outcomes.
- Evidence level: C

### 10. Before buying a spot-removing or whitening cream, check the "Guo Zhuang Te Zi" registration number on the packaging against the NMPA website; avoid creams sold by WeChat micro-sellers or mixed by beauty salons themselves
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: Nothing, and checking the number takes a few minutes. The hard part is turning down the products that acquaintances and micro-sellers push hardest.
- Plain language: Spot-removing creams need a special-use registration number before they can be sold, but numbers can be misused. Some creams with mercury thousands of times over the limit carried such numbers, though the named maker had not made that batch. Mercury may damage the kidneys. Stopping use and treatment usually helps, but recovery can take months to two years.
- Benefit: Article 16 of the Regulation on the Supervision and Administration of Cosmetics lists skin-lightening and spot-removing cosmetics as special cosmetics. Article 17 requires special cosmetics to be registered with the State Drug Administration before they can be produced or imported. Article 36 requires the label to show the special cosmetics registration number. Article 20 requires registration information to be publicly disclosed, so the number can be checked on the NMPA website. Article 62 of the Cosmetics Registration and Filing Administration Measures specifies that domestic numbers are "Guo Zhuang Te Zi" plus year and sequence number, while imported ones are "Guo Zhuang Te Jin Zi." NMPA Notice No. 14 of 2026 published two batches of spot-removing creams, with mercury measured at 4,938 mg/kg and 9,281 mg/kg. The annex limit is no more than 1 mg/kg, so these two batches exceeded it by thousands of times. They were bought at two small cosmetics shops in Sichuan and Anhui, and their packaging carried special-use registration numbers. The labeled manufacturer disputed this; after review, the Guangdong Provincial Medical Products Administration found that the company had not made the batch. A review summarized 30 cases reported in journals, all of people who developed nephrotic syndrome (large protein loss in the urine) after using mercury-containing whitening creams. All 30 were Asian women, mostly aged 18 to 52. Time from starting use to onset was 1 to 60 months, and half developed it within 5 months. Kidney biopsies mostly showed minimal change disease (67%) or membranous nephropathy (23%), two types of glomerular disease. Few people improved on their own within one and a half months of stopping. In the few recorded cases, urine protein turned negative in 1 to 9 months with chelation therapy (median 2 months); the 3 patients who did not use chelation took more than 12 months. A Beijing hospital admitted 288 patients with mercury poisoning from 2014 to 2019, and 84 (29.27%) were linked to cosmetics. Cosmetics are the main source of mercury poisoning in women. Among 18 nephrotic syndrome patients followed up, 15 had urine protein and albumin return to normal after an average of 22.67 months. Mercury in these 18 people did not all come from cosmetics.
