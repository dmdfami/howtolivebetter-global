# 2. Don't Die Slowly

Only two kinds of chronic risk are covered in this section: ones that strongly affect overall mortality, and ones with strong evidence. Some items are marked "randomized trial". That means people were randomly split into two groups and compared. This is the most trustworthy kind of result. All other figures come from cohort studies, which only follow and record people without assigning groups. The risk ratios these studies report, such as HR and RR, are affected by two kinds of distortion. The people are different to begin with, and some people changed their habits only after they became ill. So these figures only show the direction and a rough size of the effect. They do not mean that doing the same thing will cut your risk by this much.

Entries below are grouped by theme. Numbers in brackets are item numbers.

**Smoking, alcohol and betel nut**: Quit smoking as early as possible (item 1). Do not smoke at home or in the car (item 2). Get medication before you quit (item 3). Set a quit date (item 4). Go to a smoking cessation clinic or call 12320 (item 5). If you cannot quit, consider e-cigarettes as a last resort (item 6). Do not chew betel nut (item 8). Drink less or not at all (item 19). If you shake when you stop drinking, do not quit alone (item 20). Count how many drinks you have in a week (item 21).

**Foods and drinks to cut back on**: Do not drink sugary drinks (item 7). Eat less processed meat (item 18). Eat less ultra-processed food (item 28). Do not drink very hot tea or soup (item 30). Throw out a whole package once it has gone moldy (item 46). Do not eat raw fish made from freshwater fish or shrimp (item 47).

**Foods to swap or eat more of**: Switch to low-sodium salt (item 9). Eat a small handful of nuts every day (item 22). Swap red meat for fish and poultry (item 23). Swap refined white rice and white flour for whole grains (item 24). Drink tea more than three times a week (item 25). Drink three to four cups of coffee a day (item 26). Eat at least five servings of fruit and vegetables (item 27). Eat chili more than four times a week (item 33). Drink one to two servings of milk a day (item 34). You do not need to avoid eggs (item 35).

**Exercise and weight**: Walk 7,000 to 8,000 steps a day (item 11). Play racket sports (item 14). Build up short bursts of physical activity through the day (item 15). Do strength training every week (item 16). Do not sit for long stretches (item 17). Keep your BMI between 20 and 25 (item 32). Count walking your dog toward your daily walking total (item 44). Find out whether you have prediabetes (item 45).

**Sleep and night shifts**: Sleep about seven hours a night (item 13). Keep naps under half an hour (item 37). Catch up on sleep the next day if you stayed up late (item 38). If you work night shifts and can switch to day work, do it as early as you can (item 39).

**Cooking oil and kitchen smoke**: Do not burn coal or wood for cooking or heating (item 29). Do not buy loose, home-pressed peanut oil (item 40). Use vegetable oil instead of lard or butter (item 41). Turn on the range hood when you stir-fry (item 42).

**Teeth, medicines and other habits**: Brush properly and clean between your teeth (item 10). Take your medicines regularly as prescribed (item 12). Spend some time in daylight when you go out during the day (item 31). Soak in a bath when you can (item 36). If you have hot flashes around menopause, get evaluated for hormone therapy (item 43).

### 1. Quit smoking, the sooner the better
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=high gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, and saves money. A pack a day costs about 20 to 30 yuan (RMB), so quitting keeps that money. The hard part is the withdrawal period, which usually lasts a few weeks to a few months.
- Plain language: In a US study, people who smoke lived on average more than 10 years less than people who never smoked. Quitting before age 40 removes about 90% of the extra death risk from smoking. The earlier you quit, the more you gain. After a fracture, smoking makes it about 2.3 times more likely that the bone will not heal, so do not smoke until it has healed.
- Benefit: A US study that followed a group of people (recording only, with no groups assigned) found that people who still smoke live 10 or more years less than people who never smoked. Quitting before age 40 removes about 90% of the death risk from continuing to smoke. Quitting at ages 25 to 34 adds about 10 years of life, at 35 to 44 about 9 years, and at 45 to 54 about 6 years (the original reports life gained in years). In a Chinese study of the same type, male urban smokers in the 2010s had 1.65 times the death risk of non-smokers (RR 1.65), and rural male smokers had 1.22 times. After 10 years of quitting on purpose, the part of the risk caused by smoking nearly disappears. The same holds after a fracture. Pooling 19 studies that only recorded people, without assigning groups, smokers had about 2.3 times the odds of a fracture failing to heal (non-union) compared with non-smokers (OR 2.32, 95% CI 1.76 to 3.06, adjusted for other factors). The increase was also high for tibial shaft fractures and open fractures. In a Swedish randomized trial, 50 people who had emergency surgery for a fracture joined a 6-week quitting program, and 20% of them had at least one post-operative complication. Of the 55 who did not join, 38% did. For planned surgery, you can start quitting 4 to 8 weeks before the operation, with weekly coaching and nicotine replacement. Doing this lowers post-operative complications by about 58% (RR 0.42, 95% CI 0.27 to 0.65, 2 trials, 210 people).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Jha P 等 (2013). 21st-century hazards of smoking and benefits of cessation in the United States. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMsa1211128>；Chen Z 等 (2015). Contrasting male and female trends in tobacco-attributed mortality in China: evidence from successive nationwide prospective cohort studies. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)00340-2>；Oberg M 等 (2011). Worldwide burden of disease from exposure to second-hand smoke: a retrospective analysis of data from 192 countries. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61388-8>（二手烟那两个数字）；Scolaro JA 等 (2014). Cigarette smoking increases complications following fracture: a systematic review. J Bone Joint Surg Am. <https://doi.org/10.2106/JBJS.M.00081>；Nåsell H 等 (2010). Effect of smoking cessation intervention on results of acute fracture surgery: a randomized controlled trial. J Bone Joint Surg Am. <https://doi.org/10.2106/JBJS.I.00627>；Thomsen T 等 (2014). Interventions for preoperative smoking cessation. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD002294.pub4>（骨折和手术那几个数字）
- Notes: Breathing other people's smoke also kills. In 2004, about 603,000 people worldwide died from second-hand smoke, about 1% of all deaths that year. If you do not smoke, still avoid other people's smoke, and especially keep children away from it. For how to quit, see the next items: item 3 (quit-smoking medicines), item 4 (set a quit date), item 5 (go to a cessation clinic), item 6 (e-cigarettes). The bone non-union figure comes from studies that only recorded people, without groups. Smokers may also differ from non-smokers in ways that cannot all be adjusted for. The post-fracture quitting trial had only 105 people. When the authors recalculated using odds ratios, the difference was no longer statistically significant (OR 2.51, 95% CI 0.96 to 6.9).

### 2. Do not smoke at home or in the car, and do not let guests smoke in your home
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Costs nothing and takes no time. The hard part is speaking up and asking family members and guests not to smoke at home.
- Plain language: In 2004, about 600,000 people worldwide died from other people's smoke, and nearly a third of them were children. People who breathe second-hand smoke have about 30% higher risk of high blood pressure, heart disease and stroke. Smoke breathed in at home does more harm than smoke breathed outside.
- Benefit: In 2004, 603,000 people worldwide died from second-hand smoke, about 1.0% of all deaths, and 28% of them were children. Pooling 57 studies, people exposed to second-hand smoke had 1.28 times the odds of high blood pressure compared with people who were not exposed (odds ratio). The odds were 1.39 times for heart disease, 1.50 times for heart attack, and 1.36 times for stroke. The risk from second-hand smoke at home is higher than the risk from exposure outside the home.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Öberg M, Jaakkola MS, Woodward A, Peruga A, Prüss-Ustün A (2011). Worldwide burden of disease from exposure to second-hand smoke: a retrospective analysis of data from 192 countries. Lancet：「603,000 deaths were attributable to second-hand smoke in 2004, which was about 1·0% of worldwide mortality. 47% of deaths from second-hand smoke occurred in women, 28% in children, and 26% in men」，「61% of DALYs were in children」. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61388-8>；(2026). The Associations Between Secondhand Smoke Exposure and Various Cardiovascular Diseases: A Meta-Analysis. Nicotine & Tobacco Research：57 项研究，「hypertension (OR: 1.28, 95% CI: 1.15 to 1.40), heart disease (OR: 1.39, 95% CI: 1.28 to 1.50), myocardial infarction (OR: 1.50, 95% CI: 1.17 to 1.84), stroke (OR: 1.36, 95% CI: 1.18 to 1.54)」「Home exposure has a higher risk of CVD than non-home exposure」. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ntr/ntaf111>
- Notes: Start with the home, because people spend a lot of time there, and several studies taken together show that second-hand smoke at home is more harmful than outside the home. 61% of the health loss from second-hand smoke falls on children. For quitting yourself, see item 1 of this section.

### 3. Do not rely on willpower alone to quit smoking; get medication first, since varenicline can more than double your chance of quitting
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Nicotine patches and nicotine gum are over-the-counter and can be bought at a pharmacy counter. A course lasts 8 to 12 weeks and costs a few hundred to over 1,000 yuan (RMB). Varenicline and bupropion are prescription drugs, and must be prescribed at a smoking cessation clinic or a respiratory medicine department. The cost is roughly offset by the cigarettes you do not buy in the same period.
- Plain language: People who take varenicline are more than twice as likely to quit as people who take a placebo. Nicotine replacement products such as patches and gum raise the chance of quitting by about 50% compared with using no medication. Using a patch and gum together adds more than 20% on top of that.
- Benefit: Pooling 41 randomized trials with 17,395 people, the quit rate in the varenicline group was 2.32 times that of the placebo group (RR 2.32, 95% CI 2.15 to 2.51; this is the confidence range, and the evidence is high-certainty). Varenicline was also higher than bupropion (RR 1.36, about 36% higher) and higher than a single nicotine replacement product (RR 1.25, about 25% higher). Nicotine replacement compared with no medication: 133 trials with 64,640 people found a success rate about 55% higher (RR 1.55, 95% CI 1.49 to 1.61). A patch plus one fast-acting form (gum or lozenges) was about 27% more effective than one form alone (RR 1.27, 95% CI 1.17 to 1.37, 16 trials, 12,169 people, high-certainty evidence). Side effects: compared with placebo, people taking varenicline had about 2.6 times the rate of nausea (RR 2.61), and about 37% more insomnia (RR 1.37). Vivid dreams were also more common. The review says nausea is the most common side effect. Most cases are mild to moderate, and it usually fades over time. The package insert for one US nicotine patch specifies a three-step reduction: 21 mg for 6 weeks, then 14 mg for 2 weeks, then 7 mg for 2 weeks, and then stop.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Livingstone-Banks J, Fanshawe TR, Thomas KH, et al. (2023). Nicotine receptor partial agonists for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 5, CD006103. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD006103.pub8>；Hartmann-Boyce J, Chepkin SC, Ye W, Bullen C, Lancaster T (2018). Nicotine replacement therapy versus control for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 5, CD000146. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD000146.pub5>；Theodoulou A, Chepkin SC, Ye W, et al. (2023). Different doses, durations and modes of delivery of nicotine replacement therapy for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 6, CD013308. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013308.pub2>；上海市卫生健康委员会 (2021). 选对药物，让戒烟轻松一点：「市场上可见的戒烟药物主要有三种，即尼古丁替代疗法药物、安非他酮、伐尼克兰」，「尼古丁替代疗法药物属于非处方药（OTC），可通过药店柜台购买；而安非他酮、伐尼克兰属于处方药，须到医院戒烟门诊或呼吸内科就诊，凭医师处方经药师调配后才能得到」. <https://wsjkw.sh.gov.cn/jtyx/20211119/df50681e01ba49f896d54f771d8176ae.html>；Haleon (2025). NICODERM CQ (nicotine) patch, extended release. DailyMed 说明书：「use according to the following 10-week schedule: STEP 1 … 21 mg patch/day … Weeks 1-6 … 14 mg … Weeks 7-8 … 7 mg … Weeks 9-10」. <https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=93b2d1b9-83c1-40b5-b6af-90c38c8d6cef>（贴片减量那一句）
- Notes: China only: In China, the quit-smoking drugs on sale are the three types above, and only nicotine replacement products are over-the-counter. The usual side effects of varenicline are nausea, vivid dreams and poor sleep. People with a psychiatric history should tell their doctor; this is the author's advice. Nicotine replacement courses usually last 8 to 12 weeks, and the patch should be reduced step by step, as the instructions or your doctor say, before stopping. Medication only eases the discomfort of withdrawal in the first few weeks. It does not deal with the urge to smoke in particular situations, so use it together with item 4 of this section (set a quit date) and item 5 (go to a cessation clinic).

### 4. Set a quit date and stop on that day, rather than cutting down slowly first
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=high gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. You just pick a day on the calendar and tell family and colleagues ahead of time.
- Plain language: Many people plan to smoke a little less each day and slowly reach zero. But by the sixth month, 22% of people who stopped all at once on a set day had not smoked again, compared with only 15.5% of those who cut down slowly. So pick a date and stop on that day.
- Benefit: In a UK trial, 697 smokers were randomly divided into two groups. One group stopped completely on the quit day. The other cut their smoking by 75% in the two weeks before the quit day. Both groups received nurse behavioral support and used nicotine replacement around the quit day. At week 4, 49.0% of the stop-all-at-once group (95% CI 43.8 to 54.2; this is the confidence range) had not relapsed, compared with 39.2% (34.0 to 44.4) of the gradual group, relative risk 0.80 (0.66 to 0.93). At 6 months, 22.0% (18.0 to 26.6) of the stop-all-at-once group and 15.5% (12.0 to 19.7) of the gradual group had not relapsed, relative risk 0.71 (0.46 to 0.91). People who already preferred cutting down gradually also had a lower success rate at week 4 than people who preferred stopping at once (38.3% vs 52.2%).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Lindson-Hawley N, Banting M, West R, Michie S, Shinkins B, Aveyard P (2016). Gradual Versus Abrupt Smoking Cessation: A Randomized, Controlled Noninferiority Trial. Annals of Internal Medicine, 164(9), 585–592. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M14-2805>；Theodoulou A, Chepkin SC, Ye W, et al. (2023). Different doses, durations and modes of delivery of nicotine replacement therapy for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 6, CD013308（提前用尼古丁替代品那一段）. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013308.pub2>
- Notes: Disputed. Cochrane once published a review that compared these two approaches directly and concluded they were about the same (RR 0.94, 95% CI 0.79 to 1.13, 10 trials, 3,760 people). That review was withdrawn in 2019 and is no longer updated. So the best current evidence is the randomized trial above, whose direction supports stopping all at once. In that trial, both groups started using nicotine replacement before the quit day, and that practice has its own evidence: starting before the quit day gives a success rate about 25% higher than starting on the quit day itself (RR 1.25, 95% CI 1.08 to 1.44, 9 trials, 4,395 people, moderate-certainty evidence). So a reasonable plan is to set the date first, put on the patch two weeks ahead, and stop on the day.

### 5. Go to a smoking cessation clinic, or call 12320 to ask whether there are quitting services near you
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: A clinic visit costs a few to a few dozen yuan (RMB). Behavioral support usually takes 4 to 8 sessions of half an hour to an hour each, adding up to about 5 hours or less. Calling a hotline is free.
- Plain language: Medication and having someone check in on you regularly are two different things, and together they work better. People who take medication while a professional follows up with them quit at close to twice the rate of people who get only a brief piece of advice. Phone support counts too. Quitlines that call back several times on their own initiative raise success by about 25% to nearly 40%.
- Benefit: Pooling 52 trials with 19,488 people, the quit rate for medication plus behavioral support was 1.83 times that of usual care or brief advice (RR 1.83, 95% CI 1.68 to 1.98; this is the confidence range, and the evidence is high-quality). In the 43 trials recruited in healthcare settings, the figure was 1.97 times (1.79 to 2.18). Telephone counseling on its own: among people who had already called a quit hotline themselves, those offered several proactive call-backs were about 38% more likely to quit than those given only materials or a single conversation (RR 1.38, 95% CI 1.19 to 1.61, 14 trials, 32,484 people). Among people who did not call a hotline themselves but were offered phone counseling by researchers, the rate was about 25% higher (RR 1.25, 95% CI 1.15 to 1.35, 65 trials, 41,233 people). Both are moderate-certainty evidence.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Stead LF, Koilpillai P, Fanshawe TR, Lancaster T (2016). Combined pharmacotherapy and behavioural interventions for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 3, CD008286. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD008286.pub3>；Matkin W, Ordóñez-Mena JM, Hartmann-Boyce J (2019). Telephone counselling for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 5, CD002850. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD002850.pub4>；中国疾病预防控制中心 (2021). 「中国戒烟平台」微信小程序正式上线：「在健康中国行动控烟行动工作组指导下中国疾病预防控制中心和世界卫生组织驻华代表处联合制作了中国权威戒烟资源库『中国戒烟平台』微信小程序。小程序内容主要包括：戒烟热线、戒烟门诊详细信息，以及线上戒烟服务资源等」；同一站点页脚标注「健康咨询电话：12320」. <https://www.chinacdc.cn/jkyj/yckz/gzdt/202203/t20220310_296389.html>
- Notes: China only: To find help in China, search WeChat (China's main messaging app) for the China Quit Smoking Platform mini-program. It lists quitlines and cessation clinics by region. You can also call 12320 (China's national public health hotline) to ask whether there is a cessation clinic nearby. Most trials above were run outside China, and the intensity of services at local clinics varies widely, so treat the numbers as directional only. For medication, see item 3 of this section (quit-smoking medicines).

### 6. If you still cannot quit, consider e-cigarettes; people who do not smoke should never touch them
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=some gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: A device plus cartridges costs tens to hundreds of yuan (RMB) per month. In China, only tobacco-flavored products may be bought, and only from stores that hold a tobacco retail license.
- Plain language: Switching from cigarettes to nicotine-containing e-cigarettes makes people about 61% more likely to quit than nicotine replacement. This finding is reliable. But safety data for long-term use are not yet sufficient, so do not treat e-cigarettes as harmless. These trials only studied people who wanted to quit, so people who do not smoke should not use them.
- Benefit: A continuously updated review pooled 80 randomized trials with 29,861 people. Nicotine-containing e-cigarettes, compared with nicotine replacement therapy, produced about 61% higher quit rates (RR 1.61, 95% CI 1.23 to 2.12; this is the confidence range, and the evidence is high-certainty, 11 trials, 4,114 people). In absolute terms, that is about 4 more people per 100 quitting (1 to 7 more). The proportion with serious adverse events was similar in the two groups (risk difference 0.01, 95% CI −0.01 to 0.02; moderate-certainty evidence). Compared with behavioral support only, or with no support, the rate was about 75% higher (RR 1.75, 95% CI 1.39 to 2.20; low-certainty evidence).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Lindson N, Livingstone-Banks J, Butler AR, et al. (2026). Electronic cigarettes for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 8, CD010216. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD010216.pub11>；国家烟草专卖局 (2022). 电子烟管理办法（国家烟草专卖局公告 2022 年第 1 号，2022 年 5 月 1 日施行）第十八条：从事电子烟零售业务，要向烟草专卖行政主管部门申领烟草专卖零售许可证。第二十二条：「禁止向未成年人出售电子烟产品。」第二十三条第二款：「任何个人、法人或者其他组织不得通过本办法规定的电子烟交易管理平台以外的信息网络销售电子烟产品、雾化物和电子烟用烟碱等。」第二十六条：「禁止销售除烟草口味外的调味电子烟和可自行添加雾化物的电子烟。」. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2022/content_5697988.htm>；World Health Organization (2023-12-14). Urgent action needed to protect children and prevent the uptake of e-cigarettes：「E-cigarettes as consumer products are not shown to be effective for quitting tobacco use at the population level」「it is not recommended that governments permit sale of e-cigarettes as consumer products in pursuit of a cessation objective」「Whilst long-term health effects are not fully understood」. <https://www.who.int/news/item/14-12-2023-urgent-action-needed-to-protect-children-and-prevent-the-uptake-of-e-cigarettes>；Department of Health and Social Care (2023-04-11). Smokers urged to swap cigarettes for vapes in world first scheme：「almost 1 in 5 of all smokers in England will be provided with a vape starter kit alongside behavioural support」. <https://www.gov.uk/government/news/smokers-urged-to-swap-cigarettes-for-vapes-in-world-first-scheme>；最高人民检察院 (2021). 江苏苏州吴江区：办案检察官揭秘披着潮流外衣的新型毒品：「这种“上头”电子烟内含有被国家整类列管的合成大麻素成分」. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202110/t20211011_531831.shtml>
- Notes: China only: Disputed. In 2023 the WHO said that e-cigarettes sold as consumer products have not been shown to help people quit at the population level. It does not recommend that governments allow e-cigarettes to be sold as consumer products for cessation purposes, and says they should be regulated as medicines. It also says long-term health effects are not fully understood, and it worries that children and young people may start using them. From 2023, England took the opposite approach, giving about one million smokers e-cigarette starter kits together with behavioral support. China does not ban e-cigarettes outright, but regulates them strictly under the E-cigarette Management Measures. Only tobacco flavors may be sold; fruit flavors and products that let users add their own e-liquid may not. Sales are only allowed in physical stores that hold a tobacco retail license. Apart from the national e-cigarette trading platform, sales on other websites, online shops or social media are illegal, and sales to minors are prohibited. So fruit-flavored e-cigarettes that can still be bought come through illegal channels and fall outside regulation. Intoxicating e-cigarettes laced with synthetic cannabinoids have come out of exactly this kind of channel; see section 22, item 4 (do not accept cartridges from others). The review tested only regulated, nicotine-containing products, so e-liquid of unknown origin is not covered. In terms of order, try item 3 of this section (quit-smoking medicines) first. That option has stronger evidence and is cheaper.

### 7. Do not drink sugary drinks; switching to sugar-free drinks does not solve the problem
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Costs nothing and takes no time. Switching to plain water or unsweetened tea even saves money. The hard part is breaking the habit of drinking sweet drinks, including sugar-free ones.
- Plain language: In a US study, people who drank more than two servings of sweet drinks a day had about 20% higher chance of dying during the study than people who drank almost none. Those drinking one to two servings a day had more than 10% higher risk. Switching to sugar-free drinks did not show a benefit. People drinking more than two cups of sugar-free drinks a day had about a quarter higher risk.
- Benefit: Two large US studies that followed groups of people tracked about 118,000 people and 36,000 deaths. They only recorded people and did not assign groups. People drinking more than 2 servings a day had about 21% higher death risk than people drinking less than 1 serving a month (HR 1.21). Those drinking 1 to 2 servings a day were about 14% higher (HR 1.14). The European EPIC cohort study, which only recorded people without assigning groups, included 450,000 people in 10 countries. Comparing people drinking more than 2 cups a day with people drinking less than 1 cup a month: sugar-sweetened soft drinks were associated with about 8% higher risk (HR 1.08, 95% CI 1.01 to 1.16). Artificially sweetened (sugar-free) drinks were about 26% higher (HR 1.26, 1.16 to 1.35). All soft drinks combined were about 17% higher (HR 1.17, 1.11 to 1.22).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Malik VS 等 (2019). Long-Term Consumption of Sugar-Sweetened and Artificially Sweetened Beverages and Risk of Mortality in US Adults. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.118.037401>；Mullee A, Romaguera D, Pearson-Stuttard J, et al. (2019). Association Between Soft Drink Consumption and Mortality in 10 European Countries. JAMA Internal Medicine, 179(11), 1479-1490. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.2478>
- Notes: The dispute is about sugar-free drinks. The link for sugar-free drinks is stronger than for sugary ones, and this most likely reflects reverse causation: people who were already overweight or had diabetes switched to the sugar-free versions. So do not conclude that sweeteners are more harmful. But it does show that the claim "switching to sugar-free is safe" has no supporting evidence. Plain water or unsweetened tea is the safest choice. Also, these studies only tracked people and did not use a trial design. People who drink many sweet drinks often have poorer lifestyles overall, so the figures may be overstated.

### 8. Do not chew betel nut
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Costs nothing and takes no time, and saves the money you would spend on betel nut. The hard part is breaking the habit of chewing it.
- Plain language: People who chew betel nut have about 20% higher chance of dying during the study period than people who do not. Their risk of diabetes and metabolic syndrome is about 50% higher. Mouth and esophageal cancer risks must be counted separately.
- Benefit: Pooling 17 Asian studies with 388,000 people, betel nut chewers had about 21% higher death risk than non-chewers (relative risk 1.21, P=0.02). The death outcome was based on 179,600 of these people. Risk of diabetes was about 47% higher (1.47). Risk of metabolic syndrome was about 51% higher (1.51). Betel nut itself is a known risk factor for mouth and esophageal cancer. Mouth cancer: pooling 50 studies, in the Indian subcontinent, chewing betel quid without tobacco was associated with about 2.56 times the risk of oral and oropharyngeal cancer compared with not chewing (95% CI 2.00 to 3.28). Quid with tobacco was about 7.74 times (5.38 to 11.13). In Taiwan, betel quid without tobacco was about 10.98 times (4.86 to 24.84). In India and Taiwan, the more often and the longer people chewed, the higher the risk. Esophageal cancer: pooling 12 Asian studies, betel nut chewers had about 3.05 times the risk of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma compared with non-chewers (2.41 to 3.87). Chewing betel nut and smoking together carried about 6.79 times the risk (4.71 to 9.79). After quitting: pooling 13 studies, people who quit betel quid with tobacco had about 34% lower head and neck cancer risk than people who still chew (RR 0.66, 0.54 to 0.81). Those who quit betel quid without tobacco had about 27% lower risk (RR 0.73, 0.56 to 0.95). Those who had quit for more than 20 years had about 63% lower risk (RR 0.37, 0.22 to 0.61).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Yamada T, Hara K, Kadowaki T (2013). Chewing betel quid and the risk of metabolic disease, cardiovascular disease, and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis. PLoS One, 8(8), e70679. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0070679>；Guha N, Warnakulasuriya S, Vlaanderen J, Straif K (2014). Betel quid chewing and the risk of oral and oropharyngeal cancers: a meta-analysis with implications for cancer control. International Journal of Cancer, 135(6), 1433-1443. <https://doi.org/10.1002/ijc.28643>；Akhtar S (2013). Areca nut chewing and esophageal squamous-cell carcinoma risk in Asians: a meta-analysis of case-control studies. Cancer Causes & Control, 24(2), 257-265. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s10552-012-0113-9>；Shah K, Boffetta P, Seyyedsalehi MS (2025). Reversal of head and neck cancer risk after the cessation of use of smokeless tobacco and betel quid use: meta-analysis. Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention, 34(5), 619-626. <https://doi.org/10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-24-1502>
- Notes: The longer someone has chewed and the more they chew each day, the higher the mouth cancer risk. After quitting, the risk falls over time. In Indian studies, betel quid with tobacco was more dangerous than betel quid without it. In Taiwan, most people chew betel quid without tobacco, and the risk there is also high.

### 9. Replace household table salt with low-sodium salt (potassium salt)
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: A bag costs a few yuan more than regular salt. Swap it when you buy, with no extra time needed. The taste barely changes.
- Plain language: In rural China, people who had had a stroke, or who were over 60 with high blood pressure, switched to low-sodium salt at home. Over nearly five years of follow-up, their chance of death was about 12% lower, and their chance of stroke about 14% lower.
- Benefit: A randomized trial in rural China included 20,995 people who had had a stroke, or who were over 60 with high blood pressure, and followed them for 4.74 years. Results: the low-sodium salt group had about 12% lower death risk than the regular salt group (RR 0.88). Stroke was about 14% lower (RR 0.86). Major cardiovascular events were about 13% lower (RR 0.87). No statistically significant difference in hyperkalemia (high blood potassium) was seen between the two groups.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Neal B 等 (2021). Effect of Salt Substitution on Cardiovascular Events and Death. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2105675>；O'Donnell M 等 (2014). Urinary sodium and potassium excretion, mortality, and cardiovascular events. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1311889>（争议方 PURE）
- Notes: Disputed. The PURE study is a long-term cohort study that only recorded people, without assigning groups. It reports that people excreting less than 3 g of sodium a day had about 27% higher risk of death plus cardiovascular events. People excreting 7 g or more of sodium a day had about 15% higher risk. By its results, eating too little and eating too much are both harmful, and a middle level is best. But in the rural Chinese randomized trial cited in this item, low-sodium salt only replaced part of the salt and did not push sodium intake that low. Also note that the trial participants were older people at high risk. Healthy younger people would gain much less from switching salt. People with reduced kidney function, or those taking potassium-sparing medicines, should ask a doctor before switching salt.

### 10. Brush properly, clean between your teeth every day, and see a dentist if you have lost teeth
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=some gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: Dental floss or interdental brushes cost tens of yuan a year, and a professional cleaning costs a hundred or two per visit. It adds two to three minutes a day. The hard part is making between-teeth cleaning a daily habit. If a manual toothbrush does not clean well enough, you can switch to an electric one. Electric brushes cost a few hundred yuan each, and the brush heads need regular replacement.
- Plain language: In a Japanese study, people who used floss or interdental brushes had about 10% lower chance of dying during the study. Older people who had lost all their teeth had about 90% higher death risk. Those with fewer than 20 teeth had roughly double the risk. Electric toothbrushes remove 10 to 20% more plaque than manual brushes and cause slightly less gum inflammation, but it is unclear how much difference this makes to health.
- Benefit: A Japanese study of 9,676 people followed them for 6 years. People who used interdental cleaning tools had about 11% lower death risk (hazard ratio 0.89). People who used tongue-cleaning tools had about 23% lower risk (0.77). Pooling several studies of community-dwelling older adults: people with no teeth at all had about 1.87 times the odds of death (odds ratio, 95% CI 1.35 to 2.59; this is the confidence range). Those with fewer than 20 teeth had about 2.04 times the odds (1.67 to 2.49). Electric versus manual: pooling 51 randomized trials, after 1 to 3 months electric brushes left about 11% less plaque, and after more than 3 months about 21% less. Gingivitis was reduced by about 6% and 11% respectively. The review authors say it is still unclear how much these differences matter for health.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Wang K, Matsuyama Y, Kiuchi S, et al. (2026). Routine oral health practices and all-cause mortality. Journal of Dentistry. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdent.2026.106789>；Ko MJ, Seo S, So JS, et al. (2026). Deteriorated oral health and function as risk factors for physical disability and mortality in community-dwelling older adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis. European Geriatric Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s41999-025-01319-4>；Yaacob M, Worthington HV, Deacon SA, et al. (2014). Powered versus manual toothbrushing for oral health. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD002281.pub3>；Deacon SA, Glenny AM, Deery C, et al. (2010). Different powered toothbrushes for plaque control and gingival health. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004971.pub2>
- Notes: China only: Disputed. These are cohort studies that only tracked people. People with poor teeth often also have poorer overall health and financial circumstances. Whether teeth problems harm the body, or poor health harms the teeth, is unclear. But this step costs very little, and gum disease and missing teeth directly affect eating. Among electric toothbrushes, the round rotating-head type has the most trials (27), and compared with manual brushes it clearly reduced plaque and gingivitis in both the short and long term. No study has tested whether filling missing teeth reduces deaths. The advice to fill gaps is based on the fact that missing teeth affect eating; this is the author's judgment. Independent reviews have not concluded which type of electric toothbrush is best, because there are too few trials. For how much an implant costs and which dental items China's medical insurance covers, see section 24, item 12 (ask for an all-inclusive price before getting implants).

### 11. Walk 7,000 to 8,000 steps a day, or do 150 to 300 minutes of brisk walking a week
<!-- tags: money=0 time=high will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. By step count, that means about 60 to 90 minutes of walking a day, which can be built into commuting and shopping. By exercise time, it is 20 to 45 minutes a day. The hard part is sticking with it long term.
- Plain language: People who walk about 7,800 steps a day have about 45% lower chance of dying during the study than people who walk only 3,500 steps. The same holds for exercise time: brisk walking for 150 to 300 minutes a week cuts the risk by about 30% compared with not exercising. The benefit levels off after a certain amount. People over 60 need only 6,000 to 8,000 steps. Meeting either measure is enough.
- Benefit: Pooling 15 cohort studies that followed groups of people (recorded only, no groups assigned): 47,471 people and 3,013 deaths. Grouped into four tiers by daily steps, the lowest tier had a median of 3,553 steps a day. The other three tiers had medians of 5,801, 7,842 and 10,901 steps a day. Death risk in those three tiers was about 40%, 45% and 53% lower than in the lowest tier (HR 0.60, 0.55, 0.47). In people over 60, the benefit of walking more levels off after 6,000 to 8,000 steps. In people under 60, it levels off after 8,000 to 10,000 steps. Another analysis pooling several studies found benefits starting at about 3,867 steps a day. Each additional 1,000 steps a day after that cut death risk by about 15%. By exercise time: pooling several cohort studies (recorded only, no groups assigned), people whose activity was 1 to 2 times the lower bound of the guidelines had about 31% lower death risk than people who did not exercise (HR 0.69). That amount is 7.5 to 15 MET-hours per week, roughly 150 to 300 minutes of brisk walking per week. People below the lower bound were about 20% lower (HR 0.80). People at 3 to 5 times the lower bound were about 39% lower (HR 0.61), the peak of the reduction. Adding more did not lower risk further, but it did no harm (at 10 times or more, HR 0.69). Another pooled analysis used accelerometers, which are devices worn on the body that measure activity in practice: the quarter of people with the most moderate-to-vigorous activity had about 48% lower death risk than the least active quarter (HR 0.52). This pooled analysis covered 8 studies with 36,383 people, average age 62.6, with a median follow-up of only 5.8 years. The authors removed people who died in the first two years and recalculated; the results barely changed. They also said that bias from "people who are ill move less" may still remain.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Paluch AE 等 (2022). Daily steps and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis of 15 international cohorts. Lancet Public Health. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(21)00302-9>；Banach M 等 (2023). The association between daily step count and all-cause and cardiovascular mortality: a meta-analysis. European Journal of Preventive Cardiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/eurjpc/zwad229>；Arem H 等 (2015). Leisure time physical activity and mortality: a detailed pooled analysis of the dose-response relationship. JAMA Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2015.0533>；Ekelund U 等 (2019). Dose-response associations between accelerometry measured physical activity and sedentary time and all cause mortality: systematic review and harmonised meta-analysis. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.l4570>
- Notes: Step counts and exercise time measure the same thing in different ways, so meeting either one is enough. The most worthwhile stretch is going from 4,000 to 7,000 steps; you do not need to reach 10,000. Note that these studies only recorded people. Among those who walked least and moved least, some were already ill and unable to walk. They moved less because they were declining, not because moving less caused early death. So the gaps calculated are often overstated. Among the studies that measured activity with instruments, follow-up was short and participants were older. So "risk cut to 0.52 times" may be overstated; this is the book's inference. But the clear trend that more movement is better holds.

### 12. Check your blood lipids regularly once you are an adult: every 2 to 5 years before 40, then every year from 40. If you have high blood pressure or high blood lipids, take your medicine as prescribed and do not stop on your own.
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Generic blood pressure and statin drugs bought through China's national volume-based procurement cost a few to several dozen yuan a month. Taking them once a day takes little time. The hard part is staying on them and not stopping just because you feel fine. A lipid test requires a blood draw. People aged 65 and over get one free health check a year, which includes a lipid test; see section 6, item 18 (general health check package).
- Plain language: Each 10 mmHg drop in the top blood pressure number cuts the chance of death by about 13%, and the chance of stroke by about 27%. Statins lower LDL cholesterol by 1 mmol/L and cut the chance of death by about 10%. People who take their medicine on schedule have a 30% to 45% lower chance of death than people who often miss doses. Check cholesterol every 2 to 5 years before 40, and every year from 40.
- Benefit: Pooling randomized trials: each 10 mmHg drop in systolic blood pressure (the top number) lowers the risk of death by about 13% (RR 0.87). Major cardiovascular events fall by about 20% (RR 0.80), stroke by about 27% (RR 0.73), and heart failure by about 28% (RR 0.72). Statins lower LDL cholesterol. Each 1.0 mmol/L drop in LDL lowers the risk of death by about 10% (RR 0.90), and the same drop cuts major vascular events by about 22% (RR 0.78). Separately, pooling tracking studies (recording only, no randomization): people who take at least 80% of their prescribed doses have a lower risk of death than people who often miss doses. For statins alone the risk is about 45% lower (RR 0.55); for blood pressure drugs alone, about 29% lower (RR 0.71). On how often to check, the Healthy China Action plan (a national public-health program) recommends: people under 40 with normal lipids check every 2 to 5 years; people 40 and over check at least once a year; people at high risk of heart or brain vascular disease check every 6 months. The 2023 Chinese Guidelines for Lipid Management use the same wording before and after 40, and for high-risk people recommend testing as individually needed.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Ettehad D 等 (2016). Blood pressure lowering for prevention of cardiovascular disease and death: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)01225-8>；Cholesterol Treatment Trialists' (CTT) Collaboration (2010). Efficacy and safety of more intensive lowering of LDL cholesterol: a meta-analysis of data from 170 000 participants in 26 randomised trials. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61350-5>；Chowdhury R 等 (2013). Adherence to cardiovascular therapy: a meta-analysis of prevalence and clinical consequences. European Heart Journal. <https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/eht295>；健康中国行动推进委员会 (2019). 健康中国行动（2019—2030年），（十一）心脑血管疾病防治行动（湖南省卫生健康委员会转载）. <https://wjw.hunan.gov.cn/wjw/ztzl/knowledge/tzggzcfg/202003/t20200303_11359594.html>；Joint Committee on the Chinese Guidelines for Lipid Management (2023). 中国血脂管理指南（2023 年）. 中华心血管病杂志, 51(3), 221-255. <https://doi.org/10.3760/cma.j.cn112148-20230119-00038>；Li JJ 等 (2023). 2023 Chinese guideline for lipid management. Frontiers in Pharmacology, 14, 1190934（上一份指南的英文版，中文原文未取得，相关内容按英文版核对）. <https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2023.1190934>
- Notes: China only: This is for people a doctor has told to take medicine; healthy people do not need it. The check interval is official advice in China, and no trial has compared checking every few years with other intervals. If a relative has familial hypercholesterolemia (an inherited condition with very high cholesterol), all first- and second-degree relatives should have their lipids checked. Children should be checked if a parent's total cholesterol is 6.2 mmol/L or higher, or if a male first- or second-degree relative had a heart attack, stroke or sudden death before 55, or a female one before 65. The figures for the "kept taking medicine" group come from tracking records, and people who stick to medicine tend to be more disciplined overall, so the benefit is probably overstated. The figures from the two randomized trials are more reliable. This entry does not cover blood sugar separately: the evidence that tighter blood sugar control lowers overall death rates is not as consistent as for blood pressure and lipids. People with high blood pressure or diabetes should have their kidneys checked every year; see section 16, item 13 (urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio).

### 13. Sleep about 7 hours a night, on a fixed schedule
<!-- tags: money=0 time=high will=some gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. You need to set aside time to sleep, and most people do this by swapping phone time for sleep. The hard part is keeping fixed times for going to bed and getting up.
- Plain language: People who regularly sleep less than 7 hours have about a 10% higher chance of dying during the study period. The more irregular the schedule, the higher the risk. Compared with the most irregular group, the others have 20% to nearly 50% lower risk. Whether your sleep schedule is regular predicts death risk better than how long you sleep.
- Benefit: Pooling 16 tracking studies (recording only, no randomization), covering 1.38 million people and 113,000 deaths: people who sleep short have a 12% higher risk of death (RR 1.12), and people who sleep long have a 30% higher risk (RR 1.30). Another analysis grouped people by sleep length and found the lowest risk at 7 hours. For each hour under 7, risk rises about 6% (RR 1.06); for each hour over 7, about 13% (RR 1.13). UK Biobank, a large British health study, has wrist-watch records for about 61,000 people (recording only, no grouping). People were sorted into five levels of sleep regularity. The four more regular groups had 20%–48% lower risk of death than the least regular group. Regularity predicts death better than how long you sleep. Another UK Biobank study measured sleep timing with wrist watches in 51,562 people; 3,853 of them developed cardiovascular disease during follow-up. Social jet lag is the difference in sleep midpoint between workdays and weekends. People with 2 hours or more of social jet lag had about 30% higher cardiovascular risk (HR 1.30, 95% CI 1.11–1.54). The link also held among people with normal sleep duration.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Cappuccio FP 等 (2010). Sleep duration and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. Sleep. <https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/33.5.585>；Yin J 等 (2017). Relationship of Sleep Duration With All-Cause Mortality and Cardiovascular Events: A Systematic Review and Dose-Response Meta-Analysis of Prospective Cohort Studies. JAHA. <https://doi.org/10.1161/JAHA.117.005947>；Windred DP 等 (2024). Sleep regularity is a stronger predictor of mortality risk than sleep duration: A prospective cohort study. Sleep. <https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsad253>；Kumar N, Krishnamurthy S (2026). Social jet lag is associated with incident cardiovascular disease independent of sleep duration and cardiac genetic risk. Journal of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.70133>
- Notes: Fix the two problems: sleeping too little and an irregular schedule. You do not need to deliberately cut your sleep. Long sleepers are at higher risk mostly because cause and effect run the other way: depression, chronic illness and sleep apnea make people sleep more. Also, two tracking studies support a regular schedule, both recording only, so on its own this counts as B-level evidence. For catching up after staying up late, see item 38 of this section (catch up on sleep the next evening). For how to treat long-term insomnia, see section 3, item 26 (cognitive behavioral therapy first for chronic insomnia).

### 14. Play racket sports such as tennis, badminton or table tennis regularly
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Court fees run several dozen yuan per session. About 2 hours a week. The hard part is gathering people and a court, and going every week.
- Plain language: People who regularly play tennis, badminton or table tennis have about half the chance of dying during the study period compared with people who do not, and about 56% lower cardiovascular death. People who swim have about 28% lower risk. Running and football showed no clear difference in this data.
- Benefit: A British tracking study of 80,306 adults compared people who did each sport with people who did not. People who played racket sports had a 47% lower risk of death (risk ratio 0.53, 95% CI 0.40–0.69, a plausible range). Cardiovascular death was 56% lower (0.44, 0.24–0.83). Racket sports means tennis, badminton and table tennis. For swimming the two figures were 0.72 and 0.59; for aerobics, 0.73 and 0.64; for cycling, 0.85. No statistically clear difference was seen for running or football.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Oja P, Kelly P, Pedisic Z, et al. (2017). Associations of specific types of sports and exercise with all-cause and cardiovascular-disease mortality: a cohort study of 80 306 British adults. British Journal of Sports Medicine, 51(10), 812-817. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-096822>
- Notes: Disputed. These studies only track people over time. People who play sports are already healthier and more social. The result showing no difference for running also suggests that who chooses which sport may matter more than the sport itself. Do not conclude from this that running is useless; this section has another entry based on total amount of exercise.

### 15. Build up short bursts of brisk activity, such as climbing stairs and walking fast to get somewhere, to four or five minutes a day
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, and you do not need to find extra time to exercise. The hard part is remembering to take the stairs and walk faster instead of taking the easy option.
- Plain language: People who get no exercise at all have about a 40% lower chance of dying during the study period if they have three bouts of vigorous activity a day, each lasting one to two minutes. Their cardiovascular death risk is nearly half as high. Vigorous activity here means things like climbing stairs and brisk walking.
- Benefit: A UK Biobank study of 25,200 people who did no structured exercise. Average follow-up was 6.9 years, with 852 deaths. Some people had three bouts of vigorous activity a day, each lasting 1–2 minutes. Compared with people who had none, their risk of death and of cancer death was 38%–40% lower, and their cardiovascular death risk was 48%–49% lower. People whose daily total of this activity came to 4.4 minutes had 26%–30% lower risk of death and of cancer death, and 32%–34% lower cardiovascular death risk.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Stamatakis E, Ahmadi MN, Gill JMR, et al. (2022). Association of wearable device-measured vigorous intermittent lifestyle physical activity with mortality. Nature Medicine, 28, 2521-2529. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-022-02100-x>
- Notes: Disputed. This study measured activity with wearable devices, which is more reliable than asking people to recall it on a questionnaire. But it is still tracking only, with no randomized trial, and it followed people for only 6.9 years. The people studied did no exercise at all, so this entry does not apply to people who already exercise regularly.

### 16. Do 30–60 minutes of strength training a week
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: Bodyweight squats and push-ups cost nothing. Train 1–2 times a week, 20–30 minutes each time. The hard part is fitting it into your weekly schedule.
- Plain language: People who do 30–60 minutes of strength training a week have a 10% to 20% lower chance of dying during the study period than people who do not. More is not better. Strength and aerobic exercise together work best.
- Benefit: Pooling several tracking studies (recording only, no grouping): people who do strength training have a 10%–17% lower risk of death than people who do not. More is not better. The biggest reduction, about 10%–20%, comes at around 30–60 minutes a week, and more training adds no further benefit. People who do both strength and aerobic exercise have a lower risk of death than people who do neither.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Momma H 等 (2022). Muscle-strengthening activities are associated with lower risk and mortality in major non-communicable diseases: a systematic review and meta-analysis of cohort studies. British Journal of Sports Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2021-105061>
- Notes: These studies only track people over time, and participants reported their own exercise amounts. The evidence that too much strength training makes things worse is weak, so there is no need to limit your training for that reason. Strength training also helps older adults prevent falls and keep muscle; those benefits are covered in section 1. For people with knee pain from wear and tear, strengthening the thigh muscles works better than arthroscopic knee cleanup; see section 6, item 33 (arthroscopic cleanup).

### 17. Do not sit for long stretches; stand up and move every so often
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, and standing up for a few moves takes little time. The hard part is losing track of time once you sit down, so set a reminder.
- Plain language: People who sit the most have 2.6 times the risk of dying during the study period compared with people who sit the least. Sitting in one long stretch carries its own additional risk. But about 60–75 minutes a day of moderate activity largely cancels out the extra risk from sitting.
- Benefit: A US tracking study (recording only, no randomization) measured sitting with accelerometers. People were split into four groups by total daily sitting time. The group that sat the most had 2.63 times the risk of death of the group that sat the least (hazard ratio HR 2.63). Split into four groups by the length of their longest single sitting bout, the group with the longest bouts had 1.96 times the risk of the group with the shortest (HR 1.96). So total sitting time and the length of a single sitting bout are each linked to risk on their own. Another analysis pooled over a million people (recording only): people who sit 8 or more hours a day and rarely exercise have 1.59 times the risk of death of another group (HR 1.59). That other group sits less than 4 hours a day and is the most active. About 60–75 minutes of moderate activity a day can offset the extra risk from sitting. Even the most active group, sitting 8 or more hours a day, had an HR of only 1.04, with no statistically clear difference.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Diaz KM 等 (2017). Patterns of Sedentary Behavior and Mortality in U.S. Middle-Aged and Older Adults: A National Cohort Study. Annals of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M17-0212>；Ekelund U 等 (2016). Does physical activity attenuate, or even eliminate, the detrimental association of sitting time with mortality? A harmonised meta-analysis of data from more than 1 million men and women. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)30370-1>
- Notes: The reliable conclusion is that how much you move matters more than how long you sit. When your activity level is high enough, the risk from sitting largely disappears. Keep in mind that these studies only track people over time. Many of the people who sat the most already had illnesses, so the 2.63 times figure partly reflects people who got sick first and then sat more. Also, in the same pooled analysis, watching TV for more than 3 hours a day carried risk at every activity level; the most active group only pushed that threshold up to 5 hours.

### 18. Eat less processed meat (ham, bacon, sausages, luncheon meat)
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, may even save money, and takes no time. The hard part is cutting back on the kind you like best.
- Plain language: People who eat the most ham, bacon and sausages have about a 20% higher chance of dying during the study period than people who eat the least. Each extra daily serving of processed meat raises that chance by about 20% as well.
- Benefit: Pooling several tracking studies (recording only, no grouping): people who ate the most processed meat had about a 23% higher risk of death than those who ate the least (RR 1.23). Red meat overall was about 29% higher (RR 1.29). Unprocessed red meat was about 10% higher (RR 1.10), but this showed no statistically clear difference. Another analysis based on daily amount: each extra daily serving of processed meat raises the risk of death by about 23% (RR 1.23), and each extra daily serving of red meat raises it by about 10% (RR 1.10).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Larsson SC, Orsini N (2014). Red meat and processed meat consumption and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis. American Journal of Epidemiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwt261>；Schwingshackl L 等 (2017). Food groups and risk of all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition. <https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.117.153148>；Johnston BC 等 (2019). Unprocessed Red Meat and Processed Meat Consumption: Dietary Guideline Recommendations From the NutriRECS Consortium. Annals of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M19-1621>（争议方 NutriRECS 指南）
- Notes: Disputed. The NutriRECS guidelines use a common evidence-grading system (GRADE) and rate these studies as "not very reliable." They gave only a weak recommendation: keep eating as you currently do. The two sides disagree about how strong the evidence is, not about the direction. No study has shown processed meat to be beneficial. Unprocessed red meat has a small effect, and no statistically clear difference was seen. The thing to limit is processed meat.

### 19. Drink less alcohol, or none at all
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, saves money, and takes no time. The hard part is the mild awkwardness of turning down drinks at social events.
- Plain language: Above 100 g of pure alcohol a week, the more you drink, the earlier you die. 100 g of pure alcohol is about 2.5 L of beer. A 40-year-old drinking 100–200 g a week lives about six months less; 200–350 g, one to two years less; over 350 g, four to five years less. For overall health, drinking nothing is best.
- Benefit: Pooling 83 prospective studies (recording only, no grouping) covering 599,912 people who drink: the lowest risk of death is at no more than 100 g of pure alcohol per week. That is about 2.5 L of 5% beer, or 300 mL of 40% spirits (baijiu, Chinese liquor). A 40-year-old drinking 100–200 g a week has a life expectancy about 6 months shorter; 200–350 g a week, 1–2 years shorter; over 350 g, 4–5 years shorter. The original reports these three figures as years of life. The Global Burden of Disease study (GBD 2016), which adds up all types of health loss, found the lowest-risk amount is zero drinks a week. Another analysis corrected for bias in study design: people who drink 1.3–24 g a day have about a 7% lower risk of death than lifelong non-drinkers (RR 0.93), but this showed no statistically clear difference. Drinking 45–64 g a day carries about 19% higher risk (RR 1.19), and 65 g a day or more about 35% higher risk (RR 1.35).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Wood AM 等 (2018). Risk thresholds for alcohol consumption: combined analysis of individual-participant data for 599 912 current drinkers in 83 prospective studies. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)30134-X>；GBD 2016 Alcohol Collaborators (2018). Alcohol use and burden for 195 countries and territories, 1990–2016: a systematic analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study 2016. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31310-2>；Zhao J 等 (2023). Association Between Daily Alcohol Intake and Risk of All-Cause Mortality: A Systematic Review and Meta-analyses. JAMA Network Open. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.6185>；Di Castelnuovo A 等 (2006). Alcohol dosing and total mortality in men and women: an updated meta-analysis of 34 prospective studies. Archives of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archinte.166.22.2437>（争议方）
- Notes: Disputed. The side arguing that drinking less is beneficial relies on a meta-analysis of 34 long-term tracking studies. It reports that light drinkers have up to 17%–18% lower all-cause mortality, at no more than 4 drinks a day for men and 2 for women. Within that range, heavier drinkers actually had lower mortality. The opposing view holds that the "non-drinker" group includes people who quit because of illness and people whose health was already poor. One pooled analysis that removed these confounders found the protective effect disappeared. The Global Burden of Disease study also says zero is safest. The careful reading: drinking less will not noticeably shorten your life, while drinking more does harm. There is no basis for starting to drink for health. For how to cut down, see item 21 of this section (count your drinks, then talk to a doctor). If you drink every day, do not quit abruptly on your own; see item 20 of this section.

### 20. If you drink every day and get shaky or anxious when you stop, do not quit on your own
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: A psychiatry or addiction medicine appointment costs a few to several dozen yuan. Serious cases may need a few days in hospital.
- Plain language: People who drink heavily every day and suddenly stop can go into withdrawal. Mild symptoms include shaking, sweating, palpitations and insomnia. Severe cases can bring seizures or delirium tremens (severe withdrawal delirium), a medical emergency that can threaten life. In a Norwegian follow-up of more than 30,000 people, those who had delirium tremens had an annual death rate of 8% afterward. If you want to quit, go to a hospital first and tell them how much you drink each day.
- Benefit: A 2026 clinical review states that people who have drunk heavily for a long time develop withdrawal syndrome when they suddenly stop or sharply cut back. Symptoms include tremor, overactive autonomic nervous system, anxiety, insomnia, perceptual disturbances, seizures and delirium tremens, and in general medical care it is "common and potentially life-threatening." A Norwegian national registry cohort included 36,287 people diagnosed with alcohol dependence, alcohol withdrawal state or delirium tremens in 2009–2015. Annual death rates: 8.0% in the delirium tremens group, 5.0% in the withdrawal state group and 3.6% in the alcohol dependence group. The standardized mortality ratio for the delirium tremens group was 9.8 (95% CI 8.9–10.7, a plausible range), meaning their risk of death was about 9.8 times that of the same-age general population.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Caputo F, Lungaro L, Costanzini A, De Giorgio R, Addolorato G (2026). Alcohol withdrawal syndrome in hospitalized patients: a practical review. European Journal of Internal Medicine, 107103. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejim.2026.107103>；Bramness JG, Heiberg IH, Høye A, Rossow I (2023). Mortality and alcohol-related morbidity in patients with delirium tremens, alcohol withdrawal state or alcohol dependence in Norway: A register-based prospective cohort study. Addiction, 118(12), 2352–2359. <https://doi.org/10.1111/add.16297>
- Notes: How to tell if this applies to you: if you drink almost every day, get shaky, sweaty, palpitating or unable to sleep when you go a day without drinking, or need a drink in the morning to feel normal, do not stop on your own. The usual hospital approach is to use benzodiazepine drugs to get through the first few days, along with thiamine (vitamin B1). The evidence is rated B because the Norwegian cohort looks at what happened to people who had delirium tremens, not at what happens when people quit on their own; the link between the two is an inference. This is not a push to keep drinking. It is about switching to a safer way to stop, by going to a hospital. For what counts as too much drinking, see item 19 of this section; for how to cut down, see item 21 of this section (count your drinks, then see a doctor).

### 21. If you want to drink less, first count how much you drink each week, then spend a few minutes talking to a doctor
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=low scope=health -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Note your drinks once a day. Raising it at an outpatient visit adds no extra cost.
- Plain language: A doctor spends a few minutes reviewing your drinking, explaining the risks and helping you set a target. A year later, people drink on average 20 grams of pure alcohol less per week, about half a liter of beer. Spending longer talking adds no further benefit. For people who already cannot stop, two drugs abroad have been shown to work: about 12 people need to be treated for one extra person to stop returning to heavy drinking.
- Benefit: 34 randomized trials pooling 15,197 people: those who received a brief intervention drank 20 g of pure alcohol less per week one year later than those who received minimal intervention or none (95% CI 12 to 28 g less, a plausible range; moderate-quality evidence). The participants in these studies drank an average of 244 g per week at baseline. A brief intervention means advice or lifestyle counseling of no more than 5 sessions totaling under 60 minutes; longer sessions added no extra benefit. On drugs: 122 randomized trials pooling 22,803 people. For acamprosate, for every 12 people treated (95% CI 8 to 26), one additional person stopped drinking entirely. For oral naltrexone at 50 mg a day, for every 12 people treated (8 to 26), one additional person stopped returning to heavy drinking.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Kaner EF, Beyer FR, Muirhead C, et al. (2018). Effectiveness of brief alcohol interventions in primary care populations. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 2, CD004148. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004148.pub4>；Jonas DE, Amick HR, Feltner C, et al. (2014). Pharmacotherapy for adults with alcohol use disorders in outpatient settings: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA, 311(18), 1889–1900. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2014.3628>
- Notes: The benefit size is rated "small" because both sets of evidence measure how much people drink, not death rates. Under this book's convention, when only a surrogate outcome is measured, the benefit is rated "small." Most drug trials were done abroad, most participants met the diagnostic criteria for alcohol dependence, and all trials also included psychosocial intervention, so the drug figures are the extra benefit on top of that. Whether these two drugs are indicated, and whether they can be prescribed, in China is a matter for your doctor and the drug label; do not buy them online yourself. If withdrawal symptoms have already appeared, see item 20 of this section (do not quit on your own).

### 22. Eat a small handful of nuts every day
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: 28 g a day costs about 200–300 yuan a year. Easy to eat without setting aside time.
- Plain language: People who often eat nuts have about a 20% lower chance of dying during the study period than people who do not. Eating them once a week already shows a difference, about 10% lower. The difference is clearest when eating a handful a day.
- Benefit: Two US tracking studies covering 119,000 people and over 3 million person-years (number of people multiplied by the years each was followed). Compared with people who never ate nuts: those who ate them less than once a week had about a 7% lower risk of death (risk ratio 0.93, 95% CI 0.90–0.96, a plausible range). Once a week: about 11% lower (0.89, 0.86–0.93). 2–4 times a week: about 13% lower (0.87, 0.83–0.90). 5–6 times a week: about 15% lower (0.85, 0.79–0.91). 7 or more times a week: about 20% lower (0.80, 0.73–0.86).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Bao Y, Han J, Hu FB, et al. (2013). Association of nut consumption with total and cause-specific mortality. New England Journal of Medicine, 369(21), 2001-2011. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1307352>
- Notes: Buy plain, unsalted nuts. Nuts are high in calories, so do not treat them as snacks to eat freely. These studies are long-term tracking of a group of people, and nut eaters tend to have better lifestyles overall, so treat the numbers with some caution.

### 23. Swap some red meat for fish and poultry
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=low scope=health -->
- Cost: Costs nothing and takes no time. This is swapping one meat for another, not buying more. The hard part is changing your taste preferences a little.
- Plain language: Eating two extra servings a week of processed or red meat raises the chance of dying during the study period by about 3%. Swapping the same number of servings for fish or poultry shows no increase. The effect of swapping meat is small in this section; it is a change to make when it is easy, not something to force.
- Benefit: Pooling 6 US tracking studies covering nearly 30,000 people. Two extra servings a week of processed meat raises the risk of death by about 3% (risk ratio 1.03, 95% CI 1.02–1.05, a plausible range). Two extra servings a week of unprocessed red meat also raises it by about 3% (1.03, 1.01–1.05). Eating the same amount of poultry instead gives 0.99 (0.97–1.02); fish instead gives 0.99 (0.97–1.01). Neither shows a statistically clear difference.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Zhong VW, Van Horn L, Greenland P, et al. (2020). Associations of Processed Meat, Unprocessed Red Meat, Poultry, or Fish Intake With Incident Cardiovascular Disease and All-Cause Mortality. JAMA Internal Medicine, 180(4), 503-512. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.6969>
- Notes: The difference from two servings a week is very small, so do not count on swapping meat to add years to your life. If you are spending the same money on meat anyway, prioritize fish and poultry.

### 24. Swap some refined white rice and flour for whole grains
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Brown rice, oats and whole-wheat flour cost a little more than refined white rice. They take no extra time. The hard part is getting used to the texture.
- Plain language: People who eat about 90 g more whole grains a day have a roughly 17% lower chance of dying during the study period. 90 g is about three servings. Going up to about 200 g a day lowers the risk further.
- Benefit: Pooling several observational cohort studies (people were not assigned to groups): eating 90 g more whole grains a day lowers the risk of death by about 17% (RR 0.83). 90 g is about 3 servings. This benefit continues up to 210–225 g a day. In another pooled analysis, each extra serving a day was linked to about 8% lower risk (RR 0.92).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Aune D 等 (2016). Whole grain consumption and risk of cardiovascular disease, cancer, and all cause and cause specific mortality: systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of prospective studies. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.i2716>；Schwingshackl L 等 (2017). Food groups and risk of all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition. <https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.117.153148>
- Notes: You do not need to switch everything to whole grains. Swapping half is already within the range where the benefit is clearest. Keep in mind that these studies only observe. People who eat whole grains tend to be healthier to begin with, so the numbers are probably too large. Results also differ a lot between studies (I² 83%; the higher this number, the less the studies agree).

### 25. Drink tea three or more times a week
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: A few dozen to a few hundred yuan (RMB) a year. Brewing a cup takes a few minutes and little time.
- Plain language: People with a habit of drinking tea have a roughly 15% lower chance of dying during the study period. Counting from age 50, they live on average 1.26 years longer and have 1.41 more years free of heart and artery disease.
- Benefit: A Chinese study of 100,900 people (China-PAR), half of whom were followed for 7.3 years. Tea drinkers had about 15% lower risk of death (HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.79–0.90, the 95% confidence interval). Starting from age 50, tea drinkers had 1.41 more years free of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and a life expectancy 1.26 years longer.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Wang X, Liu F, Li J, et al. (2020). Tea consumption and the risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and all-cause mortality: The China-PAR project. European Journal of Preventive Cardiology, 27(18), 1956-1963. <https://doi.org/10.1177/2047487319894685>；茶叶霉菌毒素：Cui P 等 (2020). Quantitative analysis and dietary risk assessment of aflatoxins in Chinese post-fermented dark tea. Food and Chemical Toxicology. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fct.2020.111830>；Zhou H 等 (2022). Mycotoxins in Tea (Camellia sinensis (L.) Kuntze): Contamination and Dietary Exposure Profiling in the Chinese Population. Toxins. <https://doi.org/10.3390/toxins14070452>；绿茶提取物与肝损伤：Hu J 等 (2018). The safety of green tea and green tea extract consumption in adults - Results of a systematic review. Regulatory Toxicology and Pharmacology. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yrtph.2018.03.019>
- Notes: Disputed. These are observational studies, and in China, male tea drinkers are more likely to also smoke and drink alcohol. The authors adjusted for some of this, but not all of it. Also, do not drink tea scalding hot; see item 30 of this section (not drinking scalding tea). Some people worry about mycotoxins in tea. A Chinese study tested 158 samples of dark tea and found aflatoxin in 2 of them; based on how much tea people drink, the intake was below the internationally accepted limit. Another study tested 352 tea samples for 16 mycotoxins. Only the ochratoxin A in dark tea averaged above the limit. For the amount Chinese people drink, neither study found a dietary risk. Store tea somewhere dry, and do not drink tea that has become damp or moldy. Green tea extract capsules are different: swallowing a large amount of concentrated catechins at once can damage the liver. Brewed tea does not carry this problem. So this entry refers only to brewed tea. Do not use extract supplements in its place.

### 26. Drink three to four cups of coffee a day, keep caffeine under 400 mg, no added sugar or cream topping
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Brewing it at home costs one or two yuan (RMB) a day. A cup takes a few minutes and little time. If you buy large drinks, check how much caffeine you have had in a day.
- Plain language: Drinking three to four cups of coffee a day was linked to about 17% lower risk of death than drinking none. People who drank it mostly in the morning had about 12% lower risk; all-day drinkers showed no difference. Caffeine varies a lot between cups, so keep the daily total under 400 mg.
- Benefit: A review (umbrella review) combined 201 meta-analyses. The original studies under those meta-analyses were all observational, with no grouping. For overall mortality, the review cited one dose-response meta-analysis. Drinking 3–4 cups a day, compared with none, gives 17% lower risk of death (RR 0.83, 95% CI 0.79–0.88). At 7 cups a day the reduction shrinks to 10% (RR 0.90, 95% CI 0.85–0.96), with no sign of the risk rising again. A US study using the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) tracked 40,725 adults, half of whom were followed for 9.8 years. It was observational, with no grouping. The study divided coffee drinkers into two groups. Morning drinkers, who had coffee mostly between 4:00 and 11:59 and almost none in the afternoon or evening, made up 36%. Compared with non-drinkers, their all-cause mortality was 12% lower (HR 0.88, 95% CI 0.81–0.96). After adjusting for short sleep and sleep problems, it was 16% lower (HR 0.84, 95% CI 0.74–0.95). Cardiovascular death was 19% lower (HR 0.81, 95% CI 0.70–0.94). After adjusting for sleep, it was 31% lower (HR 0.69, 95% CI 0.55–0.87). All-day drinkers, who drank in the morning, afternoon and evening, made up 16%. Their all-cause mortality showed no difference (HR 0.99, 95% CI 0.90–1.10). Neither group showed a difference in cancer mortality. The daily caffeine limit has an official basis. The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) and Health Canada both set the limit for healthy adults at 400 mg a day. This is the total from all sources, so tea, cola and energy drinks count too. EFSA also requires that intake be spread through the day, with no more than 200 mg in a single dose. Caffeine per cup varies widely. In EFSA's table, a 200 mL cup of filter coffee has about 90 mg. In Health Canada's table, an 8 oz (237 mL) cup of freshly brewed coffee averages 135 mg. In the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) table, a 12 oz (about 355 mL) cup of freshly brewed coffee has 113–247 mg. So the FDA converts 400 mg to about 2 to 3 cups of 12 oz coffee, and the review converts it to about 4 cups.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Poole R, Kennedy OJ, Roderick P, et al. (2017). Coffee consumption and health: umbrella review of meta-analyses of multiple health outcomes. BMJ, 359, j5024. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.j5024>，含更正 BMJ (2018), 360, k194. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.k194>（摘要里总死亡率的可信范围由 0.83–0.88 改为 0.79–0.88）；Wang X, Ma H, Sun Q, et al. (2025). Coffee drinking timing and mortality in US adults. European Heart Journal, 46(8), 749-759. <https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehae871>；Yang R, Lei Q, Liu Z, et al. (2024). Relationship between timing of coffee and tea consumption with mortality (total, cardiovascular disease and diabetes) in people with diabetes: the U.S. National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey, 2003–2014. BMC Medicine, 22, 526. <https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-024-03736-x>；EFSA Panel on Dietetic Products, Nutrition and Allergies (2015). Scientific Opinion on the safety of caffeine. EFSA Journal, 13(5), 4102. <https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2015.4102>；EFSA 咖啡因专题页（每份含量表）<https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine>；FDA. Spilling the Beans: How Much Caffeine Is Too Much? <https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much>；Health Canada. Caffeine in foods. <https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-nutrition/food-safety/food-additives/caffeine-foods.html>；加糖：Liu D, Li ZH, Shen D, et al. (2022). Association of Sugar-Sweetened, Artificially Sweetened, and Unsweetened Coffee Consumption With All-Cause and Cause-Specific Mortality. Annals of Internal Medicine, 175(7), 909-917. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M21-2977>；Treskes RW, Clausen J, Marott JL, et al. (2023). Use of sugar in coffee and tea and long-term risk of mortality in older adult Danish men. PLOS ONE, 18(10), e0292882. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0292882>；房颤：Wong CX, Cheung CC, Montenegro G, et al. (2026). Caffeinated Coffee Consumption or Abstinence to Reduce Atrial Fibrillation. JAMA, 335(4), 317-325. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2025.21056>；Joglar JA, Chung MK, Armbruster AL, et al. (2024). 2023 ACC/AHA/ACCP/HRS Guideline for the Diagnosis and Management of Atrial Fibrillation. Circulation, 149(1). <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001193>
- Notes: Disputed. The review's authors wrote that most of the evidence comes from observational studies, and that reliable randomized controlled trials are needed to judge whether the link is causal. The claim that morning drinking is better rests on only one US study, so it is not in the headline. The authors wrote that the results cannot be read as causal; morning drinkers may simply be healthier overall. Another US study limited to people with diabetes found that drinking from dawn to morning was linked to 25% higher mortality, while drinking from morning to noon was linked to 20% lower mortality. The results were opposite. None of these studies compared noon with 2 pm as the better cut-off. Item 4 of section 3 (no caffeine after 2 pm) is about sleep; work backward from your own bedtime. It does not conflict with this entry. 400 mg is a safety ceiling, not a beneficial dose. Decaffeinated coffee has similar but smaller benefits, so do not drink more just to reach the number. Pregnant women should keep to no more than 200 mg a day (EFSA) or 300 mg (Health Canada). This entry does not apply to women at high risk of fracture. It also does not apply to people with anxiety or insomnia. This is the author's judgment; the review has no conclusions for these two groups. People with atrial fibrillation do not all need to give up coffee. In one trial, people who drank about one cup a day after electrical cardioversion for persistent atrial fibrillation had fewer recurrences than those who gave up coffee (47% vs 64%). The 2023 US atrial fibrillation guideline also does not recommend giving up coffee to prevent episodes, but people whose symptoms coffee brings on should cut back. There is no evidence for other arrhythmias; follow your doctor's advice. For mortality, no offsetting effect of added sugar was seen. But syrups and cream-topped drinks have not been studied, and they carry a lot of sugar, so the advice is still not to add sugar.

### 27. Eat five servings (about 400 g) of fruit and vegetables a day
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=some gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: A few to over ten yuan (RMB) a day. It takes some time each day to wash and cut. The hard part is buying and eating enough every day.
- Plain language: Eating about 200 g more fruit and vegetables a day is linked to roughly 10% lower chance of dying during the study period. Eating five servings a day, compared with two, is linked to about 13% lower risk. In US studies, eating more than that did not lower the risk further.
- Benefit: Pooling several observational cohort studies (people were not assigned to groups): eating 200 g more fruit and vegetables a day lowers the risk of death by about 10% (RR 0.90). This benefit continues up to 800 g a day. Two large US cohort studies, plus a pooled analysis of 26 studies: people who ate five servings a day had about 13% lower risk of death than those who ate two (HR 0.87). The best mix is 2 servings of fruit and 3 of vegetables; eating more does not lower the risk further.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Aune D 等 (2017). Fruit and vegetable intake and the risk of cardiovascular disease, total cancer and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of prospective studies. International Journal of Epidemiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyw319>；Wang DD 等 (2021). Fruit and Vegetable Intake and Mortality: Results From 2 Prospective Cohort Studies of US Men and Women and a Meta-Analysis of 26 Cohort Studies. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.120.048996>
- Notes: The two studies differ on how much is best; the US study shows no further decline beyond 5 servings. Keep in mind that these are observational studies with heavy confounding: people who eat a lot of vegetables also differ in income, education and exercise. So the "about 10% lower risk" figure should be read as a ceiling, not a floor.

### 28. Eat less ultra-processed food (chips, instant noodles, pastries, fast food)
<!-- tags: money=0 time=high will=high gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: No money cost, just a change in what you buy. Cooking at home, or taking time to choose less processed ingredients, takes some time every day. The hard part is resisting the habit of choosing whatever is convenient, over the long term.
- Plain language: People who eat the most ultra-processed food have about 20% higher chance of dying during the study period than those who eat the least. Their cardiovascular death risk is about 50% higher. But this evidence is rated uncertain, so discount the numbers somewhat.
- Benefit: A review combined several pooled analyses. The original studies under them were all observational, with no grouping. Result: people who ate more ultra-processed food had about 21% higher risk of death (RR 1.21) and about 50% higher cardiovascular death (RR 1.50) than those who ate less. The evidence grades for these two outcomes are "highly suggestive" and "convincing", respectively. But by the standard GRADE method, certainty is rated low or very low.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Lane MM 等 (2024). Ultra-processed food exposure and adverse health outcomes: umbrella review of epidemiological meta-analyses. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-077310>
- Notes: Disputed on three points. This classification (NOVA) puts foods with very different nutritional profiles in one category. It overlaps heavily with sugary drinks and processed meat (items 7 and 18), so it is unclear which factor is doing the work. By GRADE standards, the rating is also low. So after doing items 7 and 18, it is uncertain how much more benefit this entry adds. The case against it rests only on the low rating from this same review; no original study has reached the opposite conclusion. So do items 7 and 18 first (no sugary drinks, less processed meat), then look at this one.

### 29. Cook and heat with electricity or gas, not coal or wood
<!-- tags: money=high time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Rural households need to replace their stove, plus fuel costs; that comes to several hundred to over a thousand yuan (RMB) a year. Switching is a one-time job and takes no long-term time. Urban residents mostly already use electricity or gas.
- Plain language: People who cook with coal or wood have about 10% higher chance of dying during the study period. Those who heat with coal or wood have about 14% higher. People who have switched to electricity or gas have a 13%–33% lower chance of death than those still burning coal or wood.
- Benefit: A long-term follow-up study in China of 271,000 adults without cardiovascular disease. It was observational, with no grouping. People who cooked with solid fuels such as coal or wood had about 11% higher risk of death than those using electricity or gas (HR 1.11). Heating with solid fuel gave about 14% higher risk (HR 1.14). People who switched from solid fuel to clean fuel, compared with those still burning it, had 13% lower risk for cooking (HR 0.87) and 33% lower risk for heating (HR 0.67). Outdoor PM2.5 is a separate calculation. Pooling 104 cohort studies, each 10 µg/m³ increase in long-term exposure was linked to about 8% higher risk of death from natural causes (RR 1.08). "Natural causes" means non-accidental deaths from disease.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Yu K 等 (2018). Association of Solid Fuel Use With Risk of Cardiovascular and All-Cause Mortality in Rural China. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.2151>；Chen J, Hoek G (2020). Long-term exposure to PM and all-cause and cause-specific mortality: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Environment International. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envint.2020.105974>
- Notes: These are observational studies. Households that switched fuels were often also wealthier, so the "0.67" figure mixes in the effect of household income. For outdoor PM2.5, individuals can do little: move, wear a mask, or use an air purifier. No study uses death rate as an endpoint for air purifiers, so this entry gives no figure for them.

### 30. Let hot food and drinks cool before drinking; no scalding tea, soup or coffee
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: No money. Each time, you wait two or three extra minutes. The hard part is resisting the urge to drink when you are eager.
- Plain language: In a high-risk area for esophageal cancer in Iran, people who drank very hot tea had 8 times the chance of getting esophageal cancer compared with those who drank warm tea. Even just "hot" tea carried twice the risk. Drinking within two minutes of pouring carried five times the risk of drinking after four minutes or more.
- Benefit: In a high-risk area for esophageal cancer in northern Iran, 300 patients were compared with 571 people without the disease. All comparisons are with people who drank warm tea. Drinking "hot" tea was associated with about 2.07 times the odds of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (odds ratio, 95% CI 1.28–3.35). "Very hot" tea was about 8.16 times (3.93–16.9). Drinking within 2 minutes of pouring was about 5.41 times the odds for those who drank after 4 minutes or more (2.63–11.1).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Islami F, Pourshams A, Nasrollahzadeh D, et al. (2009). Tea drinking habits and oesophageal cancer in a high risk area in northern Iran: population based case-control study. BMJ, 338, b929. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.b929>；Loomis D, Guyton KZ, Grosse Y, et al. (2016). Carcinogenicity of drinking coffee, mate, and very hot beverages. Lancet Oncology, 17(7), 877-878. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(16)30239-X>；Yu C, Tang H, Guo Y, et al. (2018). Hot tea consumption and its interactions with alcohol and tobacco use on the risk for esophageal cancer: a population-based cohort study. Annals of Internal Medicine, 168(7), 489-497. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M17-2000>
- Notes: The International Agency for Research on Cancer classifies drinks above 65 °C as Group 2A, meaning probably carcinogenic. The same assessment found that coffee itself is not carcinogenic. A Chinese study followed 456,000 people for a median of 9.2 years. People who drank very hot tea and also had 15 g or more of alcohol a day had the highest risk of esophageal cancer. Compared with people who rarely drank tea and drank less than 15 g of alcohol a day, they had about 5 times the risk (HR 5.00, 3.64–6.88). Those who smoked and drank hot tea every day had a hazard ratio of 2.03 (1.55–2.67). The paper's authors concluded that hot tea combined with heavy drinking or smoking raises esophageal cancer risk further.

### 31. Get some daylight outdoors during the day; do not stay out of the light all day
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: No money. About ten-plus minutes a day; a commute and a lunch break are enough.
- Plain language: In a Swedish study, women who deliberately avoided the sun had a life expectancy 0.6 to 2.1 years shorter than those who got the most sun. Non-smoking women who avoided the sun had about the same life expectancy as smokers in the most-sun group.
- Benefit: A Swedish study of 29,500 women, followed for 20 years. Compared with the group with the most sun exposure, women who deliberately avoided the sun had a life expectancy 0.6 to 2.1 years shorter. The authors wrote: "Non-smoking women who avoid sun exposure have a life expectancy comparable to smokers in the highest sun exposure group."
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Lindqvist PG, Epstein E, Nielsen K, et al. (2016). Avoidance of sun exposure as a risk factor for major causes of death: a competing risk analysis of the Melanoma in Southern Sweden cohort. Journal of Internal Medicine, 280(4), 375-387. <https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.12496>
- Notes: Disputed. Sweden is at a high latitude with little sunshine, so the conclusion cannot be applied directly to China. Also, people who avoid the sun may already be frail or simply stay indoors; the authors raise this as a possibility. The paper's authors themselves note that women who often get sun have a higher risk of skin cancer. So avoid sunburn, and cover up under strong midday sun.

### 32. Keep BMI between 20 and 25; lose weight if overweight
<!-- tags: money=0 time=high will=high gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: No money. Diet and exercise take time every day. This is the most willpower-demanding item in the section: you must control what you eat and stay active over the long term, and keeping weight off after losing it is harder than losing it.
- Plain language: Mortality is lowest at a BMI of 20–25. At BMI 27.5–30 the risk is about 20% higher, at 30–35 about 45% higher, and at 35–40 nearly double. For East Asians with a BMI above 25, each 5-point increase is linked to about 40% higher risk.
- Benefit: Individual data from 239 prospective studies were pooled (observational, with no grouping). This analysis kept only three groups of people: never-smokers, people without chronic disease at entry, and people who survived at least 5 years after entry. Results: mortality risk was lowest at BMI 20–25. BMI 25–27.5 was about 7% higher (HR 1.07). 27.5–30 about 20% higher (HR 1.20). 30–35 about 45% higher (HR 1.45). 35–40 about 94% higher (HR 1.94). 40–60 was 2.76 times (HR 2.76). For people with BMI above 25, each 5 kg/m² increase was linked to about 39% higher risk in East Asian populations (HR 1.39).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Global BMI Mortality Collaboration (2016). Body-mass index and all-cause mortality: individual-participant-data meta-analysis of 239 prospective studies in four continents. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)30175-1>；Flegal KM 等 (2013). Association of all-cause mortality with overweight and obesity using standard body mass index categories: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2012.113905>（争议方）
- Notes: Disputed. One side argues that "being a bit heavier means living longer." Their pooled analysis found that overweight people had about 6% lower risk of death, and mildly obese people about 5% lower. The main disagreement is whether to exclude three kinds of data: people who smoke, people who already had disease, and data from the first few years after enrollment. These are excluded because seriously ill people often lose weight first. When the 239-study analysis excluded those three groups, the risk linked to being overweight reappeared. Also note that no randomized trial has shown that weight loss by itself lowers all-cause mortality. The benefits above come from comparing people of different weights at one point in time; they do not mean that losing weight will reduce risk by that much. People who want to lose weight do not need to fine-tune meal timing: breakfast and 16:8 intermittent fasting have no added benefit. See section 6, item 26 (do not count on breakfast or 16:8 to control weight).

### 33. Eat chili peppers more than four times a week
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: No money and no extra time.
- Plain language: In an Italian study, people who ate chili peppers more than four times a week had about 23% lower chance of dying during the study period than those who rarely ate them. Their cardiovascular death risk was about one-third lower.
- Benefit: An Italian study of 22,800 people, half of whom were followed for 8.2 years; 1,236 died in total. Eating chili peppers more than 4 times a week was linked to 23% lower mortality risk compared with rarely eating them (HR 0.77, 95% CI 0.66–0.90). Cardiovascular death was about 34% lower (0.66, 0.50–0.86).
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Bonaccio M, Di Castelnuovo A, Costanzo S, et al. (2019). Chili Pepper Consumption and Mortality in Italian Adults. Journal of the American College of Cardiology, 74(25), 3139-3149. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2019.09.068>
- Notes: Disputed. This observational study was done in a Mediterranean diet context; people who eat chili may have an overall diet closer to traditional local eating. Also note that people with gastroesophageal reflux, hemorrhoids or irritable bowel syndrome may find chili worsens their symptoms, so do not force yourself. This comes from the author's own experience; no dedicated study has been found.

### 34. Drink one or two servings of milk or yogurt a day
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: A few yuan a day. It takes no time.
- Plain language: People who drink more than two servings of milk or yogurt a day have about 17% lower chance of dying during the study period than people who do not drink them.
- Benefit: The PURE cohort study covers 136,000 people in 21 countries, followed for an average of 9.1 years, with 6,796 deaths. People who ate more than two servings of dairy a day had about 17% lower risk of death than those who ate none (HR 0.83, 95% CI 0.72–0.96), trend P=0.0052.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Dehghan M, Mente A, Rangarajan S, et al. (2018). Association of dairy intake with cardiovascular disease and mortality in 21 countries from five continents (PURE): a prospective cohort study. Lancet, 392(10161), 2288-2297. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31812-9>
- Notes: Disputed. PURE includes many low- and middle-income countries. There, being able to have dairy every day already suggests a fairly good income, and this confounding is hard to remove completely. Also, people with lactose intolerance can switch to yogurt or low-lactose milk; they do not need to force themselves to drink regular milk.

### 35. Eggs need not be avoided; keep it to one a day or less
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=low scope=health -->
- Cost: No money and no time.
- Plain language: Eating an extra half a whole egg a day is linked to about 7% higher risk of death. Swapping that half egg for egg white, fish, poultry or nuts is linked to lower risk. Other studies found no link between up to one egg a day and cardiovascular disease, so you do not need to avoid eggs.
- Benefit: A US study of 521,000 people, with 129,000 deaths. Eating an extra half a whole egg a day is linked to about 7% higher risk of death (HR 1.07, 95% CI 1.06–1.08). Replacing that half egg with the same amount of egg white, poultry, fish, dairy, or nuts and legumes lowers the risk of death, and also lowers the risk of death from cardiovascular disease, cancer and respiratory disease. The other side of the evidence: three large US studies with 216,000 people, followed for up to 32 years. People who ate at least one egg a day, compared with people who ate less than one a month, showed no difference in cardiovascular risk (HR 0.93, 95% CI 0.82–1.05). The same paper pooled 33 datasets with 1.72 million people; eating one more egg a day did not change cardiovascular risk (RR 0.98, 0.93–1.03). The authors concluded that up to one egg a day is not linked to cardiovascular disease.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Zhuang P, Wu F, Mao L, et al. (2021). Egg and cholesterol consumption and mortality from cardiovascular and different causes in the United States: A population-based cohort study. PLoS Medicine, 18(2), e1003508. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1003508>；Drouin-Chartier JP, Chen S, Li Y, et al. (2020). Egg consumption and risk of cardiovascular disease: three large prospective US cohort studies, systematic review, and updated meta-analysis. BMJ, 368, m513. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m513>；U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, U.S. Department of Agriculture (2015). 2015–2020 Dietary Guidelines for Americans, Chapter 1：「The Key Recommendation from the 2010 Dietary Guidelines to limit consumption of dietary cholesterol to 300 mg per day is not included in the 2015 edition, but this change does not suggest that dietary cholesterol is no longer important to consider」「individuals should eat as little dietary cholesterol as possible while consuming a healthy eating pattern」. <https://www.dietaryguidelines.gov/sites/default/files/2019-05/2015-2020_Dietary_Guidelines.pdf>
- Notes: Strongly disputed. Other long-term studies of large groups, and meta-analyses pooling several studies, found no link between one egg a day and cardiovascular disease. The 2015 US Dietary Guidelines dropped the daily 300 mg dietary cholesterol limit. But they also say this does not mean cholesterol is no longer important, and that people should eat as little dietary cholesterol as possible within a healthy eating pattern. One egg a day shows no link to cardiovascular disease so far. The "up to one egg a day" in the title is based on that result; more than one a day has not been directly tested.

### 36. Take a bath when you can, rather than only showering
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Somewhat higher water and electricity bills. Each bath takes about half an hour.
- Plain language: In a Japanese study, people who bathed almost every day had about 28% lower risk of heart and blood vessel events than people who bathed less than twice a week. Their risk of brain hemorrhage was about 46% lower.
- Benefit: A Japanese study of 30,000 people aged 40 to 59, followed for 19 years, recorded 2,097 cardiovascular events. People who bathed almost every day had about 28% lower risk of cardiovascular events than people who bathed no more than twice a week (hazard ratio 0.72, 95% CI 0.62–0.84; this is the plausible range). Coronary heart disease was about 35% lower (0.65, 0.45–0.94). Stroke was about 26% lower (0.74, 0.62–0.87). Cerebral hemorrhage was about 46% lower (0.54, 0.40–0.73).
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Ukai T, Iso H, Yamagishi K, et al. (2020). Habitual tub bathing and risks of incident coronary heart disease and stroke. Heart, 106(10), 732-737. <https://doi.org/10.1136/heartjnl-2019-315752>；消費者庁. 年末年始、高齢者の事故に注意しましょう（页面没写发布日期，数字引自日本厚生劳动省 2024 年人口动态统计）：「「浴槽内での及び浴槽への転落による溺死及び溺水」による死亡者数は、令和6年に7,776人となっています」「暖かい室内と寒い脱衣所や浴室との温度差などによる急激な血圧の変動や、熱いお湯をはった浴槽内に長くつかることによる体温上昇での意識障害などが挙げられています」. <https://www.caa.go.jp/policies/policy/consumer_safety/caution/caution_084/>
- Notes: Disputed. The result comes from a Japanese population with a bathtub culture, and most Chinese households cannot bathe daily. Also, the water should not be too hot and the bath should not be too long. For older people and people with heart or cerebrovascular disease, very hot water or a long soak can be dangerous. In Japan in 2024, 7,776 people drowned in bathtubs. Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency says common causes are the temperature difference between a warm room and a cold changing room or bathroom, and staying too long in hot water. Do not bathe after drinking alcohol, or in hot springs or saunas; see item 11 of section 22 (no hot springs after drinking alcohol).

### 37. Keep afternoon naps within half an hour, and never past one hour. If you need to sleep one or two hours to get through the day, find out why
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Set an alarm before you nap, and you hardly need willpower.
- Plain language: People who nap for no more than an hour do not have higher risk of death or chronic disease, and their thinking is a bit sharper. People who nap for over an hour have about 30% higher risk of coronary heart disease, and about 20% higher risk of diabetes and obesity. So set an alarm and wake up after half an hour. If you need a one- or two-hour nap every day just to function, that is a signal to get your body checked.
- Benefit: An umbrella review pooled 16 meta-analyses covering 244 health outcomes. An umbrella review is a review of existing pooled studies. Its conclusion: people who napped less than 60 minutes showed no increase in total mortality or chronic disease risk, and their brain-function gains were the largest (SMD 0.69, 95% CI 0.37–1.00; this is the plausible range). Short naps of 20 to 30 minutes gave the biggest improvement in athletic performance (SMD 0.99, 0.67–1.31). People who napped more than 60 minutes had about 30% higher coronary heart disease risk, and about 20% higher risk of diabetes and obesity. Another study had 1,338 people aged 56 and over wear wrist watches to measure their naps, instead of relying on their own questionnaire answers. Followed for up to 19 years, 926 of them died. Each extra hour of napping was linked to about 13% higher risk of death (HR 1.13, 95% CI 1.04–1.23). Each extra daily nap was linked to about 7% higher risk (HR 1.07, 1.02–1.13). People who napped in the morning had about 30% higher risk than people who napped earlier in the afternoon (HR 1.30, 1.03–1.64).
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Du P, Li J, Hua Z, et al. (2026). Multiple Health Outcomes of Daytime Napping: A Comprehensive Umbrella Review. Public Health Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.3389/phrs.2026.1609013>；Gao C, Cai R, Zheng X, et al. (2026). Objectively Measured Daytime Napping Patterns and All-Cause Mortality in Older Adults. JAMA Network Open. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.7938>；Dashti HS et al. (2021). Genetic determinants of daytime napping and effects on cardiometabolic health. Nature Communications. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-20585-3>
- Notes: Disputed. Both of the first two studies only recorded data and did not split people into groups. People who nap for a long time are already more likely to have sleep apnea, anemia, an underactive thyroid, depression or other chronic conditions. So it is unclear how much of "people who sleep longer die more" is caused by napping itself. Genetic causal analysis (Mendelian randomization, 453,000 plus 541,000 people) supports only one thing: the more often people nap, the slightly higher their blood pressure and waist size. It found no causal effect of napping on coronary heart disease or diabetes. So do not treat cutting down on naps as a way to lower death risk. The "find out why" part is the authors' suggestion and is rated C. If you are so sleepy in the day that you need a long nap, check your night-time sleep first. If you snore or stop breathing in your sleep, check for sleep apnea. Then check a blood count and thyroid function. For how to nap briefly to feel alert, see item 11 of section 3 (nap 10 minutes when sleepy in the afternoon). For how long to sleep at night, see item 13 of this section (seven hours a night). For how to check and treat snoring and breathing pauses, see item 27 of section 3 (sleep apnea).

### 38. After staying up late, catch up on sleep the next night rather than saving it for the weekend
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. The hour or two you make up is sleep you already wanted.
- Plain language: People who lost sleep and did not make it up the next night had about 15% higher death risk than people with regular sleep. People who lost a lot of sleep and never made it up had about 42% higher risk. People who did make up the sleep showed no higher risk. So if you stay up late, go to bed earlier the next night.
- Benefit: UK Biobank, 85,618 people with an average age of 61.8, wore wrist watches that recorded their sleep every day, with a median follow-up of 8 years. The study first worked out how much sleep each person needed. A "sleep-loss night" was one with at least 2.5 hours less sleep than the person needed. The first night after a sleep-loss night counted as a "catch-up night" if the person slept more than they needed. This gave five patterns. Compared with people with regular sleep, people who lost sleep and did not catch up had about 15% higher total death risk (HR 1.15, 95% CI 1.01–1.31; this is the plausible range). Those who accumulated more than 3.5 hours of lost sleep without catching up had about 42% higher risk (HR 1.42, 1.24–1.63). Both groups that caught up showed no significant link. For those who caught up after losing sleep, the figure was HR 1.12 (0.98–1.28). Among people who already slept little, not catching up was linked to about 19% higher risk (1.19, 1.01–1.40), and severe loss without catching up to about 38% higher risk (1.38, 1.17–1.63). The result was repeated in a separate US health survey of 4,586 people.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Li X, Zhang M, Li Z, et al. (2026). Acute sleep rebound following sleep restriction is associated with reduced mortality risk. Nature Communications. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-72461-1>
- Notes: This is an observational study without grouping by intervention. People who did not catch up may have been busier or in poorer health, and this cannot be fully ruled out. "No higher risk in the groups that caught up" only means that no difference was detected. Their point estimates were still above 1, so catching up does not mean there is no risk. So pay back lost sleep as soon as you can, but do not use this as a reason to stay up late. The catch-up in this study means sleeping a little longer on the first night after losing sleep. It does not include saving sleep up for one big catch-up at the weekend. Making "up late on weekdays, catch up on weekends" a fixed weekly pattern is different. It is called social jet lag, and it is itself linked to cardiovascular disease; see item 13 of this section (about seven hours of sleep a night, at a steady time). The advice to get up at the same time on weekends too is item 2 of section 3. It does not conflict with this item: this item asks you to go to bed earlier tonight, not to sleep until noon the next day.

### 39. The longer you work night shifts, the higher your cardiovascular risk. If you can move to other work, do it as early as possible
<!-- tags: money=high time=mid will=high gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Changing posts or jobs may mean less pay and no night-shift allowance. Finding a new job takes time, and making the decision is hard. If you counted this cost when you chose your job in the first place, the cost is zero.
- Plain language: People who work night shifts have about 13% more cardiovascular disease than people who do not, and about 27% more cardiovascular deaths. The longer you do it, the more the risk builds: every extra 5 years of night shifts adds about 7% more cardiovascular disease. So count this by the years you work, and move to other work as early as you can.
- Benefit: This pools 23 follow-up studies. They only recorded data and did not split people into groups. People who work night shifts had about 13% higher risk of cardiovascular events (RR 1.13, 95% CI 1.10–1.16; this is the plausible range). Cardiovascular death was about 27% higher (RR 1.27, 1.18–1.36). By years worked: every extra 5 years of night shifts raised the rate of cardiovascular disease by about 7% (RR 1.07, 1.04–1.09), and cardiovascular death by about 5% (RR 1.05, 1.03–1.06). By condition: coronary heart disease occurrence was about 22% higher (1.22, 1.16–1.28); coronary heart disease death 22% higher (1.22, 1.10–1.36); ischemic heart disease death 39% higher (1.39, 1.06–1.84); stroke death 49% higher (1.49, 1.04–2.12). The result for stroke occurrence was not significant (1.06, 0.95–1.18).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Xi J, Ma W, Tao Y, et al. (2025). Association between night shift work and cardiovascular disease: a systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis. Frontiers in Public Health. <https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2025.1668848>；Esposito G, Bravi F, Santucci C, et al. (2025). Night shift work and breast cancer risk in healthcare workers: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Occupational Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1093/occmed/kqaf040>；Shen QM, Li ZY, Tan YT, et al. (2026). Night shift work and risk of total and site-specific cancer: results from a prospective cohort study among Chinese men. Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment & Health. <https://doi.org/10.5271/sjweh.4290>；Czeisler CA, Johnson MP, Duffy JF, et al. (1990). Exposure to bright light and darkness to treat physiologic maladaptation to night work. New England Journal of Medicine, 322(18), 1253-1259. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199005033221801>；Eastman CI, Stewart KT, Mahoney MP, Liu L, Fogg LF (1994). Dark goggles and bright light improve circadian rhythm adaptation to night-shift work. Sleep, 17(6), 535-543：「Both bright light and goggles were significant factors for producing circadian rhythm phase shifts. The combination of bright light plus goggles was the most effective」「Goggles were necessary for producing phase delays」. <https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/17.6.535>；Crowley SJ, Lee C, Tseng CY, Fogg LF, Eastman CI (2003). Combinations of bright light, scheduled dark, sunglasses, and melatonin to facilitate circadian entrainment to night shift work. Journal of Biological Rhythms, 18(6), 513-523：「With bright light during the night shift, almost all of the earlier participants achieved complete re-entrainment, and the phase delay shift was so large that darker sunglasses and melatonin could not increase its magnitude」. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0748730403258422>
- Notes: All of these are observational studies that did not split people into groups. Night-shift workers differ from others in smoking, weight and income, and these differences cannot be fully adjusted for. The evidence that night shifts cause cancer is much weaker than commonly claimed. In a pooled analysis of 12 studies with 12,132 breast cancer cases, people who had done night shifts had only about 5% higher risk, and the confidence interval crosses 1 (RR 1.05, 0.96–1.14). The group with 20 years or more had about 25% higher risk (1.25, 1.01–1.55). But after the authors ran a sensitivity analysis, that result was no longer significant. After correcting for possible publication bias, the estimate was close to 1, and the authors wrote that the link is "far from established". Another study followed 8,202 cancer cases among Chinese men for 16.1 years. It found no link between night shifts and total cancer or most common cancers. Only pancreatic cancer in the group with 11 to 20 cumulative years showed about 59% higher risk (HR 1.59, 1.09–2.31). So this item bases the calculation on cardiovascular risk, not cancer. This book does not argue that everyone should leave night work. It lays out the year-based calculation so you can weigh it yourself. If you are already on night shifts, change what you can first: quit smoking (item 1 of this section, quit as early as possible), control blood pressure and lipids (item 12 of this section, take prescribed medication for high blood pressure or high cholesterol as directed), and catch up on sleep after night shifts (item 38 of this section, catch up the next night). Two other countermeasures are worth trying: eat during daylight hours, and use bright light plus strict daytime darkness to shift your body clock. The second has moved the body clock in experiments. Under ordinary indoor lighting, six night shifts in a row did not shift the body clock at all. With bright light of 7,000 to 12,000 lux and near-total darkness during the day, the clock fully reversed in four days. **Keep the light bright at night, keep the daytime sleep strictly dark, and wear sunglasses on the way home to block the morning light. The researchers recommend all three together.** In a simulated night-shift study of 50 people, bright light at night and sunglasses after work each worked on their own, and the two together worked best. People who did not wear sunglasses shifted their body clock only earlier, not later. The author believes daylight on the way home was blocking the shift. In another study of 67 people, those whose body clocks already ran early mostly reversed completely with bright light at night, and darker sunglasses added nothing. Only with indoor lighting did darker sunglasses help. The endpoints of both countermeasures were metabolic markers and sleep only. No trial has tested whether following them lowers cardiovascular risk. If you go back to a daytime routine on your days off, your body clock will be pushed back again. Dosage, original figures, other sources, and how the body senses time are in [docs/body-clock-and-night-shifts.md](../docs/生物钟和夜班.md).

### 40. Buy prepackaged cooking oil with an SC number, not bulk home-pressed peanut oil from small workshops
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: Buying elsewhere costs about the same. Check the packaging for a food production licence number starting with "SC". The hard part is convincing older family members who are used to buying home-pressed oil.
- Plain language: Peanuts that get damp and moldy can produce aflatoxin, a confirmed carcinogen that mainly harms the liver. After Guangzhou tightened control of small workshops, residents who often ate home-pressed oil had about 35% fewer abnormal liver function results. In Guangxi, pregnant women who ate home-pressed peanut oil had about 90% higher risk of giving birth to a baby with low birth weight, and about 80% higher risk of preterm birth.
- Benefit: Aflatoxin is a Group 1 carcinogen according to the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), meaning it is known to cause cancer in humans. It mainly causes liver cancer. A Guangzhou study measured aflatoxin B1 in home-pressed peanut oil from small workshops from 2010 to 2022. The median was 1.29 μg/kg, and the middle half of the samples fell between 0.12 and 6.58. After the local regulation on small food workshops took effect, the toxin level in the oil dropped right away by 2.865 μg/kg, and then fell by a further 2.593 each year. The share of abnormal liver function results among residents of the area with high consumption fell in step (PR 0.650, 95% CI 0.469–0.902; this is the plausible range), about 35% lower. A Guangxi study followed 1,611 pregnant women, of whom 81.7% ate home-pressed peanut oil. ### 41. Use vegetable oil instead of lard and butter for cooking. You don't need to switch oils for health, and don't expect flaxseed oil to protect your heart
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: No extra money. Vegetable oil is already the main cooking oil in most households. The hard part is breaking the habit of stir-frying and seasoning with lard.
- Plain language: Replacing saturated fats such as lard and butter with vegetable oil cuts heart attacks and strokes by about 17%. Total death risk, however, showed no change. Eating more of the main fats in soybean and corn oil showed no benefit and no harm. Eating more flaxseed oil did not show a lower risk of heart disease.
- Benefit: Cochrane pooled 15 randomized trials with 56,675 people, each lasting at least two years. Cutting saturated fat lowered combined cardiovascular events by about 17% (RR 0.83, 0.70–0.98). All-cause mortality was almost unchanged (RR 0.96, 0.90–1.03), and cardiovascular death was unchanged (RR 0.95, 0.80–1.12). Replacing saturated fat with polyunsaturated fats (the main fat in soybean and corn oil) or with carbohydrates made no clear difference. Replacing it with monounsaturated fats (the main fat in rapeseed and olive oil) had too little data to say. A second Cochrane review pooled 19 randomized trials with 6,461 people. Eating more omega-6 (linoleic acid) gave all-cause mortality RR 1.00 (0.88–1.12) and cardiovascular events RR 0.97 (0.81–1.15), showing neither benefit nor harm. A third review pooled 86 randomized trials with 162,796 people. Eating more plant-based omega-3 (the main fat in flaxseed and perilla oil) gave all-cause mortality RR 1.01 (0.84–1.20) and coronary events RR 1.00 (0.82–1.22). It had essentially no effect.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Hooper L, Martin N, Jimoh OF, et al. (2020). Reduction in saturated fat for cardiovascular disease. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, (5), CD011737. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD011737.pub3>；Hooper L, Al-Khudairy L, Abdelhamid AS, et al. (2018). Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, (3), CD003177. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD003177.pub5>；中国营养学会 (2022). 中国居民膳食指南（2022）. 人民卫生出版社
- Notes: Disputed. Some argue that soybean and corn oil contain too much omega-6, which promotes inflammation, so people should eat less of them and switch oils. The randomized trials in the omega-6 review found no harm, and the authors rated the quality of that evidence as low. The benefit size is rated medium, based on the 17% cut in cardiovascular events, while total mortality did not change. Any oil should be used in limited amounts. Dietary guidelines recommend 25 to 30 grams of cooking oil per person per day.

### 42. Turn on the range hood when stir-frying or frying, from the moment you light the stove until the cooking is done
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: If your home already has a range hood, the only cost is a little extra electricity. If you don't have one, buying and installing one costs several hundred to over a thousand yuan. The hard part is remembering to switch it on every time you cook, and not turning it off because it is noisy.
- Plain language: Among Chinese women who have never smoked, those who rarely or never ran a range hood while stir-frying had about twice the lung cancer risk of those who always did. Living in a house with poor kitchen ventilation also raised risk by about 50%. The effect was most marked in households that burn coal.
- Benefit: This pools 10 case-control studies, all in Chinese women who have never smoked. Case-control studies compare lung cancer patients with healthy people and ask them about past habits. Exposure to cooking oil fumes gave about 1.74 times the odds of lung cancer (odds ratio, 95% CI 1.57–1.94; this is the plausible range). Four of the studies compared people exposed to fumes without a range hood, and the figure there was about 2.11 times (1.54–2.89). A Taiwanese study compared 1,302 never-smoking women with lung cancer against 1,302 healthy women. Looking only at women who had cooked for more than ten years, those who ran the range hood in more than two-thirds of their cooking years had about half the risk of those who ran it in less than a third (OR 0.49, 0.32–0.76). A prospective study in Shanghai followed 71,000 never-smoking women from 1996 to 2009 and identified 429 lung cancers. Women who reported living in a house with poor kitchen ventilation had about 49% higher risk (HR 1.49, 1.15–1.95). Women who burned coal in poorly ventilated kitchens for more than 20 years had about 2.03 times the risk (HR 2.03, 1.35–3.05). Looking at coal use alone showed no difference (HR 1.03, 0.84–1.26). The type of cooking oil made no difference either.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Xue Y, Jiang Y, Jin S, Li Y (2016). Association between cooking oil fume exposure and lung cancer among Chinese nonsmoking women: a meta-analysis. OncoTargets and Therapy, 9, 2987-2992. <https://doi.org/10.2147/OTT.S100949>；Chen TY, Fang YH, Chen HL, et al. (2020). Scientific Reports, 10, 6774. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-63656-7>；Kim C, Gao YT, Xiang YB, et al. (2015). Home kitchen ventilation, cooking fuels, and lung cancer risk in a prospective cohort of never smoking women in Shanghai, China. International Journal of Cancer, 136(3), 632-638. <https://doi.org/10.1002/ijc.29020>
- Notes: Disputed. The figures supporting range hoods come mainly from case-control studies. Patients who recall past habits after diagnosis may under-report how often they ran the hood, so the figures may be overstated. In the Shanghai prospective study, the higher risk was concentrated in households that burned coal and had poor ventilation. That study could not give a separate figure for households using only gas, so how much running a hood helps with gas is unknown. The evidence grade is therefore B. The benefit is rated large because of the roughly twofold risk difference. Not burning coal or wood for cooking is covered in item 29 of this section (no coal or wood for cooking and heating). The main beneficiary of this item is whoever usually does the cooking at home.

### 43. If hot flashes and night sweats around menopause affect daily life, get a gynecological assessment for hormone therapy. Don't just tough it out, don't buy the drugs yourself, and don't take them to prevent heart disease
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no gain=high scope=health who=women+35plus -->
- Cost: One visit to a gynecology or menopause clinic. The doctor will ask about your medical history, check whether you have any condition that makes hormones unsuitable, and prescribe if appropriate. Follow the doctor's instructions for follow-up checks once you start. The hard part is letting go of the fear that "hormones cause cancer", and not going out to buy them just because you heard they slow aging.
- Plain language: For hot flashes and night sweats around menopause, hormone therapy cut the number of hot flashes by about 75% compared with a placebo. The cost is a small rise in breast cancer, blood clots and stroke, about 8 extra cases each per 10,000 women per year. Women who start between ages 50 and 59 show no rise in overall death risk. It should not be used to prevent heart disease or dementia.
- Benefit: Cochrane pooled 24 randomized trials with 3,329 people, comparing oral hormones with a placebo. Women on hormones had about 75% fewer hot flashes per week than placebo (95% CI 64.3–82.3; this is the plausible range). The severity of hot flashes also fell clearly (OR 0.13, 0.07–0.23). People on placebo also had about 57.7% fewer hot flashes, so any claim that something treats hot flashes has to be compared with placebo to count. Risk figures come from the US Women's Health Initiative (WHI) randomized trial: 16,608 women with a uterus, aged 50 to 79, taking estrogen plus progestin, followed for an average of 5.2 years. Coronary heart disease was about 29% higher (HR 1.29, 1.02–1.63). Invasive breast cancer was about 26% higher (HR 1.26, 1.00–1.59). Stroke was about 41% higher (HR 1.41, 1.07–1.85). Pulmonary embolism was about 2.13 times higher (HR 2.13, 1.39–3.25). In absolute terms, per 10,000 women per year: 7 more coronary heart disease cases, 8 more strokes, 8 more pulmonary embolisms and 8 more invasive breast cancers. Over the same period there were 6 fewer colorectal cancers and 5 fewer hip fractures. Women who had had their uterus removed and took estrogen alone, followed for 13 years cumulatively, had about 21% less breast cancer (HR 0.79, 0.65–0.97). Combining the two trials and following them for 18 years, total mortality showed no change (HR 0.99, 0.94–1.03). By age at start: the 50 to 59 group had about 31% lower total mortality during the years of use (HR 0.69, 0.51–0.94). The 60 to 69 group had 1.04 (0.87–1.25), and the 70 to 79 group 1.13 (0.94–1.36), neither significant. Followed to 18 years, the 50 to 59 group was 0.89 (0.79–1.01), no longer significant. Among women over 65 on combined estrogen and progestin, dementia rose from 9 to 11–30 cases per 1,000 people over 4 years. In 2022, the US Preventive Services Task Force recommended against using hormones to prevent chronic disease (grade D), concluding there is no net benefit.

### 44. If you already want a dog, count the daily walks as part of your walking. Don't get a dog just to live longer

- Cost: Owning a dog is an ongoing expense: food, vaccines, deworming, vet bills and licences, all for the dog's whole life. You need to set aside time every day for walks, and find someone to look after the dog when you travel. The hard part is keeping up the walks every day. Once you have a dog, you cannot simply stop. China has no official statistics on what it costs to keep a dog, so no figure is given here.
- Plain language: On average, dog owners had about a quarter lower chance of dying during the same period, and people who had had coronary heart disease gained even more. But after accounting for other differences, large studies in the UK and Norway found no difference, and cat ownership showed no link at all. So do not get a dog just to live longer. If you already want one, count your daily walks as walking.
- Benefit: This pools 10 follow-up studies with about 3.84 million people, followed for an average of 10.1 years. Dog owners had about 24% lower risk of death (RR 0.76, 95% CI 0.67–0.86; this is the plausible range). Cardiovascular death was about 31% lower (RR 0.69, 0.67–0.71). Among people who had coronary heart disease, those with a dog at home had about 65% lower risk (RR 0.35, 0.17–0.69). The authors note that this pooled estimate did not adjust for other factors. A Swedish national registry study of about 3.43 million people did adjust for income, marital status and other factors. Across everyone, dog owners had about 20% lower risk of death (HR 0.80, 0.79–0.82). Dog owners who lived alone had about 33% lower risk (HR 0.67, 0.65–0.69). Those who lived with others had about 11% lower risk (HR 0.89, 0.87–0.91). Among Swedish heart attack patients, those who lived alone with a dog had about 33% lower risk of death (HR 0.67, 0.61–0.75), and those who lived with a partner or children had about 15% lower risk (HR 0.85, 0.80–0.90). After an ischemic stroke, the figures were about 27% lower (HR 0.73, 0.66–0.80) and 12% lower (HR 0.88, 0.83–0.93). The counter-evidence: six UK population health surveys with 59,352 people, followed for an average of 11.5 years. After full adjustment, dog ownership showed no link to death (HR 1.03, 0.98–1.09) or to cardiovascular death (HR 1.07, 0.96–1.18). A Norwegian study followed people for a median of 18.5 years and found almost the same risk of death for dog owners (HR 1.00, 0.91–1.09). Dog owners there were no more physically active than others. In a Swedish twin registry, 34,202 people could be asked about their lifestyle. In them, the link between dog ownership and lower cardiovascular disease and death risk did not reproduce. An Australian study of 15,735 people followed for 4 years found dog owners had about 23% lower risk of death (OR 0.77, 0.59–0.99), part of which could be explained by more physical activity. Cat, bird and fish ownership showed no link to death. The American Heart Association's 2013 statement said that pet ownership, especially dog ownership, "may be reasonable" for reducing cardiovascular risk. This is the weakest level of recommendation. The same statement explicitly advised against adopting, rescuing or buying pets mainly to reduce cardiovascular risk.

### 45. If you have prediabetes (blood sugar high but not yet diabetic), use less food, less weight and more movement to push diabetes back, and recheck your blood sugar every six months

- Cost: No cost, and you may even save a little by eating less. Moderate activity such as brisk walking for at least 150 minutes a week works out to about twenty minutes a day. Eat 400 to 500 kcal less per day than before. The hard part is keeping it up, and keeping the weight off afterwards. A fasting blood test every six months costs a little over ten yuan.
- Plain language: People with high blood sugar who are not yet diabetic can cut their risk of developing diabetes by 30 to 60% by eating less, losing some weight and exercising more. In China's Da Qing trial, which followed people for 30 years, those who made these changes had about a quarter lower risk of death and lived about one and a half years longer on average. However, similar trials in the US and Finland did not show a difference in death rates.
- Benefit: The Da Qing study was a randomized trial in Da Qing, Heilongjiang, with groups assigned by clinic. 577 adults with impaired glucose tolerance (a form of prediabetes) were assigned to a control group or one of three intervention groups: diet only, exercise only, or both. Cumulative diabetes rates over 6 years were 67.7% in the control group, 43.8% in the diet group, 41.1% in the exercise group and 46.0% in the diet-plus-exercise group. Lean and overweight people (BMI cut at 25) saw similar reductions. Followed for 23 years, cardiovascular death in the intervention groups was about 41% lower (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.36–0.96; this is the plausible range). Followed for 30 years, the median time to diabetes in the intervention groups was delayed by 3.96 years. Cardiovascular events were about 26% lower (HR 0.74, 0.59–0.92). Severe small-vessel complications of the eye, kidney and nerves were about 35% lower (HR 0.65, 0.45–0.95). Cardiovascular death was about 33% lower (HR 0.67, 0.48–0.94). A two-hour blood glucose after a sugar drink of 7.8 to 11.1 mmol/L (not including 11.1) also counts. It recommends that overweight or obese people bring their BMI down to or near 24, or lose at least 7% of body weight. Total daily energy intake should be cut by at least 400 to 500 kcal, and moderate activity should be at least 150 minutes a week. Check fasting or two-hour post-meal blood glucose every six months.

Stop sweet drinks first; see item 7 of this section (no sugary drinks). To check whether your activity is enough, compare with item 11 of this section (7,000 to 8,000 steps a day). For where your weight should be, see item 32 of this section (keep BMI between 20 and 25). To find out whether you have it, see item 8 of section 1 (check fasting blood glucose once). Metformin also reduces diabetes, but less well than changing lifestyle. Whether to take it is up to a doctor. The benefit is rated large based on the 26% lower total mortality in Da Qing. The US and Finnish trials did not reproduce this. The main beneficiary of this item is you.

### 46. If peanuts, corn, nuts, rice or flour at home have gone moldy, throw out the whole package. Don't pick out the moldy bits and eat the rest. If a nut tastes bitter when you chew it, spit it out at once
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: Throwing out a bag of peanuts or nuts, or a bag of rice or flour, costs a few to several dozen yuan. Store grain and nuts somewhere dry, ventilated and cool, buy small packs, and avoid stockpiling. The hard part is being unwilling to throw food away, especially when convincing older family members.
- Plain language: Moldy peanuts and corn can produce aflatoxin, which can cause liver cancer. In a Shanghai study, people with the toxin detected in their urine had about 3.4 times the liver cancer risk of people without it. People who were also hepatitis B carriers and had the toxin detected had about 59.4 times the risk of people with neither. The toxin is hard to remove by stir-frying, boiling or deep-frying.
- Benefit: Aflatoxin is a Group 1 carcinogen according to IARC, meaning it is known to cause cancer in humans. It causes liver cancer. It is most common in peanuts and corn, and ordinary cooking such as stir-frying, boiling and deep-frying barely destroys it. A Shanghai study followed 18,244 middle-aged men from 1986, with each man giving a urine sample when he joined. Follow-up of about 70,000 person-years identified 55 cases of liver cancer. The authors compared 50 of these cases with 267 cancer-free men from the same group. Detecting aflatoxin in urine was linked to about 3.4 times the risk (RR 3.4, 95% CI 1.1–10.0; this is the plausible range). Hepatitis B surface antigen positivity was linked to about 7.3 times (2.2–24.4). Having both was linked to about 59.4 times (16.6–212.0), compared with men who had neither. In an earlier analysis of the same group, after adjusting for hepatitis B, smoking, drinking and education, detected toxin was linked to about 3.8 times (1.2–12.2). Qidong in Jiangsu was once a high-incidence area for liver cancer. From the 1980s, the local staple switched from corn to rice. The median level of the toxin marker in residents' blood fell from 19.3 pg/mg in 1989 to undetectable (below 0.5) by 2009. Liver cancer death rates among residents under 35 fell by more than half for the birth cohorts from the 1960s to the 1980s. All of these cohorts were born before hepatitis B vaccination was routine for newborns. The authors estimate that about 65% of the drop in liver cancer deaths can be attributed to eating less of the toxin, and 83% of that fell on people infected with hepatitis B. Global estimates suggest that hepatitis B carriers have about 30 times the cancer-causing potency of people without the virus, for the same amount of toxin eaten. Each year in China, a share of liver cancers can be attributed to this toxin. About 5,300 to 14,400 cases occur in hepatitis B carriers, and about 1,990 to 4,430 in people without the virus.
- Evidence level: B

### 47. Don't eat raw freshwater fish or shrimp, including fish sashimi, fish congee and drunken shrimp. If you have eaten them regularly, get a stool test for liver fluke once, and take praziquantel as prescribed if positive
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=some gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: Not eating raw fish costs nothing. The hard part is giving up a long-held taste and turning down invitations to group meals. For people who have eaten it regularly, a stool test for eggs costs varying amounts depending on where you live. If the test is positive, take praziquantel at the dose the doctor prescribes, usually for 1 to 2 days.
- Plain language: Eating raw freshwater fish or shrimp can infect you with liver flukes. This parasite is a confirmed carcinogen, and infected people are about four to five times more likely to develop bile duct cancer than people who are not infected. Vinegar, alcohol and wasabi cannot effectively kill it. If you are infected, two days of medicine cures more than nine in ten people.
- Benefit: Clonorchis sinensis is commonly called the liver fluke. In 2009, a working group of the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), part of the World Health Organization, judged that long-term infection causes cancer in humans. It causes bile duct cancer, Group 1. The 1994 review had given it only Group 2A (probably carcinogenic). Pooling the published studies, people infected with liver fluke had about 4.8 times the risk of bile duct cancer. The original figure is a relative risk of 4.8 (95% CI 2.8–8.4; this is the plausible range). This figure also includes another liver fluke species found in Southeast Asia. In the same paper, hepatitis B was 2.6 times and hepatitis C was 1.8 times, and the liver fluke was the strongest of the three. Another review pooled 36 studies. In case-control studies, bile duct cancer was about 4.37 times (OR 4.37, 2.84–6.72). In cohort studies it was about 10.43 times (RR 10.43, 2.90–37.47). Cholangitis and cholecystitis were about 7.80 times (RR 7.80). Gallstones were about 2.42 times (RR 2.42). Hepatocellular carcinoma was about 4.69 times (OR 4.69). Cirrhosis showed no significant link. The heavier the infection, the more of these diseases there are. A national survey in 2014 and 2015 found an infection rate of 0.47%, about 6 million people. Guangdong, Guangxi, Heilongjiang and Jilin were the four provinces with rates above 1%. Using surveillance data from 2016 to 2021, the national rate in 2020 was estimated at about 0.67%, about 9.46 million people. Guangxi was about 8.92% and Guangdong about 2.99%. The 2024 prevention plan, issued by 15 departments including the National Disease Control and Prevention Administration, uses the 2018 to 2022 average infection rate at surveillance sites as its baseline. Guangxi was 8.75%, Jilin 4.38%, Heilongjiang 3.16% and Guangdong 2.95%. The National Food Safety Risk Assessment Center's 2026 warning says that alcohol, vinegar, wasabi and other condiments cannot effectively kill liver flukes. Freezing must be continuous, below minus 20°C, for at least 7 days. Heating must bring the centre of the food to above 70°C and hold it there for more than 10 minutes. The IARC also notes that salted or smoked freshwater fish can still transmit the infection. For treatment, a 2022 review pooled 26 trials with 3,340 people. The WHO-recommended praziquantel dose is 25 mg per kg of body weight per dose, three times a day, for 2 days. The predicted cure rate for Clonorchis sinensis is 98.5% (85.4%–99.9%).
