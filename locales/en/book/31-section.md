# 31. Which Routes Are Open After Age 18

This section works out the money and time involved. For conscription, it also covers personal liberty. It is a map of the routes, not a guide to any single one. It first puts the legal entry requirements for each route side by side. Then it goes through the details that matter only once you are on a given route: what military service pays, which routes into the state system exist besides the civil service exam, which two majors on the gaokao (national university entrance exam) application link directly to official posts, whether you can add a degree later, what to look for in an employer if you want to work abroad, how social insurance works if you do not join an employer, and whether there is a state-subsidised loan to help you start a business.

There are many more paths after age 18 than this section covers. It only includes routes where national-level public documents set out the requirements, benefits and deadlines, so that each can be checked line by line. Joining a state-owned enterprise, working in a factory, serving as a seafarer, freelancing, or continuing into senior high school or university are also valid paths. They have no single official document to check, so this book does not invent their requirements from impression.

Which path to take is your own decision. This section gives only two things: where the entry requirements are written down, and which deadlines and eligibility rules expire once you are on a path. How to weigh study against work, and how to choose a skill, is covered in section 23. Overtime pay, work injury and severance after you join an employer are in section 19. Costs and exit when opening a shop or company are in section 12. Studying abroad is in section 32. Protection after losing a job is in section 7. None of these is repeated here.

### 1. Check the entry requirements first: for most of the twelve routes, the age and education limits are written into law
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=time -->
- Cost: Nothing to pay. Ten minutes to check whether you meet the requirements.
- Plain language: The entry requirements for these twelve routes are mostly written down in official documents. Working requires you to be at least 16. Military service requires men to be at least 18, and college graduates can be accepted up to age 24. The civil service generally takes people aged 18 to 35 with at least a junior-college diploma. Self-study exams have no age or education limit, but full-time students still in school cannot sign up. Running a sole proprietorship only requires "business ability."
- Benefit: Military service: under the Military Service Law, "male citizens who have reached the age of 18 shall be conscripted for active service. Those not conscripted in that year may still be conscripted before they reach 22. The conscription age for ordinary university graduates may be relaxed to 24, and for postgraduates to 26." Civil service: Article 18 of the Civil Service Recruitment Regulations. The requirements include "aged 18 or over and under 35" and "holding a junior-college diploma or higher." Provincial-level or higher civil service authorities may approve adjustments to these two requirements. Article 19 of the same regulations lists five categories of people who may not apply, for example people who have been criminally punished, people dismissed from public office, and people placed on the list of subjects of joint disciplinary measures for breach of trust. Firefighters: the Recruitment Measures for Firefighters of the National Comprehensive Fire and Rescue Force. Article 5 requires being "aged 18 or over and under 22" and having "high-school education or above." Article 6 says that people with a junior-college degree or above, and retired PLA and People's Armed Police soldiers, may be recruited up to age 24. Military civilian staff: Article 9 of the PLA Civilian Personnel Regulations lists "aged at least 18" among the basic conditions. Article 19 states that the maximum age for first recruitment to lower-grade posts is 35. Lower-grade posts are entry-level positions. Public institutions: Article 8 of the Regulations on Personnel Management of Public Institutions states that new staff "shall be recruited openly from society." Individual businesses: the Regulations on Promoting the Development of Individual Businesses apply to "citizens with business ability who register as individual businesses under the law." Self-study: Article 3 of the Interim Regulations on Higher Education Self-Study Examinations states that Chinese citizens "are not restricted by gender, age, ethnicity, race or prior education" and may take the exams. Article 20 states that "students enrolled in any full-time school may not sign up." Public-funded teacher training: Article 2 of the Measures for Public-Funded Teacher Education at Ministry-Affiliated Normal Universities (linked bachelor's and master's programmes). It recruits only at six ministry-affiliated normal universities: Beijing Normal University, East China Normal University, Northeast Normal University, Central China Normal University, Shaanxi Normal University and Southwest University. Undergraduate and postgraduate study are designed as one programme, assessed in stages, so you continue from your bachelor's degree into postgraduate study. Tuition and accommodation fees during study are paid by central government, and a living allowance is also provided. Article 5 of the Measures states that "admission of public-funded teacher trainees is conducted in an early admissions batch," meaning they are admitted before the ordinary batch. Targeted medical students: the notice on employment placement and compliance management for rural targeted free-training medical students. It states that, "after admission and before receiving the admission notice, students must sign an agreement with the training school and with the county-level health administration and the human resources and social security authority of the targeted employment area." Working abroad: the Regulations on the Administration of Overseas Labour Cooperation. Article 5: anyone engaged in overseas labour cooperation must obtain business qualification from the commerce authority. Article 7, paragraph 4: "No one may engage in overseas labour cooperation without legally obtaining the business qualification certificate and registering." A company without this certificate that sends people abroad to work is itself acting unlawfully. Article 8, paragraph 2: "No entity or individual may organise labour personnel to work abroad under the name of business, tourism, study abroad or similar." Platform gig work and flexible employment have no statutory education requirement. For self-enrolment in social insurance and protection against occupational injury, see items 11 and 12 of this section. Work: the Compulsory Education Law states, "Employers are prohibited from hiring school-age children and adolescents who should be receiving compulsory education." The Child Labour Prohibition Regulations, Article 2, state that no one under 16 may be hired. Penalties are in item 1 of section 23 (under 16).

- Notes: China only: Some routes do not fix their requirements in law. The public institutions regulations only require open recruitment; age and education limits are set in each year's job announcement. Platform work has no statutory education requirement. For civil servants, provincial-level or higher authorities may adjust the age and education limits. Meeting the limits is only the first hurdle. After that come political eligibility checks, a medical examination and a political background review. These have no public scoring criteria, and none can be found in the texts. The public-funded teacher and targeted medical student routes are fixed on gaokao application day; their cost is set out in item 13. Eligibility to work abroad does not depend on you but on whether the employer is qualified; how to check this is in item 14. This section does not compare which route pays best.

### 2. Register for military service before 31 October of the year you turn 18; active conscript service is two years
<!-- tags: money=0 time=high will=high gain=mid scope=time -->
- Cost: Nothing to pay. Registration is completed once online and takes a few minutes. Actual service costs two full-time years, during which you cannot do anything else.
- Plain language: Military service registration is a legal duty, not an application to join the army. Registering does not mean you have to serve. Men who turn 18 this year must complete online registration before 31 October. If you do not register, the county will order you to correct this within a set time. If you still do not, you can be compelled to fulfil your military obligations and fined. If you do serve, active conscript service lasts two years.
- Benefit: Registration is governed by the Military Service Registration Regulations. They state: "County, autonomous county, non-divisional city and municipal district people's governments' military service agencies shall, before 31 October each year, organise initial military service registration for male citizens who turn 18 by 31 December of that year." The same regulations state: "Initial military service registration is mainly conducted online, with citizens completing it themselves through the military service registration platform." The consequences of not registering are set out in Article 57, paragraph 1 of the Military Service Law. This paragraph covers "those who refuse or evade military service registration." The penalty is that "the county-level people's government orders correction within a set time; if not corrected by the deadline, the county-level people's government compels the person to fulfil military service obligations and imposes a fine." Article 15 of the Military Service Law sets the target for initial registration as "male citizens who turn 18 by 31 December each year." Article 20, paragraph 2 states that "where military needs require, women citizens may be conscripted for active service under the preceding provisions." Article 15 of the Registration Regulations states that "where military needs require ... qualified women citizens may be organised for reserve registration." The Military Service Law also states that "the period of active service for conscripts is two years." "Where military needs and personal willingness allow, citizens who are 17 but under 18 may be conscripted for active service." Non-commissioned officers "who serve 12 years of active service or meet other conditions set by the state shall be placed in employment by the county-level or higher people's government at their resettlement location." Those who "serve 30 years of active service, or reach age 55, or meet other conditions set by the state shall be retired." Placed in employment means the government finds you a job. Retirement placement means you leave service on a pension. The Conscription Regulations state that "priority shall be given to ordinary university graduates and recruits with special requirements for political, physical or professional skills." They also state that "where conscripts are full-time students or recent graduates, they may be conscripted before enrolment, at their household registration location or at their school location" (nationwide).

- Notes: China only: Registration and conscription are two separate matters, with very different costs. The rule barring you from leaving the country or studying for two years applies only to people who refuse conscription after being called up; see item 3 (refusing military service). The Military Service Law sets initial registration only for men. Women are conscripted as military needs require, and may also be registered for reserve service as needed. Soldiers who sit the military academy entrance exam, or good soldiers promoted to officer, have conditions set in internal army rules that cannot be found publicly. The Military Service Law says only two things: active-duty officers are selected from among "outstanding active-duty soldiers" and others, and military academy students are not subject to the conscription age limit. This book does not give the cut-off scores or quotas.

### 3. Refusing military service after being called up bars you from civil service and state jobs, and from leaving the country or studying for two years
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Nothing to pay. The cost is that you must decide whether you will go before you sign up.
- Plain language: If you sign up and then refuse to serve, and still refuse after being ordered to correct it, the consequences add up. The civil service cannot hire you, and state-owned enterprises and public institutions cannot hire you either. For two years you cannot leave the country, enrol in school or return to study. You will also be placed on the list of serious dishonest subjects for national defence obligations. So "sign up first and back out later if it does not work" is a very costly idea.
- Benefit: Article 57, paragraph 1, item 2 of the Military Service Law covers "conscripted citizens who refuse or evade conscription for active service." The penalty is the same: the county-level people's government orders correction within a set time. If it is not corrected by the deadline, the county-level government compels the person to fulfil service obligations and imposes a fine. Paragraph 2 of the same article states: "Where a person commits the act in item 2 of the preceding paragraph and still refuses to correct it, that person may not be recruited as a civil servant or as staff managed under the Civil Servants Law. They may not be recruited or employed by state-owned enterprises or public institutions. They may not leave the country or enrol or re-enrol in school for two years, and shall be placed on the list of serious dishonest subjects for national defence obligations for joint disciplinary measures." Joint disciplinary measures means several government departments use the same list and together enforce the restrictions above. Article 58 applies the same penalties to people who have already enlisted. If a serviceman evades service by refusing duties or leaving his unit, the outcome depends on the decision. Those removed from the military roll, expelled from military service, or criminally prosecuted are penalised under Article 57, paragraph 2. Those removed from the roll are also fined (nationwide, in force from 1 October 2021).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2021). 兵役法（2021 年修订，第五十七、五十八条）. <http://www.mod.gov.cn/regulatory/2021-08/20/content_4892491.htm>（国防部刊登）
- Notes: China only: These restrictions concern personal freedom and eligibility for work, not money. The penalties apply to only two groups: people who refuse or evade call-up for active service, and people who, after enlisting, are removed from the roll, expelled or prosecuted. Simply failing to register is not included; see item 2 for registration. The penalty depends on refusing to correct, and it is determined and imposed by the county-level government. Local governments set the fine amount; this book does not give it. Whether you go is your own decision, but make it before you sign up.

### 4. Students who serve get up to 20,000 yuan a year towards tuition, keep their place, and get a postgraduate quota
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Nothing to pay. After discharge, you apply through your school with the required documents. This takes one or two trips.
- Plain language: If you are a current student, a recent graduate or a new student and you join the army, the state pays for your tuition during those years. Undergraduate and junior-college students get up to 20,000 yuan a year. Postgraduates get up to 25,000 yuan. If your tuition is lower than that, you get only what you paid. Your student status is kept during service, and you can return to school within two years of discharge. There is also a postgraduate quota reserved only for discharged student soldiers.
- Benefit: A 2024 notice from the Ministry of Finance and three other departments says students who serve get three benefits: tuition compensation, repayment of the national student loan used for tuition, and tuition reduction. Repayment means the state pays off that student loan for you. The standards have been "adjusted to: undergraduate and junior-college students no more than 20,000 yuan per person per year, and postgraduates no more than 25,000 yuan per person per year." The new standards apply from the autumn semester of 2024. How much you receive is set by this sentence: "compensation is based on whichever is higher: the tuition actually paid by the student, or the national student loan received (including principal and all interest until it is fully repaid)." A further sentence reads: "any amount above the standard will not be compensated, repaid or reduced." So each year, first take the higher of your actual tuition and your student loan, then compare that with the cap and use the lower of the two. If your tuition is 5,000 yuan a year, you get 5,000 yuan. Years are counted by years of study, not years of service. The 2019 Measures on Management of Student Financial Assistance state: "Where the prescribed period of study has been completed before enlistment, that is the period of tuition compensation or student loan repayment. Where the remaining study period is completed after discharge and return to school, that is the period of tuition reduction." Recent graduates who enlist receive compensation for the full length of their programme. Student status is governed by the Veterans Protection Law: "Active-duty personnel who were admitted to, or were enrolled at, an ordinary higher education institution before enlistment keep their admission rights or student status during active service. Within two years of discharge they may enrol or re-enrol, and may transfer to another major at the same institution under national rules." The same law states: "Higher education institutions may, under national planning, recruit discharged veterans through separately allocated quotas or separate admissions." The postgraduate route is the "Discharged University Student Soldier Programme." The 2026 Ministry of Education notice states that places are "allocated separately within the total national master's enrolment quota and used for special purposes only, and may not be changed to ordinary or other special quotas. Institutions decide ... initial exam score requirements (generally not higher than the requirement for ordinary-quota candidates in the same discipline at that institution) ... among equally qualified candidates, those who served longer are admitted first." "Allocated separately, used for special purposes" means these places are set aside and may only be used to admit discharged student soldiers. In 2026, 532 ordinary higher education institutions are running this programme (nationwide).

### 5. After discharge, only six situations bring a government job; if you find your own, you must report within 30 days
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Nothing to pay. Bring your discharge certificate and visit the veterans' affairs office in your resettlement area.
- Plain language: The idea that military service guarantees a job is no longer the norm. Only six situations lead to a government-arranged job. Conscripts serve two years and are discharged, and most of them do not fall into those six. The route of finding your own job is called self-employment, and it has a firm deadline: you must report to your resettlement authority within 30 days of leaving active service. Without good reason, being more than 30 days late means you are treated as having waived your resettlement benefits.
- Benefit: Article 25 of the Veterans Resettlement Regulations lists six situations in which the government of the resettlement area arranges employment. The text reads: "(1) non-commissioned officers who have served 12 years of active service; (2) individuals who have received medals or honorary titles during active service; (3) individuals who received a third-class war merit or a second-class merit award or higher during active service; (4) individuals who received a first-level commendation during active service; (5) those disabled in combat and rated at disability levels 5 to 8; (6) children of martyrs." The regulations also state: "The people's government of the resettlement area shall complete the work of arranging employment for discharged non-commissioned officers and conscripts within 6 months of receiving them." Employers "shall sign a medium- or long-term employment contract or labour contract of at least 3 years with them in accordance with regulations. In the case of enterprises receiving non-commissioned officers with 10 or more years of military service, they shall sign an open-ended labour contract." An open-ended contract means the contract has no end date. While waiting for placement, "a living allowance shall be paid monthly at the local monthly minimum wage." If you are not placed in a job through no fault of your own, "living expenses shall be paid monthly at a rate not less than 80% of the average wage of comparable staff in the unit until you take up a post." Placement by employment does not apply to people expelled from the Communist Party of China or those who have received criminal punishment. The deadline for self-employment is in Article 51: "Discharged non-commissioned officers and conscripts who are self-employed shall report to the veterans' affairs department of the resettlement-area people's government within 30 days of the date they are approved for discharge. Failure to report within 30 days without good reason is deemed a waiver of resettlement benefits." (nationwide, in force from 1 September 2024)

For other resettlement methods, you report "within the prescribed time." When you report, you are issued a discharged veteran's preferential certificate, and the discharge payment, employment and start-up support, and education assistance all connect from there.

### 6. Three things to know after discharge: a one-off discharge payment, service counted as work seniority, and a tax cut for three years if you run your own business
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Nothing to pay. At discharge, the military transfers your social insurance records and money. You then complete the transfer at the social insurance office in your resettlement area.
- Plain language: After discharge, three sums matter. The first is a one-off discharge payment for people who find their own work. It is paid according to years of active service, with extra for those who have earned awards. The second is service counted as work seniority, where your years of contributions are combined with those before and after service. The third is tax: if you run your own business, for three years you can reduce your tax each year by up to 20,000 yuan, with the limit raisable by up to 20%.
- Benefit: The first sum is governed by the Veterans Resettlement Regulations. They state: "A one-off discharge payment shall be paid to discharged non-commissioned officers and conscripts who find their own jobs, according to their years of active service." Central government funds cover it: "the specific standard shall be determined by the national veterans' affairs authority together with the Political Work Department of the Central Military Commission and the national finance department, and adjusted from time to time." Second-class war merit, first-class merit or second-class commendation adds 15%. Third-class war merit or second-class merit adds 10%. Fourth-class war merit or third-class merit adds 5%. Treated as contribution years means that years you did not pay in are counted as if you had. Work seniority means years in employment. Service years and post-discharge years are added together. "The active service of discharged non-commissioned officers and conscripts is treated as basic-level work experience." The third sum is governed by Notice No. 14 of 2023 from the Ministry of Finance, the State Taxation Administration and the Ministry of Veterans Affairs. ### 11. Not working for an employer means flexible employment: you must enrol for pension and medical insurance yourself where you work, and the hukou restriction has now been lifted
<!-- tags: money=high time=low will=some gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: You must pay the full pension and medical contributions yourself, based on the local contribution base. A contribution base is the reference figure used to calculate premiums. Typically this is RMB 1,000-plus a month. It is the fixed cost most often overlooked in flexible employment. The difficulty is paying on time every month.
- Plain language: Not working for an employer does not mean you have no social insurance. It just means no one pays the employer's half for you. Documents require localities to lift the hukou (household registration) restriction on flexible workers enrolling where they work, and a few very large cities may phase it in. So do not treat "freedom" as meaning you can skip social insurance. If you stop paying, your pension and medical reimbursement are calculated under the rules for broken contributions.
- Benefit: Enrolment: see the Guiding Opinions on Protecting Labour Rights of Workers in New Forms of Employment. It states: "Localities shall lift the hukou restriction on flexible workers enrolling for basic pension and basic medical insurance where they work. Where a very large city cannot do this in one step, it should work within local conditions to lift it gradually. Flexible workers who have not joined the employee basic pension and basic medical insurance shall join the urban and rural resident basic pension and medical insurance as required, so that everyone who should be covered is covered. Companies shall be urged to pay social insurance as the law requires. Companies should guide and support new-form workers who do not fully meet the criteria for an employment relationship to join the appropriate social insurance according to their situation." The same guidance divides work arrangements into three types: "Where the criteria for an employment relationship are met, the company shall sign a labour contract with the worker according to law. Where the criteria are not fully met but the company manages the worker's labour, guide the company to sign a written agreement with the worker... Where individuals run their own business on a platform or work as freelancers, their rights and obligations are governed by civil law" (nationwide, from 2021).

### 14. Thinking of working abroad: first check whether the company holds a qualification to run overseas labour cooperation, because it may not charge you a deposit
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: No cost. Spend about fifteen minutes checking the company's qualifications before you leave, and read both contracts through.
- Plain language: The law regulates the company that sends you abroad, not you. Only a firm that holds an overseas labour cooperation business qualification certificate and has registered can do this work. If you hear "get a tourist visa first, then switch to a work visa later," that alone shows the other side is breaking the law. No company may charge you a deposit under any name, or ask you to put property up as security.
- Benefit: Article 5 of the Regulations on Overseas Labour Cooperation: engaging in overseas labour cooperation requires approval from the provincial or prefecture-level commerce authority and a business qualification. That qualification is the certificate that allows the organisation of people to work abroad. Without it, no one may be sent overseas to work. Article 7: the list of holders is reported to the national commerce authority and notified to Chinese embassies and consulates abroad. The article also states: "Without a lawfully obtained overseas labour cooperation business qualification certificate and registration, no one may engage in overseas labour cooperation." Article 8: "No entity or individual may organise labour personnel to work abroad under the name of commerce, tourism, or study." Article 9: within 5 working days of registration, the company must deposit at a designated bank a risk-handling reserve fund of not less than RMB 3 million, and the list of depositors is published. Article 10: the reserve pays service fees the company refuses or cannot pay, labour remuneration, and legally required compensation. It also covers "costs of returning workers home or providing emergency assistance when an emergency occurs." Article 11: workers may not be organised to go abroad for work related to gambling or pornography. Article 12: no departure without training. Article 13: the company must buy personal accident insurance for work abroad. Articles 21 and 23: the company must sign separate written contracts with the foreign employer and with each worker, and without them no one may depart. Article 25: "No deposit may be demanded from labour personnel under any name, nor may they be asked to provide property as security." Article 26: the contract must be filed with the commerce authority within 10 working days of signing. Article 29: if workers' actual rights differ from the contract, the company must help them claim compensation, and if they do not receive what they are owed, workers may hold the company liable. Article 31: if the safety situation in a country or region cannot guarantee personal safety, the company may not send workers there. Article 37: the commerce authority keeps a bad-credit record and public notice system. Penalties come in later articles. Article 39: anyone without qualification to do overseas labour cooperation is investigated and shut down, and criminally prosecuted if the conduct is a crime. Article 40: organising departure under commerce, tourism or study names, lending out a qualification, or organising work related to gambling or pornography results in revocation of the qualification certificate. Article 43: failing to sign contracts, or concealing or falsifying information to organise departure, is fined RMB 100,000 to 200,000, and the person in charge is fined RMB 20,000 to 50,000 (nationwide).

### 15. Working remotely from home for an overseas company: for tax, the income counts as domestic income; you file the personal income tax yourself, and the money comes in under "employee remuneration"

- Plain language: Whether income counts as domestic or foreign depends on where you do the work, not on where the money is paid from. - Benefit: Whether income is domestic depends on Article 3 of the Implementing Regulations of the Individual Income Tax Law (State Council Decree No. 707, effective 1 January 2019). Wage and salary income is "income from wages, salaries, bonuses, year-end pay rises, labour dividends, allowances, subsidies and other income related to employment or engagement." Remuneration for services is "income from services, including design, decoration, installation, drafting, testing, medical care, legal, accounting, consulting, lecturing, translation, copy-editing, calligraphy and painting, carving, film and television, sound and video recording, performance, advertising, exhibition, technical, introduction, brokerage and agency services, and other services." The 20% deduction applies only to these three items. Wage and salary income is not included, so it counts at the full amount, and the two categories produce different taxes. Article 9: "Individual income tax is paid by the taxpayer, and the unit or individual paying the income is the withholding agent." Article 10: "Taxpayers must file returns in the following cases: ... (2) received taxable income with no withholding agent; ... (4) received income from overseas." Article 11: "Resident individuals with comprehensive income calculate individual income tax on an annual basis... where an annual reconciliation is required, it must be completed between 1 March and 30 June of the following year." Article 9: "Annual total quota management applies to individuals' sale and purchase of foreign exchange. Within the annual total, banks handle transactions on presentation of a valid ID. ### 16. Working for yourself: start with a subsidised entrepreneurship guarantee loan, up to RMB 300,000 per person, with the government covering half the interest

The online claim that "paying social insurance means you are no longer a fresh graduate" reflects some exams' interpretation, not a nationwide rule. The Fujian and Chongqing examples above are just examples, and each province may change its rules every year, so use the notice for the year you sit. Those who have completed grassroots service programmes such as "Three Support, One Assist" may still apply for fresh-graduate posts within two years of finishing; see item 7 of this section (grassroots service programmes). The same guide states that this exam does not designate study materials, and no organisation or individual may run training classes for it. Article 4 of the Civil Servant Recruitment Regulations states that civil servants are recruited by "open examination, strict assessment, equal competition and selection of the best." So anyone who takes money and promises "internal quotas" or "guaranteed placement" is running a scam; report them to the police, see section 8, item 3 (anti-fraud hard rules). The national-exam rules here follow the 2027 notice as of October 2026; check the notice for the year you sit each time.
