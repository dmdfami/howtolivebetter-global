# 12. Starting a business: do not lose your family's savings

This section counts only money and legal liability. It does not convert them into years of life. When you open a shop or set up a company, which steps could cost you your own money, your family's money or your spouse's money? What does the law say, and can you separate them in advance? Claims based only on experience, with no literature behind them, are rated grade C.

The entries in this section are grouped by theme below. Item numbers are in brackets.

**Keep your savings out of it before opening**: start only with money you can afford to lose (item 1), do not sign a personal guarantee for a company loan (item 2), choose the right legal form before opening (item 3), do not be a nominee shareholder (item 4), check the filing before joining a franchise (item 5), ask the right questions before paying anyone to run your online store for you (item 24), raise money for a business only from relatives, friends and employees (item 26), and put a partnership in writing (item 28).

**Registration and licences**: settle the name and business scope before registering (item 6), do not open before your licence is granted (item 7), and before renting a shop, check that the premises may be used for your business (item 27).

**Food**: first work out which tier your food business falls into (item 8), labels on pre-packaged food (item 9), ordinary food may not claim to cure illness (item 10), and the criminal threshold for food offences (item 11).

**Tax, annual reports and invoices**: even with no income, file on time and truthfully (item 12), issue invoices only for real transactions (item 13), anyone who claims to be the tax bureau and presses you to pay is a scammer (item 14), and report the previous year's annual report online in the first half of each year (item 25).

**Contracts and staff**: when you take money, the contract should use the legal term for a deposit with penalty effect (pinyin *dìngjīn*), not a plain advance payment (same pinyin, different character) (item 15). Sign employment contracts within the first month of hiring (item 16), pay wages as the contract and the law require (item 17), and follow the operating procedures on the job (item 22).

**Products and purchasing**: sell first, then make (item 18), check samples against the mass-production checklist (item 19), keep receipts and supplier details for purchases (item 20), and either design artwork yourself or buy a licence to use it (item 21).

**Exit**: when a business loses money, exit through the proper procedure (item 23).

### 1. Start a business only with money you can afford to lose; do not touch your savings or borrow to open
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Nothing in cash. Before you start, set a figure for yourself: if this money is lost in full, you must still be able to pay the mortgage, support your children and cover medical care. Do not invest above that figure. Money from family members and borrowed money do not count against this figure. The hard part is when the business looks like it is picking up: resist the urge to keep pouring money in.
- Plain language: If you run a sole proprietorship, its debts are paid from your own assets. If it is unclear whether the family runs it, family assets can be used too. In a company, creditors can demand early payment of shares you promised but have not yet paid in. So size your investment so that you could lose it all and still pay the mortgage and medical bills.
- Benefit: Losses are not only lost money. The debts of a sole proprietorship run by you alone must be paid from your own personal assets. If the family runs it together, or it is unclear who runs it, the family's assets must pay. A debt both spouses signed for is a joint debt that both repay. A debt that one spouse agreed to afterwards also counts. A debt one spouse took out in his or her own name is still repaid by both if the creditor can prove the money went into a business the couple ran together. A company has a similar rule. The capital a shareholder promised in the registered capital is called subscribed capital. If the company cannot repay a matured debt, creditors can demand that any part of that subscribed capital not yet due be paid in early (nationwide).
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第五十六、一千零六十四条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>；全国人大常委会 (2023). 公司法（2023 年修订，第四十七、五十四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/yaowen/liebiao/202312/content_6923395.htm>
- Notes: China only: "Within your limit" means that losing this money does not force you to sell your home or borrow from relatives to plug the gap. Official statistics on how many businesses survive, and for how long, could not be verified on the websites of the National Bureau of Statistics or the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), so this entry gives no such figures. It only sets out the legal consequences, and is rated B.

### 2. Do not sign a personal guarantee for a company loan; your spouse should not sign either
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Nothing in cash. When a bank, supplier or landlord asks you to sign a "shareholder joint and several guarantee" or a "spouse signature", stop and think. Signing that page means giving up the protection that a company's debts do not reach you personally (giving up limited liability). Do not sign if you can avoid it. If you must sign, sign only a "general guarantee", and write down the maximum amount you guarantee. The hard part is when the other side says "no signature, no loan": you have to hold your ground.
- Plain language: A "joint and several guarantee" means creditors can come to you directly if the company does not pay. A "general guarantee" means they must first pursue the company and sell its assets. If your spouse also signs, both family estates are at risk, and the company's "limited" status no longer protects you personally.
- Benefit: The guarantee letter means "if the borrower does not pay, you pay". With a joint and several guarantee, the creditor can skip the company and come to you directly. With a general guarantee, the creditor must first sue the company or go to arbitration, and ask a court to force the sale of company assets to settle the debt. Only the shortfall left after that falls to you. A debt both spouses signed for is a joint debt that both repay. A debt one spouse agreed to afterwards also counts. Once a spouse signs, both family estates are put at risk together (nationwide, since 2021).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第六百八十一、六百八十七、六百八十八、一千零六十四条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>
- Notes: China only: From the author's experience, banks lending to small businesses often require shareholders to sign personal joint guarantees. This book found no official statistics on it. A loan can still be obtained, but make sure you know exactly what the page you are signing commits you to before you sign. Keep the guarantee amount within the "can afford to lose" figure in item 1 of this section. The general rules on IOUs and guarantee letters are in item 18 of section 8.

### 3. Choose the right legal form before opening: sole proprietors and partners have unlimited liability; only a limited company is "limited"
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Registration is done online through your local government service website and takes a few hours. Once you have a limited company, you must keep accounts and file tax returns every month. This is an ongoing cost: doing it yourself takes time, hiring someone to keep accounts costs money, and prices vary by area.
- Plain language: Sole proprietors and general partners owe what they owe, with no limit. A limited company shareholder loses only the amount they registered to contribute. But you must pay that full amount in within 5 years, so a large registered amount can mean owing all of it. Mixing company and personal accounts, or using the company to dodge debts, can cancel the protection.
- Benefit: The debts of a sole proprietorship are paid from personal assets, or from the family's assets. A general partner in a general partnership bears unlimited joint liability for the partnership's debts, meaning owing everything until it is paid off. Creditors can pursue any one partner for the full amount. A limited partnership is different: its limited partners are liable only up to the capital they committed (limited to subscribed capital). A shareholder of a limited liability company is likewise liable only up to the amount they committed (limited to subscribed capital). But the limit is lost in three situations. First, the committed capital must be fully paid in within five years of the company's formation. If the company cannot repay a matured debt, creditors can demand early payment. Second, capital that was paid in and then withdrawn (withdrawal of capital) must be returned. Third, if the company is used as a shell to evade debts and seriously harms creditors, the shareholder must share the company's debts jointly. A single-shareholder company is held to a stricter standard: the sole shareholder must prove that the company's property is independent of his or her own. If the shareholder cannot prove this, he or she must share the company's debts jointly. Courts mainly check whether money and accounts were kept separate. Using company money freely without records, or mixing company and personal books, both count as commingling (nationwide, since July 2024).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第五十六条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>；全国人大常委会 (2006). 合伙企业法（2006 年修订，第二条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2006/content_413955.htm>；全国人大常委会 (2023). 公司法（2023 年修订，第四、二十三、四十七、五十三、五十四条；第二十三条第三款：「只有一个股东的公司，股东不能证明公司财产独立于股东自己的财产的，应当对公司债务承担连带责任。」）. <https://www.gov.cn/yaowen/liebiao/202312/content_6923395.htm>；最高人民法院 (2019). 全国法院民商事审判工作会议纪要（第 10 点「人格混同」）：「最主要的表现是公司的财产与股东的财产是否混同且无法区分」「公司账簿与股东账簿不分，致使公司财产与股东财产无法区分的」. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/199691.html>
- Notes: China only: Keep company and personal accounts separate. Do not receive customers' payments on a personal card, do not pay household expenses from company money, and keep complete records. Do not register an inflated capital figure just to look impressive.

### 4. Do not be a nominee shareholder; do not hold shares on someone else's behalf
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Nothing in cash. If someone says "lend me your ID to be a shareholder" or "the shares will be in your name for now", refuse every time.
- Plain language: Once your name is on the register, the law treats you as a shareholder. You must pay in the capital you promised on time, and if you do not, you may have to compensate the company for its losses. If another founding shareholder never paid in fully, you may have to cover that gap too. The business may belong to someone else, but the debts sit in your name.
- Benefit: A person on the register is a shareholder in law. You must pay in the full amount of capital you promised, by the deadline set in the company's articles (paying in the subscribed capital in full). If you do not pay on time, you must make it up and may have to compensate the company for losses. If a founding shareholder did not actually pay in capital, the other founding shareholders share liability for the gap (joint liability). If the company cannot repay a matured debt, creditors can demand early payment of any capital not yet due. The person who really runs the business is not on the register, yet the debts are recorded in your name (nationwide, since July 2024).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2023). 公司法（2023 年修订，第四十七、四十九、五十、五十四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/yaowen/liebiao/202312/content_6923395.htm>；最高人民法院 (2020 修正). 关于适用《中华人民共和国公司法》若干问题的规定（三）（法释〔2011〕3 号，2014 年法释〔2014〕2 号、2020 年法释〔2020〕18 号两次修正，第二十四、二十五条）：「如无法律规定的无效情形，人民法院应当认定该合同有效」「实际出资人未经公司其他股东半数以上同意，请求公司变更股东……人民法院不予支持」「名义股东处分股权造成实际出资人损失，实际出资人请求名义股东承担赔偿责任的，人民法院应予支持」. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff808181799def980179ac07a9ca117c>（国家法律法规数据库）；最高人民法院 (2025). 关于适用《中华人民共和国公司法》若干问题的解释（征求意见稿）第九十条. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/477881.html>
- Notes: China only: Between a nominee shareholder and the real investor, a holding agreement is valid unless it falls into an invalid category. But if the real investor wants their own name to replace the nominee's, more than half of the other shareholders must agree. If the nominee sells or pledges the shares, courts apply the Civil Code rules on good-faith acquisition. If the buyer did not know, paid a fair price and has completed registration, the real investor cannot get the shares back and can only claim compensation from the nominee. In September 2025 the Supreme People's Court published a draft of a new interpretation. Once a final version takes effect, the whole 2020 provision will be repealed. As of September 2026, the 2020 amended version is still in force, and this entry follows it. The risks of being a nominee legal representative are in item 28 of section 8.

### 5. Before joining a franchise, check the Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) filing, get the written disclosure, and write the cooling-off period into the contract
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Nothing in cash. Go to the Ministry of Commerce's "Commercial Franchise Information Management System" website and check whether the brand owner has filed. Then ask the brand owner to give you the required information in writing before you sign, together with the contract text. This takes a few hours.
- Plain language: The brand owner must first run at least 2 of its own stores for over a year. It must file with the commerce authority within 15 days of signing. Before you sign, it must give you a written disclosure of 12 items, covering fees, deposit refunds, and how its existing stores perform. If there is no filing, it refuses to disclose, or it wants payment before you can see the contract, walk away.
- Benefit: The rules require the brand owner (called the franchisor) to have at least two stores that it owns and runs itself, and to have operated them for more than one year. Within 15 days of the first franchise contract being signed, it must file with the commerce authority. A brand that does not file is fined RMB 10,000 to RMB 50,000. If it misses the deadline, it is fined RMB 50,000 to RMB 100,000 and the failure is publicly announced. Before you sign, it must give you the required information in writing: 12 items in total. These include the types of franchise fee and how much each costs, when the deposit is refunded, and how many franchised stores exist and how well they do. They also include a summary of the audit report for the past two years and a summary of lawsuits and arbitrations in the past five years. The contract must also state that the franchisee may terminate it unilaterally within a set period after signing. If the brand owner hides information or gives false information, the franchisee may cancel the contract (nationwide, since May 2007).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2007). 商业特许经营管理条例（国务院令第 485 号，第七、八、十二、二十二、二十三、二十五条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-03/28/content_4179.htm>；商务部 (2012). 商业特许经营信息披露管理办法（商务部令 2012 年第 2 号，第五、九条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2012/content_2177025.htm>；商务部. 商业特许经营信息管理系统. <https://txjy.syggs.mofcom.gov.cn/>
- Notes: China only: If there is no filing, the brand will not show the disclosure documents, or it wants payment before you see the contract, walk away. The "cooling-off period" is the few days after signing during which you can withdraw unilaterally. The regulation only says "a certain period", so the number of days is set by the contract, and you should insist that it is written in. When the brand discloses "average sales, gross profit and net profit of existing franchised stores", ask where the figures come from, since the measures require the brand owner to explain the source. For schemes that avoid the word franchise and only charge "outsourced operation" or "store-opening training" fees, see item 24 of this section (outsourced operation and store-opening training).

### 6. Before registering, settle the name, premises, business scope and registered capital; once the documents are complete, the licence is issued on the spot
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Registration itself costs nothing. Registration fees for companies and sole proprietorships have been abolished since 1 January 2015. You can apply online through your local government service website or at a government service hall, which takes a few hours. Engraving a company seal, hiring someone to keep accounts, and renting a registered address (address hosting) are market services you buy, not government fees.
- Plain language: Registration is free, and the fee was abolished in 2015. You need five documents: an application form, proof of identity or qualification, proof of premises, the articles or partnership agreement, and any other documents the rules require. If the set is complete, the licence is issued on the spot. Otherwise it is issued within 3 working days, or 3 more for complex cases.
- Benefit: The regulation fixes five required documents: (1) an application form; (2) documents proving the applicant's qualification and the natural person's identity; (3) documents on the registered address or main business premises; (4) the articles of association of a company, non-company enterprise legal person or farmers' professional cooperative (or union), or the partnership agreement of a partnership; (5) any other documents required by laws, administrative regulations and rules of the State Administration for Market Regulation. In plain terms: an application, proof of who you are or your right to do this, proof that you can use the premises, and the articles or partnership agreement. Anything else named by law must also be submitted. A company must also submit appointment documents and ID for its legal representative, directors, supervisors and senior managers. A sole proprietor does not submit articles. The name is "independently declared by the applicant under the law": you choose it and declare it yourself. The registered capital follows a subscribed registration system: you state the amount you agree to contribute, without paying it in on the spot. The business scope is chosen from the categories published by the registration authority. If the documents are complete and in the right format, the authority "confirms and registers on the spot". If it cannot be completed on the spot, it must be finished within 3 working days, or 3 more for complex cases. The date printed on the business licence is the date the market entity is established: it is the date your company or shop legally came into being (nationwide, since March 2022).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2021). 市场主体登记管理条例（国务院令第 746 号，第十、十三、十四、十六、十九、二十一条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2021/content_5636139.htm> ; 市场监管总局 (2022). 市场主体登记管理条例实施细则（市场监管总局令第 52 号，第十二、二十五、二十六条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-03/02/content_5676403.htm> ; 财政部、国家发展改革委 (2014). 关于取消、停征和免征一批行政事业性收费的通知（财税〔2014〕101 号，附件 1）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/2014-12/23/content_5023513.htm> ; 国务院办公厅 (2018). 关于进一步压缩企业开办时间的意见（国办发〔2018〕32 号，第二部分第三项）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-05/17/content_5291643.htm>
- Notes: China only: Registration itself is free. Anyone who charges you is charging for agency or seal-engraving services, and the market sets those prices. A 2018 State Council General Office document states that "applicants independently choose the company that makes the official seal" and that "designating a seal-making company is strictly prohibited". So you need not accept anyone telling you that you "must engrave at a designated place". The registered capital does not change how the business looks; it only sets the cap on your liability, and it must be fully paid in within 5 years (see item 3 of this section). If the business scope includes licensed items, you cannot open until the licence is granted (see item 7 of this section).

### 7. Licensed trades: do not open until the licence is granted
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Nothing in cash. Before you start, check whether the business you plan needs a licence. Catering and food sales need a food business licence. If you only sell pre-packaged food made by others, no licence is needed, but you must file instead. Open the doors only once the licence is in hand.
- Plain language: If you open without a licence, the agency that issues it can penalise you under its rules. Without a business licence and with no specific penalty in law, you may be ordered to stop, have your earnings confiscated, and be fined up to RMB 10,000. Selling monopoly goods, or doing securities, futures, insurance or payment settlement, can be the crime of illegal business operation, with up to 5 years in prison for serious cases.
- Benefit: If you run a business without the required permission, the department designated by law or regulation investigates. If you trade without a business licence and the law sets no specific penalty, you must stop, have your illegal income confiscated, and pay a fine of up to RMB 10,000. There is also a heavier tier: trading in goods that the law makes exclusive, monopoly or restricted without permission, or doing securities, futures, insurance or capital payment and settlement without approval. In these two situations, serious cases are the crime of illegal business operation. The penalty is up to 5 years in prison or criminal detention (short-term custody), plus a fine of 1 to 5 times the income you earned. Especially serious cases carry more than 5 years (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2017). 无证无照经营查处办法（国务院令第 684 号，第五、六、十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2017-08/23/content_5219861.htm>；市场监管总局 (2023). 食品经营许可和备案管理办法（市场监管总局令第 78 号，第四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2023/issue_10606/202307/content_6894763.html>；全国人大 (1997). 刑法（第二百二十五条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>
- Notes: China only: The licence conditions for finance, healthcare and education-training are each governed by separate laws. This entry only verifies the general enforcement rules and the criminal code provision. Milk tea shops and snack shops count as catering services and need a food business licence. Renovating before applying for the licence, or trading before it is issued, are common pitfalls. For which food categories need which licence, and whether selling fresh meat and vegetables needs one, see item 8 of this section.

### 8. For food, first work out your tier: making and catering need a licence; selling only pre-packaged food means filing; fresh meat and produce need no licence
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: The licence itself is free. But premises and facilities must meet the standards, and the on-site inspection plus the application can take several weeks. If you sell meat, you must also keep the quarantine certificate and the meat-quality inspection certificate for each batch.
- Plain language: Making food to sell, such as snacks, marinated food, baked goods or homemade pickles, needs a licence. Small workshops and stalls follow provincial rules. Selling only standard pre-packaged food needs a filing, not a licence. Fresh meat and vegetables need no licence. Selling without a required licence can bring a fine of RMB 50,000 to RMB 100,000, even when the goods are worth under RMB 10,000.
- Benefit: Under the Food Safety Law, you must first obtain a licence to do food business. Producing food, selling food and providing catering services all require a licence. "However, selling edible agricultural products and selling only pre-packaged food do not require a licence. Those selling only pre-packaged food must file with the food safety supervision department of the local people's government at county level or above." Edible agricultural products are unprocessed items such as fresh meat and vegetables. Pre-packaged food is judged by the applicable standard. You cannot judge it only by whether someone else made it or whether it is wrapped. The specific definition is in item 9 of this section. "Detailed management measures for small food workshops and food stalls are formulated by provinces, autonomous regions and municipalities directly under the central government." These two categories have local rules. If you produce or sell food without a licence, your earnings, the food, and the tools, equipment and raw materials are all confiscated. If the total sale value of the batch (goods value) is under RMB 10,000, you also pay a fine of RMB 50,000 or more but no more than RMB 100,000. If the goods value is RMB 10,000 or more, the fine is 10 to 20 times the goods value. Pig slaughter regulations: pigs may be slaughtered only at a state-designated slaughterhouse, with official centralised quarantine. "Except for individuals in rural areas slaughtering for their own household consumption, which are not subject to designated slaughter, no unit or individual may engage in pig slaughter without designation." "Pig products produced by units and individuals who sell pig products or process meat, and by catering operators and centralised dining units, must come from designated pig slaughterhouses (sites) and have passed quarantine and meat-quality inspection." So the meat you sell and use must come from a designated slaughterhouse and must pass both quarantine and meat-quality inspection. Slaughtering pigs privately at a non-designated site means the operation is shut down, and the pigs, tools, equipment and earnings are all confiscated. The fine is the same as the no-licence tier above. Animal Epidemic Prevention Law: animal products sold or transported must carry the quarantine certificate and quarantine mark. If not, you are ordered to correct it, and may be fined up to one times the goods value of comparable qualified goods. The judicial interpretation covers punitive damages, meaning compensation beyond the refund. For small workshops and food stalls, if the food they make or sell does not meet food safety standards, buyers may demand tenfold compensation, and courts should support it (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2025 年第三次修正). 食品安全法（第三十五、三十六、一百二十二条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=7b5a76d0461745a08d3f964916b87ef3>；国务院 (2021). 生猪屠宰管理条例（国务院令第 742 号，第二、二十三、三十一条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-07/22/content_5626534.htm>；全国人大常委会 (2021 修订). 动物防疫法（第五十一、一百条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff8080817703add2017737395a973e31>；最高人民法院 (2024). 关于审理食品药品惩罚性赔偿纠纷案件适用法律若干问题的解释（法释〔2024〕9 号，第四条）. <http://gongbao.court.gov.cn/Details/bf531a70471f10d377788f28d3d8f8.html>
- Notes: China only: The thresholds for small workshops, small catering businesses and food stalls are set by each province, so this entry gives no figures here. Check your province's market regulator website and search for the food small workshop, small catering and food stall management regulations to find the local version. Selling homemade food online does not reduce the licence requirement just because it is "online". If a licence is required, you still need it. For your legal entity and business registration, see item 6 of this section. For other trades that need a licence, see item 7 of this section. For how much compensation buyers can claim, see item 31 of section 5 (tenfold compensation for food).

### 9. Before selling pre-packaged food, check the label: production date, shelf life and ingredient list must all be shown correctly
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: A label costs a few fen to a few jiao each (tenths of a yuan). Each batch needs the dates and ingredients entered correctly, which takes some time.
- Plain language: Whether food counts as pre-packaged depends on whether it is pre-measured and labelled with a uniform weight or volume, not on whether it has a bag. Food weighed and bagged when you buy it, made to order, or sold as takeaway in a box does not count just because it has a bag or box. If a pre-packaged food is missing required labelling and its goods are worth under RMB 10,000, the fine is RMB 5,000 to RMB 50,000. Loose food must also show its name and date.

### 11. The food trade has a criminal line: selling diseased meat or goods over the limit is enough for a crime; adding toxic or harmful substances is judged regardless of sales value, starting at up to five years
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Do what item 8 describes (first work out which tier your food business falls into). Keep quarantine certificates and purchase records, and use only ingredients whose approval numbers you can check.
- Plain language: The earlier items punish money; this one targets people. Selling diseased meat, meat that fails inspection, or goods with severe pesticide, veterinary drug or heavy metal residues carries up to 3 years. It is enough that the goods are sufficient to cause serious poisoning; no one needs to have actually been poisoned. Adding toxic or harmful non-food ingredients to food starts at up to 5 years, regardless of how much you sold.
- Benefit: Article 143 of the Criminal Law covers this tier. The food you make or sell does not meet food safety standards. It is enough that it is "sufficient to cause a serious food poisoning incident or other serious foodborne disease." The penalty is up to 3 years' fixed-term imprisonment or criminal detention (short-term custody), plus a fine. If the health harm is severe, or there are other serious circumstances, the penalty is 3 to 7 years' imprisonment plus a fine. If the consequences are especially serious, the penalty is 7 years to life imprisonment, plus a fine or confiscation of all personal property. Article 144 covers adulteration. Adding toxic or harmful non-food ingredients to food you make or sell, or selling it knowing it was adulterated, carries up to 5 years' imprisonment and a fine. If the health harm is severe, or there are other serious circumstances, the penalty is 5 to 10 years' imprisonment and a fine. If someone dies, or there are other especially serious circumstances, Article 141 applies (10 years or more, life imprisonment, or death). The Supreme People's Court and Supreme People's Procuratorate issued an interpretation (the "two supreme courts" interpretation) that lists the situations counting as "sufficient to cause a serious food poisoning incident or other serious foodborne disease." First, goods containing pathogenic microorganisms, pesticide residues, veterinary drug residues, biotoxins, heavy metals or other contaminants that seriously exceed the standard limits. Second, meat and meat products from diseased animals, animals that died of unknown causes, or livestock, poultry, animals or aquatic animals that fail inspection or quarantine. Third, goods that the state has expressly banned from production or sale for special needs such as disease control. Fourth, special medical-purpose formulas and staple foods for infants and young children whose nutritional content seriously fails food safety standards. Misusing additives, overdosing, or using them in the wrong place, to the point where it causes the consequences above, is also convicted under Article 143. What counts as a "toxic or harmful non-food ingredient" is not for you to decide. One category is substances that laws and regulations forbid adding to food. Another is substances on lists published by the relevant State Council departments, such as banned pesticides or drugs banned for use on food animals. ### 15. Write "earnest money," not "deposit"; state the penalty amount in the contract; treat payment terms like a loan
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. Write "earnest money" in the contract, and keep it no more than 20% of the total contract value. Collect payment before shipping. Before you agree to let a buyer take goods first and pay later (credit terms), check the buyer thoroughly.
- Plain language: Only the words "earnest money" trigger the double-return rule. If you breach, you cannot get that money back. If the other side breaches, they must refund it double. Any part above 20% of the contract value does not count. Writing "deposit" gives you none of these rules under the law. Offering credit terms means lending your own money to the buyer interest-free. If the buyer fails, that becomes a bad debt.
- Benefit: Earnest money takes effect only once the money has actually been handed over. If the party that paid the earnest money breaches, the money is not returned. If the party that received it breaches, it must return the amount and pay the same amount again (double return). The part above 20% of the total contract value (the subject-matter amount of the main contract) is not treated as earnest money. If an agreed penalty is far higher or far lower than the actual loss, the court can adjust it downward or upward at one party's request. If a contract includes both a penalty and earnest money, a breach lets you choose one of the two, not both (nationwide, since 2021).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第五百八十五、五百八十六、五百八十七、五百八十八条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>
- Notes: China only: In the Civil Code, "deposit" does not carry the earnest-money rules; it is generally treated as a prepayment. This is common practice; this section has not verified case law. Buying a new home and paying a subscription deposit, followed by no formal contract being signed, has special rules. See section 15, item 11 (subscription deposits). Credit terms are, in effect, an interest-free loan of your money to the other party. If they fail, you have a bad debt. Before giving credit terms, check the other party's record on the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System to see whether it has been sued.

### 16. Sign a written contract with each employee within the first month; register for social insurance within 30 days
<!-- tags: money=high time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: The employer's share of social insurance must be paid ongoing, and the rate varies by region. Signing the contract costs nothing; use the standard template from your local human resources and social security department.
- Plain language: If an employee has worked a month without a signed written contract, you must pay double wages from the second month until the first anniversary. Social insurance must be registered within 30 days of starting work. If you do not register and do not correct it by the deadline, the fine is 1 to 3 times the contributions owed. The law has no "employee voluntarily waives social insurance" option.
- Benefit: If an employee has worked more than one month but less than a year without a signed written contract, you must pay double wages for each month. Social insurance registration must be done within 30 days from the first day of work. If you do not do it, you are first ordered to correct it. After the deadline passes, a fine of 1 to 3 times the social insurance contributions owed applies, and the person responsible for the matter faces an extra fine of RMB 500 to 3,000. If contributions are late or short, a late fee of 0.05% per day applies from the day the arrears began. If you still do not pay after the deadline, a further fine of 1 to 3 times the arrears applies. The employer must declare and pay the full amount on time. Only legal grounds that cannot be resisted, such as natural disasters, allow late or reduced payment. There is no "employee voluntarily waives" option (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2007). 劳动合同法（第十、八十二条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2007/content_711013.htm>；全国人大常委会 (2010). 社会保险法（2018 年修正，第五十八、六十、八十四、八十六条）. <https://www.mohrss.gov.cn/xxgk2020/fdzdgknr/zcfg/fl/202011/t20201102_394629.html>
- Notes: China only: Double wages run from the second month until the first anniversary, so the cost grows the longer you wait. An employee with no contract and no social insurance who is injured at work, or who leaves and goes to arbitration, means you must make up contributions, pay fines and add compensation. The total often far exceeds what you saved. For how employees claim double wages, see section 19, item 20 (double wages).

### 17. Pay wages as the contract and the law require; put raises, loans and bonuses in writing
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. Spell out wages in the contract. For raises, bonuses, advances and loans, always sign and keep one paper record. Make no verbal promises, and do not pay the company's wages out of your own pocket. The hard part is applying the same rule when a friend or relative asks.
- Plain language: Wages must be paid on time and in full, as the contract and the law require. If wages are withheld, the employee can apply directly to the court for a payment order. Raises, bonuses and advances agreed only verbally are a problem: when a dispute arises, the side without evidence loses. If you lend your own money to an employee, the law does not require a written contract. Even so, write an IOU stating the amount, the interest and the repayment date.
- Benefit: A labour contract must state how wages are paid and how social insurance is contributed. The employer must pay wages in full and on time, as the contract and national rules require. If the employer withholds pay, the employee can apply directly to the court for a payment order (a court document demanding payment). If a boss lends personal money to an employee, that is a loan contract. The Civil Code states: "A loan contract shall be in written form, unless the loan between natural persons has otherwise been agreed." A loan from an individual boss to an employee is between two natural persons, so the law does not require writing. A contract "generally includes the type of loan, currency, purpose, amount, interest rate, term and repayment method." Writing an IOU with these items is the author's suggestion. A bonus promised verbally leaves no paper trail; when a dispute arises, the side without evidence loses (nationwide).
- Evidence level: C
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2007). 劳动合同法（第十七、三十条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2007/content_711013.htm>；全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第四百六十九、六百六十八条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>
- Notes: China only: This is not about refusing to help people. It is about running help through proper procedures too. A favour that becomes a habit causes disputes when you want to stop it. If private loans are mixed with company accounts, a bad debt lands on you personally when the company loses money. The legal provisions have been verified; the recommendations are experience, hence level C.

### 18. Sell first, then build: validate demand with pre-orders and small orders before investing in production
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=some gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: A few dozen to a few hundred RMB to make a sample or a single page. Then find a few dozen real customers and ask them to pay in advance or place small orders. This is a few weeks slower than starting production straight away. The hard part is resisting the urge to build the product first.
- Plain language: An Italian study found that founders who set hypotheses first, then test them against market feedback, did better business. They were also more likely to change direction in time, and did not give up earlier. So if no one will pay in advance or place a small order, do not trust the idea that the product will sell once it is made.
- Benefit: A randomized controlled trial followed 116 Italian start-ups for about a year. A randomized controlled trial randomly splits people into two groups, gives training to only one, and compares the results. The result: founders who were trained, and who made decisions by "stating hypotheses first, then rigorously testing them against market feedback," performed better. They were also more likely to pivot to other plans in time, and the share who gave up early was not higher. Projects that look promising but cannot succeed, the author calls false-positive projects. The author explains that this approach reduces the chance of continuing to pour money into such projects (Italy, published 2020).
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Camuffo A, Cordova A, Gambardella A, Spina C (2020). A Scientific Approach to Entrepreneurial Decision Making: Evidence from a Randomized Control Trial. Management Science 66(2):564-586. <https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2018.3249>
- Notes: Only this one study exists, its sample is small, and it was conducted in Italy, hence level B. The title, citation and abstract were checked against Crossref and Semantic Scholar; the effect figures in the full text were not verified for this item. In practice: if no one will pay in advance or place a small order, do not believe that the product will sell once it is made.

### 19. Before production, run the sample through a mass-production checklist, then start
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=some gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: A few hundred RMB for a small trial run. Then a few hours to work out the yield: how many out of 100 you can sell. Also itemise the supplier's minimum order quantity and payment terms, how much money the stock will tie up, packaging and logistics, certification and licences, and trademarks. The hard part is resisting the urge to skip this and start production.
- Plain language: The difference between one sample and a thousand units is where the money is tied up. Yield sets the real cost. The minimum order quantity and payment terms set how much you must front. Stock sets when the money comes back. If the product is on the compulsory certification catalogue, it may not be shipped or sold without certification and the certification mark.
- Benefit: The difference between one sample and a thousand units is where the money is tied up. Yield is how many out of a batch are qualified and can be sold. The supplier's minimum order quantity is the least you must order at once, and the payment terms are how long after delivery you get paid. Together they decide how much money you must front. Stock decides when the money comes back. Products on the compulsory product certification catalogue must pass certification and carry the certification mark before they can be shipped and sold. Shipping and selling without certification is punished under the Regulations on Certification and Accreditation. Running a restaurant or selling food requires a food business licence (nationwide).
- Evidence level: C
- Sources: 质检总局 (2009). 强制性产品认证管理规定（质检总局令第 117 号，第二、四十九条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2010/content_1533513.htm>；市场监管总局 (2023). 食品经营许可和备案管理办法（市场监管总局令第 78 号，第四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2023/issue_10606/202307/content_6894763.html>
- Notes: China only: The checklist itself is an experience-based method; the legal provisions were verified against the original text. Before production, search the China National Intellectual Property Administration trademark database for similar registrations, then apply for your own trademark. For the red lines on using other people's trademarks and designs, see items 20 and 21 (keep receipts for purchases; create your own designs or buy licences). The compulsory certification catalogue follows the latest list published by the State Administration for Market Regulation.

### 20. Keep invoices and supplier details for every purchase, and refuse goods priced far below market: if staff buy fakes for the company, the boss can be sentenced too
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. For every batch, keep these: the purchase contract, the invoice, the payment record, and the supplier's business licence and authorisation documents. Set one internal rule: whoever buys the goods signs for them, and anything priced clearly below market is not bought. The hard part is turning down cheap goods that look like a great deal.
- Plain language: Selling fakes that earn 30,000 yuan, or sell 50,000 yuan worth, can mean up to 3 years in prison. Unsold stock counts if it is worth three times the sales threshold. "I didn't know" will not settle it: if you bought far below market and cannot explain why, you can be found to have known. Keep contracts, invoices, payment records and supplier details.
- Benefit: Knowingly selling goods that falsely carry a registered trademark is a crime once the illegal gains reach 30,000 yuan, or the sales amount reaches 50,000 yuan. The penalty is up to 3 years in prison plus a fine, or a fine alone. Ten times that threshold brings 3 to 10 years plus a fine. Goods still in the warehouse also count if their value is at least three times the sales threshold. You do not have to admit "knowing" yourself. A 2025 judicial interpretation by the Supreme People's Court and the Supreme People's Procuratorate lists five situations that establish knowledge directly, unless you can prove you really did not know. First, you knew the registered trademark on the goods had been altered, swapped or covered. Second, you forged or altered the trademark owner's authorisation document, or knew it had been. Third, you were criminally or administratively punished for selling counterfeit goods and then sold the same kind again. Fourth, you bought or sold at a price clearly below market without good reason. Fifth, after enforcement authorities or courts found you, you moved or destroyed evidence such as infringing goods and accounting vouchers, or gave false proof. When a company commits this offence, the company is fined, and the person in charge and other directly responsible staff are punished under the same article. So even if an employee bought the goods, the person who made the decision can still be sentenced. Trademark law does leave one way out: if you can show that you legally obtained the goods and name the supplier, you do not owe compensation. Whether that works depends entirely on invoices and supplier records. Without them, that route is closed. (Nationwide; judicial interpretation in force since 26 April 2025)
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2020). 刑法修正案（十一）（第十七、二十四项，即刑法第二百一十四、二百二十条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/zdgz/202012/t20201227_503682.shtml>；最高人民法院、最高人民检察院 (2025). 关于办理侵犯知识产权刑事案件适用法律若干问题的解释（法释〔2025〕5 号，2025 年 4 月 26 日起施行）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/202504/t20250424_693977.shtml>；全国人大常委会 (2019). 商标法（2019 年修正，第五十七、六十四条；2026 年 6 月 26 日修订后为第七十二、七十八条，自 2027 年 1 月 1 日起施行）. <https://www.cnipa.gov.cn/art/2019/7/30/art_95_28179.html>、<https://www.cnipa.gov.cn/art/2026/6/26/art_95_206942.html>
- Notes: China only: Cosmetics, alcohol, baby products, electronics and clothing are the categories that most often appear in these cases. The common thread is a big price gap on the same product. Claims such as "channel goods", "end-of-line stock" or "overseas duty-free" cannot replace invoices. Beyond prison there is also an administrative penalty. Once infringement is established, the infringing goods and tools are confiscated, and where the illegal business turnover is 50,000 yuan or more, a fine of up to 5 times can be added. If you find out after buying that goods are wrong, stop selling at once, seal them, and contact the brand owner or the market regulator (the market supervision authority). Do not keep selling, and never destroy goods or records, since destroying evidence falls under the fifth situation above. For using other people's designs, see item 21 (make your own designs or buy a licence). One common scam targets exactly this rule: someone turns up unannounced and says a batch of pens or small goods is free; you put them on sale and split the proceeds. A few days later a buyer arrives, collects evidence on the spot, and sues for infringement damages. Free goods fail all three conditions for the defence: there is no purchase contract or payment record, no real supplier can be named, and the price is plainly abnormal. So refuse goods offered for free, goods with no invoice, and goods where the only contact is a WeChat (Chinese messaging app) ID. If you really want to act as a consignment seller, sign a written consignment contract, keep copies of the other party's ID card and business licence, and state in the contract who owns the goods and who is liable for infringement.

### 21. Use only images you made yourself or licensed for products, packaging, tags and marketing; changing a colour or adding an icon does not make it "altered"
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=some gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Licences from legitimate image libraries start at a few dozen yuan per image. Hiring someone to create an original design costs a few hundred to a few thousand yuan. Keep a licence certificate and a purchase record for every image used on a product. The hard part is resisting the urge to use images you found with a quick online search.
- Plain language: Recolouring someone else's picture, changing its background or adding your own icon is still using their work without permission. Copying artwork for profit is a criminal offence if it earns 30,000 yuan, reaches 50,000 yuan in business, or totals 500 copies. The penalty is up to 3 years in prison. For trademarks, a mark that is similar enough to cause confusion is already infringement.
- Benefit: The Copyright Law treats "use of a work without the copyright owner's permission by adaptation, translation, annotation or similar means" as infringement, alongside direct copying. So changing the colour or background of an original image, or adding your own icon, is still unauthorised use. The criminal threshold is reached when, for profit and without permission from the copyright holder, you print and sell copies of an artwork (reproduction and distribution). Any one of three figures is enough: illegal gains of 30,000 yuan, illegal business turnover of 50,000 yuan, or a total of 500 copies. Once any one is met, the offence of infringing copyright applies, with up to 3 years in prison plus a fine, or a fine alone. Ten times the threshold brings 3 to 10 years. Selling infringing copies that someone else printed is a separate crime: if you earn 50,000 yuan or more, or sell 100,000 yuan or more, the sentence is up to 5 years. For trademarks, two situations are already within the infringement scope, both described as "likely to cause confusion". One is using a mark similar to a registered trademark on the same goods. The other is using an identical or similar mark on similar goods. "Similar" is written into the law as an infringing case. A trademark you alter slightly is still infringing if it is likely to confuse buyers. (Nationwide; judicial interpretation in force since 26 April 2025)
- Evidence level: A

- Notes: China only: Three areas commonly go wrong. First, printing cartoon characters or celebrity images found online onto clothing, cups or phone cases. Second, fonts: most commercial fonts need a separate licence, and a free preview does not mean free commercial use. Third, unlicensed material hidden in design drafts made for clients; the seller remains responsible. To keep it simple, use a commercially licensed image library and save the licence page. Or have the designer state in the contract where the material came from and what the licence covers, and make the designer responsible if there is a problem. Beyond criminal liability there is civil compensation and administrative fines: where the public interest is harmed, illegal gains can be confiscated and a fine of 1 to 5 times the illegal business turnover applied. Check trademarks before production, see item 19 (run samples through a mass-production checklist). The rules also protect you: if your own images are used by others, you can enforce your rights the same way. ### 22. At work, follow the procedures; before doing a favour outside the rules, work out who is responsible
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. At work, follow the rules, medical orders and procedures. Do not make decisions for someone else outside your own authority. Before you help, ask yourself one question: if something goes wrong, who is responsible? The hard part is holding firm in the moment when someone asks you to "just make an exception".
- Plain language: Take nurses as an example. The regulations say you must notify the doctor at once if a patient's condition is critical, and raise any order that breaks the rules with the prescribing doctor. Other jobs work the same way: first check where your own procedures require reporting. Separately, if you voluntarily give emergency aid and injure the person, you do not have to pay compensation.
- Benefit: Using nurses as the example, the regulations set the boundaries of the role as follows. If a patient's condition is critical, notify the doctor immediately. In an emergency, necessary first-aid measures may be taken first to save a critically ill patient. If a medical order is illegal or breaks the rules or technical standards for treatment, raise it with the doctor, and report to the head of the department where necessary. The Civil Code makes its own provision. ### 23. If a business is losing money, exit through the proper process: deregister by simplified procedure if you can, file for bankruptcy if debts exceed assets, and do not just leave it
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Simplified deregistration you can do yourself: post a notice on the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (the official company registry) for 20 days. If debts remain, you must first sort the accounts, collect what is owed and pay what is due. This step is called liquidation. Bankruptcy requires an application to the court and takes several months. The hard part is facing up to the losses instead of letting the business drift.
- Plain language: With no debts and no unpaid wages, taxes or social insurance, and all investors signing a written commitment, a 20-day public notice with no objection lets you deregister simply. Sole traders need only 10 days. A company that cannot repay its debts can apply for bankruptcy. Leave a company alone for 2 years without filing annual reports, and unreachable, and its licence is revoked; the responsible legal representative is then barred for 3 years.
- Benefit: You owe no one and no one owes you, or all debts have been paid. You have no unpaid wages, social insurance or taxes. All investors have signed a written commitment. ### 24. Before paying someone to run your online store, buying a shop-training course, or starting a "no-stock" business, ask whether you will keep paying more and where the sales will come from
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Before paying, get three things: a written contract, the full legal name of the company, and the conditions for a refund. Pay only the company named in the contract, never a personal account. The hard part is staying calm when the other side shows screenshots of "students earning over 10,000 yuan a month" and you are tempted to pay on the spot.
- Plain language: These scams take one payment first, then keep asking for more: "upgrade packages", advance payments for goods, and "unfreezing fees". The back-end orders and earnings may be fabricated, so withdrawals become impossible. Using fake orders to inflate sales is illegal in itself, and an operator caught doing it will be fined.
- Benefit: The Guangdong High People's Court ruled on an operations scam. Jiang, Xu and 53 others (55 people in total) set up several companies in Guangzhou, Nanning and Hangzhou. The court found they knew they had no real ability or technical means to run online stores. They still lured 7,711 victims into buying and upgrading "service packages" in the name of operating Taobao shops, and took more than 52,994,000 yuan in total. The two ringleaders were sentenced to 14 years and 13 years 6 months for fraud. The court said the scheme was "seamless" and that "multiple victims were deceived again and again." In 2025 the Jiangsu Provincial Public Security Department broke down a "no-stock" cross-border e-commerce scam into four steps. Step one: post messages on social platforms promising "zero experience" and "no need to stock". Step two: add you as an "operations adviser" and have you download a designated platform. Step three: once you open a shop, a first order comes quickly. You are told to pay the cost price first, that buyers' payments are frozen on the platform until delivery, and that they will be unfrozen then. Mass orders are then created on the back end, and you keep paying more. ### 25. Companies and sole traders must file last year's annual report online between 1 January and 30 June each year, even if they had no business; filing is free
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Log into the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (the official company registry) yourself and file once a year. Sole traders can also file on paper. The annual report goes to the market regulator, and it is separate from the monthly or quarterly tax returns filed with the tax bureau. Filing taxes does not count as filing the annual report. The hard part is that it happens only once a year, so it is easy to forget.
- Plain language: Companies and sole traders must file their annual report online each year by 30 June. Miss it and you are placed on the Abnormal Operations List, and you can apply for removal after filing late. Filing is free. A caller who says you missed a filing and asks you to pay through a mini-program or public account is a scammer. Check the official system first.
- Benefit: A company must "between 1 January and 30 June each year, report the previous year's annual report to the market regulator through the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System, and make it public." If the deadline is missed, the company is placed on the Abnormal Operations List and "is subject to administrative penalties according to law." The list is published on the system, and anyone can check it. In government procurement and construction tenders, companies on the list may be restricted or barred. Sole traders must also file last year's annual report between 1 January and 30 June each year, online or on paper. Sole traders that opened in the current year start filing the following year. A sole trader may choose not to publish its contents, but failing to file still puts it on the list. A company placed on the list for failing to file can apply to the market regulator where it is registered for removal, once it has filed the missing years. If a company fails to file for 2 consecutive years, stays on the list uncorrected, and cannot be reached at its registered address, its business licence is revoked. Company annual reports must be made public. Where a sole trader chooses not to publish its contents, the system still shows that it has "submitted its annual report". So whether you filed, and whether you are on the list, can be checked on the system yourself. The market regulator has warned about a scam. Someone "impersonates a market regulator official" and phones to say you missed the filing and are on the Abnormal Operations List. They then "guide you to search for a so-called mini-program or public account" and ask you to appoint an agent and pay. The regulator's position is that "annual report filing is free". Filing an annual report and removal from the list must be done "through the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System or other official channels". Courts have already convicted such scams as fraud. The Jingde County People's Court in Anhui concluded one case. A registration agency had its call staff "deliberately conceal their identity", so sole traders thought the callers were from the market regulator. The staff then steered them to a third-party mini-program and collected "manual filing fees" of 98 to 398 yuan. They defrauded 10,099 sole traders nationwide, totalling more than 3.19 million yuan. Three ringleaders were convicted of fraud and sentenced to 10 years 6 months to 10 years 8 months. In a case decided by the Nanhu District People's Court in Jiaxing, the mini-program's small print actually said that "filing is free". The court still convicted for fraud, reasoning that the call staff "deliberately concealed the fact that operators can file online, and made up large fines". Nine people in that case were sentenced to between 8 months and 4 years. (Nationwide)

### 26. To raise money for a business, meet only relatives, friends and employees in person; do not recruit investors publicly on social media or in groups, and do not let relatives recruit others for you
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Borrow or sell stakes only to relatives, friends and employees you know, one at a time, in person. Do not post on social media, in groups, print flyers or hold investment presentations. If a relative says "let me ask around for you", stop them on the spot. The hard part is that when you are short of cash, "recruiting more people" looks like the fastest way to get money.
- Plain language: Borrowing from relatives, friends and staff, or selling them shares, is not illegal in itself. What is illegal is advertising publicly, taking money from strangers, and promising repayment with interest or returns. Taking 1 million yuan, or money from 150 people, can bring up to 3 years in prison. If the money is used for genuine business and fully refunded before prosecution, criminal punishment may be waived.
- Benefit: Article 176 of the Criminal Law covers "illegally absorbing public deposits or disguised public deposit-taking, disrupting financial order". Conviction carries up to 3 years in prison or detention, plus a fine, or a fine alone. Where the amount is large or there are other serious circumstances, it is 3 to 10 years. Where the amount is especially large or there are other especially serious circumstances, it is over 10 years. The Supreme People's Court's judicial interpretation says four conditions must all be met. First, you collect money without lawful approval from the relevant authorities, or using the shell of a lawful business. Second, you publicly advertise through the internet, the media, investment presentations, flyers or mobile messages. Third, you promise to repay principal and interest, or give returns, within a set period, in money, goods or equity. Fourth, you take money from members of the public who are not specific people. The same clause continues: "Where no public advertising is made and funds are raised within relatives or a unit, targeting specific people, it does not count as illegal or disguised public deposit-taking." However, a 2014 joint opinion by the Supreme People's Court, the Supreme People's Procuratorate and the Ministry of Public Security sets out two exceptions that still count as raising money from the public. First, you take money from relatives or employees while knowing they are raising it for you from strangers, and you do nothing about it. Second, you recruit outsiders as employees in order to take money from them. There are three thresholds, and reaching any one is enough: 1 million yuan or more collected, 150 or more people, or direct losses of 500,000 yuan or more for participants. Collecting 5 million yuan or more, 500 people or more, or causing losses of 2.5 million yuan or more falls into the 3 to 10 year bracket. Amounts are counted on the full sum collected. If the money is mainly used for normal production and operations, and all of it is refunded before prosecution, criminal punishment may be waived. Where the circumstances are obviously minor and the harm is small, it is not treated as a crime. Administrative penalties apply even below the criminal level. The lead department in local illegal fundraising cases fines the offender 20% to 1 times the amount raised. For an organisation that raised money, the legal representative and directly responsible persons receive a warning and a fine of 500,000 to 5 million yuan. (Nationwide; judicial interpretation amended and in force since 1 March 2022)

### 27. Before signing a lease, check the property can be used for your shop: converting a flat to commercial use needs every owner in the building to agree, and new restaurants that produce fumes are not allowed in residential buildings
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Before you sign the lease, pay a deposit or start fitting out, spend a few hours on three checks. First, the land use on the property certificate: residential or commercial? Second, is the building purely residential, mixed-use or a standalone shop, and does anyone live upstairs? Third, for food businesses, is there a dedicated exhaust flue, and can you get a food operating licence from the local market regulator? The hard part: when a landlord or agent says "no problem, the last tenant ran a business here", you still have to check for yourself.
- Plain language: Converting a flat into a shop needs every other owner in the same building to agree; one objection blocks it. Neighbours can sue to stop the business, restore the property and claim damages, and the court will back them. New restaurants that produce fumes are banned in residential buildings, and in mixed-use buildings without a dedicated flue. If you break the rule, you will first be ordered to fix it. If you refuse, the business is closed and fined 10,000 to 100,000 yuan, so your fit-out costs and deposit may be lost.
- Benefit: Article 279 of the Civil Code says: "When an owner changes a residential unit into commercial premises, in addition to complying with laws, administrative regulations and the management rules, the owner shall obtain the unanimous consent of the owners concerned." The Supreme People's Court's judicial interpretation is more detailed. All other owners in the same building count as "owners concerned". Owners in other buildings of the same estate count only if they can show that the value or quality of their living conditions is affected. If the change is made without unanimous consent, the owners may ask the court to stop it, restore the property, or award damages, and the court should back them. ### 28. Before opening a shop with partners, put in writing: each person's capital, how profits are split, who makes decisions, who takes a salary, and how partners can leave
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Partners meet once or twice to settle about a dozen points, then each signs. Partnership agreement templates are available online; fill in the ten items the Partnership Enterprise Law lists, one by one. The hard part is that among friends, people feel "talking about money hurts relationships" and find it awkward to raise.
- Plain language: Without a written agreement, the law sets the rules. Decisions need everyone's agreement. Profits follow the capital each person actually paid in, and a partner who works in the shop every day gets no salary by default. Anyone can leave with reasonable notice if there is no fixed term. To change these defaults, you must write the change into the agreement in advance.
- Benefit: Article 4 of the Partnership Enterprise Law states that the partnership agreement is agreed by all partners and made in writing. Article 18 lists ten items it must contain. These include the form, amount and payment deadline of capital contributions; how profits are distributed and losses shared; who executes partnership affairs; admission and withdrawal of partners; how disputes are resolved; dissolution and liquidation; and liability for breach. A group that works together without setting up a partnership enterprise is governed by the Civil Code's rules on partnership contracts. Where the contract is silent, the Civil Code has default rules. Decisions on partnership matters need the agreement of all partners, unless the partnership contract says otherwise. A partner who executes partnership affairs is not entitled to remuneration, unless the contract provides for it. Profits and losses are shared as the contract provides. Where there is no agreement, the partners first negotiate. If they cannot agree, profits are shared in proportion to actual capital contributions. If that cannot be determined either, they are shared equally. Partners bear joint and several liability for partnership debts, and a partner who pays more may recover the excess from the others. Where there is no fixed term, the partnership is at will: a partner may withdraw at any time, provided reasonable notice is given to the others. For a registered partnership enterprise with no fixed term, a partner may withdraw by giving the other partners 30 days' notice. The agreement also cannot give all profits to only some partners, or make some partners bear all the losses. (Nationwide)
