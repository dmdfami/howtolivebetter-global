# 11. Red lines that programmers and tech workers often cross

This section covers personal freedom and money. Its aim is to keep you out of jail and to keep you from paying out large sums. People who write code or work in tech often treat the things below as "just a technical question." But each one maps to a specific crime in the Criminal Law, or to a specific sum you may have to pay in a civil case. Every item gives the figures from the law, along with cases published on the websites of the Supreme People's Procuratorate and the Supreme People's Court. The criminal law text cited is the consolidated version republished by the Beijing Municipal Public Security Bureau Traffic Management Bureau, labelled "amended per Amendment (XI) to the Criminal Law." Item 1 is a general one. It explains how to judge before you act, and what to do first after something goes wrong.

The items in this section fall into the groups below. The item numbers are in brackets.

**Before you act**: before you act, ask whether anyone might report it to the police (item 1).

**Cheats, scripts and cracks**: game cheats and aids (item 2), ticket-grabbing, order-inflating and coupon-hunting scripts (item 3), crawlers that only scrape public pages that need no login (item 4), tools that automatically skip or block ads in other companies' apps (item 18), crack patches and key generators (item 20).

**Touching other people's systems**: running your own program on someone else's computer (item 8), testing someone else's system without written authorization (item 9), reporting vulnerabilities as required (item 10), DDoS traffic attacks (item 21).

**Client work and side jobs**: development jobs for gambling, fraud or porn apps (item 5), circumvention tools and VPN accounts (item 11), AI API relay sites that resell access to AI models (item 19).

**Leaving a job and ownership**: handing over all account access when you leave (item 6), not taking source code when you leave (item 7), non-compete agreements (item 12), code written with company resources belongs to the company (item 13), GPL and other open-source code (item 14).

**Shipping products**: collecting only the personal information the business needs (item 15), completing ICP filing (China's mandatory registration of websites) before launch (item 16), the security assessment and algorithm filing for generative AI (item 17).

### 1. Before you act, ask: will the vendor, platform or company report it? If you are investigated, get a criminal lawyer right away
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Before you act, swap "Is it technically possible?" for a different question: will the vendor, platform or company report it to the police? The hard part is resisting the thought that "no one will care about something this small." Server logs, payment records and group chat records can all be obtained by the authorities.
- Plain language: Before you act, ask one question: will the vendor, platform or company report it? Server logs, payment records and group chat records can all be obtained by the authorities. If you are criminally detained, a decision on whether to arrest you must be made within 37 days at most. From your first questioning you can hire a lawyer. The lawyer can tell the procuratorate that arrest should not be approved, and can apply for release on bail.
- Benefit: Anyone who finds evidence of a crime has the right to report it. If the authorities find there are facts of a crime, they must open a case. The courts, procuratorates and police have the power to collect and obtain evidence from relevant organizations and individuals, who must provide it truthfully. After criminal detention, the police must request approval for arrest from the procuratorate (formally applying to the prosecutor's office for an arrest) within 3 days. In special circumstances this can be extended by 1 to 4 days. For roving crimes (serial offences committed across regions) the period can be extended to 30 days. The procuratorate then has 7 days to decide. Added together, a decision on whether to arrest can take up to 37 days. When the procuratorate reviews an arrest application, "if the defense lawyer requests it, it shall hear the defense lawyer's opinion." Lawyers also have the right to apply for a change of coercive measures, such as replacing detention with release on bail, and the authorities must respond within 3 days. From the first interrogation, or from the point you are subject to a coercive measure (such as detention or arrest), you can appoint a lawyer. While the case is still at the investigation stage with the police, you can only appoint a lawyer. A lawyer's request to meet you must be arranged by the detention centre within 48 hours at the latest. For offences that may result in public surveillance or short-term detention, which are lighter than imprisonment, or where only supplementary penalties are imposed and no principal penalty, release on bail may be granted. Release on bail means paying a bond or finding a guarantor, so you can go home while the case is handled. Bail can last up to 12 months (nationwide).
- Evidence level: C
- Sources: 全国人大 (2018). 刑事诉讼法（2018 年修正，第三十四、三十九、五十四、六十七、七十九、八十八、九十一、九十七、一百一十、一百一十二条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/zdgz/201810/t20181027_396818.shtml>
- Notes: China only: These 37 days are counted from detention to the procuratorate's decision on whether to approve arrest. They are not a cap on detention. After arrest is approved, there are further custody limits during the investigation, so keep counting. Within these 37 days, a lawyer can meet you, file a written opinion with the procuratorate that arrest should not be approved, and apply for release on bail. The author's view is that if you wait until after prosecution to find a lawyer, the arrest-approval stage has already passed. The other items in this section all deal with the same question: whether the other side has a reason to report it.

### 2. Don't make or sell game cheats and aids, including AI aids that only read the screen and never touch memory, and don't sell modified builds that add features to apps like WeChat (China's main messaging app)
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. The cost is giving up income from this kind of work and from "small tools." The hard part is that the money comes in fast, and it is not obvious who will come after you.
- Plain language: Sell cheats to 20 or more people, or earn 5,000 yuan or more from them, and that is enough for up to 3 years in prison. Especially serious cases carry 3 to 7 years. One person made wallhack and aimbot cheats and sold more than 80,000 yuan worth in 3 months. He got 1 year, suspended for 1 year, and a 5,000 yuan fine. Someone ran a private game server using server-side programs and collected over 20 million yuan in top-ups. The heaviest sentence was 4 years, with fines of up to 10 million yuan.
- Benefit: Providing programs or tools designed specifically to intrude into or illegally control computer information systems, in serious cases, is punishable by up to 3 years, with a fine or a fine alone. "Plus fine" means a fine is imposed on top of the prison term. "Fine alone" means only a fine. Especially serious cases carry 3 to 7 years plus a fine. The threshold for "serious" is providing tools to 20 or more people, or illegal gains of 5,000 yuan or more. Illegal gains means the money made from this activity. Deliberately bypassing or breaking technical protection measures set by rights holders may also constitute the crime of infringing copyright, punishable by up to 3 years, and 3 to 10 years in serious cases. Official case: Wang and others made and sold cheats that read and changed game memory to enable wallhacks and aimbots. They sold more than 80,000 yuan worth in 3 months and earned over 30,000 yuan. Sentence: 1 year, suspended for 1 year, and a fine of 5,000 yuan. A 1-year suspended sentence means the 1-year term is imposed but not carried out at once. If the person commits no further offence in that year, they do not go to prison. Xiang and others sold a "TZ" series of cheats with sales over 200,000 yuan. Xiang was sentenced to 3 years, suspended for 3 years, and fined 20,000 yuan. Liu and others obtained game server programs, changed parameters to run private servers, and collected over 20 million yuan in top-ups. Sentences ranged from 4 years down to 2 years 6 months, with fines from 10 million yuan down to 1.3 million yuan. It is not only games. A Shanghai company made a custom firmware package that gave the WeChat app on phones dozens of extra functions, such as automatic forwarding, auto-liking and mass messaging. 2,381 of these phones were sold as "marketing phones." In 2019 the Xuhui District People's Court in Shanghai, convicting under the crime of copyright infringement, fined the company 500,000 yuan and sentenced the person in charge to 1 year 6 months, suspended for 1 year 6 months (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 刑法（根据刑法修正案（十一）修正，第二百八十五条第三款、第二百一十七条第六项）. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>；最高人民法院、最高人民检察院 (2011). 关于办理危害计算机信息系统安全刑事案件应用法律若干问题的解释（第三条）. <https://ga.sz.gov.cn/ZWGK/ZCFG/ZCJD/content/post_1304363.html>（深圳市公安局转载）；最高人民检察院 (2026). 上海静安：严惩制售游戏外挂黑灰产共护清朗网络空间. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202608/t20260825_735480.shtml>；最高人民检察院 (2021). 2020 年度检察机关保护知识产权典型案例（案例五）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/xwfbh/wsfbt/202104/t20210425_516525.shtml>；国家版权局 (2020). 2019 年度上海十大版权典型案件发布（上海市版权局发布，案例八：上海昱宫网络科技有限公司等侵犯著作权案）. <https://www.ncac.gov.cn/xxfb/ywxx/202004/t20200430_47269.html>
- Notes: China only: The published cheat rulings all involve reading and writing game memory and bypassing anti-cheat systems. There is no official ruling yet on aids that only take screenshots or simulate key presses. But the ticket-grabbing case in item 3 shows that a tool that does not touch memory, and only sends requests, can still be convicted of illegally obtaining data. Whether something is a crime, and under which offence, is decided by the authorities based on the material the vendor submits when it reports the matter. The author does not decide this. Selling cheats to 20 people is enough to count as "serious," so the threshold is low.

### 3. Don't write or sell ticket-grabbing, flash-sale, order-inflating or coupon-hunting scripts, even if they only automate button clicks
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, and takes no time.
- Plain language: A script that does not alter memory or crack anything, but only sends requests faster than a person can, can still lead to a conviction. Making 5,000 yuan, or causing 10,000 yuan of loss to someone else, is enough. One person used purchased 12306 accounts (China's national railway ticketing platform) to grab more than 200 train tickets for others, earning over 20,000 yuan. He was sentenced to 1 year 6 months, suspended for 1 year 6 months, and fined 5,000 yuan. Someone who sold ticket-grabbing software through an online shop was ordered to pay 20,000 yuan in damages for unfair competition.
- Benefit: Intruding into a computer information system, or using other technical means to obtain the data in it, in serious cases, is punishable by up to 3 years, with a fine or a fine alone. Especially serious cases carry 3 to 7 years. The threshold for "serious" is illegal gains of 5,000 yuan or more, or losses of 10,000 yuan or more. Official case: someone used a plug-in to batch-import purchased 12306 accounts, and submitted purchase requests on many threads at high intensity. He grabbed over 200 train tickets for others and made over 20,000 yuan. The court convicted him of illegally obtaining computer information system data and sentenced him to 1 year 6 months, suspended for 1 year 6 months, and a fine of 5,000 yuan. Zheng sold ticket-grabbing software aimed at a ticket-sales app through an online shop. He was found to have engaged in unfair competition and ordered to pay 20,000 yuan (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 刑法（根据刑法修正案（十一）修正，第二百八十五条第二款）. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>；最高人民法院、最高人民检察院 (2011). 关于办理危害计算机信息系统安全刑事案件应用法律若干问题的解释（第一条）. <https://ga.sz.gov.cn/ZWGK/ZCFG/ZCJD/content/post_1304363.html>；最高人民检察院 (2026). 上海静安：查办利用外挂软件批量代抢火车票牟利案. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/dfjcdt/202607/t20260707_731426.shtml>；最高人民法院 (2025). 2024 年人民法院知识产权典型案例（案例七，抢票软件不正当竞争案）. <https://enipc.court.gov.cn/zh-cn/news/view-4205.html>
- Notes: China only: The script in the ticket case does not alter memory and does not crack anything. It is only "faster than a person," and it was still convicted under Article 285 of the Criminal Law. Charging for grabbing tickets for others and selling scripts both count as profit-making. I found no official case of someone using a script just once for themselves.

### 4. Crawlers should only scrape public pages that need no login. Don't get around anti-scraping measures, don't touch personal information, and don't sell the data you collect
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. A crawler is a program that automatically goes through web pages and pulls the data from them. Before you crawl, read the robots file and any API authorization terms. robots is the file a website places in its root directory, stating which pages must not be crawled. Do not collect data that needs a login, a token (a temporary access credential), or breaking a signature. The hard part is that such data can often be grabbed straight away, so holding back depends entirely on your own discipline.
- Plain language: Getting data by bypassing protections such as logins, tokens and signatures can lead to up to 3 years in prison in serious cases, and 3 to 7 years in especially serious cases. Collecting personal information is worse. Fifty records of movements, communication content, credit or property information count as serious. So do 500 records of accommodation, health or transaction information, 5,000 records of other types, or illegal gains of 5,000 yuan.
- Benefit: Getting data out of a system by bypassing protections carries the same sentence as item 3 (ticket-grabbing, flash-sale and order-inflating scripts): up to 3 years, or 3 to 7 years. Illegally obtaining, selling or providing citizens' personal information counts as "serious" if any one of the following applies: 50 or more records of movements, communications, credit or property information; 500 or more records of accommodation, communication logs, health or transaction information; 5,000 or more records of other personal information; or illegal gains of 5,000 yuan or more. Serious cases carry up to 3 years, with a fine or a fine alone. Especially serious cases carry 3 to 7 years. Official case: software developer Li disguised a feature that stole photos from people's phone albums as a "face attractiveness test" app, and obtained 1,751 face photos. He also resold "social engineering database" data, and was sentenced to 3 years, suspended for 3 years. A social engineering database is a collection of personal information leaked from various sources, compiled into one database. A web technology company used a crawler to obtain store and order data from a food delivery platform, causing losses of over 40,000 yuan. It was prosecuted for illegally obtaining computer information system data (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 刑法（根据刑法修正案（十一）修正，第二百八十五条第二款、第二百五十三条之一）. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>；最高人民法院、最高人民检察院 (2017). 关于办理侵犯公民个人信息刑事案件适用法律若干问题的解释（第三、四、五条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/201705/t20170509_190088.shtml>；最高人民检察院 (2022). 检察机关依法惩治侵犯公民个人信息犯罪典型案例（案例二）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/xwfbh/wsfbt/202212/t20221207_594915.shtml>；最高人民检察院 (2025). 检察机关依法惩治侵害企业数据安全犯罪 服务保障企业创新发展（案例一）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202501/t20250124_680670.shtml>
- Notes: China only: A 2017 judicial interpretation says that giving lawfully collected information to someone else without the person's consent also counts as "providing." Exporting a user table to an outside partner inside a company falls under this sentence too. 5,000 records is only the size of a small table.

### 5. Don't take development jobs for gambling, fraud or porn apps, and don't set up payment channels or unban accounts for "clients"
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. The cost is passing up outsourced work that pays well but has vague requirements. The hard part is that these clients often offer far more money than a normal job.
- Plain language: If you knowingly take a development job for a gambling app, you will be treated as an accomplice to running a gambling operation. Running a gambling operation carries up to 5 years, and 5 to 10 years in serious cases. That is heavier than simply helping, which tops out at 3 years. In one case involving an esports betting app, 28 people were sentenced to between 1 year and 3 years 6 months for running a gambling operation. Someone who unbanned QQ accounts (a Tencent messaging app) for a fraud gang received 1 year 6 months and a 20,000 yuan fine.
- Benefit: Knowingly providing technical support, payment settlement or similar help to someone using online crime, in serious cases, is punishable by up to 3 years, with a fine or a fine alone. If it also constitutes another crime, the heavier charge applies. Running a gambling operation carries up to 5 years plus a fine, and 5 to 10 years in serious cases. Official case: Zhang Sheng's group developed a gambling game, then sold it as a turnkey package, charging site setup fees and report fees. The total involved was nearly 7 billion yuan. Zhang Sheng was sentenced to 9 years 9 months and fined 50 million yuan. Wang Qiang and 78 others, who were contracted to develop the game, received sentences ranging from 6 months to 9 years 9 months. Chen Dong and others developed and ran an esports betting app. The technical team wrote the code in Hainan. Twenty-eight people were convicted of running a gambling operation and sentenced to between 1 year and 3 years 6 months, plus fines. Zhang, who ran a studio that unbanned QQ accounts for a fraud gang, was sentenced to 1 year 6 months and fined 20,000 yuan (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 刑法（根据刑法修正案（十一）修正，第二百八十七条之二、第三百零三条第二款）. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>；最高人民检察院 (2023). 建赌博网站并出售，涉案近 70 亿元. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202307/t20230711_621248.shtml>；最高人民检察院 (2022). 上海闵行区：办理一起新型开设网络赌场案. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202208/t20220805_569952.shtml>；最高人民检察院 (2025). 依法惩治帮助信息网络犯罪活动及相关犯罪典型案例（案例一）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/202507/t20250728_702377.shtml>
- Notes: China only: "Knowing" does not require the other party to say out loud what the money is for. A quote far above market price, a product whose function is deposits, withdrawals and odds, or a client who will not say what the work is for: investigators routinely use these facts to infer that you knew. A technical person who develops a gambling app is treated as an accomplice to running a gambling operation. They are not placed in the lower band of "helping information network crime," which is capped at 3 years. "Helping information network crime" (帮信罪) is the short name for the crime of helping information network criminal activity.

### 6. When you leave, hand over all account access. Don't delete databases, leave backdoors or change passwords to lock systems, even if the company owes you money
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. If the company owes you wages, recover them through labor arbitration rather than acting on your own (see section 7 on labor arbitration). The hard part is holding back from touching the system while you are angry.
- Plain language: Account passwords were given to you by the company. That does not mean you can still use them after you leave. Illegally controlling a system carries up to 3 years in serious cases. Deleting data so that the system cannot run, with serious consequences, carries up to 5 years, and more than 5 years in especially serious cases. One dismissed engineer used the original admin account to delete data and logs, causing losses of over 120,000 yuan. He was sentenced to 3 years, suspended for 5 years, and fined 30,000 yuan.
- Benefit: Illegally controlling a computer information system, in serious cases, carries up to 3 years plus a fine or a fine alone. Especially serious cases carry 3 to 7 years. Deleting or modifying system functions or data so that the system cannot operate normally, with serious consequences, carries up to 5 years. More than 5 years applies in especially serious cases. Official case: senior IT engineer Lü was dismissed, then used his original admin account to log into the company's shared server. He changed the password, deleted data and logs, and caused losses of over 120,000 yuan. He was sentenced to 3 years, suspended for 5 years, and fined 30,000 yuan. Bai, an employee of a software company, deliberately added faulty code to an upgrade program, so that a customer's system could no longer log in. The losses were over 40,000 yuan. He was prosecuted for the crime of destroying computer information systems (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 刑法（根据刑法修正案（十一）修正，第二百八十五条第二款、第二百八十六条）. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>；最高人民法院、最高人民检察院 (2011). 关于办理危害计算机信息系统安全刑事案件应用法律若干问题的解释（第一、十一条）. <https://ga.sz.gov.cn/ZWGK/ZCFG/ZCJD/content/post_1304363.html>（深圳市公安局转载）；最高人民检察院 (2024). 检察机关「依法惩治网络犯罪 助力网络空间综合治理」典型案例（案例六）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202402/t20240223_644505.shtml>；最高人民检察院 (2025). 检察机关依法惩治侵害企业数据安全犯罪 服务保障企业创新发展（案例七）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202501/t20250124_680670.shtml>
- Notes: China only: Account passwords were given by the company. That does not mean they still work after you leave. Logging in beyond the scope of your authorization counts as intrusion, under Procuratorial Guiding Case No. 36. See item 9 (don't test someone else's system without written authorization). In Lü's case, after logging in he also changed the admin password and deleted data. The procuratorate found that the system did not stop functioning as a result, so it did not charge him with destroying computer information systems. It charged him with illegal control of computer information systems instead. Losses include the necessary costs of recovering the data. Under the 2011 interpretation by the Supreme People's Court and the Supreme People's Procuratorate, losses of 10,000 yuan or more count as "serious," and losses of 50,000 yuan count as especially serious. Lü's losses of over 120,000 yuan were classed as especially serious.

### 7. When you leave, don't take source code, customer lists or technical documents. Don't upload them to personal cloud storage, and don't reuse them at your next job
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Before you leave, clear company material off your personal computer and personal cloud storage. The hard part is that some files you wrote line by line yourself, and it is hard to let them go.
- Plain language: Code you write at work generally belongs to the company for copyright purposes. Customer lists may count as trade secrets. Taking them means taking the company's property. A former employee took the source code and core algorithms of a security screening system to a new company. He was sentenced to 5 years and fined 500,000 yuan, and the new company was fined 4 million yuan. Someone exploited a system vulnerability to download more than 33,000 design files, and was sentenced to 1 year 10 months. The company can also seek compensation from you separately.
- Benefit: Breaching a confidentiality duty by disclosing a trade secret, or using one yourself, in serious cases, is punishable by up to 3 years, with a fine or a fine alone. Especially serious cases carry 3 to 10 years plus a fine. The Anti-Unfair Competition Law also lists two things as infringement: obtaining trade secrets through "electronic intrusion" (intruding into systems by technical means), and inducing or helping others to breach confidentiality duties. Official case: former employees Sun and others took the source code and core algorithms of a security screening system and used them in a new company's product. Sun was sentenced to 5 years and fined 500,000 yuan, and the new company was fined 4 million yuan. Tian exploited a system vulnerability to download more than 33,000 design files, and was sentenced to 1 year 10 months and fined 100,000 yuan. Engineer Yang stored the source code of an app module on his personal computer and in the cloud. After switching jobs to build a similar app, he was prosecuted for the crime of infringing trade secrets (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 刑法（根据刑法修正案（十一）修正，第二百一十九条）. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>；全国人大常委会 (2025). 反不正当竞争法（2025 年修订，第十条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699862.shtml>；最高人民检察院 (2025). 检察机关知识产权保护典型案例（案例七）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202504/t20250423_693691.shtml>；最高人民检察院 (2020). 2019 年度检察机关保护知识产权典型案例（案例十六）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202004/t20200425_459623.shtml>；最高人民检察院 (2025). 检察机关依法惩治侵害企业数据安全犯罪 服务保障企业创新发展（案例五）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202501/t20250124_680670.shtml>
- Notes: China only: Code you wrote yourself generally belongs to the company as copyright owner. See item 13 (code written with company resources belongs to the company). Taking it means taking the company's property. Customer lists are business information and may also be trade secrets. Beyond criminal penalties, the company can bring a separate civil claim against you.

### 8. Don't run your own programs on other people's computers, servers or cameras, and don't use company machines for crypto mining
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, and takes no time.
- Plain language: Controlling someone else's computer, server or camera without permission is a crime at 20 units or more, punishable by up to 3 years. Especially serious cases carry 3 to 7 years. Even where it does not reach the level of a crime, you can be detained for up to 5 days and fined 50,000 to 500,000 yuan. A person who has been criminally punished can never again take a key job in network security management or network operations.
- Benefit: Illegally controlling a computer information system, in serious cases, carries up to 3 years plus a fine or a fine alone. Especially serious cases carry 3 to 7 years. Illegally controlling more than 20 units counts as "serious." Where the act falls short of a crime, public security organs confiscate the illegal gains and may detain the person for up to 5 days, with a fine of 50,000 to 500,000 yuan. More serious cases carry detention for 5 to 15 days and fines of 100,000 to 1,000,000 yuan. Anyone punished for a public-order offence is barred from key jobs in network security management and network operations for 5 years. Anyone criminally punished is barred for life. Official case: Li bought the IP addresses of network cameras and used software to control 105 of them to spy on people's privacy. He was sentenced to 3 years, suspended for 5 years, and fined 20,000 yuan. Wu and others implanted a trojan in phone mainboards, controlling more than 3.3 million phones and sending back their verification codes. They were sentenced to between 2 years and 4 years 6 months (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A

This item is therefore written under the provision on controlling someone else's system without permission. "Lifetime bar from key jobs" is a consequence the Cybersecurity Law adds on top of fines and prison. For tech people it is heavier than the fine.

### 9. Don't test someone else's system without written authorization. "Good intentions" and "reporting afterwards" are not grounds for acquittal under the statute
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Before you join a vendor's SRC (security response centre) or a crowdsourced testing programme, get a document that sets out the authorized scope, the target list and the permitted testing window. An SRC is a security emergency response centre run by a vendor itself, which receives vulnerability reports from outsiders. Once you have verified that a vulnerability exists, stop. The hard part is giving up the "quick look" you were going to take.
- Plain language: The law looks at whether you had permission and how much you obtained. It does not ask what you meant. Getting 500 ordinary account-password pairs, or making 5,000 yuan, or causing 10,000 yuan of loss, is enough for up to 3 years. To prove a vulnerability, someone may export a user table, and that alone can easily go past 500 pairs. If you hold account passwords, using them outside the authorized scope still counts as intrusion.
- Benefit: Ordinary company and personal systems fall outside the fields of state affairs, national defence and cutting-edge science and technology, and are computer information systems. Intruding into such a system, or using other technical means to obtain the data stored, processed or transmitted in it, in serious cases, carries up to 3 years' fixed-term imprisonment or criminal detention, plus a fine or a fine alone. Especially serious cases carry 3 to 7 years plus a fine. "Serious" has several thresholds: identity authentication information (such as account passwords) for online financial services such as payment settlement, securities trading or futures trading, 10 sets or more; other identity authentication information, 500 sets or more; illegal control of 20 or more computer systems; illegal gains of 5,000 yuan or more, or economic losses of 10,000 yuan or more. Five times or more any of these figures is "especially serious." Official case: Procuratorial Guiding Case No. 36 established the principle that "using accounts and passwords to log into a computer information system beyond the authorized scope is an act of intrusion into the computer information system." In other words, if you already hold the account and password, but use them beyond the authorized scope, that act itself is intrusion. In this case three people used accounts, passwords and tokens they had from work to log in remotely to the company's internal system. They downloaded data outside their work scope and sold it online, with illegal gains of 37,000 yuan. The three were sentenced to 4 years, 3 years 9 months and 4 years, and each was fined 40,000 yuan (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 刑法（根据刑法修正案（十一）修正，第二百八十五条第一款、第二款）. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>；最高人民法院、最高人民检察院 (2011). 关于办理危害计算机信息系统安全刑事案件应用法律若干问题的解释（第一条）. <https://ga.sz.gov.cn/ZWGK/ZCFG/ZCJD/content/post_1304363.html>（深圳市公安局转载）；最高人民检察院 (2017). 第九批指导性案例（检例第 36 号，卫梦龙、龚旭、薛东东非法获取计算机信息系统数据案）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/jczdal/201710/t20171017_202593.shtml>；央广网 (2016-06-29). 白帽子发布漏洞后被抓 世纪佳缘否认“钓鱼”使其上套（记者赵珂，实习记者刘香玺）. <https://m.cnr.cn/news/yctt/20160629/t20160629_522531823.html>（网页存档 <https://web.archive.org/web/20261005043859/https://m.cnr.cn/news/yctt/20160629/t20160629_522531823.html>）
- Notes: China only: Procuratorial Guiding Case No. 36 is itself a case about selling data for profit. It is cited here only for the sentence that logging in beyond authorized scope also counts as intrusion. It does not mean that good-faith testing would be sentenced as heavily. Article 285 of the Criminal Law and the 2011 interpretation do not list good intentions or after-the-fact reporting as grounds for not being a crime. The author therefore infers that neither your intentions nor whether you reported afterwards can cancel out the offence. They can only affect how heavy the sentence is and whether prosecution goes ahead. A vendor may thank you first and report you later. These two things do not contradict each other, and thanks are not authorization. In 2016, a security researcher known as a "white hat" submitted a vulnerability in the Shiji Jiayuan dating website (a Chinese dating platform) to a third-party vulnerability platform. Shiji Jiayuan later reported it to the police, and he was arrested. Shiji Jiayuan told reporters it had called the police because data had been taken, and at the time it did not know the person taking the data was the one who had submitted the vulnerability. This case appears only in media reports. There is no judgment and no official notice, and no official account of how it was handled. I could not find a good-faith testing case on the Supreme People's Court or Supreme People's Procuratorate websites that could be checked word for word, so this item is graded only on the statute and the penalty standards. To do it properly, use the vendor's public SRC or a written commission for testing, with the scope, targets and time window all put in writing. Prove that a vulnerability exists using the least evidence possible, and do not bulk-export data. Those who fall short of a crime still face public-order penalties and an occupational ban, with fine amounts and time limits the same as in item 8 (running your own programs on other people's machines). For what to do after finding a vulnerability, see item 10 (report vulnerabilities as required).

### 10. Report vulnerabilities as required. Don't disclose details before a fix, don't publish exploit tools, and don't hand them to overseas parties
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Report the vulnerability to the product provider, or to an official platform. Until the vendor has fixed it, do not release the details or any proof-of-concept code.
- Plain language: Individuals are also covered by the Provisions on the Management of Network Product Security Vulnerabilities. Until the vendor has a fix ready, you may not disclose the vulnerability, and you may not disclose details of a vulnerability in a system that is in use. You may not publish programs or tools designed to exploit vulnerabilities. When you disclose, you must also explain how to fix the problem. Vulnerabilities that have not been disclosed may not be provided to overseas organizations or individuals other than the vendor.
- Benefit: These provisions cover three groups: network product providers in China, network operators, and organizations or individuals engaged in discovering, collecting or publishing vulnerabilities. No organization or individual may use a vulnerability to carry out activities that endanger network security, or illegally collect, sell or publish vulnerability information. Publishing vulnerability information to the public requires following five rules. First, do not publish before the product provider has made a fix available. Second, do not publish details of vulnerabilities in networks, information systems and equipment that network operators are using. Third, do not exaggerate harm or risk, and do not use vulnerability information for malicious hype or fraud. Fourth, do not publish or provide programs or tools designed specifically to exploit vulnerabilities for network security attacks. Fifth, publish the fix or protective measures at the same time. In addition, unpublished vulnerability information may not be provided to overseas organizations or individuals other than the product provider. The provisions encourage reporting vulnerabilities to four official platforms. One is the Network Security Threat and Vulnerability Information Sharing Platform of the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology. Two is the vulnerability platform of the National Network and Information Security Notification Centre. Three is the vulnerability platform of the National Computer Network Emergency Response Technical Team/Coordination Center. Four is the China Information Security Evaluation Center vulnerability database. Anyone who collects or publishes vulnerability information in breach of the provisions will be dealt with by the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology and the Ministry of Public Security, within their respective remits. Where the conduct meets the circumstances set out in the Cybersecurity Law, it is punished under that law. Publishing system vulnerabilities or other network security information must comply with national rules. Violators are ordered by the competent authority to correct the problem and given a warning, and may be fined 10,000 to 100,000 yuan. Those who refuse to correct the problem, or whose cases are serious, face fines of 100,000 to 1,000,000 yuan. The authorities may also order suspension of the relevant business, a halt for rectification, or the closure of the website or app. They may revoke the relevant business licence or the business licence itself. The directly responsible supervisors and other directly responsible persons are fined 10,000 to 100,000 yuan (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 工业和信息化部、国家互联网信息办公室、公安部 (2021). 网络产品安全漏洞管理规定（工信部联网安〔2021〕66 号，第二、四、九、十、十四条，2021 年 9 月 1 日施行）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2021/content_5641351.htm>；全国人大常委会 (2025). 网络安全法（2025 年修正，2026 年 1 月 1 日施行，第二十八、六十五条；2016 年文本为第二十六、六十二条，规定第十四条援引的是 2016 年文本的条号）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm>
- Notes: China only: The provisions name individuals explicitly as subject to them. "I'm just an amateur" is not an exception. Submitting a vulnerability in a domestic system to an overseas bug bounty platform falls under the rule that unpublished vulnerabilities may not be provided to overseas parties. Whether you are qualified to test is covered by item 9 (don't test someone else's system without written authorization). How you handle a vulnerability once you have it should be considered separately. A vulnerability found lawfully can still be penalized if it is disclosed in breach of the rules. A vulnerability found without authorization raises problems in both the testing and the disclosure. The provisions do not separately address the case where you have notified the vendor and the vendor does not fix the problem. The author recommends keeping a record of the negotiation and reporting process, and reporting to all four official platforms listed above.

### 11. Do not sell circumvention tools or VPN accounts, and do not set up such nodes for others
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs no money and no time.
- Plain language: Building your own line to connect abroad, or using international channels other than the national public network, can get you ordered by police to stop connecting, given a warning, and fined up to 15,000 yuan, with illegal gains confiscated. If you make money this way, prosecutors can charge illegal business operation, and serious cases carry up to 5 years. This covers selling and setting up nodes. For personal use only, the penalty is capped at a warning plus a fine.
- Benefit: Direct international internet access must use the international gateway channels provided by the national public telecom network. No unit or individual may set up or use other channels on its own: you may not build your own line abroad, and you may not use one someone else built. Violators are ordered by the public security authority to stop connecting and given a warning, and may also be fined up to 15,000 yuan, with illegal gains confiscated. If done as a business, the authorities may treat it as illegal business operation. In serious cases, the sentence is up to 5 years, plus or instead a fine of 1 to 5 times the illegal gains. In especially serious cases, the sentence is 5 years or more (nationwide).
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: 国务院 (1997). 计算机信息网络国际联网管理暂行规定（第六、十四条）. <https://nyncw.cq.gov.cn/wsdw/zz/zhfb/wlyxxaq/202406/t20240619_13306645.html>（重庆市农业农村委员会转载）；全国人大 (2020). 刑法（根据刑法修正案（十一）修正，第二百二十五条第四项）. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>
- Notes: China only: When this section was written, no published circumvention case had been found on the Supreme People's Court or Supreme People's Procuratorate websites, and local handling varies widely, so the grade is B. ### 12. You signed a non-compete: if the company stops paying monthly compensation after you leave, send a written demand, and you can terminate it if it still has not paid after 3 months; in a role with no access to trade secrets, you can ask for the clause to be confirmed invalid
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Costs no money. When you leave, keep the original non-compete agreement and any evidence that the company did not pay monthly.
- Plain language: A non-compete bars you from working for a competitor for up to 2 years after you leave, and the company must pay you monthly for that time. With no agreed amount, the pay is 30% of your average monthly salary over the 12 months before you left, and never below the local minimum wage. If the company fails to pay for 3 months through its own fault, you can end the non-compete. Since 2025, if your job never involved trade secrets, you can ask a court to rule the clause invalid.
- Benefit: A non-compete may bind only senior managers, senior technical staff and others with a confidentiality duty. It may last up to 2 years. During that time, the company must pay monthly economic compensation. With no agreed amount, the pay is 30% of your average monthly salary over the 12 months before you left, and not less than the local minimum wage. If the company fails to pay for 3 months for its own reasons, you can ask to end the non-compete. If the company ends it early, it must pay an extra 3 months of compensation. Since 2025, workers whose jobs did not know of or access trade secrets can ask for the clause to be confirmed invalid. Any part of the scope that goes beyond the secrets you actually knew is invalid. Official case: a pharmaceutical company sued its chief technology officer, Mr. Zheng, for liquidated damages of 7,100,000 yuan. The court found that his new employer's products were not substitutes for the former company's, that the non-compete scope should be limited to the secrets he actually knew, and dismissed the claim in full (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2012). 劳动合同法（2012 年修正，第二十三、二十四条）. <https://yjglj.fushun.gov.cn/008/20260622/d846d3c2-df3d-4c4c-af16-898ede43e1ee.html>（抚顺市应急管理局转载）；最高人民法院 (2020). 关于审理劳动争议案件适用法律问题的解释（一）（第三十六至四十条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/282121.html>；最高人民法院 (2025). 关于审理劳动争议案件适用法律问题的解释（二）（法释〔2025〕12 号，第十三至十五条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/472691.html>；最高人民法院 (2025). 劳动争议典型案例（案例四）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/472681.html>
- Notes: China only: The statute only asks whether the company failed to pay for 3 months for its own reasons; the written demand is the author's suggestion for keeping evidence. In the other direction, if the agreement is valid and the company has paid monthly, and you still go to a competitor, you must pay the liquidated damages in the agreement. You may then also be required to finish the rest of the non-compete period. A company may also agree with senior managers, senior technical staff or others with a confidentiality duty that the non-compete applies during employment, and that agreement stays valid even if the company pays nothing extra. Apart from non-competes and paid training service periods, a company cannot make you pay liquidated damages; see section 19, item 22 (liquidated damages on resignation).

### 13. Work done during working hours or with company resources belongs to the company; keep your own open-source projects on your own time and equipment, and do not mix in company code
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Costs no money. Read the intellectual property clauses in your employment contract when you join. The hard part is that personal projects and company work often end up on the same computer, so keep them separate.
- Plain language: Software written mainly with company equipment, the internal network or business data, for which the company takes responsibility, belongs to the company in copyright. You keep only the right to be named as author, and the same holds if your contract says so. Code you write after hours can still count as the company's if you used company computers or business data. Inventions made mainly with company resources are also owned by the company as patents.
- Benefit: A work created to complete a task for an employer is a "job work" (something made while doing your job). In general, copyright belongs to the author, the employer has priority right to use the work within its business scope, and for 2 years the author may not license the same use to a third party. But two situations differ. One is engineering designs, software and similar works created mainly with the employer's material and technical resources, where the employer bears responsibility. The other is works the contract assigns to the employer. In both cases the author keeps only the right of attribution. "Material and technical resources" means the employer's equipment, internal network and business data. Inventions created to carry out the employer's tasks, or mainly using its resources, are "job inventions." The right to apply for a patent and the patent itself belong to the employer (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2020). 著作权法（2020 年修正，第十八条）. <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202011/t20201119_308796.html>；全国人大常委会 (2020). 专利法（2020 年修正，第六条）. <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202011/t20201119_308800.html>
- Notes: China only: Software falls directly into the "copyright belongs to the employer" category, so programmers almost never fall into the tier where copyright belongs to the author and the employer has 2 years of priority use. Code written "after hours" may still be treated as mainly made with the employer's equipment, internal network or business data if you used any of them. To keep a personal project clean, use your own machine, do not touch the company repository, and do not overlap with the company's business.

### 14. Using GPL and other open-source code in a product: open-source it or change the license as required, and do not treat it as "nobody will check"
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Costs no money. Before using someone else's open-source code, check its license (the conditions of use the author set). The GPL family requires that a product using it also release its source code, which conflicts with closed-source commercial sales; if they conflict, switch to another library. The hard part is giving up a library you have already gotten comfortable with.
- Plain language: Infringing copyright means paying the other side's losses or the profit you made. If it was intentional and serious, the award can reach 1 to 5 times. One company hired a rival's former employee, who built software substantially similar to the rival's. The company argued that the rival's software used GPLv2 and should have been open-sourced. The Supreme People's Court held that this did not affect the rival's copyright, and ordered the company to stop and pay 500,000 yuan.
- Benefit: Copyright infringement is compensated by the rights holder's actual losses or the infringer's profit. If neither can be calculated, the amount is set by reference to what a normal license would have cost. Intentional and serious infringement can be awarded 1 to 5 times the damages. Where public interest is harmed, illegal gains may be confiscated and a fine imposed. Official case: (2021) Zuigaofa Zhiminzhong No. 51. The defendant hired the plaintiff's former employee, and the gateway software it developed was substantially similar to the plaintiff's software. The defendant argued that the plaintiff's software was based on GPLv2 and should have been open-sourced in any case. The court held that "whether a developer itself complied with the GPLv2 agreement and whether it holds software copyright are two separate questions," ordered the infringement stopped, and awarded 500,000 yuan in damages (nationwide).
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2020). 著作权法（2020 年修正，第五十三、五十四条）. <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202011/t20201119_308796.html>；最高人民法院知识产权法庭 (2022). 涉「开源软件」著作权侵权案（（2021）最高法知民终 51 号）. <https://ipc.court.gov.cn/zh-cn/news/view-3042.html>
- Notes: China only: This case shows that "the other side used GPL code" is not a reason for copying it yourself. In the other direction, GPL authors suing commercial users who do not open-source have already obtained final judgments in China, but this section did not find the original text on the Supreme People's Court website, so it does not cite them. The grade is B because compliance with open-source licenses is governed only by contract and the general rules of copyright law; there is no specific statutory provision.

### 15. When building an app, collect only the personal information the business needs; get parental consent for users under 14; write a clear privacy policy
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs no money. Before launch, go through the app's permission list and privacy policy side by side, and drop any permission you do not need. The hard part is that product teams often want to keep extra data "in case it is useful later."
- Plain language: Collect only the information the business needs. Users under 14 need guardian consent. Violations are first ordered corrected, and the app may be suspended. If the operator refuses to correct, the fine is up to 1,000,000 yuan, and responsible managers are fined 10,000 to 100,000 yuan. Serious cases: fines up to 50,000,000 yuan or 5% of prior-year turnover, and managers are fined 100,000 to 1,000,000 yuan.
- Benefit: Processing personal information must have a clear and reasonable purpose directly related to that purpose, and must use the method with the least impact on individual rights. Processing requires the person's consent or another lawful basis. For anyone under 14, consent from a parent or guardian is required. Violations: the regulator orders correction, issues a warning, confiscates illegal gains, and may order the illegal app to suspend or terminate its service. Refusing to correct: fines up to 1,000,000 yuan, and directly responsible managers and other directly responsible persons are fined 10,000 to 100,000 yuan. Serious cases: fines up to 50,000,000 yuan or up to 5% of the previous year's turnover, with responsible persons fined 100,000 to 1,000,000 yuan. These people may also be banned for a set period from serving as directors, supervisors or senior managers of related companies. If selling or providing personal information is a crime, see item 4 (nationwide).
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2021). 个人信息保护法（第六、十三、三十一、六十六条）. <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202108/t20210820_313088.html>
- Notes: China only: The statute does not say which positions count as "other directly responsible persons." The author's view is that coders and product staff could also be counted, but the book found no case that can be checked word for word. The Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) publishes periodic notices of removed non-compliant apps, but this section did not verify which notice, so it is not cited.

### 16. Before launching a website or app, complete ICP filing (the mandatory registration for websites in China); keep network logs for 6 months or more as the multi-level protection scheme requires
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Filing is free and handled through your cloud provider; the author's experience is that it takes a few days to a few weeks. Multi-level protection scheme (MLPS) assessments are charged by level. MLPS is the state's set of security management measures that classifies systems by importance and requires websites and systems to meet them.
- Plain language: Filing costs nothing. A server in China that is not filed, and is not corrected, can be shut down. If someone claims filing is free but actually charges money, they can be fined 3 to 5 times their illegal gains, or 100,000 to 1,000,000 yuan. Logs must be kept for at least 6 months. Refusing to fix a problem, or causing harm, brings a fine of 10,000 to 100,000 yuan.
- Benefit: Commercial internet information services require a license; non-commercial ones require filing. Without a license or filing, you may not operate. If the service is unfiled, the telecom regulator orders correction, and if the operator refuses, orders the website closed. Operating without a license: illegal gains are confiscated and a fine of 3 to 5 times the illegal gains is imposed, or a fine of 100,000 to 1,000,000 yuan. Not displaying the filing number on the homepage: a fine of 5,000 to 50,000 yuan. Network operators must, under MLPS, set security management rules, prevent attacks, keep network logs for at least 6 months, and classify, back up and encrypt data. Failure to comply: order to correct, and a warning. Refusing to correct or causing consequences: fine of 10,000 to 100,000 yuan, and 5,000 to 50,000 yuan for the responsible managers (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A

### 17. Before offering generative AI services to the public, complete the security assessment and algorithm filing required by the rules, and label generated content
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs no money, but you must go through the Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC) process, which takes some time. A tool that does not offer service to the public in China does not fall under this measure.
- Plain language: Generative AI services open to the public that can shape public opinion or mobilize people need a security assessment and algorithm filing before launch. Changes, suspensions and cancellations also require procedures. Generated images and videos must be labeled. Small tools you use yourself and do not open to the public need none of this. Violations are punished under laws including the Cybersecurity Law.
- Benefit: Providers of generative AI services with public opinion attributes or social mobilization capacity must carry out a security assessment as required by the state. They must also make an algorithm filing under the rules on algorithmic recommendation, and complete later procedures for changes and cancellation. "Public opinion attributes or social mobilization capacity" means the ability to influence public views, or to organize and mobilize people. Providers must label generated content such as images and videos. The measure states that enterprises, research institutions and others that develop or apply generative AI technology, "if they do not provide generative AI services to the public in China, are not subject to the provisions of this measure." Violators are "punished under the Cybersecurity Law, the Data Security Law, the Personal Information Protection Law, the Science and Technology Progress Law and other laws and administrative regulations." The measure took effect on 15 August 2023 (nationwide).
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: 国家互联网信息办公室等七部门 (2023). 生成式人工智能服务管理暂行办法（第二、十二、十七、二十一条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891752.htm>
- Notes: China only: The measure itself sets no fine amount. For penalties, you must look to the Cybersecurity Law, the Data Security Law and the Personal Information Protection Law, which is why the grade is B. This section did not verify on the Supreme People's Court website any civil case about AI-generated content infringing copyright or portrait rights, so none is cited. For resellers of overseas model interfaces at low prices, see item 19 of this section (AI relay stations).

### 18. Do not build or distribute tools that automatically skip or block other apps' ads; even free ones can be ruled unfair competition
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Costs no money. The price is giving up a popular little tool. The hard part is that you may feel you earn nothing and are only clicking "skip" for users.
- Plain language: Software that skips or blocks another app's ads on a user's behalf can get you sued for unfair competition. Charging nothing may still count, since the law covers any operator, including individuals who provide services. One browser that blocked the opening ads on Mango TV was ordered to pay 800,000 yuan. One app that let users skip Mango TV's opening ads paid 80,000 yuan in losses.
- Benefit: The law says operators may not use technical means to hinder or sabotage the normal operation of network products or services lawfully provided by other operators. Operators here means natural persons, legal persons and unincorporated organizations that provide goods or services. If damage is caused, compensation is the victim's actual loss or the benefit you gained, plus the victim's reasonable costs of stopping the infringement. The market regulator may also fine 100,000 to 1,000,000 yuan, and 1,000,000 to 5,000,000 yuan in serious cases. Official case: the built-in function of the 720 Browser blocked the pre-roll and pause ads on the Mango TV website by default. The first-instance court dismissed Mango TV's suit. On appeal, the Guangzhou Intellectual Property Court reversed, found unfair competition, and awarded 800,000 yuan in economic losses and reasonable costs. The Supreme People's Court listed it as a typical case in 2021. The app "Lanjingling" let users skip Mango TV's opening ads directly. The first instance ordered it to stop and awarded 80,000 yuan in economic losses and 10,000 yuan in reasonable costs. In April 2024, the Beijing Intellectual Property Court upheld this on appeal, finding it had actually blocked the opening ads, not merely let users click "skip" (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2025 年修订). 反不正当竞争法（第二、十三、二十二、二十九条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff808181971552b40197b1016efc5437>；最高人民法院 (2021). 人民法院反垄断和反不正当竞争典型案例（案例五：「720浏览器」不正当竞争纠纷，（2018）粤73民终1022号）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/324491.html>；北京政法网 (2024-04-18). 「芒果TV」起诉「拦精灵」不正当竞争案，二审胜诉（来源：京法网事）. <https://www.bj148.org/sa1/ajbb/202404/t20240418_1664299.html>；《财经》客户端 (2023-09-10). - Notes: China only: Both judgments applied the pre-amendment text. After the 2025 amendment, this provision moved to Article 13 and added data, algorithms and platform rules. The appeal court said these cases concern blocking; users who click "skip" themselves are not affected. In August 2023, Litiaotiao, a free Android tool for skipping ads, received a lawyer's letter from Tencent. The letter said it blocked ads in Tencent's browser, constituted unfair competition, and demanded removal within 48 hours. The developer said it was non-profit and public-interest; reports said it only received occasional tips. It still announced an indefinite halt to updates, and several similar tools also stopped. This is documented only by media reports: there was no lawsuit and no judgment. An individual developer who receives such a letter must either stop, or pay for their own lawyer to respond. The first-instance ordinary procedure is required to conclude within 6 months, extendable, and lawyer fees are generally borne by each party. If you write a script only for your own use, the author found no case pursuing it. Selling ticket-grabbing software counts as unfair competition with compensation; see item 3 of this section (ticket-grabbing scripts). The beneficiary is yourself.

### 19. Do not run "AI relay stations" that resell overseas large-model interfaces at low prices: a Shanghai operator has already been criminally detained
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs no money. The price is giving up a low-barrier side business that looks profitable on paper. If you really want to run a model aggregation service, take the legitimate route: obtain a business license and connect to officially authorized models. That takes considerable money and time.
- Plain language: An AI relay station is a website that resells overseas large-model interfaces at low prices. A Shanghai operator said he was criminally detained for 37 days, is now on bail pending trial, will almost certainly be sentenced, and has already repaid and surrendered his illegal gains. China's Ministry of State Security has warned that some relay stations lack operating qualifications, and some have sent user data overseas without permission.
- Benefit: In June 2026, China's Ministry of State Security issued a risk alert. It says AI relay stations are proxy layers between users and the official services of model providers. Some lack operating qualifications. Some have no compliance qualification for cross-border data transfer and have not gone through the statutory security assessment, yet send user input to overseas servers. Some also let users get around restrictions on internet access, official authorization and cross-border transfer. China News Weekly reported on a case in June 2026. In May 2026, a Shanghai relay station operator posted a shutdown notice in a technical community. He said Shanghai police had criminally detained him for 37 days for illegally obtaining large-model interface access, and that he is now on bail pending trial. He said "I will certainly be sentenced in the future," and that he has already repaid and surrendered the illegal gains and must pay a fine. The report says such relay stations register or buy overseas model subscriptions in bulk, use servers abroad as relays, and resell cheaply to domestic users (nationwide / Shanghai).

### 20. Do not write, sell or distribute software crack patches, keygens or cracked versions; once the amount involved reaches the threshold, it is the crime of copyright infringement
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs no money. The price is giving up side businesses that make quick money from selling cracked versions, serial codes or cracked hardware dongles. Do not post crack patches on forums or cloud drives either. The hard part is that cracking looks like a technical exercise rather than theft.
- Plain language: Registration checks, hardware dongles and similar verification are technical measures in software. Bypassing them without permission is infringement, and you must pay compensation even if you charge nothing. Cracking, or selling cracking tools, for profit: earning 30,000 yuan or handling 50,000 yuan in sales is enough for a criminal conviction, with a maximum sentence of 10 years. In one case, three people who sold cracked dongles bundled with pirated software were sentenced to between 1 year and 3 years 2 months.
- Benefit: Start with civil liability. The Copyright Law allows rights holders to add technical measures to works. Technical measures are effective technologies, devices or components that prevent or limit others from using a work without permission. Without the rights holder's permission, no one may intentionally circumvent or destroy technical measures. No one may make, import or provide to the public devices or components for the purpose of circumventing them, and no one may intentionally provide technical services to others for circumventing them. Computer software is a work, so doing any of these is infringement, and you must stop and pay compensation. The amount is based on the rights holder's actual losses or your profit; if that cannot be calculated, use the cost of a normal license. Intentional and serious infringement can be awarded 1 to 5 times the damages. Where public interest is also harmed, the copyright authority may confiscate illegal gains. Illegal business turnover of 50,000 yuan or more: a fine of 1 to 5 times the turnover. Under 50,000 yuan, or not calculable: a fine of up to 250,000 yuan. Now criminal liability. Amendment (XI) to the Criminal Law added "intentional circumvention or destruction of technical measures" to the crime of copyright infringement, provided it is done for profit. Illegal gains of 30,000 yuan or more, or illegal business turnover of 50,000 yuan or more: up to 3 years, with a fine or a fine alone. At 10 times those thresholds: 3 to 10 years, plus a fine. A person who knowingly provides devices, components or technical services mainly used for circumventing technical measures to someone committing copyright crimes is prosecuted under the same crime once the same thresholds are reached. Official case 1: From March 2019 to July 2022, Mr. Liu made dongles that circumvent software technical measures in order to earn money. He also copied pirated software and sold it together with the dongles. His illegal business turnover exceeded 1,060,000 yuan. The Shanghai Third Intermediate People's Court found the circumstances especially serious and convicted him of copyright infringement. The Supreme People's Procuratorate said it was the first nationwide case of "intentional circumvention or destruction of technical measures" after Amendment (XI) took effect. In April 2023, Shanghai courts sentenced the three defendants in this series of cases to between 1 year and 3 years 2 months, partly suspended, with fines of 700,000 yuan to 80,000 yuan. Official case 2: Medical equipment repair technician Mr. Liu paid 80,000 yuan in a QQ (Tencent messaging app) group to buy 50 cracked dongles, then resold them online for several thousand yuan each. Plugging one in bypassed the identity verification of medical equipment and let the buyer enter the repair software. The Shanghai Putuo District court sentenced him to 1 year and 2 months, suspended, and a fine of 80,000 yuan. Official case 3: From May 2021, Mr. Li and others from Xuzhou hired people to make cracking programs for game consoles and produced cracked devices to sell. Their illegal business turnover exceeded 39,000,000 yuan. In November 2024, 10 people were sentenced to between 3 years 6 months and 2 years 6 months, partly suspended, with fines of 11,000,000 yuan to 1,500,000 yuan (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2020). 刑法修正案（十一）（第二十项，即刑法第二百一十七条第六项）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/zdgz/202012/t20201227_503682.shtml>；全国人大常委会 (2020). 著作权法（2020 年修正，第三条，第四十九、五十、五十三、五十四条）. <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202011/t20201119_308796.html>；最高人民法院、最高人民检察院 (2025). 关于办理侵犯知识产权刑事案件适用法律若干问题的解释（法释〔2025〕5 号，第十三条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/202504/t20250424_693977.shtml>；最高人民法院、最高人民检察院 (2025). 知识产权刑事保护典型案例（案例六：刘某生、刘某侵犯著作权案）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/202504/t20250424_693978.shtml>；最高人民检察院 (2024). 最高检：指导办理全国首例「故意避开或者破坏技术措施型」侵犯著作权案. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/2024zgjtzwlft/202402/t20240228_645416.shtml>；检察日报 (2023-04-18). 售卖盗版加密狗 解锁医疗设备安全系统（最高检官网转载）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202304/t20230418_611586.shtml>；国家版权局 (2025). 2024 年度全国打击侵权盗版十大案件（案件六：江苏徐州李某某等人破坏技术措施侵犯著作权案）. <https://www.ncac.gov.cn/xxfb/ywxx/202508/t20250801_925366.html>

The Copyright Law leaves some openings: testing the security of computer systems and networks, encryption research and software reverse-engineering research may circumvent technical measures. But the same article says you may not provide the technology, devices or components used for circumvention to others. So researching for yourself is allowed; distributing or selling it is not. Freely posting a crack patch does not reach the criminal threshold, because that crime requires profit. ### 21. Do not help launch DDoS attacks; do not sell or rent "stress test" traffic platforms; if you lose a game, do not attack the other side's servers
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs no money. A DDoS (distributed denial-of-service) attack means controlling many machines to send requests to a target at the same time, jamming its servers until they stop working. The price is giving up a business that charges per attack or sells traffic packages, and holding back a moment of anger. If you clash with someone in a game or in business, go through reporting, complaints or the police, and do not pay someone to attack them.
- Plain language: Using DDoS to make someone's website, server or game inaccessible is the crime of damaging computer information systems. Serious cases carry up to 5 years; especially serious cases, 5 years or more. Anyone who pays a platform to carry out the attack is also a joint offender. One person who ran a DDoS website and took orders for membership fees was sentenced to between 10 months and 5 years 6 months.
- Benefit: Interfering with the functioning of a computer information system, so that it cannot operate normally, carries up to 5 years' fixed-term imprisonment or criminal detention in serious cases. In especially serious cases the sentence is 5 years or more. Courts treat DDoS as this kind of "interference." "Serious consequences" has several tests. One is that the main software or hardware of 10 or more computer information systems cannot operate normally. Another is illegal gains of 5,000 yuan or more, or economic losses of 10,000 yuan or more. A third is that a system serving 10,000 or more users cannot operate normally for 1 hour or more in total. When the number of systems or the amount of money reaches 5 times these figures, it counts as especially serious consequences. Knowingly providing programs or tools for such sabotage to someone is treated as joint crime. The threshold is illegal gains of 5,000 yuan or more, or 10 or more person-times of assistance. Providing server hosting, technical support or similar help is also covered if illegal gains are 5,000 yuan or more. Some people provide programs or tools specifically for intruding into or illegally controlling computer information systems. Providing them for 20 or more person-times, or with illegal gains of 5,000 yuan or more, carries up to 3 years, with a fine or a fine alone. Below the criminal threshold, interfering with computer information system functions, or providing such tools, causing harm, is punished with detention of up to 5 days; serious cases get 5 to 15 days' detention. Official case 1 (Procuratorate Guiding Case No. 69): In early 2017, Yao Xiaojie and others were hired to set up the "Dark Night Group" overseas. They bought large amounts of server resources and used trojan software to control DDoS attacks. They attacked the IP addresses of three game companies hosted on one internet company's cloud servers three times; the games could not log in and users were constantly disconnected. The victim company spent over 40,000 yuan on emergency repairs. In June 2018, the Shenzhen Nanshan District court convicted 11 people of damaging computer information systems and sentenced them to 1 to 2 years. Official case 2: In March 2017, Tang and Wang built a DDoS website that allowed membership top-ups and the submission of attack tasks. They took orders to launch traffic attacks on more than 80 target IPs. The Nantong Haimen court convicted Tang and others of damaging computer information systems and sentenced them to between 10 months and 5 years 6 months. Mr. Zhang, who placed orders on this website to suppress rival companies, submitted more than a hundred attack tasks against 11 IPs or domains of one rival company. The court found that the orderer and the site operator were joint offenders, and that the orderer was the instigator and a principal offender. Official case 3: Mr. Wang built a DDoS platform and openly sold attacks in packages named "Bronze," "Silver," "Gold" and "Diamond." The Beijing Haidian procuratorate charged him with illegal use of information networks. In 2020, the first instance sentenced him to 1 year and a fine of 50,000 yuan (nationwide).
