# 10. Is Marriage Worth It? Cost-Benefit Accounts for Love and Marriage

This section works out whether marriage is worth it by splitting the question into separate accounts: money, time, health, legal risk and relationship quality. Each account uses only numbers that can be traced to a source, limited to official statistics or meta-analyses (studies that pool the data from several studies and recalculate it). It makes no moral judgement and does not draw a conclusion for you. The health accounts are calculated as risk of death, the time accounts as minutes per day, and the legal accounts from how the law is written. The three methods are not converted into each other. The method for working out the accounts, a fill-in checklist and common mistakes are in the [marriage cost-benefit method note](../docs/结婚划不划算.md).

The entries in this section are grouped by theme below. The item numbers are in brackets.

**Finding a partner and dating**: meeting more people (item 1), stopping once someone has refused (item 2), judging interest from behaviour (item 3), taking turns answering questions that go deeper step by step (item 5), and long-distance being no worse than living in the same city (item 6).

**Whether the relationship is good**: looking at how you feel in it (item 4), arguing about the issue rather than the person (item 17), and going to couples therapy together (item 20).

**The cost-benefit of marriage**: start with the registration data (item 7), discount health gains that come from observational data (item 8), count time as unpaid work (item 9), work out the cost of leaving (item 15), keep a separate account for marrying to please elders (item 16), and when divorcing, write down child support and visitation (item 23).

**Registering and the pre-marriage health check**: registering needs only your ID card (item 13), have one health check before marriage (item 14), and if you hold a wedding banquet, still go and register the marriage (item 21).

**Money and debt**: check the legal default rules for money first (item 10), if parents fund a flat, write down whether it is a loan or a gift (item 11), deal with a large debt taken on by one spouse (item 12), and watch for a spouse using joint money to tip a livestreamer or transfer it to someone outside the marriage (item 22).

**Same-sex partners and sexual orientation**: same-sex partners should set up a voluntary guardianship and a will (item 18), and should not marry to hide their sexual orientation (item 19).

### 1. Meet more people instead of fixating on one: whether two people click cannot be predicted before they meet
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some gain=mid scope=time -->
- Cost: No money. Your time goes into meeting more people, not replaying the same person in your head. The hard part is holding back from fixating.
- Plain language: Before they meet, a questionnaire can show who is generally easy to like, but not whether two specific people click. You only find that out by meeting. So meeting several people is a better bet than betting everything on one.
- Benefit: In two speed-dating studies, participants filled in more than 100 self-rated questionnaire items on dating-related traits before meeting anyone. The researchers used a random forest model (a pattern-finding algorithm) to analyse the data. It could estimate how easily a person is generally liked, and these questionnaires accounted for 4%–18% of the variance. Variance here means the differences between people, and the questionnaires explained only a small part of it. It could also estimate how popular a person is overall, accounting for 7%–27%. But whether two specific people are attracted to each other could not be predicted, no matter how the traits and preferences measured beforehand were combined. The same researchers had earlier run a speed-dating experiment. Participants reported their ideal-partner preferences beforehand, such as whether they valued looks or financial prospects. Those stated preferences did not predict their on-the-spot interest in a particular person.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Joel S, Eastwick PW, Finkel EJ (2017). Is romantic desire predictable? Machine learning applied to initial romantic attraction. Psychological Science, 28(10), 1478-1489. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797617714580>；Eastwick PW, Finkel EJ (2008). Sex differences in mate preferences revisited: do people know what they initially desire in a romantic partner? Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 94(2), 245-264. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.94.2.245>
- Notes: The two studies only show that attraction cannot be predicted before meeting. They do not show that effort is useless. Whether two people click can only be learned by meeting, so spending your time meeting several people, each for a real first date, beats betting on one. The participants were European and American university students. Attraction and partner choice in China work differently, so discount any application to your own situation.

### 2. After someone clearly refuses you, stop: continuing to pursue them is a public-security offence, not "sincerity"
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=high gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: No money and no time. The hard part is restraint: stop messaging and stop waiting for them.
- Plain language: Once someone has clearly refused you, repeatedly messaging, waiting around or following them, if it disrupts their normal life, has been a public-security offence since January 2026. The penalty is up to 5 days' detention or a fine of up to 1,000 yuan (RMB). Serious cases get 5 to 10 days' detention. Police can also order you to stay away from them, and breaking that order brings another 5 to 10 days.
- Benefit: The Public Security Administration Punishment Law, in force from 1 January 2026, lists pestering and stalking as a separate offence. The text reads: "repeatedly sending obscene, insulting or threatening messages, or using harassment, pestering, stalking or other methods, disrupting another person's normal life." Such conduct is punished with up to 5 days' detention, or a fine of up to 1,000 yuan. Serious cases receive 5 to 10 days' detention, and may also be fined up to 1,000 yuan. This offence has one more layer: with the approval of the head of the public security organ, the offender can be ordered not to contact the victim for a set period. Breaking a no-contact order brings a further 5 to 10 days' detention.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: National People's Congress Standing Committee (2025 revision). Public Security Administration Punishment Law (Article 50, paragraph 1, item 5, and paragraph 2; Article 136). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml> (reprinted by the Supreme People's Procuratorate)
- Notes: China only: The idea that pursuing someone relentlessly will move them has no research behind it, but detention and no-contact orders are written in law. Records of such offences are sealed and not provided or made public to ordinary organisations or individuals, although relevant bodies can check them as national rules allow. The law looks at whether you repeatedly harass, pester or follow someone and disrupt their life, not at what you think. Continuing after the other person has clearly refused is the situation most likely to be judged as disruptive. This sentence is the author's judgement.

### 3. To judge whether someone is interested, look at what they do, not at "signals": who initiates, who arranges, who follows up
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=time -->
- Cost: No money and not much time. The hard part is changing a habit: stop analysing the other person's expressions and tone, and record facts instead.
- Plain language: When strangers chat for ten or so minutes, judging that the other person is not flirting is fairly reliable, but spotting flirting is hard. So do not rely on one conversation's facial expressions and tone. People who like you more are more likely to message you first and arrange to meet, though the link is weak. Track what they do, not how they seem.
- Benefit: Two studies tested how accurately people can detect flirting. In the first, 52 pairs of unacquainted opposite-sex strangers each talked for 10 to 12 minutes. Afterwards each reported whether they had been flirting, and whether they thought the other person had been. The result: interactions with no flirting were judged more accurately, and interactions that did involve flirting were judged less accurately. The second study used 26 one-minute videos, judged by 261 observers, and reached the same conclusion. People can fairly reliably see that someone is not flirting, but find it hard to see that someone is. The third study looked at behaviour. It pooled three rapid-dating events, with 559 people and more than 6,600 dates, and then followed up. The stronger a person's liking for a particular date at the meeting, the more likely they were to message that person later and arrange to go out. The average correlation for this link is about 0.15 (a weak link). This suggests interest shows up in behaviours such as initiating contact and arranging to meet. But the study did not compare behaviour with facial expressions and tone to see which is more accurate.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Hall JA, Xing C, Brooks S (2015). Accurately detecting flirting: error management theory, the traditional sexual script, and flirting base rate. Communication Research, 42(7), 939-958. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0093650214534972>；Perilloux C, Kurzban R (2015). Do men overperceive women's sexual interest? Psychological Science, 26(1), 70-77. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614555727>（争议方）；Baxter A, Maxwell JA, Bales KL, Finkel EJ, Impett EA, Eastwick PW (2022). Initial impressions of compatibility and mate value predict later dating and romantic interest. PNAS, 119(45), e2206925119. <https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2206925119>
- Notes: Disputed. Another line of research argues that men do not overestimate women's sexual intent; rather, women under-report their intent on questionnaires. Which side judges better remains unresolved. The first two studies did not test behavioural cues. The third shows that liking leads to messaging and arranging to meet. But no study has directly compared whether behaviour is more accurate than facial expressions and tone; that is the author's inference. The author suggests looking at whether the other person initiates contact, whether they arrange the next meeting, and whether they keep following up when you do not push things forward.

### 4. Whether a relationship is good depends mainly on how you feel in it, not on your partner's qualifications
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=time -->
- Cost: No money and not much time. The hard part is shifting your view: put the mate-selection checklist away and look at how the time together actually goes.
- Plain language: What best shows whether a relationship is good is your own feelings, not your partner's qualifications. For example, whether you feel they are serious about you and appreciate you, and how often you argue. Your own feelings predict this two to four times better than your partner's account. But whether the relationship later improves or worsens can barely be predicted by any questionnaire.
- Benefit: A machine-learning analysis of 43 longitudinal couple datasets from 29 labs. "Longitudinal" means following the same couples over time. The strongest predictors of relationship quality all came from each person's own view of the relationship: feeling the partner's commitment, appreciation, sexual satisfaction, feeling that the partner is satisfied, and conflict. The strongest individual-level items were life satisfaction, negative emotions, depression, avoidant attachment and anxious attachment. The relationship items alone explained up to 45% of the variance at the start of the survey. Variance here means the differences in relationship quality between couples; 45% is the part these items could account for. By the end of the study they explained up to 18%. The predictive power of self-reported items was two to four times that of partner-reported items. Adding individual differences and partner reports on top of self-reported relationship feelings added no extra predictive power. Whether the relationship got better or worse during the study was largely unpredictable from any combination of self-report variables. The original reads: "largely unpredictable from any combination of self-report variables".
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Joel S, Eastwick PW, Allison CJ, et al. (2020). Machine learning uncovers the most robust self-report predictors of relationship quality across 43 longitudinal couples studies. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 117(32), 19061-19071. <https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1917036117>
- Notes: To judge whether the relationship is good right now, asking yourself how you feel is more accurate than checking your partner against a list of conditions. But do not expect it to predict how the relationship will develop. For the account linking relationship quality to physical health, see item 17 of this section (treating relationship quality as a health account).

### 5. To get closer, take turns answering a set of questions that go deeper step by step; don't learn scripted lines
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=low scope=time -->
- Cost: No money. Takes 45 minutes.
- Plain language: Two strangers who take turns answering 36 questions that go deeper step by step feel closer after 45 minutes than people who simply chat for the same time. What works is taking turns to speak honestly and going deeper layer by layer, not using scripts. This only shows they felt closer at that moment. It does not mean a long-term relationship will follow.
- Benefit: Strangers were paired and took turns answering three sets of progressively deeper questions, 36 in total. The questions ranged from "Would you like to invite anyone to dinner?" through to "When did you last cry in front of someone?" After 45 minutes, closeness was significantly higher than in a control group who chatted for the same length of time. It was also higher than the average rating that the university students in the study gave to their closest real-life relationship.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Aron A, Melinat E, Aron EN, Vallone RD, Bator RJ (1997). The experimental generation of interpersonal closeness: a procedure and some preliminary findings. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 23(4), 363-377. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167297234003>
- Notes: There has been only one experiment, with university students. It measured closeness over a short time only, not whether it could develop into a long-term relationship. In the experiment, closeness came from two people taking turns to disclose their real situation at progressively deeper levels; it did not depend on scripts, tactics or one-sided performance. So the approach that works in conversation is: "I share something real, and ask one question of similar depth", taking turns on both sides.

### 6. Long-distance is no worse than living in the same city; the real hurdle is the first three months after moving in together
<!-- tags: money=high time=high will=high gain=mid scope=time -->
- Cost: Travel fares and the time spent on the road. You meet less often than couples in the same city. The hard part is holding on for the long term without seeing each other.
- Plain language: Long-distance couples tend to be more stable while they are apart, the research suggests, because they idealise each other more when they see each other less. The real test comes after they move in together. In tracking data, about half of long-distance couples reached the stage of living together; the other half ended while still apart. Of those who reunited, a third split up within three months.
- Benefit: Two studies reached the same conclusion. Long-distance relationships are more stable than same-city relationships while the partners are still apart. The reason is that long-distance partners idealise each other to a clearly greater degree. The studies measured idealisation distortion, romantic love, relationship recollection and perceived consistency. And the less they meet, the more they idealise. But this stability only holds while they stay apart. Once they live in the same city, long-distance partners are more likely to split up. The study that tracked long-distance couples as they moved closer found this: about half of long-distance couples reached the step of moving in together, and the other half ended while still apart. Of the half who reunited, a third split up within three months of reuniting. Participants described changes after reuniting: loss of personal space, knowing more about each other's good and bad sides, difficulty scheduling time, and more conflict and jealousy. The longer the gap between visits while apart, and the stronger the idealisation, the less stable the relationship after reunion.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Stafford L, Merolla AJ, Castle JD (2006). When long-distance dating partners become geographically close. Journal of Social and Personal Relationships, 23(6), 901-919. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407506070472>；Stafford L, Merolla AJ (2007). Idealization, reunions, and stability in long-distance dating relationships. Journal of Social and Personal Relationships, 24(1), 37-54. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407507072578>；Dargie E 等 (2015). Journal of Sex & Marital Therapy, 41(2), 181-202. <https://doi.org/10.1080/0092623X.2013.864367>（备注里那项异地研究）
- Notes: The subjects were American university students in dating relationships, not marriages, and the research is older, so do not apply the absolute proportions directly. Another study surveyed 717 long-distance and 425 same-city people. The quality difference between the two types of relationship was very small. Relationship quality depends mainly on each person's circumstances and the relationship itself, not on distance. So when deciding whether to go long-distance, do not use "long-distance always ends" as a reason. What to prepare for in advance is the months after reunion, when personal space shrinks, knowledge of each other grows, and conflict and jealousy increase.

### 7. Look at the registration data before listening to elders: what the marriage and divorce rates actually measure
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=low scope=time -->
- Cost: No money. Reading the marriage registration section of the Ministry of Civil Affairs' annual bulletin takes a few minutes.
- Plain language: In 2024, marriage registrations fell by 20.5% compared with the year before. Online claims that more than half of marriages end in divorce divide one year's divorces by one year's marriages. But these two counts involve different people, so the result is not a rate of marriages ending. The official rates use the whole population as the base, not married people.
- Benefit: In 2024, 6,106,000 couples completed marriage registration under the law, down 20.5% on the previous year, a marriage rate of 4.3‰. In the same year, 3,513,000 couples completed divorce procedures under the law: 2,622,000 registered divorces with civil affairs departments, and 891,000 divorced by court judgment or mediation. The divorce rate was 2.5‰. The bulletin states that marriage (divorce) rate = number of marriages (divorces) in the year / average total population in the year × 1000‰. This is a "crude rate", with the whole population as the denominator, not the married population.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Ministry of Civil Affairs (2025). 2024 Statistical Bulletin on the Development of Civil Affairs (Section 5(2)1 Marriage Registration Services, Note 5), web version and PDF version. <https://www.mca.gov.cn/n1288/n1294/n1554/c1662004999980006190/content.html>、<https://www.mca.gov.cn/gdnps/n2445/n2451/n2458/n2681/c1662004999980006189/attr/400985.pdf>
- Notes: The online claim of "a divorce rate above 50%" divides the number of divorces in a year by the number of marriages in that year: 351.3 / 610.6 ≈ 57.5%. This is called the divorce-to-marriage ratio. The two numbers do not describe the same group of people, and the bulletin has no such indicator, so it cannot be read as "half of marriages end". To calculate the probability that a given marriage ends in divorce, you would need to follow the same group of marrying couples over time. This section found no official data of that kind.

### 8. Count the health benefits of marriage, but discount observational data
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=low scope=health -->
- Cost: No money and no time. Just remember one point: these figures show that married people die less often, two things occurring together. It is unclear whether marriage is what makes people live longer.
- Plain language: Among older people, married people have about 12% lower probability of dying in the same period than unmarried people, and never-married people have about 24% higher probability than married people. But these are two things occurring together, and it is unclear whether marriage is responsible. The figures come mostly from Europe and North America, so treat them as rough orders of magnitude for China.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis of older adults, which pooled several studies and recalculated the results, covering 53 comparisons and over 250,000 people. All the pooled studies were cohort studies, meaning follow-up records with no control group. Married compared with unmarried: all-cause mortality RR 0.88, 95% CI 0.85–0.91 (RR is the risk ratio, CI is the confidence interval), meaning about 12% lower probability of death in the same period. In the same analysis: widowed 1.11, divorced or separated 1.16, never married 1.11, meaning roughly 11%, 16% and 11% higher. There was no difference between men and women. The most conservative estimate is RR 0.94, only about 6% lower. Another meta-analysis compared never-married (never married) with married people, using 95 papers and over 500 million people. The result was HR 1.24 (1.19–1.30), meaning about 24% higher probability of dying in the same period. The gap is smaller the older people are. A meta-analysis split by sex, covering 21 studies and more than 7,890,000 people, found that for both men and women, being unmarried was linked to all-cause, cancer and cardiovascular deaths. The gap in cardiovascular and all-cause death was larger for men. Unmarried men compared with unmarried women had 31% more stroke deaths and 9% more all-cause deaths.
- Evidence level: A (disputed)
- Sources: Manzoli L, et al. (2007). Marital status and mortality in the elderly: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Soc Sci Med 64:77–94. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2006.08.031>；Roelfs DJ, et al. (2011). The rising relative risk of mortality for singles: meta-analysis and meta-regression. Am J Epidemiol 174(4):379–389. <https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwr111>；Wang Y, et al. (2020). Sex differences in the association between marital status and the risk of cardiovascular, cancer, and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of 7,881,040 individuals. Glob Health Res Policy 5:4. <https://doi.org/10.1186/s41256-020-00133-8>
- Notes: Disputed. The dispute is that people who marry may already have been selected; for example, people who were healthier to begin with may be more likely to marry. The author draws this from the study design. None of the three meta-analyses calculated how large that selection effect is. All three used observational data from existing populations, which are follow-up records with no control group. The authors themselves note publication bias or methodological limits. Roelfs also found that the more rigorous the study, the smaller the gap it calculated. These figures are mainly based on European and North American populations, so for China treat them only as rough orders of magnitude.

### 9. Count time as unpaid work, and agree on the division of labour before getting your marriage certificate
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some gain=high scope=time -->
- Cost: No money. Spend one or two hours before marriage turning unpaid work, such as chores, caring for children and older people, and whose job gives way, into a clear division of labour, then follow it. The hard part is talking it through openly rather than putting it off.
- Plain language: Nationwide time-use surveys count unpaid work such as chores and caring for family. Among people who did such work that day, men averaged 1 hour 52 minutes and women 3 hours 29 minutes. The surveys did not split people by marital status, so this gap is only a rough upper reference. You can agree on a different division of chores before you register.
- Benefit: The 2024 National Time Use Survey. Unpaid work means chores and caring for and keeping company with family members, which carry no pay. People who did such work that day spent on average 2 hours 45 minutes a day on it: men 1 hour 52 minutes and women 3 hours 29 minutes. The share of people who did such work at some point in the day was 67.5% of men and 83.9% of women. Residents' household chores averaged 1 hour 17 minutes a day; counting only those who did them, 1 hour 59 minutes, which is 28 minutes less than in 2018. Caring for and keeping company with family members averaged 30 minutes a day; counting only those who did it, 1 hour 46 minutes. The 2018 figures were: household chores, men 45 minutes and women 2 hours 6 minutes; caring for and keeping company with children, men 17 minutes and women 53 minutes.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: National Bureau of Statistics (2024). Bulletin of the Third National Time Use Survey (No. 2 and No. 3). <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202410/t20241031_1957216.html>、<https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202410/t20241031_1957215.html>；National Bureau of Statistics (2019). Bulletin of the 2018 National Time Use Survey. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202302/t20230203_1900224.html>
- Notes: Both surveys split people only by sex, urban or rural residence, and age, not by marital status. So there is no official figure for how much housework increases after marriage, and the sex gap can only serve as an upper reference. These are national averages. The split of work between the two of you can be agreed in a completely different way.

### 10. For money in marriage, check the legal default rules first, then decide whether to sign a written agreement
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: No money. Read the sections of the marriage and family part of the Civil Code that deal with property. If needed, the two of you can sign a written property agreement. The law only requires it to be in writing, not notarised. The hard part is raising money matters early.
- Plain language: Without an agreement, the law says that wages, bonuses, business and investment income, and inherited or gifted property earned during marriage are jointly owned. Property you owned before marriage, or that a will or gift contract gives only to one person, is personal. You can sign a written agreement to keep things separate, and it binds you both. A spouse who did more of the child or elder care can ask for compensation at divorce.
- Benefit: Without another agreement, the default rule is that wages, bonuses, labour remuneration, income from production and business or investment, income from intellectual property, and inherited or gifted property are jointly owned by the spouses (Article 1062). Property from before marriage, compensation for personal injury, property that a will or gift contract explicitly assigns to one party, and daily items for one party's exclusive use are personal property (Article 1063). The two parties can agree in writing that property from before and after marriage belongs to each of them, is held jointly, or is partly separate and partly joint. The agreement is legally binding on both (Article 1065). A spouse who has taken on more duties in raising children, caring for elders or helping the other's work has the right to ask for compensation at divorce (Article 1088).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code of the People's Republic of China (in force from 1 January 2021; Articles 1062, 1063, 1065 and 1088). National Laws and Regulations Database <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?title=%E4%B8%AD%E5%8D%8E%E4%BA%BA%E6%B0%91%E5%85%B1%E5%92%8C%E5%9B%BD%E6%B0%91%E6%B3%95%E5%85%B8&id=ff808081729d1efe01729d50b5c500bf>；full text as reprinted in the Gazette of the Supreme People's Court <http://gongbao.court.gov.cn/Details/7f184078694d811fb3314f6af9accf.html>
- Notes: China only: No official statistics exist for bride price (caili), wedding costs or marital homes, so this entry gives no figures. The compensation under Article 1088 is first negotiated by the two people. If they cannot agree, the court decides, with no fixed formula. A written property agreement helps both sides: the higher earner benefits, and so does the spouse who does more unpaid work such as housework and care.

### 11. If parents pay for a flat, write down at the time of transfer whether it is a loan or a gift
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: No money. On the day the money is paid, write a one-page statement saying whether it is a gift to one person or a loan to one person, signed by the parents and the child. If it is a loan, have both spouses sign, and put the same wording in the transfer note.
- Plain language: Parents who pay for a flat are presumed to be giving it to their own child, unless they state otherwise. Since February 2025, if one spouse's parents paid the full price during marriage without saying the gift was only for their child, a court can award the flat to that child at divorce. Write any loan down on the day, signed by both sides.
- Benefit: If parents fund the purchase of a flat for both spouses before marriage, it is treated as a gift to their own child, unless the parents state that the gift is for both. For money paid during marriage, if there is an agreement, follow the agreement. If there is no agreement or it is unclear, treat it as jointly owned marital property. From 1 February 2025 another rule applies: if during marriage one spouse's parents paid the full price and the gift contract does not say it is only for their own child, the court may award the house to that child when dividing property at divorce. After that, the court weighs several factors to decide whether and how much to compensate the other spouse. These factors are: shared life and having children together, fault in the divorce, each side's contribution to the family, and the market price of the house at divorce. Where one spouse's parents paid only part, or both sets of parents paid, the court awards the house to one party based on the source and proportion of the funds and orders reasonable compensation (applies nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Supreme People's Court (2020). Interpretation (I) on the Application of the Marriage and Family Part of the Civil Code (Fa Shi [2020] No. 22, Article 29). <https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/282071.html>；Supreme People's Court (2025). Interpretation (II) on the Application of the Marriage and Family Part of the Civil Code (Fa Shi [2025] No. 1, Article 8). <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/452771.html>；National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code (Article 1062, paragraph 1, item 4). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>
- Notes: China only: Writing it down clearly helps both sides. Parents who paid no longer need a loan IOU at divorce to protect their contribution. After Interpretation (II) took effect, who paid is itself the basis for dividing the house, not just whose name is on the title deed. The party who receives the money does not have to wait until divorce to learn what it counts as. If it is meant to be a loan, treat it as one: both sides agree it is a loan, agree how it will be repaid, state it in the transfer, and keep repayment records where needed. Missing any one of these means having to explain it in court.

### 12. Large debts one spouse borrows without your signature or later acceptance do not automatically become your debt
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: No money. Do not sign as "joint borrower" or "guarantor" on your spouse's IOU or guarantee letter. Also avoid saying things in chat like "we will pay this back," which could be read as you accepting the debt afterward. The hard part is staying quiet when you are pressed to speak.
- Plain language: If your spouse borrows money behind your back, and it clearly goes beyond the household's everyday spending, it does not automatically become your debt, as long as you did not sign and did not later accept it. The lender has to prove the money was used for your shared life or shared business. But if the money went into a home that became the two of you living in or co-owning, the lender can often prove that much.
- Benefit: Money borrowed in these situations counts as a joint debt of the spouses. First, both spouses signed together. Second, one spouse later ratified it, meaning later acknowledged the debt and both accept it. Third, one spouse borrowed in their own name during the marriage for the family's everyday needs. ### 17. Treat relationship quality as a health account: argue about the issue, not the person, and do not name-call or mock
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=low scope=health -->
- Cost: No money. In an argument, talk only about the matter in front of you. Do not insult, mock, or dig up old grievances. The hard part is keeping your mouth shut in the heat of the moment.
- Plain language: Couples with good relationships tend to be somewhat healthier and have a lower risk of death. The link is small, roughly as large as the link between diet and health. In one experiment, couples who stayed hostile throughout an argument healed small wounds at only 60% of the speed of calm couples.
- Benefit: Pooling 126 studies with more than 72,000 people (a meta-analysis), the higher the marital quality, the better the physical health, with correlation coefficients r between .07 and .21. A correlation of 0 means no relationship and 1 means fully in step. The link with lower risk of death was r = .11. During arguments, cardiovascular response is smaller, r = -.13. The authors say these effects are all small, roughly as large as the link between health habits such as diet and health. In another crossover experiment, 42 healthy couples each spent two 24-hour hospital stays. In one stay they had a supportive conversation; in the other they discussed a point of disagreement. After the argument, experimental blister wounds healed more slowly. Couples who showed more hostility in both sessions healed at only 60% of the speed of low-hostility couples. On the morning after the argument, their blood inflammatory markers (IL-6, tumor necrosis factor) rose more.
- Evidence level: B

Archives of General Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archpsyc.62.12.1377>
- Notes: The reason for rating this B: most of the pooled studies are only follow-up records with no group controls, so it cannot be told whether a bad relationship makes people sick or poor health makes the relationship worse. Some studies still show the link after adjusting for age and income. Robles's original text also notes that some results may be affected by publication bias. Studies that explicitly compared men and women mostly found no sex difference. This section found no pooled analysis that directly compares mortality in "bad marriages" with mortality among people who never married, so "a bad marriage is more harmful to health than staying single" is only an inference. - Cost: No money. Both of you go to the marriage registration office together; it takes a few dozen minutes.
- Plain language: If you held a wedding but did not register after February 1994, you are not legally spouses, only cohabitants. If your partner dies, you cannot inherit as a spouse. If you separate, the court will not handle it as a divorce. Completing registration later fixes this, and the effect can be backdated.
- Benefit: The Civil Code provides that "marriage is established once the marriage registration is completed; parties who have not registered shall complete registration." The Supreme People's Court's interpretation divides unregistered couples who live as spouses into two groups. Those who already met the marriage conditions on or before 1 February 1994 are treated as de facto marriages. For those after that date, the court "shall inform them that they should complete marriage registration." If they do not, they are treated as cohabitants. When one partner dies, the survivor inherits as a spouse under the same principle. A spouse is in the first order of heirs, so an unregistered partner is excluded. For cohabitation itself, the court does not accept claims to "dissolve" it; it only handles disputes over property and child custody. After registration is completed, the marriage takes effect "from the time both parties met the substantive conditions for marriage under the Civil Code." Children are not affected. The "children" in the Civil Code's inheritance chapter expressly include children born out of wedlock, who are first-order heirs on the same footing as children born in marriage.
- Evidence level: A

- Notes: China only: If your partner died before you registered, you are not an heir, but you are not entirely shut out. The Civil Code says a person who "provided more support" to the deceased "may be given an appropriate share of the estate." You must prove this yourself; if you cannot agree, you will have to litigate. Under the ordinary first-instance procedure, cases must be decided within six months, which can be extended; lawyer fees are yours; and the judge decides the amount. So while you are both alive, go and register. Registration works the same as the first time, and both must attend in person; for documents, see item 13 of this section (ID card only). Bride price given before registration can be claimed back; see item 24 of section 8 (bride price paid by transfer). The beneficiaries are you and your spouse.

### 22. If your spouse secretly spends joint money in large sums on livestreamers or transfers it to someone outside the marriage: you can ask the court to divide the joint property without divorcing, and money transferred to an outsider can be sued back
<!-- tags: money=high time=mid will=some gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Litigation. Lawyer fees are yours and are not part of the court costs the losing party must pay. Under the ordinary first-instance procedure the rule is six months, extendable; the simple procedure is three months. You also need to track down the transfer records and tip records yourself, one by one.
- Plain language: If your spouse gives large tips to livestreamers with joint money without consulting you, you can ask the court to split the shared property without divorcing. In a divorce, the court can also give your spouse a smaller share or none. For money transferred to someone outside the marriage, you can sue that person to return it, and saying it has been spent does not excuse them. But all of this requires lawsuits, your lawyer fees are yours, and first-instance rulings take several months.
- Benefit: The Civil Code provides that spouses "have equal rights to deal with joint property." If one spouse hides, transfers, sells, damages, or squanders joint property during the marriage, the other may ask the court to divide the joint property without first divorcing. When property is divided on divorce, the spouse who did this may receive a smaller share or none. Problems discovered after divorce can be raised in a new suit for a fresh division. Interpretation (II), effective 1 February 2025, covers two situations. Article 6 covers tips. If one spouse, without the other's consent, uses joint property to tip on a livestreaming platform in an amount clearly beyond the household's normal level of spending, that counts as "squandering." The other spouse may request division during the marriage, or on divorce the court must support giving the tipping spouse a smaller share or none. Article 7 covers gifts to outsiders. If one spouse gives joint property to someone else for bigamy, cohabitation, or another purpose that breaches the duty of fidelity, the other spouse may argue the gift is void, and the court must support that. Once it is void, the recipient must return it. The other spouse may also use this as the basis for a request for division during the marriage, or for a smaller share or none for the offending spouse on divorce. In a typical case the Supreme People's Court published at the same time, one spouse transferred 730,000 yuan over more than four years during the marriage to a person with whom they had an improper relationship, and the other spouse received 170,000 yuan back. The court ordered the recipient to return the 560,000 yuan actually received; the claim that "part of the money has been spent" was not accepted (nationwide).

- Notes: China only: The tipping provision has a threshold: it requires no consent, an amount clearly beyond household normal spending, and serious harm to joint property. Occasional small tips do not qualify. Interpretation (II) provides only property division and a smaller share as the remedy for tipping; it does not say the money can be recovered from the streamer or the platform. For transfers to outsiders, what you can recover is the money the recipient actually received; amounts they passed back to the spouse must be deducted. You need to gather the evidence yourself: bank and payment app statements, tip records, and chat records that show the relationship. For the division and divorce procedures, see item 15 of this section (exit costs). For which money counts as joint property, see item 10 of this section (money first: the default legal rules). For a spouse borrowing money outside the marriage, see item 12 of this section. To avoid tipping yourself, see item 8 of section 5 (do not tip livestreamers). The beneficiary is you. Conversely, if you are the outsider who received the money, you must return it, and money already spent must still be repaid.

### 23. For divorcing couples with minor children, write into the divorce agreement which parent the children live with, the monthly child support, and the visitation method and schedule: the more specific it is, the less you will litigate after divorce
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some gain=mid scope=money who=infant,child -->
- Cost: Writing it into a consensual divorce agreement costs nothing. The hard part is that you must sit down and talk through the details, such as which day of the month payment is due, which account it goes to, which weekend each parent collects the child, and how summer and winter holidays are split. If you cannot agree, you will have to litigate. The divorce case filing fee is 50 to 300 yuan per case. Lawyer fees are yours and are not part of the court costs the losing party must pay. Under the ordinary first-instance procedure the rule is six months, extendable; the simple procedure is three months. Disputes over child support or visitation that arise after divorce require a separate lawsuit.
- Plain language: Custody, monthly child support, and visitation can be agreed and written down. If you cannot agree, the court decides; with a steady income, support is usually 20 to 30 percent of the payer's monthly income. If the other parent blocks visits, the court can fine or detain them, but it cannot forcibly bring the children to you.
- Benefit: Under the Civil Code, the amount and duration of child support, and the method and timing of visitation, are first agreed by the parties; if they cannot agree, the court decides. Children under two years old generally stay with their mother. For children aged two and over, the decision follows "the principle most favourable to the minor child." For children aged eight and over, their own wishes must be respected. From February 2025, if one parent has committed domestic violence, gambling or drug abuse, cohabited with someone else, or concealed or abducted the child, the court gives priority to the other parent for custody. The child support amount depends on the child's actual needs, the parents' ability to pay, and local living standards. With a steady income, it is generally 20% to 30% of total monthly income. For two or more children the proportion can be raised, but generally not above 50% of total monthly income. Without a steady income, the proportion is applied to the previous year's total income or the average income in the same industry. For example, with a monthly income of 8,000 yuan, this proportion gives 1,600 to 2,400 yuan per month (own calculation). Child support is generally paid until the child turns 18. Even after an agreement or judgment sets the amount, the child may still claim reasonable costs above that amount when necessary, such as illness or schooling costs that exceed it. An agreement that one parent pays no child support is valid, but if the custodial parent's standard of living drops significantly or the child's necessary costs rise significantly, the child can still claim support from the other parent. Unpaid child support under an agreement can be recovered by lawsuit. Once a judgment or mediation document takes effect and the other party does not comply, enforcement can be requested. A person who refuses to help with visitation can be detained or fined, but "the child's person and visitation cannot be directly enforced" (nationwide).

When writing an agreement, specify the payment date, the account, and the end date of payments.
