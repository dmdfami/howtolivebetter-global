# 26. Building a website or platform: licenses, filings and servers

You want to build a website or an app. This section answers four questions: what kind of business it is under the law, which permits you need, where the servers should sit, and who is responsible for what users post and for the money they pay. The lens is personal freedom and money. You are a technical employee hired to build it, and the red lines are in section 11. How to register a company and pay taxes is in section 12, which this section does not repeat. A comparison table of permits and a decision table for choosing a server are in [permit guide for platforms](../docs/做平台要办哪些证.md).

### 1. Don't collect money and pass it on to sellers yourself; have payments settled directly through a licensed payment institution
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. It takes some time to connect to a licensed payment institution, meaning a company that holds a People's Bank of China payment license. Use its split-payment or escrow feature, so buyers' money goes straight to sellers and never enters the platform's own account.
- Plain language: Buyers' money goes into your account first, and you pay sellers from it. That is a payment business. It needs People's Bank of China approval and 100 million yuan of paid-in capital. Without approval, you face shutdown, confiscation of earnings, fines, and possibly prison. The safe route: the money never enters your account.
- Benefit: The regulation defines a payment business as "transferring monetary funds based on electronic payment instructions submitted by the payee or payer." Put simply, it means moving money for others on their instructions. To run one, you must "obtain approval from the People's Bank of China and a payment business license." The minimum registered capital is 100 million yuan, and it must be paid-in monetary capital, meaning the money must actually be deposited in the company's account. Anyone who sets up a non-bank payment institution without lawful approval, or engages in payment business or disguised payment business, will be shut down and have illegal gains confiscated by the People's Bank of China. If illegal gains are 500,000 yuan or more, you also pay 1 to 5 times those gains. If there are no illegal gains, or they are under 500,000 yuan, the fine is 500,000 to 2 million yuan. The legal representative and the person directly responsible pay an extra 100,000 to 500,000 yuan. The Criminal Law also treats "illegally engaging in fund payment and settlement business" as illegal business operation. Serious cases carry up to 5 years in prison, and especially serious cases carry more than 5 years. The Supreme People's Court and Supreme People's Procuratorate set the line for "serious" in their interpretation: illegal business volume of 5 million yuan or more, or illegal gains of 100,000 yuan or more (nationwide, from May 2024).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2023). 非银行支付机构监督管理条例（国务院令第 768 号，第二、六、八、二十七、四十七条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2024/issue_11086/202401/content_6924970.html> ; 中国人民银行 (2024). 非银行支付机构监督管理条例实施细则（中国人民银行令〔2024〕第 4 号，第六十八条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2024/issue_11546/202408/content_6970979.html> ; 全国人大 (1997). 刑法（第二百二十五条第三项）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml> ; 最高人民法院、最高人民检察院 (2019). 关于办理非法从事资金支付结算业务、非法买卖外汇刑事案件适用法律若干问题的解释（第一、三、四条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/201901/t20190131_407161.shtml>
- Notes: China only: Keeping the money out of your account is the only safe design. Industry slang calls pass-through payments "erqing" (double clearing), but official documents do not use that term. The implementation rules use the phrase "disguised payment business," meaning moving money for users on their instructions without approval. The Supreme People's Court and Supreme People's Procuratorate interpretation lists only three situations: fake transactions to cash out, using corporate accounts to cash out, and using checks to cash out. It does not name platform pass-through payments, so you cannot say that such payments "definitely constitute a crime."

### 2. Paid websites usually need a license, self-run shops that don't let others sell don't, marketplaces need a different license, and free sites only need a filing
<!-- tags: money=high time=high will=no gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: The filing is free. The value-added telecom business operating license costs money. It governs whether a website that charges users may operate, and you need a company before you can apply. A business operating only within one province needs registered capital of at least 1 million yuan. A cross-province business needs at least 10 million yuan. Review takes 60 days, and the license is valid for 5 years.
- Plain language: A paid website usually needs a value-added telecom license, which individuals cannot get. The Guangdong provincial telecom regulator says a site that sells only its own goods, with no third-party sellers, does not need one. Neither do shops on WeChat and Alipay (Chinese all-in-one messaging and payment apps). Charging without the license can mean confiscated income, fines, or closure.
- Benefit: The regulation says: "The state licenses commercial internet information services and files non-commercial ones. No one may provide internet information services without a license or completed filing." So a site that charges needs a license first, and a free site needs a filing first. "Commercial" means "providing information or web-design services to online users for payment." Put simply, it means charging users. The marketplace-type license covers online data processing and transaction processing, known in the industry as EDI. The regulation defines it as "providing online data processing and transaction/affair processing services to users over public communications networks or the internet, using application platforms connected to those networks." It "includes transaction processing, electronic data interchange, and network/electronic device data processing." In other words, a site that processes data online for users, or matches buyers and sellers and processes their deals. Operating without a license, or beyond its scope, means confiscation of illegal gains plus a fine of 3 to 5 times those gains. "Beyond scope" means holding a license but doing things it does not cover. If there are no illegal gains, or they are under 50,000 yuan, the fine is 100,000 to 1 million yuan. In serious cases, the website is ordered closed (nationwide). Charging money does not automatically require this license. A Guangdong Communications Administration FAQ from October 2025 says that, per MIIT requirements, three cases do not need it. First, the company sells its own or other companies' goods or services directly on its own website or app, with no third-party sellers joining. Second, the company posts only its own information on its own site or app, and offers no platform for others to publish. Third, the company does business only through platform mini-programs, official accounts, video accounts, or shops such as those on WeChat and Alipay, "with no other independent platform." Conversely, if you let others open shops, or let users post on your site, you fall outside these three cases.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 广东省通信管理局 (2025). 关于办理增值电信业务经营许可证的常见问题解答（2025 年 10 月 17 日，第一项）. <https://gdca.miit.gov.cn/bsfw/bszn/jyxk/tzgg/art/2025/art_6bcbfa4f041247868c0de08e765b33e9.html> ; 国务院 (2000). 互联网信息服务管理办法（国务院令第 292 号，2024 年 12 月 6 日第二次修订，第三、四、七、十九条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1860864.htm> ; 国务院 (2024). 关于修改和废止部分行政法规的决定（国务院令第 797 号，第十八项）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2025/issue_11786/202501/content_6997034.html> ; 国务院 (2000). 电信条例（2016 年第二次修订，第七、九、十三、六十九条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2016/content_5139478.htm> ; 工业和信息化部 (2017). 电信业务经营许可管理办法（工信部令第 42 号，第六、九、十一、十四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2017/content_5240090.htm>
- Notes: China only: Whether your business falls under the EDI type is a judgment you make by checking the definition in the Telecom Services Catalogue. No official text says in plain words that e-commerce platforms with matching and fund handling must hold an EDI license. The MIIT application guide says only "apply for the telecom business license that matches the business definition." So you must decide by reading the definition yourself. Also, this license cannot be held by an individual; the applicant must be a lawfully established company. The three self-run exemptions come from the Guangdong FAQ. It says it follows MIIT requirements, but no equivalent public text was found for other provinces. If unsure, call your local communications administration first. The FAQ also says you do not need an agent: you file the application yourself on the MIIT Telecom Business Market Comprehensive Management Information System, and the official fee is zero.

### 3. Live streaming needs an online culture operating license, and the audiovisual programme license is almost never available to private companies
<!-- tags: money=high time=high will=no gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: The Online Culture Operating License, known as "wangwen zheng," governs whether you can earn money from live streaming or game commentary. A provincial culture authority approves it. Results come within 20 days of acceptance. The license lasts 3 years, and you renew it within 30 days before expiry. The audiovisual programme license governs whether you can put programmes online, and its applicant must be a wholly state-owned or state-controlled entity.
- Plain language: Live streaming and game commentary need a provincial online culture operating license, and its business scope must list online performance. Results come within 20 days of acceptance, and the license lasts 3 years. You must keep every streamed video for at least 60 days. Operating without one can bring a halt order, a warning, and a fine of up to 30,000 yuan. If you keep going, you are placed on the culture-market blacklist.
- Benefit: Companies doing online performance must obtain the license first. The regulation says: "You shall apply to the provincial culture administration for an Online Culture Operating License under the Interim Provisions on Internet Culture Administration, and its business scope must expressly include online performance." Game-playing shows and commentary for viewers fall under the same rules: the regulation says they are "managed by reference to these Measures." All video material from online performances "must be kept for no less than 60 days," so you must keep streamed videos for 60 days yourself. Operating commercial internet culture activities without approval means the authority orders you to stop, gives a warning, and fines you up to 30,000 yuan. If you refuse to stop, you are placed on the culture-market blacklist. Putting programmes online requires a different license: the "Information Network Dissemination Audiovisual Programme License." The applicant must "have legal-person status and be a wholly state-owned or state-controlled entity." Private companies almost never qualify. Without this license, you get a warning and an order to correct, and can be fined up to 30,000 yuan. Serious cases are punished under the Radio and Television Administration Regulations (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 文化部 (2016). 网络表演经营活动管理办法（文市发〔2016〕33 号，第二、四、六、十三、二十条）. <https://zwgk.mct.gov.cn/zfxxgkml/scgl/202012/t20201206_918194.html> ; 文化部 (2011). 互联网文化管理暂行规定（文化部令第 51 号，2017 年经文化部令第 57 号修订，第七、八、九、十六、二十一、二十八条）. <https://zwgk.mct.gov.cn/zfxxgkml/zcfg/bmgz/202012/t20201204_905340.html> ; 广电总局、信息产业部 (2007). 互联网视听节目服务管理规定（第 56 号令，2015 年修订，第二、七、八、二十四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/2022-08/23/content_5722664.htm> ; 国家网信办等七部门 (2021). 关于加强网络直播规范管理工作的指导意见（国信办发文〔2021〕3 号，第 9 项）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-02/10/content_5586472.htm>
- Notes: China only: The 2021 guidance from seven national departments is the clearest on which business needs which license. A platform that earns money from live streaming needs the online culture license plus an ICP filing, which is the website registration with MIIT. Anyone putting programmes online needs the audiovisual license or must complete a registration. News publishers need an internet news information service license. Also, the text that gov.cn reposted in 2025 of the Interim Provisions on Internet Culture Administration is the unamended 2011 version. Check the current text from the Ministry of Culture and Tourism.

### 4. Servers inside mainland China must be filed, apps and mini-programs must be filed before listing, and access providers must hold their own value-added telecom licenses
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Filing is free. It is submitted through your hosting provider along with the server, and usually takes a few days to a few weeks. App and mini-program filings go through the service provider or app store, and are completed within 20 working days once complete materials are submitted. Checking a provider's credentials takes ten minutes.
- Plain language: A server located inside China must be filed before the site opens. Filing is free and usually takes days to weeks. Without it, you must correct the problem and pay a 10,000-yuan fine, and if you refuse to fix it, the site is shut down. Apps and mini-programs also need filing first. Without it, app stores and mini-program platforms may not list you.
- Benefit: The regulation says: "Without filing, no one may conduct non-commercial internet information services inside mainland China." A website that charges nothing must still file if it is hosted inside China. Without filing, you are ordered to correct within a deadline and fined 10,000 yuan. If you refuse to correct, the site is closed. The provider that connects your server to the internet is also regulated: it "may not provide internet access services to any organization or individual conducting non-commercial internet information services without filing." The same applies to providers that collect payments for you. The regulation says such providers "may not provide access or payment-collection services to any unit or individual that has not lawfully obtained an operating license or completed the non-commercial filing." In other words, a legitimate provider should not accept a site that has neither a license nor a filing. Also, machine-room hosting and cloud hosting are called Internet Data Center services in the regulation, and broadband access is called Internet Access services. Both require a license, so the provider must hold one itself. A website whose homepage does not show its filing number must correct it and pay a fine of 5,000 to 50,000 yuan. From September 2023, apps and mini-programs also require filing. An MIIT notice says an app operator inside mainland China "that has not completed filing may not provide internet information services through the app." Distribution platforms include mini-program platforms. The notice requires app stores, mini-program platforms, and phone makers "not to provide network access, distribution, or pre-installation services to apps that have not completed filing." When a provincial communications administration receives complete and accurate materials, it "shall complete the filing within 20 working days." The filing number must be displayed prominently in the app. The Anti-Telecom and Online Fraud Law also says that setting up an app "shall complete licensing or filing with the telecom authority in accordance with national rules" (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 信息产业部 (2005). 非经营性互联网信息服务备案管理办法（信产部令第 33 号，2024 年经工信部令第 68 号修订，第五、十三、十八、二十二条）. <https://www.miit.gov.cn/gyhxxhb/jgsj/cyzcyfgs/bmgz/xxtxl/art/2024/art_84a0cfa0ebd049bbbe751dca9a008e56.html> ; 工业和信息化部 (2017). 电信业务经营许可管理办法（工信部令第 42 号，第二十、二十四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2017/content_5240090.htm> ; 工业和信息化部 (2015). 电信业务分类目录（2015 年版，B11、B14、B21、B25）. <https://www.miit.gov.cn/zwgk/zcwj/wjfb/tg/art/2020/art_e98406cd89844f7e92ea1bcf3b5301e0.html> ; 工业和信息化部 (2023). 关于开展移动互联网应用程序备案工作的通知（工信部信管〔2023〕105 号，第二部分第一、七、八、九项，第三部分第二项）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202308/content_6897341.htm> ; 全国人大常委会 (2022). 反电信网络诈骗法（第二十三条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202209/t20220902_575631.shtml>
- Notes: China only: Check a provider's credentials before you negotiate on price. Look up the provider on the MIIT Telecom Business Market Comprehensive Management Information System, tsm.miit.gov.cn, by full company name or license number. Check website filings at beian.miit.gov.cn. A "machine room" without the matching license means that on the day something goes wrong, you won't even know whom to complain to. If your website is already filed, the app filing only adds app information and does not require re-entering the operator's identity. Moving your servers offshore can avoid the website filing requirement, but it cannot avoid it for an app you want listed in domestic app stores. Users and money both stay in China, so the obligations in items 5 to 10 of this section apply in full, and you add the cost of cross-border data transfer.

### 5. If you let users sell on your platform, you must verify and register sellers, report their information, and keep records for three years
<!-- tags: money=0 time=high will=some gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. You need seller identity checks built into your back office, plus a registration file for each seller. You report tax information every quarter and keep transaction data for three years. The hard part is that this is ongoing work, not a one-off task.
- Plain language: If you let others sell on your platform, you must first check each seller's identity, address, contact details, and licenses, then register them. After that, re-check at least every six months. Report seller identities to the market regulator, and report tax information every quarter. Keep product and transaction records for three years. If you miss any item, you must correct it, and if you don't, the fine is 20,000 to 100,000 yuan.
- Benefit: The law sets out a duty for platforms. In its words, the platform "shall require operators applying to sell goods or provide services on the platform to submit true information on their identity, address, contact details, and administrative licenses, verify and register it, establish registration files, and verify and update them regularly." An operator who wants to sell must give you its identity, address, contact details, and any license it needs. You must check and register these, then re-check them from time to time. How often is "regularly"? The departmental rules are more specific: "at least once every six months." You must also report operator identity information to the market regulator. The tax rules add that you must report it to the tax authority: "in accordance with tax collection and administration laws and regulations, report operators' identity information and tax-related information to the tax authorities." So seller identities go to the market regulator, and seller identity and tax details go to the tax authority. Product, service, and transaction information must be kept "for no less than three years from the day the transaction is completed," counted from the day each transaction ends. If you miss any of these, you are ordered to correct within a deadline. If you don't, the fine is 20,000 to 100,000 yuan. In serious cases, you are ordered to suspend operations for rectification and fined 100,000 to 500,000 yuan. Tax-related information must be reported "within the month after the end of each quarter," so report it in the month after each quarter closes. Late reporting carries the same penalties (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2018). 电子商务法（第十、二十七、二十八、三十一、八十条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf8af050b81> ; 市场监管总局 (2021). 网络交易监督管理办法（市场监管总局令第 37 号，第八、二十四、二十五、三十一条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2021/content_5602020.htm> ; 国务院 (2025). 互联网平台企业涉税信息报送规定（国务院令第 810 号，第二、三、四、十条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2025/issue_12146/202507/content_7030983.html> ; 国家税务总局 (2025). 关于互联网平台企业报送涉税信息有关事项的公告（税务总局公告 2025 年第 15 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202506/content_7029727.htm>
- Notes: China only: Sellers must also register as market entities themselves. The law lists exemptions: individuals selling their own farm produce or household handicrafts, individuals using their skills for legally permitted small-scale services, and small, occasional transactions. "Small and occasional" has a specific figure. Article 8 of the Online Trading Supervision Measures sets it at 100,000 yuan of annual transaction volume. The platform has two more jobs: mark registered and unregistered sellers separately, and do not delete consumer reviews.

### 6. You must manage what users post: review mechanisms, a visible report button, and immediate takedown and reporting of illegal content
<!-- tags: money=low time=high will=high gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: You must pay people to review content, which is the platform's largest long-term expense. Report channels and a record of every action must be kept. The hard part is that someone has to watch all the time, and a single day's gap can cause trouble.
- Plain language: You are responsible for what users post. Registration, post review, comment review, daily patrols, and emergency response all need written rules. Put a report button where users can see it. When you find illegal content, stop its spread at once, keep records, and report it to the regulator. Failing to do so can bring fines of 50,000 to 500,000 yuan, and more in serious cases.
- Benefit: The regulation says a platform "shall fulfill its primary responsibility for information content management." You are responsible for what users post. Specifically, you must "establish sound systems for user registration, account management, information posting review, reply and comment review, page and forum ecosystem management, real-time patrols, emergency response, and handling of online rumors and black-market chain information." Every step, from registration, posting, and comments through daily patrols and incident response, needs written rules. You "shall set up a convenient complaint and report entry in a prominent place," so the report button must be visible and easy to tap. If you find illegal information, you "shall take disposal measures immediately in accordance with law, keep the relevant records, and report to the competent authority." The Cybersecurity Law is more specific. It says: "When you find information whose publication or transmission is prohibited by law or administrative regulations, you shall immediately stop transmitting it, take removal and other disposal measures to prevent its spread, keep the relevant records, and report to the competent authority." If you fail to do so, you are ordered to correct, given a warning, and publicly reported, and may be fined 50,000 to 500,000 yuan. If you refuse to correct, or the case is serious, the fine is 500,000 to 2 million yuan. You may also be ordered to suspend operations, have your site or app closed, or lose your license or business license. The directly responsible person faces an extra fine of 50,000 to 200,000 yuan (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国家互联网信息办公室 (2019). 网络信息内容生态治理规定（网信办令第 5 号，第八、九、十、十六、三十七条）. <http://www.cac.gov.cn/2019-12/20/c_1578375159509309.htm> ; 全国人大常委会 (2025). 网络安全法（2025 年修正，2026 年 1 月 1 日施行，第四十九、六十九条）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm> ; 国务院 (2000). 互联网信息服务管理办法（第十五、十六、二十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1860864.htm>
- Notes: China only: You do not need to invent review rules; copy them from the regulation. Article 15 of the Internet Information Service Measures lists the nine types of content that may not be published. Live-streaming operators have three more duties. They must "establish a live-stream content review platform." They must be able to cut off a live broadcast in progress at any time, which the law calls "immediate blocking." And they must keep live-stream content and logs for 60 days. Online-performance videos also need at least 60 days of retention.

### 7. Information publishing and instant-messaging services must require real identity information from users
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: You need a real-name verification channel, and these channels charge per check. Design the product on a "real name in the back end, voluntary on the front end" basis: store the real name in the back end and show only a nickname on the page.
- Plain language: Sites that let people post or chat must check users' real identity at sign-up and refuse service to anyone who will not give it. Live-streaming platforms keep the real name in the back end and let users pick a nickname for the page. Sites that do not check are first ordered to fix this; if they refuse, or the case is serious, the fine is RMB 50,000 to 500,000.
- Benefit: The law says: "Network operators who provide users with network access or domain-name registration, handle fixed-line or mobile phone registration, or provide information publishing, instant messaging or similar services must require users to provide real identity information when they sign an agreement or confirm the service. Operators may not provide the relevant services to users who do not provide real identity information." If you did not ask for real names, or you let users without real identity keep using the service, you are ordered to correct. If they refuse to correct, or the case is serious, the fine is RMB 50,000 to 500,000. You may also be ordered to suspend the business, halt operations for rectification, have your website or app closed, or lose your licence or business registration. The person directly responsible faces a separate fine of RMB 10,000 to 100,000. Under the live-streaming rules, verification may be based on a mobile phone number or similar means, with the real name held in the back end and the displayed nickname chosen by the user. Live-stream selling requires stricter checks, based on ID document information, the unified social credit code and other real identity information. The unified social credit code is the long number printed on a business licence (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2025). 网络安全法（2025 年修正，2026 年 1 月 1 日施行，第二十六、六十四条；2016 年文本为第二十四、六十一条）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm> ; 国家互联网信息办公室 (2016). 互联网直播服务管理规定（第十二、十六条）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2016-11/04/c_1119847629.htm> ; 国家网信办等七部门 (2021). 网络直播营销管理办法（试行）（国信办发文〔2021〕5 号，第八条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-04/23/content_5601682.htm>
- Notes: China only: The article numbers have moved. The Cybersecurity Law was amended in 2025 and took effect on 1 January 2026. The real-name rule moved from Article 24 to Article 26, and the penalty from Article 61 to Article 64. The wording did not change. If your materials still cite the old numbers, correct them.

### 8. Do not open live-streaming accounts for minors under 16, and handle tips by age
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Add a step at both registration and payment: first check age, then confirm that a guardian has consented.
- Plain language: Anyone under 16 cannot open a streaming account with you. Users aged 16 and over must be identity-checked and need a parent or guardian's consent. Tipping has three tiers: under 8 cannot tip at all; 8 to 16 needs guardian consent; over 16 needs consent or proof of income. A guardian can reclaim tips a child made without consent, and courts will support the refund.
- Benefit: The law says: "Live-streaming service providers must not provide streaming-publisher account registration to minors under 16. For minors aged 16 and over, they must verify identity and obtain consent from a parent or other guardian." The rules also require "a dynamic mechanism for verifying the real identity of streaming publishers", so you must be able to re-check who is streaming at any time. You must also "reasonably limit the single and daily cumulative spending of minors at different ages", which means setting per-purchase and daily spending caps by age. Tipping has three tiers: "no tipping service may be provided to minors under eight; tipping service for minors aged eight to sixteen requires guardian consent; for minors over sixteen, guardian consent or proof of income documents is required." Another rule: where a person with limited civil capacity tips without guardian consent, the guardian may ask for the money back, and courts "shall support" the claim. A person with limited civil capacity is a minor who is not yet fully able to make their own legal decisions (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2020). 未成年人保护法（第七十四、七十六条）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2020-10/22/c_1604928959588622.htm> ; 国务院 (2023). 未成年人网络保护条例（国务院令第 766 号，第三十一、四十四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2023/issue_10806/202311/content_6913813.html> ; 中央网信办秘书局 (2026). 关于加强网络直播打赏规范管理的通知（第 7 项）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-04/13/c_1777815804150225.htm> ; 最高人民法院 (2020). 关于依法妥善审理涉新冠肺炎疫情民事案件若干问题的指导意见（二）（法发〔2020〕17 号，第九条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/230181.html>
- Notes: China only: If a refund dispute arises, handle it in the way most favourable to the minor. The position has changed. The 2022 four-department opinion and the 2020 notice from the State Administration of Radio and Television both said minors may never tip. The April 2026 notice switched to age bands of 8 and 16 but did not say the earlier two documents were void, and officials have not explained how the three fit together. So the statement "minors may never tip" is no longer accurate.

### 9. Act promptly on infringement notices, and restore content if no complaint or lawsuit follows 15 days after the seller's rebuttal is forwarded
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Set up a fixed process: receive the notice, forward it to the seller, receive the seller's rebuttal, and publish the outcome. Keep a record at every step.
- Plain language: When someone sends an infringement notice with initial evidence, you must promptly delete, block or unlink the material and forward the notice to the seller. If you delay, you share liability with the seller for the extra loss. If the seller rebuts, pass the rebuttal to the rights holder and wait 15 days. If the rights holder neither complains nor sues, restore the material.
- Benefit: The rights holder (the owner of a trademark, copyright or patent) "has the right to notify the e-commerce platform operator to take necessary measures such as deletion, blocking, disconnection of links, termination of transactions and services. The notice must include initial evidence of infringement." A bare claim is not enough; it must come with initial evidence. When the platform receives such a notice, it "must promptly take necessary measures and forward the notice to the in-platform seller; if it does not take necessary measures in time, it is jointly liable with the seller for the expanded part of the damage." So if you delay, you share liability with the seller for the extra loss. After the seller rebuts, the platform "shall promptly terminate the measures taken if, within 15 days after the forwarded statement reaches the IP rights holder, it has not received notice that the rights holder has filed a complaint or lawsuit." In plain terms: forward the seller's rebuttal to the rights holder and wait 15 days. If the rights holder neither complains nor sues, restore what was removed or taken down. A platform that knows or should know of infringement and does nothing is jointly liable with the infringer. The IP authorities may also order you to correct within a deadline; if you miss it, the fine is RMB 50,000 to 500,000, or RMB 500,000 to 2,000,000 in serious cases. Conversely, "a person who maliciously sends a wrong notice and causes losses to an in-platform seller shall pay double damages" (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2018). 电子商务法（第四十二、四十三、四十四、四十五、八十四条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf8af050b81> ; 全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第一千一百九十五、一千一百九十六、一千一百九十七条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/233181.html>
- Notes: China only: If you are not an e-commerce platform but an ordinary website, the notice-and-takedown rules of the Civil Code apply instead. The difference is how long you wait after the rebuttal: the E-Commerce Law gives a clear 15 days, while the Civil Code says only "a reasonable period". Publishing the notices received, the sellers' rebuttals and your decisions is an explicit requirement of Article 44 of the E-Commerce Law. A platform that itself organises the upload of pirated material, or knowingly leaves infringement in place, cannot rely on the "delete when notified" rule. See section 9, item 24 (fan-translated comics and re-posted films and TV dramas).

### 10. Do not casually send user data abroad; cross-border transfers have legal conditions and a headcount threshold
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. The simplest option is to keep user data in mainland China. If it really must go abroad, use the standard contract or get certified.
- Plain language: Sending mainland users' information to machines abroad counts as export, even if the servers are overseas. You generally need a security assessment by the cyberspace regulator, or certification, or a standard contract. If the cumulative total is under 100,000 people and no sensitive information is involved, all three are waived. You must also tell users who abroad receives the data and what it is for, and get their separate consent.
- Benefit: Providing mainland users' personal information to overseas companies, or storing it on machines abroad, is legally called providing personal information overseas, and it "shall meet" one of four conditions. First, pass a security assessment organised by the cyberspace regulator. Second, obtain personal information protection certification from a professional body. Third, sign the standard contract with the overseas recipient, in the form set by the cyberspace regulator. Fourth, meet other conditions set by law or administrative regulation. You must also tell users the name and contact details of the overseas recipient, the purpose and method of processing, the types of personal information, and how they can exercise their rights. And you must "obtain the individual's separate consent", asking users about this specifically. The thresholds depend on cumulative headcount. An ordinary company is a data processor other than an operator of critical information infrastructure. Such a company has an exemption line, which reads: "where, from 1 January of the current year, the personal information (excluding sensitive personal information) of fewer than 100,000 individuals has been provided overseas cumulatively", the three routes above are waived: security assessment, certification and standard contract. From 100,000 up to 1 million people, you must sign the standard contract or pass certification. At 1 million people or more, or sensitive personal information of 10,000 people or more, you must apply for a security assessment (nationwide, from March 2024).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2021). 个人信息保护法（第三十八、三十九、四十条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202108/t20210820_527244.shtml> ; 国家互联网信息办公室 (2024). 促进和规范数据跨境流动规定（网信办令第 16 号，第三、四、五、七、八条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2024/issue_11366/202405/content_6954192.html> ; 国家互联网信息办公室 (2022). 数据出境安全评估办法（网信办令第 11 号，第四条）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2022-07/07/c_1658811536396503.htm>
- Notes: China only: Servers located abroad do not mean the data has "not left the country". If a user registers in mainland China and you send their information to a machine abroad, that is an export. One category avoids all three routes of security assessment, certification and standard contract: cross-border shopping, cross-border delivery, and booking flights or hotels. These are cases where transfer is genuinely necessary "to conclude or perform a contract to which the individual is a party". Also note how headcount is counted: cumulatively from 1 January of the current year, not the twelve months back from today.

### 11. Choose a server by first asking whether you can live with downtime, then whether anyone can run it, and only then comparing prices
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Mainstream cloud providers charge hundreds to thousands of RMB a month for a cloud host. Small-provider VPS plans start at tens of RMB a month. A VPS is a virtual small server. Dedicated server colocation costs from several thousand RMB a year, and you must buy the hardware yourself.
- Plain language: Price matters less than what you can rely on when something goes wrong. Big providers have public licences, outage notices and compensation terms, so you can find someone to deal with. Small providers can be half the price, but if they oversell, disappear or get cut off upstream, you have no one to appeal to. A static site with no user data is fine on a cheap VPS of tens of RMB a month.
- Benefit: The difference between the three tiers is not price but what you have on the day something goes wrong. Major cloud providers have a website filing channel, public qualifications, outage notices, and compensation terms in their contracts, so you can find someone when there is a problem. Small providers can be half the price, but when they oversell, disappear or are cut off by upstream providers, you have no one to appeal to. So before negotiating price, check the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) system to see whether the provider holds a value-added telecommunications business operating licence. Dedicated colocation pays off only if both conditions hold: bandwidth or hardware is clearly cheaper, and you have your own people to run it. How to choose: a static site with no user data can use the cheapest VPS. A site with user sign-ups and transactions should choose a cloud host that scales elastically and supports snapshot backups, with backups kept in at least two places.
- Evidence level: C
- Sources: Author's experience; no direct literature. The legal requirements on provider qualifications and filing are in item 4 of this section. Six-month log retention and Multi-Level Protection Scheme obligations are in section 11, item 16.
- Notes: China only: The above is an experience-based judgement with no literature behind it. The one thing you can actually check is the provider's licence. If you want to save money, cut on configuration and bandwidth, not on qualifications.

### 12. If user information leaks from your website or app, act at once: remediate, notify users and regulators, and report attacks to the police
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Write a response plan in advance: who makes the decision, which gap to close first, and what to tell users. It is hard because the instinct during a leak is to hide it and keep users in the dark.
- Plain language: If user information has leaked or may leak, you must fix it immediately and notify the regulator and the affected users. If you hide the leak, you are first ordered to correct it; if you still do not, the fine is up to RMB 1 million, and up to RMB 50 million in serious cases. If users are harmed and you cannot prove you were not at fault, you must pay compensation. If you had protections in place, followed the plan and reported promptly, you may get a lighter penalty or none.
- Benefit: The Personal Information Protection Law says: "Where personal information has leaked, been tampered with or lost, or may have leaked, been tampered with or been lost, the personal information processor shall immediately take remedial measures and notify the departments performing personal information protection duties and the individuals concerned." The notice must state three things: what kind of information leaked, the cause and possible harm; what remedial steps you took and what users can do to reduce the harm; and your contact details. If your remediation actually avoids harm, you may skip notifying users. But if the regulator considers harm possible, it can require you to notify. The Network Data Security Regulations say notice may be given "by telephone, SMS, instant messaging tools, email or public announcement". If you find signs of a crime while handling the incident, you must report it to the public security organs or the state security organs. Failing these duties leads to an order to correct, a warning, confiscation of illegal gains, and, for an unlawful app, an order to suspend or terminate service. If you refuse to correct, the fine is up to RMB 1 million, and the person directly responsible faces RMB 10,000 to 100,000. In serious cases, the fine is up to RMB 50 million or 5% of the previous year's turnover, and the responsible managers face RMB 100,000 to 1 million. Under civil law, if users are harmed and you "cannot prove you were not at fault, you shall bear liability for damages and other tort liability". Large leaks must also be reported on time. The Cyberspace Administration of China's reporting measures classify a leak of 1 million or more people's personal information as a "major cybersecurity incident". For these, ordinary websites and apps must report to the cyberspace regulator of their province "no later than 4 hours" afterwards; one channel is the 12387 hotline, a national cybersecurity reporting line. A summary report is due within 30 days after handling ends. Late, missing, false or concealed reports that cause serious harm are punished more heavily. Conversely, where you have taken reasonable and necessary protective measures, handled the incident according to the plan and reported it promptly, you "may be given a lighter penalty or not be pursued" (nationwide; reporting measures effective from November 2025).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2021). 个人信息保护法（第五十七、六十六、六十九条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202108/t20210820_527244.shtml> ; 全国人大常委会 (2025). 网络安全法（2025 年修正，2026 年 1 月 1 日施行，第二十七、四十四、七十一条；2016 年文本为第二十五、四十二条）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm> ; 国务院 (2024). 网络数据安全管理条例（国务院令第 790 号，第十一条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2024/issue_11646/202410/content_6980863.html> ; 国家互联网信息办公室 (2025). 国家网络安全事件报告管理办法（第四、八、九、十、十一条，附件《网络安全事件分级指南》）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-09/15/c_1759583017717009.htm>
- Notes: China only: A small site that leaks a few hundred or a few thousand records must still remediate and notify under the Personal Information Protection Law; it simply falls below the 4-hour reporting threshold. When you notify users, be specific about what they can do: which password to change, and beware of calls from people posing as customer service. Keep logs for at least six months, so you can later establish where the leak came from; see section 11, item 16 (log retention under the Multi-Level Protection Scheme). How users can protect themselves after a leak is covered in section 14.

### 13. If you sell auto-renewing memberships, remind users clearly before sign-up and before each charge, and make cancellation easy to find
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Spell out auto-renewal on the sign-up page, send a separate reminder before each charge, and provide a cancel option on the account page that works at any time. The difficulty is that some users will cancel as soon as they see a reminder.
- Plain language: If you sell an auto-renewing membership, remind users prominently before they sign up and before every charge. During membership, give them a clear, easy way to cancel or change at any time, with no unreasonable fees. Charging without a reminder can cost you one to five times the illegal gains; if there are no gains, the fine is up to RMB 300,000.
- Benefit: The Online Trading Supervision Measures say that where a service is provided through automatic extension or auto-renewal, the operator must "notify the consumer prominently before the consumer receives the service and before each automatic extension or auto-renewal date, so that the consumer can choose freely". During the service period, the operator must "provide a prominent and simple option to cancel or change at any time, and may not charge unreasonable fees". The Implementing Regulations of the Consumer Rights Protection Law contain the same reminder requirement. If no reminder is given, the penalty under the regulations is an order to correct, possibly a warning, confiscation of illegal gains, and a fine of one to five times the illegal gains. If there are no illegal gains, the fine is up to RMB 300,000. In serious cases, the operator may be ordered to suspend business for rectification or have its business licence revoked. If the cancellation option is not easy to use, you may be ordered to correct within a set time and fined up to RMB 30,000 (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国家市场监督管理总局 (2021, 2025 修正). 网络交易监督管理办法（总局令第 37 号，第十八、四十一条）. <https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/fgs/art/2025/art_4b47c79b8d994a42bba4835997688faa.html> ; 国务院 (2024). 消费者权益保护法实施条例（国务院令第 778 号，第十条第二款、第五十条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202403/content_6940159.htm>
- Notes: China only: The rules do not define what a "prominent" notice looks like. The safe approach is to send a separate reminder before each charge and keep a record of it, so you have evidence if you are complained about. For how users can turn off auto-renewal from their side, see section 5, item 1 (turn off all auto-renewals).
