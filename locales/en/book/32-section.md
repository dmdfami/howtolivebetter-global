# 32. Studying Abroad: Status, Work, Insurance and Degree Recognition on Return

This section runs the numbers on two things: money and personal freedom. It does not discuss whether you should study abroad, and it does not compare schools. The four things below will cost you if you do not know them: what counts as keeping your visa status unbroken, where the limit on working hours is, which insurance and fees you must pay, and whether your degree will be recognised when you return to China.

It covers the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom and Australia, and lists the figures for each country separately. The sources are the official websites of each country's immigration authority and of China's Ministry of Education. **Study-abroad rules change often, more often than China's own regulations do. All figures in this section are current as of September 2026. Before you leave, and before every visa renewal, check again yourself using the links in the sources lines.**

For how to reach your embassy or consulate if something goes wrong abroad, what 12308 (the Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs' consular protection hotline) can do, and how to buy overseas medical and evacuation insurance, see section 21. For the cost-benefit of studying versus working, see section 23. For a stolen account, see section 14; for telecom fraud, see section 8. None of these are repeated here.

### 1. Before paying tuition, check whether the school is on CSCSE's list of recognised institutions
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. Search for the school once in the institution search of CSCSE (China Service Center for Scholarly Exchange, under the Ministry of Education). It takes a few minutes.
- Plain language: To use this degree in China for study, jobs or exams, you can apply to CSCSE for recognition. CSCSE publishes a list of schools whose degrees it has recognised in the past five years. Being on the list does not guarantee approval, and not being on it does not mean refusal. Check before paying tuition.
- Benefit: Article 1 of CSCSE's Measures on the Recognition of Academic Degrees and Credentials Earned Abroad states that recognition is meant to meet applicants' needs for further study, employment and exams. Article 2 states that recognition is carried out on the applicant's own application. CSCSE has an institution search where you look up a school by name. Its user notes say the list shows the foreign institutions named in applications that passed recognition in the past five years, and that it is "for reference only and is not a guarantee of, or basis for, recognition." The notes also say the scope of applications is "not limited to the institutions shown by this search function," that each application is assessed separately, and that whether a school is legitimate and has the authority to issue credentials is only one factor. The list is updated once a year. The 2025 update removed 270 schools that had no recognised credential issued in the past five years. The Ministry of Education's foreign-education supervision information network also publishes regulatory information on Sino-foreign cooperative schools and overseas-run schools.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 中国（教育部）留学服务中心. 认证院校查询（含使用须知）. <http://yxcx.cscse.edu.cn/>；中国（教育部）留学服务中心 (2023). 国（境）外学历学位认证办法（第一、二条）. <http://zwfw.cscse.edu.cn/zwfw/lxfwzxwsfwdt2020/xlxwrz32/409891/rzbf/2026042823373762003/index.html>；教育部教育涉外监管信息网. <https://jsj.moe.gov.cn/>
- Notes: China only: An agent who says the school is "recognised by the Ministry of Education" means nothing; go by what you find yourself on the official site. The list changes, so a school on it when you enrol may not be on it when you graduate. Check it again every year while you study. Sino-foreign cooperative programmes have a separate registration system for recognition. If you enrolled in 2008 or later, you can look up your registration number on the supervision network using your name and Chinese ID number.

### 2. Track the US F-1 rule case: the planned "up to four years" limit was suspended by a court the day before it was due to take effect on September 15, 2026, so "until you finish" still applies
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Copy the programme end date on your I-20 into your calendar, with a reminder 90 days ahead. The I-20 is the form your school issues to confirm your eligibility to study. Check your international student office's notices once each term to see whether the case has moved on.
- Plain language: The US planned to limit F-1 students to four years, with 30 days to leave after finishing. A federal court suspended that rule the day before it was due to take effect. So the old rule still applies: you can study for as long as your programme lasts, then have 60 days to leave.
- Benefit: The Department of Homeland Security published a rule on July 17, 2026, due to take effect on September 15, 2026. Under it, F-1 students would no longer be admitted for "duration of status" (until they finish their programme). Instead they would get a fixed admission period of up to four years, and would have to apply for an extension if they needed more time. After finishing a programme or practical training, they would have only 30 days to prepare to leave. On September 14, 2026, Judge Saylor of the US District Court for the District of Massachusetts postponed the rule's effective date under Section 705 of the Administrative Procedure Act. The postponement applies nationwide and covers the whole rule. The court did not strike down the rule. It denied the request to do so, but that request may be brought again later. The case is still ongoing. Under this court order, people holding F, J and I visas are still admitted for "duration of status," the 60-day departure grace period still applies, and no separate extension application is needed.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: DHS (2026). Establishing a Fixed Time Period of Admission and an Extension of Stay Procedure for Nonimmigrant Academic Students, Exchange Visitors（2026-07-17 公布，原定 2026-09-15 生效）. <https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/17/2026-14439/establishing-a-fixed-time-period-of-admission-and-an-extension-of-stay-procedure-for-nonimmigrant>；Presidents' Alliance on Higher Education and Immigration v. U.S. Department of Homeland Security, No. 1:26-cv-13799-FDS (D. Mass. Sept. 14, 2026)，法院案卷. <https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/74661796/presidents-alliance-on-higher-education-and-immigration-v-united-states/>；Yale Office of International Students & Scholars (2026-09-14). Important Update: Court Action on the D/S Rule. <https://oiss.yale.edu/news/important-update-court-action-on-the-ds-rule>；AILA (2026). One Day Before Taking Effect, Federal Court Postpones the F, J, and I Fixed Admission Period Rule. <https://www.aila.org/blog/think-immigration-one-day-before-taking-effect-federal-court-postpones-the-f-j-and-i-fixed-admission-period-rule>；8 CFR 214.2(f). <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2>
- Notes: This entry is current as of September 25, 2026. The government can appeal to the First Circuit Court of Appeals, and DHS can also reissue a revised rule. So if you will study for several years, do not assume the old rule will stay the same. The court has scheduled a status hearing for October 2, 2026, where both sides will report on progress, so check your school's notices again after that date. If the new rule takes effect later, an extension must be applied for before the admission period expires; it will not renew automatically. Under the old rule, if you leave a programme early, your departure period is different from the one for those who finish, so ask your international student office first. J visas are covered by the same rule, but the details differ from F-1, so check the rules for your own visa category.

### 3. Before you take a job while studying in the US, Canada, the UK or Australia, check the hour limits and count term time, holidays and job types separately
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Keep your own weekly record of hours. The hard part is an employer saying "a few extra hours won't be checked." Don't believe it.
- Plain language: Check the limit for your country, the type of work and whether it is term time. During term, the US allows 20 hours a week on campus and Canada 24 hours a week off campus. The UK allows 20 or 10 hours a week, and Australia 48 hours a fortnight. Holidays, internships and remote work follow different rules.
- Benefit: US F-1 students may work on campus for no more than 20 hours a week during term. An off-campus job approved under paragraph (f)(9) of 8 CFR 214.2 is also capped at 20 hours a week during term. Eligible holiday periods allow full-time work. Curricular practical training (CPT) and similar work follows its own rules, depending on the type of placement and the scope of approval, so the caps above cannot be applied to all off-campus work. For UK student visas, working hours also depend on whether the school and course meet the rules. Students on full-time degree-level or higher courses sponsored by a higher education institution with a good compliance record may work 20 hours a week during term. For full-time courses below degree level sponsored by such an institution, the limit is 10 hours a week. Eligible short exchange programmes at overseas higher education institutions also allow 20 hours a week during term. Other courses, including part-time courses, do not allow work. Self-employment is generally not allowed (working for yourself), and neither is work as a professional sportsperson or as an entertainer. The basis is the student part of the UK Immigration Rules, Appendix Student, paragraphs ST26.1, ST26.5 and ST26.8. Eligible students in Canada may work off campus for up to 24 hours a week during regular academic sessions, and hours from several off-campus jobs are added together. The basis is section 186(v) of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Regulations (IRPR). Eligible students may work full-time during regular scheduled school breaks. Remote work for an employer outside Canada does not count toward the 24-hour limit, but the student must still meet the study permit conditions. For eligibility during your last term and between programmes, see item 4 of this section (full-time status, leave of absence and transfers). Australian student visa holders may work up to 48 hours per fortnight while their course is running. Research-based master's and doctoral students and their family members are not subject to this limit.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 8 CFR 214.2(f)(9)、(10). <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2>；UK Home Office. Immigration Rules Appendix Student（ST26.1、ST26.5、ST26.8）. <https://www.gov.uk/guidance/immigration-rules/immigration-rules-appendix-student>；IRCC. Work off campus as an international student. <https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/work/work-off-campus.html>；IRCC. Can I continue to work during scheduled school breaks? <https://www.ircc.canada.ca/english/helpcentre/answer.asp?qnum=499>；Australian Government Department of Home Affairs. Student visa (subclass 500). <https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/student-500>
- Notes: This entry's working conditions were last checked on October 21, 2026. In the US, off-campus work requires advance authorisation for your category. Where you must apply to USCIS for permission, you may not start work while your Form I-765 is still pending. CPT is authorised by your school's designated official and endorsed on your I-20; not all off-campus work requires an I-765. In Canada, older permits printed 20 hours a week. As long as you still meet the conditions, you may work up to 24 hours in practice; the current rules apply. "Regular holidays" means breaks scheduled by the school, not time off you take by stopping classes yourself. In the UK, the self-employment restriction covers taking private clients, running an online shop and freelancing. Exceptions that apply during a visa switch must be checked against the official UK rules.

### 4. Before cutting your course load to part-time, taking a leave, withdrawing or transferring, check your work eligibility and exceptions first
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Before you cut courses, take leave, withdraw or change schools, speak to your school's international student office and find out your immigration status.
- Plain language: Whether you can work depends on your country and your study status. In Canada, if you are on approved leave or transferring and not studying, you cannot work off campus on your study permit. A light course load in your last term, or moving between programmes, can still allow work if you qualify.
- Benefit: In Canada, if you work off campus under your study permit, you may not do so during an approved leave of absence. If you are transferring and not studying, you also may not work off campus until you resume your studies. There is one exception for your final term: if you move to part-time only because you no longer need a full course load to finish, and you were full-time before, you may still work off campus. All other work conditions must also be met. Between finishing one programme and starting another, a separate set of conditions allows full-time work, and all of them must be met together. You must already have been eligible to work off campus during your earlier studies. You must hold a valid study permit, or have applied to extend it before it expired. You must also have written confirmation from your original school that you completed the programme, and an acceptance letter for a new full-time programme at a designated learning institution (DLI). The new programme must start within 150 calendar days of receiving the confirmation of completion. This is different from leaving school before finishing and transferring: a new acceptance letter alone is not enough to start work. In the US, to work on campus, an F-1 student's SEVIS record must show "Active." SEVIS is the US system that records international students' status, and "Active" means you are in valid study. Students must also meet the academic requirements for keeping their status, so ask your school to check whether an exception applies before reducing your course load. In the UK, the right to work depends on the school and type of course, and part-time courses do not allow work.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: IRCC. Work off campus as an international student. <https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/work/work-off-campus.html>；U.S. Department of Homeland Security, Study in the States. Working in the United States. <https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/students/work/working-in-the-united-states>；U.S. ICE SEVP, SEVIS Help Hub. Terminate a Student. <https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/sevis-help-hub/student-records/completions-and-terminations/terminate-a-student>；UK Home Office. Immigration Rules Appendix Student（ST26.1）. <https://www.gov.uk/guidance/immigration-rules/immigration-rules-appendix-student>
- Notes: Before you cut your course load, ask what load would stop counting as full-time. This goes wrong sooner than work does. In the US, if your school terminates your SEVIS record because you broke the status rules, there is no grace period. You must either apply to restore your status or leave immediately. If your early withdrawal is approved, you have 15 days to leave. The rules in the other three countries differ, so check the immigration authority's pages for your country. Do not wait for a formal letter from the school before acting.

### 5. US: report a new address to immigration within 10 days of moving
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Submit the address change online once. It takes ten minutes.
- Plain language: If you move house in the US, the law requires you to tell immigration within 10 days. Failing to report is not a small matter. It breaks the law in itself, and immigration letters sent to you may not reach you.
- Benefit: Under US federal immigration regulation 8 CFR 265.1, a foreign national who must register under the law must report a change of address within 10 days. Report it to USCIS (US Citizenship and Immigration Services), using its required method. The report covers both the change of address and the new address.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 8 CFR 265.1. <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-265/section-265.1>；USCIS. AR-11, Alien's Change of Address Card. <https://www.uscis.gov/ar-11>；UK Government. Report a change of circumstances if you have a visa or expired BRP. <https://www.gov.uk/change-circumstances-visa-brp>；Australian Government Department of Home Affairs. See your visa conditions（Student visa subclass 500）. <https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/already-have-a-visa/check-visa-details-and-conditions/see-your-visa-conditions?product=500>
- Notes: Once you have reported to immigration, the school's SEVIS record (your student status file) must be updated separately. In the UK, if you hold a visa or a BRP (biometric residence permit), you must report changes in circumstances, and an address change is made in your UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) account. Australian student visas carry condition 8533, titled "inform the school of your address." I did not find an equivalent rule for Canada in this check. Deadlines and methods differ by country, so follow the official immigration website of your country.

### 6. Before you leave and while you study, check the Ministry of Education's study-abroad alerts
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Check once before you leave, then once each term.
- Plain language: China's Ministry of Education issues alerts about specific countries or regions where recent security or policy changes may be harmful to Chinese students. These are official public risk warnings, published in writing, so they are more reliable than agents' claims. Checking costs nothing.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Education publishes study-abroad alerts through its foreign-education supervision information network. Four were issued in 2025. Alert No. 1 pointed out that some US states' higher-education laws contain provisions negative toward China (April 9, 2025). Nos. 2 and 3 pointed out that cases targeting Chinese citizens are common in the Philippines and that the study environment is poor (July 18 and August 30, 2025). No. 4 said that Japan's security situation and study environment are poor, and advised caution in planning to study there (November 16, 2025).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 教育部教育涉外监管信息网. 预警信息. <https://jsj.moe.gov.cn/>；教育部发布 2025 年第 4 号留学预警. <https://jsj.moe.gov.cn/n2/2/2/2060.shtml>；第 1 号. <https://jsj.moe.gov.cn/n2/2/2/2001.shtml>
- Notes: China only: An alert is not a ban, and it does not mean you cannot go. It gives you an official basis for weighing local risks. The alert list changes with events, and this section is not updated regularly, so use the current page on the website. For personal safety abroad and consular protection, see section 21.

### 7. Australia: OSHC must cover your whole stay with no gaps, or you may be refused entry at the border
<!-- tags: money=high time=low will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Several thousand to tens of thousands of yuan (RMB). You buy it up front for the length of your visa, so a multi-year course means a multi-year policy
- Plain language: Australia writes student health insurance into the visa conditions. It is not optional. The policy must cover your whole stay. When you renew and switch policies, there can be no gap in cover. If you cannot show proof of insurance on arrival, you may be refused entry on the spot. Family members who join you need this cover too.
- Benefit: The Australian Department of Home Affairs requires student visa applicants and their family members to hold Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC), and to keep it for the entire time they are in Australia, with no gaps. The cover must come from an insurer approved in Australia. People who qualify for an exemption do not need to buy it. There must be no gap between the new policy and the previous visa's policy. If you cannot show proof of cover on arrival, you may be refused entry. If you arrive before your course starts, the policy start date must be the day you actually arrive in Australia.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Australian Government Department of Home Affairs. Student visa (subclass 500). <https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/student-500>
- Notes: If your school arranges the insurance, ask for the insurer's name, the policy start and end dates, and the policy number. You need to enter these on the visa application. A policy that is too short will cause problems when you renew, so it is safer to buy cover that runs a while past graduation.

### 8. UK: on top of the visa fee, you pay an annual health surcharge of £776 per student, paid upfront when you apply
<!-- tags: money=high time=low will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Visa fee £558. Health surcharge £776 per year. Both are paid in full when you apply
- Plain language: Going to the UK costs more than the visa fee. You also prepay the health surcharge for the length of your visa. Once paid, you can use the UK's National Health Service. A two-year visa means a single payment of £1,552. Add the visa fee, and the full amount is due before you leave. Include it in your budget.
- Benefit: The UK student visa fee is £558. This applies whether you apply from outside the UK, or extend or switch within the UK. There is also the Immigration Health Surcharge. You pay it when you apply, for the full length of your visa, and it gives you access to the National Health Service. Students and their dependants pay £776 per year. For example, a two-year visa costs £1,552. Other applicants pay £1,035 per year. Visas longer than 6 months but shorter than 1 year are charged as a full year.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: UK Government. Student visa. <https://www.gov.uk/student-visa>；UK Government. Pay for UK healthcare as part of your immigration application. <https://www.gov.uk/healthcare-immigration-application>
- Notes: Amounts and exchange rates change, so use the figures currently shown on the two pages listed in Sources. Students on degree courses who are 18 or older can generally stay up to 5 years. Below degree level, the maximum is 2 years. Use that length to estimate how much surcharge you will owe.

### 9. Allow time before you return home: CSCSE verification takes 10 to 20 working days
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no gain=mid scope=time -->
- Cost: Apply and pay online. Preparing documents plus the review takes about a month in total, counted in working days
- Plain language: A foreign degree must be verified before you can use it in China. The review alone takes 10 to 20 working days, and preparing your documents takes extra time. Autumn graduate recruitment, civil service exam registration and hukou (household registration) applications all have deadlines, so do not wait until you need the certificate.
- Benefit: Foreign qualifications are verified through the online service hall of the China Service Center for Scholarly Exchange (CSCSE), an agency under China's Ministry of Education. The process has four steps: register and complete real-name verification, submit your application and documents online, pay online, then wait for assessment and review. The published processing time is 10 to 20 working days. Working days exclude weekends and public holidays. This period is the CSCSE's review time.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 中国（教育部）留学服务中心. 网上服务大厅·学历学位认证. <http://zwfw.cscse.edu.cn/>
- Notes: China only: The documents you need are a diploma certificate, a passport or travel document, a residence card or visa stamp, an ID photo, and an authorisation statement. The system pulls your entry and exit records automatically, so keep your visa pages from time abroad. The CSCSE website also posts notices about which certificates have been invalidated and which schools' applications are suspended. Check whether your school is affected before you apply.

### 10. Before you apply, search your school's name in CSCSE notices: schools under enhanced review must wait at least 60 working days
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. Before you apply, search your school's name in the notices section of the CSCSE website
- Plain language: The CSCSE names some foreign universities for enhanced review of their qualifications, and it suspends applications from some schools. Searching your school's name before you enrol is much quicker than appealing later.
- Benefit: The CSCSE has been publishing notices on enhanced verification for some foreign universities, with an extra check on their degrees. The latest, notice No. 9 (October 28, 2025), named four schools. During enhanced review, applications from these schools are, in principle, processed for no less than 60 working days, counting from and including the day the application is submitted. The basis is Article 28 of the CSCSE's Measures for Verification of Overseas Academic Qualifications. It allows the CSCSE to start enhanced review when certification data is abnormal or it receives reports of problems. It can then ask applicants, schools and others for materials, and extend the verification period. In May 2025 the CSCSE also issued a notice of renewed enhanced verification for three schools. It has also issued notices suspending applications from certain foreign institutions. A fraud-prevention notice dated August 2025 states: "Any institution or individual that claims to offer 'guaranteed certification', 'internal-channel certification', 'pandemic back-filling', 'expedited certification' or similar services is engaging in false advertising."
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 中国（教育部）留学服务中心. 关于对部分国外院校学历学位认证加强认证审查的公告（九）（2025-10-28）. <https://www.cscse.edu.cn/cscse/sy/tzgg/2025102809225023345/index.html>；中国（教育部）留学服务中心 (2025-08-29). 关于谨防借国（境）外学历学位认证实施诈骗的重要提示. <https://www.cscse.edu.cn/cscse/sy/tzgg/2025082913242989351/index.html>；中国（教育部）留学服务中心 (2023). 国（境）外学历学位认证办法（第二十八条）. <http://zwfw.cscse.edu.cn/zwfw/lxfwzxwsfwdt2020/xlxwrz32/409891/rzbf/2026042823373762003/index.html>；中国（教育部）留学服务中心. 通知公告. <https://www.cscse.edu.cn/cscse/sy/tzgg/index.html>
- Notes: China only: "Enhanced review" means one more check and a longer wait. It does not mean the degree will definitely fail to be verified. According to CSCSE warnings, any agency that promises "guaranteed certification" or "internal-channel fast certification" is engaging in false advertising. Hiring someone across borders to write your thesis or take online classes for you is a serious risk. If discovered, the qualification can be voided, and it may also affect your visa record.

### 11. US: after graduating on OPT, count your days without a job. The total cannot exceed 90 days, and with a STEM extension the combined total cannot exceed 150 days
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Record the start and end dates of each period without a job. Once you find work, report your employer to your school straight away. The difficulty is that when job hunting goes badly, the day count keeps climbing
- Plain language: OPT is the US permission that lets F-1 students work after graduation. Your status depends on having a job during this period. If the days without work add up to more than 90, your status may be terminated. If you have a STEM extension, the total across both periods must not exceed 150 days. If your school's system has no employer on record, the days count as unemployed.
- Benefit: US immigration regulation 8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(ii)(E) says that during post-graduation OPT, your status depends on whether you are employed. Total days without work must not exceed 90. If you have a 24-month STEM extension, the total for the whole OPT period must not exceed 150 days, including the days before the extension. The official guidance from the Student and Exchange Visitor Program (SEVP) states that if there is no employer information in the SEVIS record, the days count as unemployed. Once the total reaches 90 days, an SEVP official can manually terminate the student's status. SEVIS is the US database of student status records. According to US Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) guidance, you may apply from 90 days before graduation, and no later than 60 days after graduation. After your school recommends you in SEVIS, you must file within 30 days.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(ii)(E)、(f)(11)(i)(B). <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2>；U.S. ICE SEVP, SEVIS Help Hub. F-1 Optional Practical Training (OPT). <https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/sevis-help-hub/student-records/fm-student-employment/f-1-optional-practical-training-opt>；USCIS. Optional Practical Training (OPT) for F-1 Students. <https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/students-and-exchange-visitors/optional-practical-training-opt-for-f-1-students>；DHS (2026). Optional Practical Training Fees（拟议规则，2026-10-08 公布）. <https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/08/2026-20660/optional-practical-training-fees>
- Notes: This entry is current as of October 14, 2026. The OPT rules are changing, and two things need watching. First, in item 2 (the court case over the new F-1 rule), the suspended new rule changed the 60-day filing limit after graduation to 30 days. The eCFR website currently shows the new rule's text, so the safest approach is to file your application before you graduate. Second, on October 8, 2026, the Department of Homeland Security proposed fees for OPT: USD 70,000 for the first OPT, and USD 30,000 for each later one. This is only a proposal. Public comment runs until November 9, 2026, and it is not in effect. If you plan to finish your studies and stay in the US to work, count this possible cost into your budget before paying tuition, and check again before you file.

### 12. Canada: before applying for a study permit, get a provincial attestation letter (PAL/TAL) through your school. From 2026, master's and doctoral students at public universities do not need one
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: No extra cost. Apply through the school that admitted you. In most cases you must accept the offer and pay some or all of your tuition before the school issues the letter
- Plain language: To study in Canada, most people need a provincial letter when they apply for a study permit. It shows you have a place in that province's study quota. You cannot add it later. If your permit is approved or refused and you apply again, or you transfer schools, you need a new letter. Master's and doctoral students at public universities do not need one.
- Benefit: Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) says the attestation letter is issued by the province or territory where you will study. It confirms you have been allocated a study place there. Most study permit applications must include it, submitted together with the application. It cannot be provided later. Students in kindergarten through Grade 12 do not need one. From January 1, 2026, students in master's or doctoral degree programs at public institutions also do not need one. Students going to Quebec use a Certificate of Acceptance (CAQ) issued by Quebec instead. Exchange students who pay no tuition, students renewing at the same school and level, and several other special cases are also exempt. Those who are exempt must include proof of exemption with their application. A letter is generally valid only in the year it was issued, so a letter issued in 2026 is valid until December 31, 2026. It only needs to be valid when you apply. It does not matter if it expires before classes start. After a study permit is approved or refused and you apply again, you need a new letter. From January 22, 2025, transferring schools also requires a new letter.
- Evidence level: A
