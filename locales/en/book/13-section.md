# 13. Emergencies: what to do first

This section covers what to do first, what to do next, and what not to do in an emergency. The entries on first aid and wilderness emergencies measure specific causes of death and survival rates. The entries on threats and scams measure money and personal freedom. The two kinds of numbers cannot be converted into each other. These entries are written first for you, your spouse, and your immediate family. Cardiac arrest happens at home in 79.2% of cases, so the people you are most likely to save are the people in your home. Friends and colleagues come second. Helping strangers also has some benefit, but it ranks lowest, and you can be falsely accused or drawn into a legal case. That is why these entries put the steps that protect you, and the legal protections, up front.

This section only covers actions that take minutes. Recognizing a scam, or dealing with someone who uses private material to demand money, takes days to handle, so it is not covered here. The hard anti-fraud rules and the seven most common scams are in section 8, item 3 (Hard anti-fraud rules). Being threatened with intimate photos or recorded video is in section 8, item 32 (When someone uses compromising material to demand money). If money has already been transferred, call 110 (the police) or 96110 (the anti-fraud hotline) immediately to request a payment stop, see section 8, item 2 (Payment stop).

The entries in this section are grouped by theme below. Item numbers are in parentheses.

**CPR and choking:** someone collapses and is not breathing normally (item 1), an elderly person has fallen, so do not rush to lift them (item 2), someone is choking and cannot speak (item 26), an infant is choking and cannot cry (item 43), infant CPR (item 44).

**Sudden medical emergencies:** sudden facial droop and weakness on one side (item 3), spinning dizziness and unsteadiness (item 4), one eye suddenly going dark (item 5), one eye swollen and painful (item 6), chest pressure pain (item 7), tearing-type severe pain (item 8), "the worst headache of my life" (item 9), an elderly person who becomes sluggish after hitting their head (item 10), a leg that suddenly swells (item 11), anaphylactic shock (item 15), someone having a seizure and collapsing (item 16), a diabetic who suddenly shakes (item 17).

**Injuries and burns:** heavy bleeding, press on the wound first (item 12), dog or cat bites (item 13), cool a burn with cold water (item 14), do not pull out an object stuck in the body (item 40), suspected fracture, do not move (item 41), a finger severed (item 47), a permanent tooth knocked out whole (item 48).

**Poisoning, fire, and accidents:** someone shocked by electricity, cut the power first (item 18), a carbon monoxide alarm is sounding (item 19), accidental swallowing of cleaning products, pesticides, or medicine (item 20), chemicals splashed on the body (item 21), dizziness with no sweating in high heat (item 22), a heatstroke patient drinks lightly salted water (item 23), crawl low in a fire (item 24), someone is drowning (item 25), during an earthquake, check the building first (item 30), a child swallowed a button battery or magnets (item 45), someone is drunk, do not let them sleep alone (item 46).

**Wilderness:** lost in a remote area, stay where you are (item 27), shivering and slurred speech are signs of hypothermia (item 28), snakebite, do not cut or suck (item 29), encounters with bears, wild boars, or wild dogs (item 31), thunderstorms in the wild (item 32), going to high altitude, ascend slowly (item 33), a tick attached to the body (item 34), boil water in the wild before drinking it (item 35).

**Conflict and after rescuing:** demanded money in a remote area (item 36), coming across a group fight (item 37), injured while rescuing and losing money (item 39).

**Sexual assault and HIV exposure:** possible HIV exposure (item 38), after sexual assault (item 42).

### 1. A person collapses, does not respond, and is not breathing normally: press hard on the chest now, and have someone call 120 and find an AED
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. A CPR course from the Red Cross or a hospital takes half a day to a full day, and one course is enough for years. Even without training, you can do this: stack your hands and place them at the midpoint of the line between the nipples, then press down hard about twice a second, without stopping.
- Plain language: Cardiac arrest often happens at home, and people who get chest compressions right away are more likely to leave the hospital alive. If someone does not respond and is not breathing normally, start pressing hard on the chest. Occasional gasping does not count as normal breathing. Stack your hands and press, while someone else calls 120 and finds an AED.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis combined 79 studies with 142,740 people. Only 7.6% of people whose hearts stopped outside a hospital left the hospital alive. Bystander CPR was linked to higher survival to discharge. After stratifying by each study's original survival rate, survival to discharge among people who received bystander CPR was 3.9% to 16.1%. These two figures come from different stratifications and cannot be read as the change before and after compressions. Bystanders saw the arrest in 53% of cases, but only 32% actually received bystander compressions. China's BASIC-OHCA registry recorded 38,227 emergency cases. Of these, 30,282 (79.2%) cardiac arrests happened at home. In this group, the bystander compression rate was 20.3%, and 1.2% left the hospital alive. The order is to start compressions first, then have a second person call and get the AED. Follow the machine's voice prompts once it is switched on. Whether to start depends on whether breathing is normal. The 2025 International Red Cross first aid guidelines state that in the first few minutes after the heart stops, a person may still breathe irregularly and with effort, or gasp loudly. This is not normal breathing. If you are unsure whether breathing is normal, treat it as abnormal. Any abnormal breathing, including gasping, irregular breaths, or no breathing, requires CPR immediately.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Sasson C 等 (2010). Predictors of survival from out-of-hospital cardiac arrest: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Circulation: Cardiovascular Quality and Outcomes. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCOUTCOMES.109.889576> ; Zheng J 等 (2023). Incidence, process of care, and outcomes of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest in China: a prospective study of the BASIC-OHCA registry. The Lancet Public Health. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(23)00173-1> ; International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies (2025). International First Aid, Resuscitation and Education Guidelines 2025，第 154 页：「If the person is breathing abnormally (gasping, taking irregular breaths or not breathing), start CPR immediately」；第 161 页：「In the first few minutes after cardiac arrest, a person may have agonal breathing (an irregular and laboured breathing pattern, or gasping noisily). This type of breathing is not normal. If there is any doubt about whether breathing is normal, assume it is not」. <https://www.ifrc.org/sites/default/files/2026-03/IFRC%20International%20First%20Aid,%20Resuscitation%20and%20Education%20Guidelines%202025.pdf> ; 全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第一百八十四条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>
- Notes: China only: Civil Code Article 184 says a rescuer who voluntarily gives emergency aid and injures the person helped is not civilly liable. Infants under 1 year need a different method that includes breaths, see item 44 of this section (infant CPR). Do not pinch the philtrum first, do not look for family first, and do not film a video first. Do not hold back for fear of breaking ribs.

### 2. An elderly person or someone else has fallen: squat down, call out to them, and call 120, but do not rush to lift them up. For strangers, walking away is also legal in China. If you stop, do not lift them
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. Asking one question and making one phone call takes a few minutes. The hard part is holding back the urge to reach out and help. For strangers, there is another cost to count: calling 120 leaves your phone number, which may lead to a callback, and you may be asked to give a statement. If the family targets you, a lawsuit can take months to begin. And even if you win in the end, you pay your own lawyer's fees.
- Plain language: This approach is mainly for your own elderly relatives and people you know. Squat down, call their name, and check whether they are breathing normally. If they have no normal breathing, follow item 1. If they are breathing normally, do not lift them, because forced lifting can make a stroke or fainting worse, and anyone with back pain or a suspected fracture must not be moved.
- Benefit: The Chinese Ministry of Health's Technical Guideline for Fall Intervention in Older Adults states two key points. One: "Falls are the leading cause of injury death among people aged 65 and over in China." The other: "When an elderly person is found to have fallen, do not rush to lift them up; handle the situation according to circumstances." If the person is unconscious, call emergency services immediately. If they are vomiting, turn the head to one side and clear the mouth and nose. If breathing or heartbeat has stopped, start chest compressions immediately. "If moving is necessary, it should be done smoothly, and the person should be laid flat where possible." If the person is conscious, first ask whether they remember how the fall happened: "If they cannot remember, it may be fainting or a cerebrovascular accident." If they have a severe headache, or a crooked mouth, slurred speech, or weak limbs, "lifting the elderly person immediately may worsen cerebral hemorrhage or cerebral ischemia and make the condition worse." If there are signs of fracture, or back or waist pain, or loss of bladder or bowel control, "if you have no relevant professional knowledge, do not move them casually." For any of these, call emergency services immediately. If the elderly person wants to stand up on their own, you may help them up slowly. "Have them sit or lie down to rest and observe, and only leave once it is confirmed that they are fine." On the worry about being falsely accused, the law reads as follows. Article 90 of the Supreme People's Court's Interpretation on the Application of the Civil Procedure Law has two sentences. The first: "A party must provide evidence to prove the facts on which its claims are based." The second: "Before a judgment is made, if a party fails to provide evidence, or the evidence is insufficient to prove its factual claims, the party bearing the burden of proof bears the adverse consequences." The original text of Article 1198 of the Civil Code reads: "Operators and managers of business and public premises, such as hotels, shopping malls, banks, stations, airports, sports venues and entertainment venues, or organizers of mass activities, who fail to fulfill their duty to ensure safety and cause harm to others, shall bear tort liability." Civil Code Article 184: "A rescuer who voluntarily gives emergency aid and causes harm to the person helped shall not bear civil liability." The Supreme People's Court has published a typical case. A pharmacy operator performed CPR on a person whose heart had stopped and broke that person's ribs. The recipient was hospitalized for 18 days and claimed more than 9,000 yuan (RMB) in compensation. The court ruled that the rescuer bore no civil liability.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: 卫生部 (2011). 老年人跌倒干预技术指南（「老年人跌倒后的处理」）. 全文 PDF 见广东省疾病预防控制中心 <http://cdcp.gd.gov.cn/gcdcdfiles/editor/uploadfile/20111027094832117.pdf>，现场处理部分全文见贵南县人民政府转载 <https://www.guinan.gov.cn/lnb/zc1__zwgk/zc/aztfl/qt/content_27609778>；全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第一百八十三、一百八十四条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>；最高人民法院 (2023). 人民法院抓实公正与效率践行社会主义核心价值观典型案例（案例十三，齐某某诉孙某某健康权纠纷案，辽宁省康平县人民法院）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/408162.html>；最高人民法院 (2022 年第二次修正，2022 年 4 月 10 日施行). 关于适用《中华人民共和国民事诉讼法》的解释（第九十条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/353651.html>；民法典第一千一百九十八条同上最高检转载全文；全国人大常委会 (2023 年修正，2024 年 1 月 1 日施行). 民事诉讼法（第一百五十二、一百六十四条）. 上海市发展和改革委员会转载全文 <https://fgw.sh.gov.cn/ys-syjf-zc-2.4.1-h5/20240408/073febdb2b04464390546232d4089f51.html>；国务院 (2006). 诉讼费用交纳办法（国务院令第 481 号，第六、二十九条）. <https://www.gov.cn/ziliao/flfg/2006-12/29/content_483682.htm>；全国人大常委会 (2025). 治安管理处罚法（2025 年修订，2026 年 1 月 1 日起施行，第二十六条第一项、第五十条第五项）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>；德国联邦司法部. Strafgesetzbuch (StGB) § 323c Unterlassene Hilfeleistung; Behinderung von hilfeleistenden Personen. <https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__323c.html>
- Notes: China only: Evidence level B because the guideline only says how to respond. It gives no figure for how many lives acting would save. Article 184 covers harm caused during a rescue, provided you act voluntarily and the situation is urgent. It does not cover things like taking someone to hospital in your own private car; waiting for 120 is safer. Do not hang up after calling 120; follow the dispatcher's instructions. This judgment is meant mainly for your own elderly relatives and acquaintances. For strangers on the road, walking away is not illegal, and it is the simplest option, because Chinese law does not require you to rescue. Outside China, the rules can differ. In Germany, for example, witnessing an accident or emergency and not helping, when you could help without putting yourself in serious danger, is itself a crime, punishable by up to one year in prison or a fine. If you are abroad, the local law applies. The costs of stopping must be counted in layers: time, leaving your number, being contacted by the family, being sued, being reported to your employer, and being filmed and posted online. See [Should you stop when a stranger is hurt?](../docs/遇到陌生人出事该不该停.md). If you do decide to stop, the one thing you must do is not lift the person by hand. The reason is medical, not legal: forced lifting may worsen cerebral hemorrhage or spinal injury. There is one exception. If you operate or manage the premises, or organized the activity, leaving may make you liable. Article 1198 of the Civil Code imposes a duty to ensure safety on the people who run such places. For fall prevention itself, see section 1, item 13 (balance and leg strength training). For delayed symptoms after hitting your head, see item 10 of this section. For money matters after being injured while rescuing, see item 39 of this section. For how to preserve evidence and report online abuse, see section 8, item 16.

### 3. Sudden facial droop, weakness in one arm, or unclear speech: call 120 right away. Do not wait, and do not drive yourself
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. Three actions, easy to learn by watching once: ask the person to smile, have them raise both arms level, and ask them to say a full sentence.
- Plain language: Facial droop, arm weakness on one side, or slurred speech means you should call 120 right away. Some strokes can be treated with drugs that reopen blood vessels, and the earlier the treatment, the better the chance of recovery. Even if more than 4.5 hours have passed or the start time is unknown, do not give up on emergency care; a doctor should assess the patient. Note the start time, give no food, water, or medicine, and do not drive yourself.
- Benefit: This pools individual patient data from 9 randomized trials of intravenous thrombolysis, covering 6,756 patients with acute ischemic stroke. Ischemic stroke is a stroke caused by a blocked blood vessel. Intravenous thrombolysis means injecting a drug that dissolves the clot and reopens the vessel. The study looked at whether patients had significant disability 3 to 6 months later (modified Rankin Scale 0–1). Alteplase thrombolysis given within 3 hours of onset had an odds ratio of 1.75, 95% CI 1.35–2.27 (confidence interval). The actual proportions were 32.9% in the treated group and 23.1% in the control group, a difference of 9.8 percentage points. An odds ratio is not a ratio of probabilities, so it cannot be read as 1.75 times the chance of recovery. Treatment at 3 to 4.5 hours had an odds ratio of 1.26 (1.05–1.51). Beyond 4.5 hours, it was 1.15 (0.95–1.40), and this study found no statistically clear difference. This does not mean treatment cannot be given after 4.5 hours. The 2026 American Heart Association and American Stroke Association guideline supports extending the thrombolysis window for some patients. Patients with an unknown onset time, or 4.5 to 9 hours after onset, may still be suitable for thrombolysis after specialized imaging screening. Whether a patient can receive the drug must be decided by a doctor, and the earlier the patient reaches care, the better. The 2017 Chinese Stroke Association emergency medicine branch pre-hospital consensus requires emergency workers to transport suspected stroke patients "in the shortest possible time to the nearest certified stroke center, or a hospital that can perform intravenous thrombolysis and/or endovascular treatment," and to "notify the receiving hospital in advance before the suspected stroke patient arrives." The consensus also states that acute stroke patients may have impaired breathing for reasons such as "aspiration."
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Emberson J, Lees KR, Lyden P, et al. (2014). Effect of treatment delay, age, and stroke severity on the effects of intravenous thrombolysis with alteplase for acute ischaemic stroke: a meta-analysis of individual patient data from randomised trials. Lancet, 384(9958), 1929-1935. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(14)60584-5>；中国卒中学会急救医学分会 (2017). 脑卒中院前急救专家共识. 中华急诊医学杂志, 26(10), 1107-1114. <http://www.cem.org.cn/rhtml/20171002/index.htm>；Prabhakaran S 等 (2026). 2026 Guideline for the Early Management of Patients With Acute Ischemic Stroke. Stroke. <https://doi.org/10.1161/STR.0000000000000513>；American Heart Association (2026). Top Things to Know: 2026 Guideline for the Early Management of Patients With AIS，第 3 点. <https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/2026-guideline-for-the-early-management-of-patients-with-acute-ischemic-stroke/top-things-to-know>
- Notes: China only: Calling 120 rather than driving yourself is because, under the pre-hospital emergency consensus, emergency workers must take the patient to a hospital that can give thrombolysis, and must notify that hospital in advance so it can prepare. Record the exact time symptoms started, because doctors use it to judge treatment options. Thrombolysis works better the earlier it is given, but do not give up on emergency care just because 4.5 hours have passed. Do not give water or medicine by mouth. This comes from the author's reading of the "aspiration" point in the consensus: a stroke patient may choke. Do not wait to "rest and see."

### 4. Sudden spinning dizziness and unsteadiness, double vision, one eye going dark, or being unable to touch your own nose with a finger: treat it as a stroke and call 120
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. This adds two checks to the three in item 3 (facial droop, arm weakness, speech). The first: have the person stand up straight with eyes closed and see whether they sway, then ask whether they see double or are missing part of their vision. The second: if you suspect a stroke, have them reach out and touch their nose with a finger, then close their eyes and touch it again.
- Plain language: Checking only the face, arms, and speech misses 14.1% of strokes, because some strokes never cause facial droop or arm weakness at first. Adding unsteady walking, leg weakness, and vision symptoms to the screen lowers the missed rate to 4.4%. Sudden spinning dizziness, inability to stand steadily, double vision, or inability to touch your nose with a finger also means call 120 and note the time.
- Benefit: A 2014 review at a University of Kentucky stroke center looked at 736 medical records of acute ischemic stroke (a stroke caused by a blocked vessel). Of these, 14.1% had none of the three FAST symptoms (facial droop, arm weakness, and speech problems) at onset. Adding walking instability, leg weakness, and visual symptoms to screening reduced the missed proportion from 14.1% to 4.4%. In two US hospitals, 465 emergency records of ischemic stroke were reviewed, and 22% had been missed at first. Strokes affecting the back of the brain (posterior circulation strokes) were initially missed 37% of the time, compared with 16% for front-of-brain strokes. People with dizziness had about 1.99 times the odds of being missed (odds ratio 1.99), and people with nausea or vomiting had 4.02 times the odds. The 2025 International Red Cross first aid guidelines list, among stroke signs, "sudden trouble in seeing with one or both eyes" and "sudden trouble walking or experiencing dizziness, loss of balance or coordination."
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Aroor S, Singh R, Goldstein LB (2017). BE-FAST (Balance, Eyes, Face, Arm, Speech, Time): Reducing the Proportion of Strokes Missed Using the FAST Mnemonic. Stroke, 48(2), 479-481. <https://doi.org/10.1161/STROKEAHA.116.015169>；Arch AE, Weisman DC, Coca S, et al. (2016). Missed Ischemic Stroke Diagnosis in the Emergency Department by Emergency Medicine and Neurology Services. Stroke, 47(3), 668-673：「Thirty-seven percent of posterior strokes were initially misdiagnosed compared with 16% of anterior strokes」. <https://doi.org/10.1161/strokeaha.115.010613>；International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies (2025). International First Aid, Resuscitation and Education Guidelines 2025，第 366–367 页：「Sudden trouble in seeing with one or both eyes」「Sudden trouble walking or experiencing dizziness, loss of balance or coordination」. <https://www.ifrc.org/sites/default/files/2026-03/IFRC%20International%20First%20Aid,%20Resuscitation%20and%20Education%20Guidelines%202025.pdf>
- Notes: China only: When a vessel to the back of the brain is blocked (a posterior circulation stroke), there is often no drooping mouth or weak arm, only spinning dizziness, unsteady walking, and double vision, which is why it is most easily missed. Being unable to touch the nose accurately, or unable to stand steadily with eyes closed, falls under what the Red Cross guideline calls "problems with balance or coordination." But no single test can rule out a stroke. If the symptoms come on suddenly and do not go away, call 120 and note the exact time they started. The Kentucky study reviewed records from only one hospital, and the authors themselves say it needs further validation.

### 5. One eye suddenly goes dark, like a curtain being drawn. Even if it clears up in a few minutes, go to the emergency department the same day and treat it as a stroke
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. An emergency assessment and vascular tests take up half a day to a full day.
- Plain language: One eye suddenly going dark, like a curtain being pulled down, can mean a blocked blood vessel in the eye. The longer the blockage lasts, the less of the retina can be saved. It is also a form of brain ischemia, so go even if it clears up after a few minutes. Minor strokes and transient ischemic attacks are now assessed and treated the same day, which cut strokes within 90 days from 10.3% to 2.1%. Go the same day to an emergency department at a hospital with a stroke center, and do not wait until the next day.
- Benefit: The American Heart Association's 2021 scientific statement on central retinal artery occlusion (a blockage of the main blood vessel in the retina) states: "Acute central retinal artery occlusion is a medical emergency." The care system should recognize it as an emergency and prioritize it by severity. The longer the blood supply is cut off, the less retina can be saved. The Oxford EXPRESS study looked at two groups: patients with minor strokes, and people with brain ischemia whose symptoms resolved on their own (medically called transient ischemic attack). These patients were moved to same-day assessment and treatment. After the change, the proportion who had another stroke within 90 days fell from 10.3% (32/310) to 2.1% (6/281). The abstract of the scientific statement also notes that central retinal artery occlusion is a form of acute ischemic stroke and a harbinger of further cerebrovascular and cardiovascular events. The UK National Health Service (NHS) lists "blurred vision or loss of sight in 1 or both eyes" as a stroke symptom, and says that symptoms can stop after a short time, but you should seek medical help straight away. Anyone who has had stroke symptoms in the past 24 hours, even if they have now stopped, should also call emergency services.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Mac Grory B, Schrag M, Biousse V, et al. (2021). Management of Central Retinal Artery Occlusion: A Scientific Statement From the American Heart Association. Stroke, 52(6), e282-e294：「Central retinal artery occlusion (CRAO) is a form of acute ischemic stroke that causes severe visual loss and is a harbinger of further cerebrovascular and cardiovascular events」. <https://doi.org/10.1161/STR.0000000000000366> ；NHS. Symptoms of a stroke：「blurred vision or loss of sight in 1 or both eyes」「Symptoms of a stroke can sometimes stop after a short time, so you may think you're OK. Even if this happens, get medical help straight away」「you've had signs of a stroke within the last 24 hours even if they've now stopped」. <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/stroke/symptoms/> ; Rothwell PM, Giles MF, Chandratheva A, et al. (2007). Effect of urgent treatment of transient ischaemic attack and minor stroke on early recurrent stroke (EXPRESS study): a prospective population-based sequential comparison. Lancet, 370(9596), 1432-1442. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(07)61448-2>
- Notes: Evidence level B because EXPRESS was not a randomized trial. It compared the same population before and after a change in practice, and other conditions may have changed between the two periods, so the effect cannot be separated out. A blackout that lasts a few minutes and then recovers is easily dismissed as "tiredness" or "low blood sugar." The NHS states that you should seek help immediately even if symptoms stop. Among EXPRESS patients who were not treated the same day, 10.3% had another stroke within 90 days. A sudden loss of sight in one eye, even without pain or redness, must be taken seriously. Do not wait for an eye clinic appointment the next day; go directly to the emergency department of a hospital with a stroke center.

### 6. One eye is swollen, painful, and red, lights have rainbow halos, and there is headache and nausea or vomiting: go to an eye emergency department the same day
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. An eye emergency visit to measure eye pressure and examine the front of the eye takes half a day.
- Plain language: This may be an acute attack of glaucoma. Fluid cannot drain from the eye, pressure rises, and the optic nerve that connects to the brain can be damaged. The longer treatment is delayed, the greater the chance of permanent vision loss. During the Wuhan lockdown, patients waited an average of 241 hours before treatment, and 21.87% went blind. The following year, in the same period without lockdown, the average delay was 121 hours, and 7.84% went blind.
- Benefit: A Wuhan eye hospital compared two periods of patients with acute primary angle-closure glaucoma. In 2020, during 76 days of lockdown, 54 people (64 eyes) were included. In the same period in 2021, 46 people (51 eyes) were included. The demographic profiles of the two groups were similar. Time from symptom onset to treatment was 241.84±211.95 hours in the lockdown group and 121.53±96.12 hours in the control group (P=0.001). Eye pressure at presentation was 52.63±12.45 mmHg versus 45.16±9.79 mmHg (P=0.001). The proportion who went blind was 21.87% versus 7.84%. Glaucomatous optic neuropathy was found in 20/64 eyes (31.25%) versus 7/51 eyes (13.73%) (P=0.03). A Korean hospital reviewed 50 eyes that had had one attack and later had lens removal. One year later, 25 eyes (50%) had some degree of visual field loss. The longer the time from symptom onset to lowering eye pressure, the greater the likelihood of field loss (P=0.005). A separate prospective study had 43 people with shallow anterior chambers lie prone in a dark room for 1 hour. Eye pressure rose significantly in both eyes (P<0.01). The median rise was 3.60 mmHg in the eye they mainly used, and 2.70 mmHg in the other eye (P<0.05). The NHS states that glaucoma can sometimes develop suddenly, with symptoms including intense eye pain, a red eye, rainbow-coloured circles around bright lights, blurred vision, nausea and vomiting, and headache. If this happens suddenly, call emergency services or go to the emergency department. It usually happens when fluid in the eye cannot drain properly, raising eye pressure and damaging the optic nerve that connects the eye to the brain. Higher-risk groups include people over 50, people with a parent or sibling who has glaucoma, Asian people, and people with myopia, hyperopia, or diabetes. Zhongshan Ophthalmic Center treated 243 patients aged 40 or under with angle-closure glaucoma between 2012 and 2019.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Zhou L, Wu S, Wang Y, Bao X, Peng T, Luo W, Ortega-Usobiaga J (2022). Clinical presentation of acute primary angle closure during the COVID-19 epidemic lockdown. Frontiers in Medicine, 9, 1078237. <https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2022.1078237> ; Sung MS, Kim HJ, Park SW (2023). Predictors of long-term visual field outcome after an episode of acute primary angle closure. Clinical & Experimental Ophthalmology, 51(4), 291-299. <https://doi.org/10.1111/ceo.14206> ; Wang J, Wang J, Ng TK, Huang C (2025). ### 8. Sudden severe pain like being torn open, moving from the chest through to the back and down toward the waist: call 120 and say "it feels like tearing"

### 9. A sudden "worst headache of your life" that reaches peak pain within an hour: go to the emergency department now for a head CT
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: A visit to the emergency department itself costs little. A head CT in the emergency department costs a few hundred yuan (RMB).
- Plain language: The key question is how quickly the pain reaches its peak. A severe headache that peaks within an hour can signal a ruptured blood vessel on the surface of the brain, though this is a minority of cases. If you are over 40, have neck pain or stiffness, or someone saw you pass out, go to the emergency department for a head CT. Most people who get checked are fine, but a missed bleed can happen again.
- Benefit: Ten Canadian emergency departments ran a prospective cohort study of 2,131 cases of severe headache that reached peak pain within one hour. The criteria were set in advance, and patients were followed up with records kept along the way. Of these, 132 (6.2%) had subarachnoid hemorrhage (a rupture of a blood vessel on the surface of the brain). The Ottawa SAH Rule has six items: age 40 or older; neck pain or stiffness; witnessed loss of consciousness; onset during exertion; abrupt, thunderclap-style onset; and limited neck flexion on examination. Meeting any one item means the patient should be investigated. The rule picked up all the bleeds, with sensitivity of 100%, 95% CI 97.2%–100.0% (confidence interval). The cost is that only 15.3% of people without a bleed were ruled out by it (specificity 15.3%).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Perry JJ, Stiell IG, Sivilotti MLA, et al. (2013). Clinical decision rules to rule out subarachnoid hemorrhage for acute headache. JAMA, 310(12), 1248-1255. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2013.278018>
- Notes: What matters is not how painful the headache is, but how quickly it reaches peak intensity. A headache that bursts within seconds to a minute is the warning sign, not a migraine that builds slowly. Vomiting, neck stiffness, light sensitivity, or brief fainting make it more serious. A specificity of 15.3% means most people this rule sends for testing turn out fine. But a missed bleed can lead to another bleed, so the check is worth it. Do not take a painkiller and go back to sleep.

### 10. After a knock to the head, if an older person becomes unsteady on their feet, slow, drowsy or weak on one side within two or three weeks to a few months, get a head CT
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no gain=high scope=health who=elderly,60plus -->

- Plain language: In older people, blood often collects under the skull around the third week after a knock to the head. ### 16. If someone has a seizure and collapses, clear the area around them, roll them onto their side, time it, and put nothing in their mouth. The same applies to a child having a seizure with a fever

- Plain language: Move hard objects away and roll the person onto their side, mouth facing down, and time the seizure. ### 18. If someone is being shocked by electricity, cut the power first, or pry the source away with a dry wooden stick or similar. Do not grab the person directly
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free.

Do not hold back just because it is an electrical injury. ### 20. Do not make someone vomit after they swallow cleaner, pesticide or medicine. Take the bottle and go to a doctor at once. For splashes in the eyes or on skin, rinse with plenty of clean water for at least 15 minutes

### 22. In the heat, if someone is dizzy, nauseous, not sweating or confused, move them to shade at once, remove clothing and pour water on them to cool them. If they are unconscious, give no water and call 120 (China's ambulance number)
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free.
- Plain language: Hot red skin, slurred speech and confusion are signs of heatstroke. Body temperature can exceed 41 ℃ within 10 to 15 minutes, and severe heatstroke is very often fatal. Move the person to a cool, shady place, remove excess clothing, cool them with cold water and wet towels, and call 120 (China's ambulance number). Do not give water to anyone who is unconscious.
- Benefit: The US National Weather Service calls heatstroke "a serious medical emergency". Its signs are body temperature above 103°F (39.4°C), confusion, slurred speech and hot, flushed skin. Treatment is to call for emergency help at once, move the person to a cool place, cool them with cold water and wet cloths, and "do not give fluids". Heat exhaustion shows heavy sweating, weakness, cool clammy skin and a fast, weak pulse. Treat it by moving to a cool place, loosening clothing and giving small sips of water. Seek medical care if there is vomiting, the symptoms worsen, or they do not ease within 1 hour. The Yunnan Provincial Disease Control Bureau advises moving the person at once to a cool, ventilated place, cooling them with cold wet towels, giving salty drinks if they are conscious, and calling emergency services immediately for severe symptoms. It also says severe heatstroke "has a very high fatality rate". The China CDC advises cooling first and transporting second, and says the "golden half hour" after loss of consciousness is when cooling matters most. The Wilderness Medical Society's 2024 update on heat illness says cold-water immersion is "the most important method of treating severe heat illness" and cools fastest. The US National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (NIOSH) says body temperature can rise to 106°F (about 41 ℃) or higher within 10 to 15 minutes during heatstroke. Its listed symptoms include "hot, dry skin or profuse sweating". The China CDC's 2014 heat illness guide lists heatstroke signs as "red, hot and dry skin with no sweating", and says "do not give the patient water". If the person vomits, turn them onto their side to keep the airway clear.

### 23. Give a conscious person only small, frequent sips of cool, lightly salted water or sports drink. Do not gulp, and avoid alcohol and sugary drinks
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. A bottle of sports drink costs a few yuan (RMB).
- Plain language: Give drinks only to a conscious person. Offer cool, lightly salted water or sports drink in small sips, 500 to 1,000 ml an hour. - Benefit: The China CDC's 2014 Public Guide to Heat Illness Prevention and Emergency Treatment says to drink "at least 2 to 4 cups of cool water (500 to 1,000 ml) every hour, not too hot, in small, frequent sips". It also says, "do not drink alcoholic drinks or drinks with a lot of sugar, as they cause more body fluid loss. Avoid drinking very cold, iced drinks." For heatstroke it is explicit: "do not give the patient water". US NIOSH advises that for heat exhaustion, one should "encourage small, frequent sips of cool water". - Evidence level: B

### 24. In a fire, crawl low, touch the door before opening it, and do not open it if it is hot. Take the stairs, not the lift, and do not go back once you are out

If you cannot get out, close the door, block gaps and vents with cloth or tape, and call to report your location. The Ministry of Emergency Management's self-rescue guidance for fires says the same. Do not take the lift, because a power cut can trap you inside. When the fire is not large, run to lower floors as far as you can. Cover your nose and mouth with a wet towel or mask, and crawl or bend low to evacuate.

### 25. If you see someone drowning, first call for help and dial 110 or 120 (China's police and ambulance numbers). Reach them with a pole or throw something that floats. Do not enter the water yourself
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is resisting the urge to jump in.
- Plain language: Call for help and dial 110 or 120 (China's police and ambulance numbers). From the shore, reach out with a pole or throw something that floats. Do not enter the water yourself. If the person is unresponsive and not breathing normally once out, start CPR. Rescuers who cannot or will not give breaths should keep doing chest compressions.
- Benefit: The WHO says about 300,000 people drown each year worldwide, 92% of them in low- and middle-income countries. Drowning is the fourth leading cause of death for children aged 1 to 4 and the third for those aged 5 to 14. The WHO recommends "safe rescue and resuscitation training", including learning how to rescue without entering the water. Shout for help and dial 110 or 120 first, then reach out with a long pole or rope, or throw a lifebuoy, empty barrel or other floating object. The International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies' 2025 first aid guidelines say rescue should be attempted without entering the water: reach with something rigid, such as a pole or tree branch, or throw a rope or buoyant object. Rescuers should not touch a person who is actively drowning, because of the risk of being pulled into the water. Anyone who has to rescue from the water should understand water conditions and carry a floating aid. If a person is pulled out with no response and abnormal breathing, start CPR at once. The 2024 focused update on drowning resuscitation from the American Heart Association and American Academy of Pediatrics stresses that drowning-related cardiac arrest is usually caused by severe lack of oxygen. Trained rescuers should combine rescue breaths with chest compressions. If they cannot, will not or are unable to give breaths, they should keep doing chest compressions until rescuers arrive. When calling for emergency help, use speakerphone and follow the dispatcher's instructions.
- Evidence level: C

### 26. If someone is choking and cannot speak, for anyone over 1 year old stand behind them and give 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts. If they collapse, start CPR

The International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies' 2025 first aid guidelines say not to use a blind finger sweep in a person with a foreign-body airway obstruction, as this may push the object further down. ### 27. If you are lost in the Gobi, a desert or a remote area, or your vehicle breaks down, stay where you are or by the vehicle. Report your location first, then shelter from sun and cold, conserve water, and do not wander

### 28. Shivering, slurred speech and stumbling are signs of hypothermia. Replace wet clothing, wrap the person up and warm the core first. Give a hot sweet drink, not alcohol
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->

- Plain language: Body temperature below 35 ℃ is hypothermia: shivering, slurred speech, stumbling and muddled thinking. If there is no response or normal breathing, start CPR as in item 1 of this section.
- Benefit: The US National Weather Service says body temperature of 95°F (35°C) or below is hypothermia, and "if you feel cold, slow or unable to think clearly, see a doctor at once". "Alcohol reduces shivering", so do not give it. "Start CPR if the person is unresponsive". For judging whether to start, see item 1 of this section (no normal breathing). For frostbite, rewarm with warm water or body heat, and "do not use hot water or radiant heat such as a fireplace". ### 29. If bitten by a snake, sit down and keep still, remove rings and watches, and get to hospital quickly for antivenom. Do not cut, suck or tie the wound tightly

- Plain language: Sit down, keep still and immobilise the bitten limb. Remove rings and watches before swelling tightens them. Get to a hospital with antivenom quickly, since antivenom is the most effective treatment. Do not cut, suck, ice, rub or tie the wound, and do not try to catch the snake.

Remove jewellery, watches and tight clothing, which could restrict blood flow if swelling develops. ### 30. In an earthquake, check what kind of building you are in: in a quake-resistant building, take cover in place; in an old house that may collapse, leave through the door a few steps away
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Before anything happens, check how many steps it takes to get out of your home, which route to take, and which table is sturdy.
- Plain language: Check what kind of building you are in. In a building built to quake-resistance standards, where the exit means stairs or a hallway, take cover in place: protect your head and neck, get under a sturdy table, and hold on to its leg. In a single-story or old brick house, get out. Never jump, use the elevator, or run down stairs while the building is shaking.
- Benefit: The China Earthquake Administration, the national earthquake agency, has a science video, "Earthquake is coming: hide or run?" Its rule is "take cover nearby during the quake, evacuate quickly after it", and it states that this applies "inside buildings that meet seismic fortification requirements". The reason given is that such buildings "are less likely to collapse as a whole". The original says that "falling building parts and decorations" are "the greatest threat to life". Running blindly without any protection can make you "more likely to be hit by falling ceilings or chandeliers (fans)". To take cover, "protect your head and neck with your hands or other soft objects" and "hold tightly to a table leg". Without a table, "press quickly against a load-bearing wall and crouch", avoiding "exterior walls, windows and balconies". "If you are outdoors", "evacuate immediately to an open area". On the other side, the 1988 Armenia earthquake collapsed many Soviet-style precast-panel buildings. A case-control study of that event states that "leaving the building after the first shock is a protective action". People who stayed indoors were 4.40 times as likely to be injured as those who ran out (odds ratio 4.40), 95% CI 2.24–8.71 (the range the true value likely falls in). Being injured inside a building was 12.20 times as likely as being outdoors (3.62–63.79). In buildings of five or more floors, the risk was 3.65 times that in shorter buildings (2.12–6.33). The Ministry of Emergency Management's earthquake guidance states: "people living in old brick-concrete buildings should more actively look for a chance to evacuate quickly". People in single-story houses, "if they are by a door or window, with no other dangerous building outside the window, can immediately run to an open space in the middle of the courtyard". During an earthquake, "never use the elevator". People on the first or second floor "should also not choose to jump out", and people in high-rises "should be careful not to go down the stairs". Outdoors, stay away from buildings with glass curtain walls, overpasses, utility poles, street lamps and billboards.
- Evidence level: B

In Armenia, whole buildings collapsed, so there was nowhere to hide and leaving was the better choice. The US FEMA advice for homes is "drop, cover and hold on in place, do not run outside", because there most injuries came from falling objects and people falling while moving during the shaking. In any case, never jump from a building, never take the elevator, and never run down stairs while the building shakes. Outdoors, stay away from tall buildings, overpasses, utility poles, glass curtain walls and billboards.

### 31. Bears, wild boars or a pack of wild dogs: don't run; back away slowly, sideways
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. When you hike, bring a trekking pole, walk with others, and make noise so animals know you are there. The hard part is standing still instead of running.
- Plain language: With a bear, don't run. Bears can run as fast as racehorses, uphill and downhill. Don't climb a tree either, because both black and brown bears can climb. If a black bear charges, don't play dead: run for a car or a building, and if you can't get away, fight back with anything at hand. Dog bites are handled according to item 13.
- Benefit: The US National Park Service's guidance on bears has six key lines. One: "Do not run. Bears can run as fast as racehorses up and down hills." One: "Stay calm. Most bears do not want to attack you; they just want to be left alone." One: "Keep talking to the bear in a low, steady voice." One: "When the bear is not moving, slowly back away sideways, so you can keep watching it without tripping." One: "Make yourself look as large as possible." And: "Do not climb a tree; grizzly and black bears can both climb trees." If a grizzly attacks, "play dead: lie flat on your stomach, hands clasped behind your neck, legs spread". If a black bear attacks, "do not play dead; flee toward a car or a building, and if you cannot escape, fight back with anything you have at hand". Dog bites are handled according to item 13.

### 33. Going up to high altitude: climb by your sleeping altitude, the height where you spend the night; if symptoms worsen, descend, don't tough it out
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: One to two extra days for acclimatization. Preventive medication is prescription-only; ask a doctor before you leave. The hard part is keeping those days when the itinerary is tight.
- Plain language: What counts is the altitude where you sleep, not the highest point you reach in the daytime. Sleeping above 2,450 m carries a risk of altitude illness. If you have a headache or nausea, don't sleep any higher; if symptoms get worse after rest, go down. Staggering when you walk, or breathlessness even at rest, means descending and taking oxygen work better than pills.
- Benefit: Sleeping at 2,450 m or higher carries a risk of altitude illness. High-altitude cerebral edema and high-altitude pulmonary edema (fluid in the brain and lungs) can kill if you do not descend in time. The US CDC Yellow Book gives three rules to prevent serious illness: know the early symptoms and admit them; if you have any symptoms, however mild, do not go higher to sleep; if symptoms get worse despite rest or treatment at the same altitude, descend. Acute altitude illness usually improves quickly after a descent of 300 m or more. In remote areas, descend immediately for anyone suspected of cerebral edema, and use dexamethasone and oxygen together if you have them. For pulmonary edema, descent is usually required immediately, and descent and oxygen work far better than medication.

### 35. Boil all wild water before drinking: one full rolling boil for a minute is enough; at common travel altitudes you don't need to boil longer just because you are high up

### 37. Coming across a group fight: back off and leave; don't pull them apart, don't gather to watch, don't pick up weapons; if you want to report it, retreat to a safe distance and call 110

### 43. Infant under 1 year choking and unable to cry: give 5 back blows with the baby face-down, then 5 chest thrusts face-up, alternating; do not press the abdomen
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=health who=infant,pregnancy -->
- Cost: No money. Watching a demonstration or refreshing an infant first aid class takes a few minutes to half a day. The hard part is turning the baby face-down in a panic.
- Plain language: If the child can still cough or cry, let them cough while you watch closely. If they cannot cry and their face is turning blue, have someone call 120 (China's ambulance number). - Benefit: The American Heart Association and the American Academy of Pediatrics published their 2025 pediatric basic life support guidelines. ### 44. Infant unresponsive and not breathing normally: call 120 on speaker and start infant CPR, 30 compressions then 2 breaths

- Plain language: If the baby does not respond to a tap on the sole of the foot and is not breathing normally, call 120 on speaker and keep your hands free. Do not give coffee or caffeinated drinks, which worsen dehydration. Do not put them in a cold shower or bath, which can make them cold, cause a fall, or leave them unconscious in the water. Do not try to make them vomit. A Shaanxi Provincial Health Commission article by an emergency doctor says that a drunk person's gag reflex weakens, so when they vomit they can easily inhale it into the airway, and in severe cases this "directly causes death by asphyxiation." The emergency department often receives drunk people who have already died, with large amounts of vomit suctioned from their airways. At the deep-sleep stage, if the skin is pale, lips are blue, skin is cold and clammy, body temperature is low, and breathing is shallow, call 120. At this point lay them on their side, "do not induce vomiting or give food," cover them with a blanket, and turn them over regularly. If the heartbeat or breathing stops, start CPR at once. A hospital health-science article reprinted by the Beijing Municipal Health Commission says: "Never let a drunk person sleep alone, in case they suffocate with no one there to rescue them." A pharmacist's article reprinted by the Fujian Provincial Drug Administration says drunk people "must lie on their side," and that low body temperature can stop the heart. US CDC figures show that from 2010 to 2012 an average of 2,221 people aged 15 and over died of alcohol poisoning in the United States each year, about 6 a day.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: NHS. The benefit is rated "large" by judgement: choking and breathing stopping both happen when no one is watching, and having someone present allows early detection and an early call to 120. Advice on inducing vomiting differs. Some Chinese popular-science material says a person who can still walk and talk may have their throat stimulated to vomit. Never induce vomiting in someone who cannot be woken; on this point Chinese and foreign sources agree. The NHS advises against it in all cases. This applies first to family members, but more often it involves drinking friends and colleagues, and people who drink together also have a legal duty of care. The Taixing City People's Court ruled on a case in which two people knew that a companion at the table was drunk, did not tell his family and did not take him home; he later drowned in a river at dawn. The two were held 7% and 3% responsible, for total compensation of about 104,000 yuan (RMB; exactly 103,887 yuan). Drunk people lose body heat easily; for keeping warm, see item 28 of this section (hypothermia). ### 47. Finger or toe severed: press to stop the bleeding, wrap the severed part in damp clean gauze, seal it in a plastic bag, place the bag in ice water, and go to a hospital with hand surgery
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: No money. Keep a pack of clean gauze and a few resealable plastic bags at home. The hard part is looking after the person first in a panic, and resisting the urge to put the severed part straight into ice or water.
- Plain language: Press on the wound with your hand to stop the bleeding, and call 120 (China's ambulance number). Wrap the severed part in clean damp gauze, seal it in a plastic bag, and place the bag in ice water. Do not touch ice directly, and do not soak the part in water directly. If it stays cool, a severed finger may be reattached within about 4 to 6 hours, and more than 80% of reattached fingers survive.
- Benefit: The US National Library of Medicine's MedlinePlus first-aid page says: press directly on the wound with your hand to stop the bleeding, and raise the injured area. Bleeding that threatens life may need a tourniquet. Keep the severed part and take it along with the person. If the cut surface is dirty, rinse it gently first. Wrap it in a clean damp cloth, put it in a sealed plastic bag, and place the bag in an ice-water bath. Do not put the part directly in water or on ice without a bag. Do not use dry ice, which can frostbite the severed part. Without cold water, keep the part as cool as you can. Without cooling, the severed part "is only good for reattachment for about 4 to 6 hours." Do not think a severed part is too small to keep, and do not push the severed part or bone back in. The American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons' page on fingertip injuries and amputations gives the same method, and says to go straight to a hospital with hand specialists immediately available. Pooling 31 studies, 86.8% of reattached digits survived (1,976 successes, 300 failures). Of 1,087 people who had digit reattachment, 82.9% returned to work. A pooled analysis of 12 studies found 85.3% survival of reattached fingers. Another pooled analysis of 12 studies with 3,645 people found that digits which had been without blood flow for more than 8 hours before surgery were more likely to die after reattachment. The odds were about 2.25 times higher than for digits without blood flow for under 8 hours: OR 2.25, 95% CI 1.43 to 3.54 (confidence interval).
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: MedlinePlus (2024). - Notes: Rated B because the storage method comes from guidelines and textbook consensus, and the survival figures come from studies that only tracked cases. No controlled trial comparing storage methods was found. The benefit is rated "moderate" by judgement: what is saved is the finger, not life. Whether it can and should be reattached is for a doctor to decide after examining the injury; crushed or torn parts are harder to reattach than clean cuts. MedlinePlus also notes that saving life comes first: stop the bleeding, then deal with the severed part. ### 48. Permanent tooth knocked out completely: hold it by the crown, rinse, put it back in if possible, otherwise keep it in milk and see a dentist right away; do not put a baby tooth back
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: No money. Milk and saline are at home or at convenience stores; dental fees are extra. The hard part is being willing to push a knocked-out tooth back into its socket yourself, and telling on the spot whether it is a baby tooth or a permanent one.
- Plain language: Hold the tooth by the crown and never touch the root. If it is dirty, rinse it gently in milk or saline. Put it back in its socket if you can, and bite down on a clean cloth to hold it. If you cannot put it back, keep it in milk and see a dentist at once; do not wrap it dry in tissue. A tooth replaced at once has about a 20% risk of the root fusing to the bone, but one left dry for over an hour has over 80%. Do not put a baby tooth back.
- Benefit: The NHS dental trauma page says: hold the tooth by the crown, the white part that sticks out of the gum, and do not touch the root. If the tooth is dirty, rinse it gently in milk, saline or saliva. Try to place it back in its original socket. If it goes back in, gently bite on a clean cloth to hold it, and see a dentist as soon as possible. If it cannot go back in, keep it in milk or saliva. Do not put a baby tooth back, as it can damage the permanent tooth growing underneath. If a tooth has been knocked out and cannot be found, still book an emergency dental appointment. The International Association of Dental Traumatology's "Save your tooth" poster gives the same steps: find the tooth, hold it by the crown, and rinse it briefly under cold running tap water. Then choose one: put it back in place, put it in a cup of milk or saline, or keep it between the cheek and gum. Then see a dentist immediately. In a Copenhagen University Hospital dental trauma database, 116 people had 145 permanent teeth knocked out and replanted, and were followed for 7 months to 23 years. The outcome studied was the risk of ankylosis (the root fusing to the bone of the socket), after which the root is gradually resorbed. For teeth replanted immediately, the risk was 17.2%; for teeth soaked in saliva or saline before replanting, 55.3%; for teeth left dry for more than 1 hour, 85.7%. The same hospital had earlier followed a group of 400 teeth, re-analysed in 2020. For children's teeth whose roots were not fully formed, the fusion risk was 33.9% with dry storage under 20 minutes and 60.0% over 60 minutes. For fully formed roots, the risk rose from 59.4% to 86.4%. Both groups are estimates at 900 days after replanting. A 1995 study of these 400 teeth also found that soaking in tap water for over 20 minutes mostly led to root resorption. During follow-up, 30% of these 400 teeth were eventually extracted.
- Evidence level: B
