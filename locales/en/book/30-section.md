# 30. Children once they start school

Section 20 covered the year a child is born. Section 18 worked out what raising a child costs in money and time. This section covers the years in between, ages 3 to 18. The screen-time item by age (item 5) starts from age 0, because the official bands are 0 to 3, 3 to 6, and primary and secondary school. Splitting them up would make the guidance harder to use. This section answers one question only: when "don't fall behind at school" and "your body" clash, what cannot wait? Some things cannot wait because they have time windows, and once missed they cannot be made up. Whether schoolwork should be so competitive is not discussed here. This section only gives the windows and the numbers.

Items 1 to 8, item 12 (eye exam with dilating drops), item 13 (dental fissure sealants), items 15 to 19, and item 21 (head injury during sport) cover deaths and real physical or mental harm. Items 16 and 17 cover how families discipline children: no hitting or scolding, and attending a parenting course that teaches specific methods. Item 18 says not to send a child to a closed-management internet-addiction school. Item 19 covers teaching children the names of private body parts and telling a parent if someone touches them. Items 9 (not buying products that claim to "cure" myopia) and 20 (not riding on the road before the right age) are about money. Items 10 (official rules on sleep, homework, PE and rankings), 11 (leave of absence with school registration kept) and 14 (a child who loves gaming) are about time. Drowning, traffic, helmets and the HPV vaccine are in section 1. Infants and toddlers are in section 20. Children being scammed and game top-ups are in section 5. Red lines for minors to avoid are in section 9. Recognising emergencies and arranging transport to hospital are in section 13. Children whose parent has died are in section 29. None of these are repeated here.

Items in this section are grouped by theme below, with item numbers in brackets.

**Medical care that cannot wait**: sudden severe pain means going to hospital immediately (item 1), don't put treatment off until after exams (item 2), and read the annual student checkup report (item 7).

**Eyes and teeth**: at least 2 hours outdoors each day (item 4), screen rules by age (item 5), the higher the myopia degree, the greater the risk (item 6), don't buy "myopia cure" products (item 9), eye exam with dilating drops (item 12), dental fissure sealants (item 13).

**School matters**: report bullying to the school the same day (item 3), official rules on sleep, homework, PE and rankings (item 10), leave of absence with school registration kept (item 11).

**Mental health and family discipline**: depression screening (item 8), a child who loves gaming (item 14), a child who says they like people of the same sex (item 15), no hitting (item 16), attend a parenting course (item 17), no closed internet-addiction schools (item 18), teaching children about private body parts (item 19).

**Roads and sports fields**: no cycling on the road before the right age (item 20), stop playing right away after a head injury during sport (item 21).

### 1. If a child has sudden, worsening severe pain, go to hospital right away. Don't let them wait until school ends
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=health who=child -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Take half a day off and take the child to emergency.
- Plain language: Some conditions are measured in hours. Sudden, severe pain in one side of the scrotum may mean testicular torsion, where the blood supply to the testicle is twisted shut. Treated within 6 hours, 97.2% of testicles are saved. By 19 to 24 hours, only 42.5% are. This pain cannot wait until after school or exams.
- Benefit: The author pooled 30 case reports and 2,116 testicular torsion patients, following the rules. Of these, 1,283 cases were grouped in 6-hour bands. Counting from when pain started, the share of testicles saved was 97.2% at 0 to 6 hours, 79.3% at 7 to 12 hours, 61.3% at 13 to 18 hours, 42.5% at 19 to 24 hours, 24.4% at 25 to 48 hours, and 7.4% beyond 48 hours. Combined into broader bands: 90.4% within 12 hours, 54.0% at 13 to 24 hours, and 18.1% beyond 24 hours. The author concludes that the time during which a testicle can be saved may be longer than the commonly quoted 6 to 8 hours. So even if pain has gone on for a long time, get it treated. Don't skip treatment because "it's already too late." In three years of US national child hospital data (2000, 2003 and 2006), 2,443 boys aged from 1 month to under 18 had surgery for testicular torsion. There were two peaks of age: the first year of life, and the start of puberty.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Mellick LB, et al. (2019). A Systematic Review of Testicle Survival Time After a Torsion Event. Pediatric Emergency Care. <https://doi.org/10.1097/PEC.0000000000001287>；Zhao LC, Lautz TB, Meeks JJ, Maizels M. (2011). Pediatric testicular torsion epidemiology using a national database: incidence, risk of orchiectomy and possible measures toward improving the quality of care. Journal of Urology, 186(5), 2009-2013. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.juro.2011.07.024>
- Notes: Tell the boy ahead of time: if anything sudden and severe hurts down there, say so straight away, and don't feel embarrassed. Testicular torsion is more common in boys just entering puberty. In the author's experience, many boys are too embarrassed to speak up and just endure it. Other conditions also measured in hours include acute appendicitis (it can perforate if delayed), diabetic ketoacidosis and meningitis. This section did not check item by item how much damage each hour of delay causes for these. The author's common warning sign for them all: sudden onset, steady worsening, and unlike any previous episode. For how to recognise emergencies and arrange transport to hospital, see section 13.

### 2. Don't put needed treatment off until after exams. Some windows follow bone age, not the exam schedule
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=high gain=high scope=health who=child -->
- Cost: No direct cost. The only decision is whether to start early or late. Appointments and time off take time, and the treatment itself costs extra. The hard part: a brace must be worn at least 18 hours a day, so the child has to put up with it constantly.
- Plain language: Scoliosis is a sideways bend of the spine. A brace is a rigid shell that holds the spine straight while bones are still growing. Wearing it kept 75% of children's curves under 50 degrees, compared with 42% of those who were only watched. The window follows bone growth, not the exam calendar.
- Benefit: A multi-centre trial enrolled 242 adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis who were eligible for bracing. Of these, 116 were randomly assigned and 126 chose whether to wear a brace. Success was defined in advance: a curve reaching over 50 degrees counted as treatment failure, and a curve still below that when bones had finished growing counted as success. Children in the brace group were required to wear it at least 18 hours a day. Results were analysed by the group each child was first assigned to, whether or not they actually wore the brace enough. Among the randomly assigned, the success rate was 75% with bracing and 42% with observation only (OR 4.11, 95% CI 1.85 to 9.16). That is about 4 times the chance of success compared with observation alone. Combining the randomised and self-selected groups gives 72% versus 48%. After adjusting for the baseline differences between the two groups, the OR was 1.93 (1.08 to 3.46), still about 1.9 times. The more hours a day the brace was worn, the higher the success rate (P<0.001, which is unlikely to be chance). The trial was stopped early because the brace clearly worked.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Weinstein SL, et al. (2013). Effects of bracing in adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1307337>
- Notes: Scoliosis is used as the example because it has a randomised trial and a clear time window. It is also one of the focus items in the school checkup (see item 7). In the author's experience, the same applies to strabismus, amblyopia, tooth decay, and heart surgery for congenital defects where the timing can be chosen. The later these are treated, the worse the result may be or the higher the cost. This section did not check each one. Ask a specialist about the exact windows. Worried about missing class? During compulsory education, a child can take a leave of absence of up to 1 year, with school registration kept. See item 11.

### 3. If a child is bullied, report it to the school the same day and ask for a written process. Call the police for hitting, robbery or spreading rumours
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health who=child -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Tell the class teacher, and also the school's anti-bullying office (the body that handles bullying cases). To report to police, call 110, China's police emergency number. The hard part is holding firm against advice to "keep it quiet."
- Plain language: Bullied children are about 2.2 times as likely to have suicidal thoughts, and about 2.6 times as likely to have attempted suicide. Schools must investigate reports at once and act if bullying is confirmed. So report the same day, ask for a formal finding, and get it in writing.
- Benefit: Pooling 34 studies with 284,375 children and adolescents: bullying by peers was linked to suicidal thoughts, OR 2.23 (95% CI 2.10 to 2.37), meaning about 2.2 times the chance of having such thoughts compared with those not bullied. Nine further studies with 70,102 people linked bullying to suicide attempts, OR 2.55 (1.95 to 3.34), about 2.6 times. Results were consistent across sex, age and study quality. The link between online bullying and suicidal thoughts was stronger than for face-to-face bullying. Article 21 of the Regulations on School Protection of Minors lists five kinds of conduct: hitting, kicking, slapping, scratching or biting, and pushing or pulling; insults, mockery or abusive nicknames; grabbing, forcibly taking or deliberately damaging property; deliberate exclusion; and fabricating facts or spreading rumours online to defame. The same article says that when a stronger party (by age, physical strength or numbers) does these things deliberately or maliciously and causes personal injury, property loss or mental harm, it can be recognised as bullying. Article 22 requires staff who notice unusual emotions or physical injuries to look into them and report. Article 23 requires schools to investigate immediately after a report, submit the case to the anti-bullying body for a finding and handling, and inform parents so they can take part. Where bullying is confirmed, schools must impose educational discipline or disciplinary sanctions. Serious bullying that breaks public security rules or may be criminal must not be concealed, and must be promptly reported to the public security authorities and the education authority. (National; in force from 1 September 2021.)
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: van Geel M, et al. (2014). Relationship between peer victimization, cyberbullying, and suicide in children and adolescents: a meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2013.4143>；教育部 (2021). 未成年人学校保护规定（教育部令第 50 号，第二十一、二十二、二十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-06/02/content_5614946.htm>；教育部等十一部门 (2017). 加强中小学生欺凌综合治理方案（教督〔2017〕10 号，三（一））. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2018/content_5294435.htm>
- Notes: China only: The signs named in Article 22 are clear emotional changes and physical injury. If a child has unexplained injuries or a sudden change in mood, ask about it first. This meta-analysis pooled observational studies with no control groups. So it cannot prove that bullying alone causes suicide. But the direction and size of the risk are clear. The Comprehensive Governance Plan on Bullying in Primary and Secondary Schools also requires schools to distinguish bullying from rough play between students. So schools are obliged to make a finding, and cannot dismiss it as "kids messing around."

### 4. Give children 2 hours outdoors every day: the only free, drug-free myopia measure backed by a randomised trial
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some gain=high scope=health who=child -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. But you need to make time every day, most likely by cutting into homework or online-class time. The hard part is doing it every day, not just a few times.
- Plain language: For first graders, 40 extra minutes of outdoor lessons a day cut the share of children who became short-sighted over three years from about 40% to about 30%. National guidance is at least 2 hours of daytime outdoor time a day, or 14 hours a week in total. Cloudy days count too.
- Benefit: Twelve primary schools in Guangzhou, with first-grade pupils, were randomised by school (952 children in intervention schools, 951 in control schools; mean age 6.6 years). The intervention added one 40-minute outdoor lesson on each school day, and families were encouraged to take children outside more outside school. Over 3 years, the rate of new myopia was 30.4% in the intervention group (259 of 853 eligible children) and 39.5% in the control group (287 of 726). The difference was -9.1 percentage points (95% CI -14.1 to -4.1, P<0.001), meaning the true gap is roughly 4 to 14 percentage points. Over 3 years, the change in degree (spherical equivalent) was -1.42 D versus -1.59 D, a difference of 0.17 D (P=0.04). The intervention group's degree worsened slightly less. D is the unit for measuring lens power. For axial length (the front-to-back length of the eyeball), no difference was seen between groups (P=0.07). Drug trials also exist. In Hong Kong, 474 children aged 4 to 9 who were not yet short-sighted were split into three groups and given eye drops every night for two years. Over two years, 28.4% of those on 0.05% atropine became myopic, versus 53.0% on placebo. Those on 0.01% atropine had 45.9%, with no clear difference from placebo.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: He M, et al. (2015). Effect of Time Spent Outdoors at School on the Development of Myopia Among Children in China: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2015.10803>；Yam JC, Zhang XJ, Zhang Y, et al. (2023). Effect of Low-Concentration Atropine Eyedrops vs Placebo on Myopia Incidence in Children: The LAMP2 Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA, 329(6), 472-481. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.24162>；国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Notes: This trial lasted only 3 years, and the children all started at around age 6. A 9.1 percentage-point difference in myopia rates means the programme can prevent myopia in some children, but cannot guarantee that none will develop it. The same national guidance also gives other advice. For reading and writing, follow the "one foot, one fist, one inch" posture rule. After 20 minutes of close-up work, look into the distance for at least 20 seconds. For school-age children, non-study use of electronic screens should be no more than 15 minutes at a time and no more than 1 hour a day in total. Being outdoors is not the same as PE class. Standing outside during break also counts. The authors of the atropine trial say more trials are needed to confirm the results and to check long-term safety. Whether to use drops, and which strength, is a question for an eye doctor. The author did not find any other free, drug-free method tested in a randomised trial.

### 5. Under 3, no screens. Ages 3 to 6, avoid them where possible. School-age non-study use no more than 1 hour a day
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=high gain=mid scope=health who=infant,child -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. The hard part is taking the screen away from the child, and also cutting back where the child can see you.
- Plain language: The National Health Commission says children under 3 should have no screens, and children aged 3 to 6 should avoid them where possible. For school-age children, non-study screen use should be no more than 15 minutes at a time and 1 hour a day. The evidence linking screens to myopia is much weaker than for daily outdoor time.
- Benefit: Article 4 of the Ten Core Facts on Preventing and Controlling Myopia in Children and Adolescents says, in full: "Children under 3 should not use mobile phones, tablets, computers or other video-based electronic products. Children aged 3 to 6 should avoid contact with and use of mobile phones, tablets, computers and other video-based electronic products as far as possible. School-age children should use electronic screens for non-study purposes for no more than 15 minutes at a time, and no more than 1 hour a day in total." The same item states that "long periods of close-up use of video-based electronic products easily use up children's reserve of farsightedness, and are an important cause of early onset and high prevalence of myopia." The 2018 implementation plan from eight ministries, led by the Ministry of Education, gives parents the same requirements and adds: "the younger the child, the shorter continuous use of electronic products should be." The research evidence is much weaker. One systematic review included 33 studies, 11 of which were pooled, with participants aged from 3 months to 33 years. For mobile phones and tablets alone, the odds ratio for myopia was 1.26 (95% CI 1.00 to 1.60, I²=77%). Combining with computers gave 1.77 (1.28 to 2.45, I²=87%). The authors conclude only that the link is "possibly related", and note that none of the 33 studies had a reliable measure of screen time.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: 国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm> ; 教育部等八部门 (2018). 综合防控儿童青少年近视实施方案（教体艺〔2018〕3 号）. <http://www.moe.gov.cn/srcsite/A17/moe_943/s3285/201808/t20180830_346672.html> ; Foreman J, Salim AT, Praveen A, et al. (2021). Association between digital smart device use and myopia: a systematic review and meta-analysis. The Lancet Digital Health, 3(12), e806-e818. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500(21)00135-7> ; Madigan S, McArthur BA, Anhorn C, Eirich R, Christakis DA (2020). Associations between screen use and child language skills: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics, 174(7), 665-675. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2020.0327>
- Notes: The rating is B because the official figures are fixed guidance bands, not numbers measured in a trial. The lower bound of the odds ratio sits right at 1.00, and the authors would only say "possibly related." So treat this as a house rule that is easy to follow, not as "no screens, no myopia." The only item backed by a randomised trial is item 4 (2 hours outdoors). Children of pre-school age should also have their eyes checked. The same document says children aged 1 to 3, 4 to 6, and 7 and over should all have regular refractive screening to see how much farsightedness reserve is left. For how to check, see item 12 (eye exam with dilating drops). A meta-analysis of screen use and children's language skills pooled 42 studies with 18,905 children. Longer screen time was linked with weaker language skills (r=−0.14, 95% CI −0.18 to −0.10). But watching together, or watching educational programmes, was positively linked (r=0.16 and 0.13). This is correlation, not causation. It only suggests that using screens in place of talking to a child is not a good trade.

### 6. Treat myopia as lasting damage: higher degrees bring more retinal detachment, macular disease and glaucoma later
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=health who=child -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. There is nothing to do directly. The actions are in items 4 and 5 (2 hours outdoors, screen limits by age).
- Plain language: Myopia does not go away, and the degree cannot be reversed. Compared with people without it, the chance of myopic macular degeneration is over ten times higher with mild myopia, and several hundred times higher above 600 degrees. Retinal detachment is about 3 times more likely with mild myopia and about 13 times more likely with 600 degrees or more.
- Benefit: A systematic review and meta-analysis pooled studies up to June 2019 and grouped myopia by degree: mild (−0.5 to −3.00 D), moderate (−3.00 to −6.00 D), and high (≤ −6.00 D, i.e. 600 degrees or more). Compared with people without myopia, the odds ratios for myopic macular degeneration were 13.57 (95% CI 6.18 to 29.79), 72.74 (33.18 to 159.48) and 845.08 (230.05 to 3104.34), for mild, moderate and high myopia respectively. For retinal detachment they were 3.15 (1.92 to 5.17), 8.74 (7.28 to 10.50) and 12.62 (6.65 to 23.94). For posterior subcapsular cataract they were 1.56 (1.32 to 1.84), 2.55 (1.98 to 3.28) and 4.55 (2.66 to 7.75). For open-angle glaucoma, mild myopia gave 1.59 (1.33 to 1.91), and moderate and high combined gave 2.92 (1.89 to 4.52). For vision impairment in people over 60, the odds ratios by band were 1.71 (1.07 to 2.74), 5.54 (3.12 to 9.85) and 87.63 (34.50 to 222.58). The Ten Core Facts on Preventing and Controlling Myopia in Children and Adolescents agrees: 600 degrees or more is high myopia, and in this group the rates of blinding eye diseases, including cataract, open-angle glaucoma, myopic macular degeneration, retinal detachment and myopic optic neuropathy, are clearly higher than in other people. Its message is that "myopia can be prevented and controlled, but not reversed."
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Haarman AEG, Enthoven CA, Tideman JWL, Tedja MS, Verhoeven VJM, Klaver CCW (2020). The complications of myopia: a review and meta-analysis. Investigative Ophthalmology & Visual Science, 61(4), 49. <https://doi.org/10.1167/iovs.61.4.49> ; 国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Notes: Everything pooled here is from observational studies, and the odds ratios are unusually large. For high myopia and macular degeneration, the 95% CI runs from 230 to 3104, showing that the individual studies differ widely. Treat the figures as orders of magnitude, not exact values. The vision-impairment figures in Benefit are for people aged over 60. From this, the author infers that these harms mostly appear in middle or older age. Myopia is not fixed just by getting glasses. It is worth the effort to delay it and keep it under control. For how to get follow-up checks after diagnosis, see item 12 (eye exam with dilating drops). Don't buy products that claim to cure it. See item 9 (myopia "cure" claims).

### 7. Read the annual school checkup report yourself, and take any abnormal items to a specialist that same year
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=health who=child -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Compulsory-education checkup fees are paid from the school's public funds, so parents don't pay extra.
- Plain language: Schools hold a health checkup once a year. Focus on overweight, obesity, malnutrition, abnormal spinal curvature, poor vision and tooth decay. Don't just check height and weight and put the report away. If any of these is abnormal, see a specialist that same year.
- Benefit: The Administrative Measures for Student Health Checkups in Primary and Secondary Schools (2021 edition) require schools to hold one student health checkup each year. The surgical examination covers the head, neck, chest, spine, limbs, skin and lymph nodes. The eye examination covers the external eye, distance vision and refraction (the degree of short- or long-sightedness). The individual report must show the findings, an overall assessment and health guidance. Overweight, obesity, malnutrition, abnormal spinal curvature, poor vision and tooth decay (cavities) must be the focus of guidance. Education authorities must keep records on students with health problems and follow up on them. Checkup costs for compulsory-education students are paid from school public funds, so families do not pay extra. (National; issued September 2021.)
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: 国家卫生健康委、教育部 (2021). 中小学生健康体检管理办法（2021 年版）（国卫医发〔2021〕29 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-10/21/content_5644100.htm>
- Notes: China only: The rating is B because this is a document setting out how schools must operate, not an effectiveness study. It guarantees that checks happen every year, cover these items, and that abnormal findings are followed up. It does not guarantee that anyone acts on a finding. The checkup is only a first screen, not a diagnosis. Poor vision needs a dilating eye exam at an eye clinic (item 12). Abnormal spinal curvature needs a visit to an orthopaedic or spine specialist (item 2; don't delay treatment until after exams). Parents must take the child to both themselves.

### 8. Get children aged 12 to 18 screened for depression. Don't treat a school mental health survey as a diagnosis
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=health who=child -->
- Cost: Free to a few dozen yuan (RMB). Fill in a screening questionnaire at a community or hospital outpatient clinic. The 12355 and 12356 hotlines are free to call.
- Plain language: US guidelines recommend depression screening at ages 12 to 18, but not for children 11 and under. Suicide is the second leading cause of death for ages 10 to 19 in the US. The school's yearly mental health survey is a screening, not a diagnosis. If your child says they are struggling, see a psychiatrist or clinical psychologist directly.
- Benefit: In 2022, the US Preventive Services Task Force issued a recommendation statement. It recommends screening adolescents aged 12 to 18 for major depressive disorder (grade B: moderate certainty of a moderate net benefit, so worth doing). For children 11 and under, the evidence on screening for major depression is insufficient (grade I, meaning the evidence is not enough to give a recommendation). For screening specifically for suicide risk in children and adolescents, the evidence on benefits and harms is also insufficient (grade I). The statement notes that suicide is the second leading cause of death for people aged 10 to 19. It also notes that major depression in children and adolescents is closely linked to many later problems. These include recurrent depression in adulthood, other mental disorders, and a higher risk of suicidal thoughts, attempts and deaths. The Ten Measures to Further Strengthen Mental Health Work for Primary and Secondary School Students require a three-tier service system: class teachers, full- or part-time mental health teachers, and psychiatrists. Each student takes a mental health assessment once per school year, generally no more than once. The measures also require referral to medical care for mental illness and a mechanism for returning to school after recovery. They call for using the 12355 Youth Service Hotline and the 12356 Psychological Assistance Hotline. (National; issued October 2025.)
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: US Preventive Services Task Force (2022). Screening for Depression and Suicide Risk in Children and Adolescents: US Preventive Services Task Force Recommendation Statement. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.16946>；教育部办公厅 (2025). 进一步加强中小学生心理健康工作十条措施（教基厅〔2025〕2 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202510/content_7045528.htm>
- Notes: China only: The rating is B because the source gives a recommendation grade, not an effect size that can be turned into numbers. The guideline is also a US recommendation, and there is no equivalent official screening advice in China. A positive screen only means a further assessment is needed. It is not a diagnosis. For what to do if there are suicidal thoughts, see item 25 in section 1. For how to handle it at home, see section 29.

### 9. Don't buy products that claim to "cure" myopia or "lower" the degree
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=money who=child -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. In fact, you save the money you would otherwise have spent.
- Plain language: The market regulator has said plainly: with current medical technology, myopia cannot be cured. Ads claiming "recovery," "lower degree" or "cure" should be strictly punished. If you see such claims, skip the product without comparing prices. Eye-care devices are medical devices, and platforms must check sellers' qualifications.
- Benefit: The 2021 to 2022 special campaign notice from the General Office of the State Administration for Market Regulation states clearly: "with current medical technology, myopia cannot be cured." The notice requires strict enforcement against marketing of child and adolescent myopia prevention products that uses misleading terms such as "rehabilitation," "recovery," "lower degree," "myopia cure," "myopia nemesis" or "degree repair." The notice calls for stronger action against conduct that passes off traditional Chinese medicine or uses its theories as a false pretext to deceive consumers. It also calls for strict action against two kinds of false or illegal advertising: using a patient's name or image for before-and-after comparisons, and making guaranteed promises about the safety or effectiveness of treatment. The notice requires e-commerce platforms to strictly enforce their duty to check the business qualifications of sellers of eye-care devices and other restricted products. (National.)
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: 市场监管总局办公厅 (2021). 关于开展儿童青少年近视防控产品违法违规商业营销宣传专项整治行动的通知. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-11/12/content_5650400.htm>
- Notes: China only: The rating is B because it rests on the regulator's enforcement standard, not on a controlled test of the products. But the statement "myopia cannot be cured" is consistent across health and regulatory bodies. The Ten Core Facts say "myopia can be prevented and controlled, but not reversed." The two things with real evidence are item 4 (outdoor time) and item 12 (proper eye testing and follow-up). For blue-light glasses, see section 6.

### 10. Sleep, homework, PE and rankings are all covered by official rules. If a school falls short, you can raise it
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=time who=child -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Just know the rules, and raise them with the school or the local education authority if needed. The hard part is speaking up.
- Plain language: The rules say primary pupils should sleep 10 hours a day, middle schoolers 9, and high schoolers 8. Primary lessons should start no earlier than 8:20, and secondary lessons no earlier than 8:00. If your child is always short of sleep, check these rules before blaming them.

### 13. After the permanent molars come in, get pit and fissure sealants
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no gain=mid scope=health who=child -->
- Cost: Tens to a couple of hundred RMB per tooth. No anaesthetic and no drilling; it takes a few minutes. Many areas offer free programmes for children of the right age.
- Plain language: Sealing fills the grooves on the chewing surface of the back teeth with resin, so food scraps cannot get stuck in them. Two years after sealing, the chance of a cavity is about one-eighth of that for an unsealed tooth. Where 16% of tooth surfaces would decay without sealing, about 5% do after sealing. The protection still works four years later.
- Benefit: 38 trials with 7,924 children aged 5 to 16, pooled by Cochrane using its standard methods. The comparison was resin-based sealants against no sealant. For first permanent molars in children aged 5 to 10, at 24 months the OR for cavities was 0.12 (95% CI 0.08–0.19), so the chance of a cavity was about one-eighth that of unsealed teeth. This came from 7 trials with 1,548 children randomised and 1,322 finally examined; evidence quality was moderate. If 16% of tooth surfaces in the control group developed cavities within 24 months, the figure after sealing is 5.2% (3.13%–7.37%). At a control rate of 40%, it is 6.25% (3.84%–9.63%). At 70%, it is 19% (12.3%–27.2%). At 48 to 54 months, the OR was 0.21 (0.16–0.28), still about one-fifth that of unsealed teeth. The authors conclude that resin sealants at 24 months result in 11% to 51% fewer cavities than no sealant. None of the 4 trials that recorded adverse events reported any. The evidence is insufficient to say whether one type of glass-ionomer sealing material is better than another.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Ahovuo-Saloranta A, et al. (2017). Pit and fissure sealants for preventing dental decay in permanent teeth. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001830.pub5>
- Notes: China only: The first permanent molar (the adult tooth that is never replaced) usually erupts around age 6, and the second around age 12. ### 16. Do not hit your child, and do not yell at them or call them stupid: children who are often hit or scolded have more behaviour and emotional problems

Childhood aggression: d = 0.37 (95% CI 0.13–0.61; credible range). Externalising problems (such as hitting others and breaking rules): 0.41. Physical abuse by parents: 0.64. Adult antisocial behaviour: 0.36; adult mental health problems: 0.24. All 111 effect sizes combined: d = 0.33 (0.29–0.38). When children were 13, parents who often yelled, swore, or called the child stupid or lazy saw the child's trouble-making and depressive symptoms both increase between ages 13 and 14. ### 21. If a child hits their head during sport and then has a headache, dizziness or feels dazed, take them off play that day. If they keep vomiting, get sleepier, or the headache keeps getting worse, go to the emergency department at once
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health who=child -->
- Cost: Free. Take the child off the field, with no more practice or competition that day. The hard part is that during a close game the child says "I'm fine", and the coach and parents want them to keep playing.
- Plain language: Young athletes who kept playing after a concussion took an average of 44 days to recover, against about 22 days for those who stopped right away. If something feels wrong after a knock to the head, stop that day, and do not return until a doctor says it is safe. Repeated vomiting, growing drowsiness or being hard to wake, a headache that keeps getting worse, slurred speech or convulsions are all signs to go to the emergency department at once.
- Benefit: A concussion is a temporary disruption of brain function after a blow to the head, and it usually does not show up on scans. A US study followed 69 young athletes with sport-related concussion, average age about 15. Among the 35 who stopped right away, recovery averaged 22.0 days. Among the 34 who kept playing with symptoms, it averaged 44.4 days (P=0.003, unlikely to be chance). Those who kept playing were 8.80 times as likely to take more than 21 days to recover as those who stopped. The 2022 Amsterdam International Conference on Concussion in Sport issued a consensus. It says anyone with loss of consciousness, convulsions, unsteadiness, confusion, behaviour change or memory loss must leave play immediately, and should not return to play or training that day. The exception is when experienced medical staff check on the spot and confirm the injury is not a concussion. The US CDC's guide for parents and coaches is more direct: if concussion is suspected, take the athlete out at once, do not return them that day, and wait for a doctor to say they can go back. It also says not to judge severity yourself, since only medical staff can assess it. It lists signs that need emergency care at once: convulsions; not recognising people or places; repeated vomiting; abnormal behaviour, or increasing confusion or agitation; drowsiness after passing out, or being hard to wake; slurred speech, or weakness or numbness in the limbs; a headache that keeps getting worse and does not ease; unequal pupils, or double vision (United States / multiple countries).
