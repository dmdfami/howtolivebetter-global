# 17. Elderly parents at home

Basis: money and personal freedom. When elderly parents live at home, most of the trouble is not a lack of money. It is failing to say things clearly in advance and failing to get the paperwork signed. Bedsores from long bed rest, cataract surgery, a fall while walking the dog, and a memory check at the hospital: these four are priced by mortality, and are not compared with the other items in this section.

### 1. While the elderly person is still clear-headed, put in writing who will be their guardian
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=some gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: A notary office charges a few hundred yuan to draw up the document. Agreeing on it takes one conversation. The hard part is starting it: you have to talk about the day they can no longer manage their own affairs.
- Plain language: While they are still clear-headed, have the elderly person put in writing who will be their guardian, ideally notarized. Without that, the law's default order applies: spouse, then parents and children, then other close relatives. If relatives disagree, the neighborhood or village committee, the civil affairs department or a court decides.
- Benefit: The Civil Code lets an adult with full civil capacity, meaning someone clear-headed who can make their own decisions, agree in advance on who will be their guardian. The guardian can be a close relative, or another individual or organization willing to take on the role. The agreement must be in writing. If the person later loses the ability to decide, fully or in part, the person named in the writing takes charge. Without this document, guardianship follows the order in Article 28 of the Civil Code: spouse; parents and children; other close relatives; then any other willing individual or organization approved by the neighborhood or village committee or the civil affairs department. Article 31 says that if there is a dispute over who should be guardian, the neighborhood or village committee or the civil affairs department designates one. A person who disagrees can ask a court to designate one, or go straight to court.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第二十八条、第三十一条、第三十三条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>
- Notes: China only: This arrangement is called voluntary guardianship, meaning the person chooses their guardian in advance. That chosen person ranks ahead of the default guardian order in the law. Elderly people who live alone, have children far away, or have complicated family relationships especially need to do this early. The author recommends having it notarized at a notary office.

### 2. Make a will, and remember that a later will overrides an earlier one. A notarized will no longer takes priority automatically
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Handwriting a will costs nothing. Having a notary office certify it costs several hundred to a thousand-plus yuan. The hard part is starting the conversation with family about what happens after death.
- Plain language: The law recognizes six kinds of wills, each with its own required steps. For a handwritten will, the person must write the whole thing by hand, sign it, and write the date. If you have made several wills that conflict, the last one you made counts. Since 2021, a notarized will no longer automatically comes first.
- Benefit: The Civil Code recognizes six forms of will: handwritten, written by someone else, typed, audio or video recorded, oral, and notarized. Each has its own formal requirements. For a handwritten will, Article 1134 of the Civil Code requires that it be "written in the testator's own hand, signed, and dated with year, month and day." If you have made several wills whose contents conflict, the one made last prevails. Since 2021, a notarized will no longer automatically takes priority.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第一千一百三十四条至第一千一百四十二条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>；最高人民法院 (2020). 关于适用《中华人民共和国民法典》继承编的解释（一）（法释〔2020〕23 号，第二十四条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/282091.html>
- Notes: China only: A handwritten will must be written entirely by hand by the testator, signed, and dated with year, month and day. A printed will needs two or more witnesses. The testator and the witnesses must sign each page and write the date. Heirs, people named in the will to receive property, and people with a stake in it cannot act as witnesses. The judicial interpretation names creditors, debtors and business partners as interested parties. Whether their spouses and children, as close relatives, count is not stated directly in the law or the interpretation. The author advises not using them as witnesses, to avoid later disputes. A wrong form voids the whole will, and this is the most common mistake. For small deposits and house inheritance after death, see section 25, item 12 (do not default to going to a notary first when handling an inheritance).

### 3. Keep the elderly person's money in a separate account, and set a rule that large expenses need two people to confirm
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=money who=elderly,60plus -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Talk with family once and agree on an amount. The hard part is how to raise it without making the elderly person feel you are watching them.
- Plain language: Scammers often give small gifts and show warmth first to build trust, then take large sums. They also often say "don't tell anyone." Agree in advance on spending above a set amount that must first be mentioned to a specific person. Family then knows before money leaves, and the decision gets delayed.
- Benefit: In 2022, China's Supreme People's Procuratorate, the national prosecutors' office, published typical cases of pension fraud. It said these crimes first win trust with small favors and family-style care, then take large sums through false promotion and promises of high returns. In one case, a company selling fake collectibles sent staff to clean the elderly person's home, keep them company, and even take them on as fictive "godchildren." When selling, they sealed the goods in black bags and told the elderly not to open them, so family would not notice. The company took in over 1.92 million yuan, and its boss was sentenced to seven years and six months for fraudulent fundraising. In 2023, Beijing's local financial regulator warned elderly people not to believe scammers who say "don't tell anyone." When unsure, talk with family, children and trusted neighbors and friends, and get family opinion before investing. The US Federal Trade Commission tallied fraud losses reported by people aged 60 and over: about $600 million in 2020, rising to about $2.4 billion in 2024. The rise came mainly from cases with single losses above $100,000. Agreeing in advance that "spending above a certain amount must first be mentioned to a specific person" means family hears about it before money is paid, and it delays the decision.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: 最高人民检察院 (2022). 最高检发布打击整治养老诈骗犯罪典型案例（许某燕集资诈骗案及答记者问）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/202206/t20220617_560010.shtml>；北京市地方金融监督管理局 (2023). 揭秘专骗老年人新套路 提高防骗意识 守护好「钱袋子」. <https://jrj.beijing.gov.cn/jrxcjy/jraq/ffffjzxkt/202311/t20231127_3310210.html>；Federal Trade Commission (2025). FTC Issues Annual Report to Congress on Agency's Actions to Protect Older Adults（Protecting Older Consumers, 2024-2025）. <https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/ftc-issues-annual-report-congress-agencys-actions-protect-older-adults>
- Notes: No study has compared how much less fraud happens in families that set this rule. The grade is B because the scam tactics and the advice to consult family come from prosecutors and financial regulators. The basis is the same as item 6 (home-equity pensions). People are more easily persuaded when they are excited; see item 4 ("I need to go home and talk with my family"). General fraud prevention is in sections 8 and 13. Do not frame this as "I'm afraid you'll be scammed." Frame it as "the household keeps its money in one shared account," which elderly people accept more easily. Also do not hand all of the elderly person's money to the children to manage, as that creates a different kind of dispute.

### 4. Prepare one sentence the elderly person can say at any time to decline: "I need to go home and talk with my family"
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=money who=elderly,60plus -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. A few minutes of conversation with the elderly person.
- Plain language: Scams rely on pressure in the moment, so you pay before you can think. Strong emotions make people want advertised products more, and older people more than younger ones. Agree in advance on one sentence: "I need to go home and talk with my family." Saying it gives them a way out, so they can decide calmly later.
- Benefit: Pushy sales and scams both rely on pressure in the moment. The US Federal Trade Commission's scam-prevention guidance says scammers try to make you act before you have time to think. On the phone, they tell you not to hang up, so you cannot check the story. The FTC's advice is to tell someone before doing anything, whether a friend, family member or neighbor, because saying it aloud can make the scam obvious. The former China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission's 2021 risk warning also advised older people to discuss financial products with family before buying. One experiment recruited 116 people aged 65 to 85 and 115 people aged 30 to 40. Researchers first used a win-or-lose game to arouse some participants' emotions, then showed them exaggerated, misleading ads. Those whose emotions were genuinely aroused were more inclined to buy what the ads showed. Regardless of arousal, older people were more inclined to buy than younger ones. The authors advise waiting until emotions have calmed before making spending decisions. A second experiment recruited people who had previously lost money to mail fraud, most of them older. Those not warned in advance, who received a simulated scam sales call, nearly half agreed on the spot. Those who were called two to four weeks earlier to be warned agreed on the spot far less often. Agreeing in advance on the sentence "I need to go home and talk with my family" gives elderly people a way out, so they do not have to decide on the spot.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Federal Trade Commission (2023). How To Avoid a Scam. <https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-avoid-scam>；中国银保监会消费者权益保护局 (2021). 关于警惕「投资养老」「以房养老」金融诈骗的风险提示（2021 年第三期）. <https://dfjrjgj.hunan.gov.cn/dfjrjgj/yhlj/202106/t20210608_19450856.html>（湖南省地方金融监督管理局转载）；Kircanski K, Notthoff N, DeLiema M, et al. (2018). Emotional arousal may increase susceptibility to fraud in older and younger adults. Psychology and Aging, 33(2), 325-337. <https://doi.org/10.1037/pag0000228>；Scheibe S, Notthoff N, Menkin J, et al. (2014). Forewarning reduces fraud susceptibility in vulnerable consumers. Basic and Applied Social Psychology, 36(3), 272-279. <https://doi.org/10.1080/01973533.2014.903844>
- Notes: The grade is B because neither experiment tested this sentence itself. One tested how likely people were to buy while emotionally aroused; the other tested whether advance warnings work. No study has compared how much less fraud happens among people who prepared this sentence. Use it together with item 3 (keep the elderly person's money in a separate account). Fall-proofing the home can be done at the same time; see section 1. This sentence gives the elderly person a step down so they do not have to refuse on the spot. It is not a way of guarding against them. When salespeople surround them, the hardest thing is to say no in front of a roomful of people, which is harder than judging whether something is real.

### 5. Turn down every "pension investment" that asks elderly people to pay first. Membership cards, buying beds, senior apartments, travel-retirement programs and senior products are all the same illegal fundraising scheme
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=money who=elderly,60plus -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Read these schemes out loud to the elderly person, a few minutes' work. Afterward, refuse directly: do not attend lectures, do not visit, and do not leave a phone number. The hard part: if elderly people have already gone several times and grown familiar with the people there, they are harder to talk out of it.
- Plain language: Any "pension investment" that asks elderly people to pay upfront and promises returns is the same scam. Common forms include membership cards, buying beds, senior apartments, travel-retirement programs and senior products. Under the rules, you must bear losses from such money yourself. One scheme sold bookings for senior-apartment beds and took 136 million yuan from more than 2,100 people.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Civil Affairs lists five forms. Recognizing the scheme's name is more reliable than recognizing the person. First, using the name of a senior care institution: there is no real operation. A company rents a venue and claims a cooperation agreement with a care home to ride on its credibility. Second, offering "senior services" as cover: selling "VIP cards," "membership cards" and "prepaid cards" that require paying "service fees" in advance. The services promised clearly exceed the bed capacity the company actually has. Third, using "senior care projects" as cover for investment. Common labels include joining a wellness and elder-care base and buying imaginary senior apartments. Others include long-term rental of care beds and "time bank" mutual-aid care. The lure is "return of principal" sales, "lease-back after sale" and "buy-back agreements." Fourth, using "senior products" as cover: buy-back of goods, consignment storage, consumer rebates, free health checks, gifts, meeting-based marketing, health lectures and free expert consultations. Fifth, using "travel-retirement" as cover: discounted or free trips, stored-value rebates, and points-based retirement. Money cannot be recovered, and the regulations say so. Article 25 of the Regulations on Preventing and Handling Illegal Fundraising states that losses suffered by participants "shall be borne by the participants themselves." In one officially reported case, Dai Mou-ping and others took 136 million yuan in illegal fundraising under the name of booking senior-apartment beds, with more than 2,100 victims. Dai Mou-ping was sentenced to 15 years for fundraising fraud and fined 400,000 yuan. Cao Mou-ming raised 13.207 billion yuan through fundraising fraud under the name of investing in the senior care industry, involving more than 110,000 people. Cao was sentenced to life imprisonment, lifetime deprivation of political rights, and confiscation of all personal property. Health-supplement sales meetings are prosecuted as fraud. Ni Mou-rong used free banquets, gifts and chartered cars to take elderly people to lectures. Lecturers used "fake personas, fake cases and fake markdowns" to invent claims of cancer prevention and treatment effects. Elderly people were tricked into buying dihydroquercetin at several dozen times its cost price. Sales totaled over 620,000 yuan. Ni was sentenced to 10 years and 3 months and fined 120,000 yuan. (Nationwide; the regulations took effect on May 1, 2021.)
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 民政部 (2023). 关于养老服务领域非法集资的风险提示. <https://www.gov.cn/lianbo/bumen/202306/content_6886556.htm>（中国政府网发布）；民政部、中国银保监会 (2021). 关于养老领域非法集资的风险提示. <https://www.mca.gov.cn/n152/n164/c36171/content.html>；国务院 (2021). 防范和处置非法集资条例（国务院令第 737 号，第二、二十五、三十条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-02/10/content_5586632.htm>；最高人民检察院 (2022). 检察机关打击整治养老诈骗犯罪典型案例. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/202206/t20220617_560010.shtml>；最高人民法院 (2025). 依法惩治涉民生领域诈骗犯罪典型案例（案例五 倪某荣诈骗案）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/482861.html>
- Notes: China only: Judge by whether they ask you to pay first and promise returns, not by whether the company looks legitimate. A senior care institution is meant to sell services. If the pitch brings in investment, rebates or getting your money back, do not believe it. Being taken to lectures, trips or free clinics is not illegal in itself, and it is hard to stop. What you can stop is the payment step. Pair this with the two-person sign-off rule in item 3. If money has already been paid, call 110 (the police line) or 96110 (the national anti-fraud hotline) right away and ask for the payment to be stopped. At the same time, report to the local lead agency that handles illegal fundraising. For the home-equity route, see item 6 (reverse mortgage pension insurance). For deposit and membership-fee limits and receiving accounts at legitimate care homes, see item 11 of this section (deposits capped at 12 months of bed fees). For being taken into a shop on a cheap group tour, see section 5, item 50 (group tours that lead to shops).

### 6. Apart from the reverse mortgage pension insurance sold by insurers, avoid all other "home-equity pension" schemes. Never mortgage your home to buy investment products
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=money who=elderly,60plus -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Do not hand the house deed to anyone who comes to the door in the name of "home-equity pension." Have your children read through any mortgage, guarantee or loan agreement before you sign it. The hard part: visitors are often warm and keep coming back, so elderly people find it hard to say no.
- Plain language: Only one legitimate home-equity pension exists: the reverse mortgage pension insurance from insurers, which is still in pilot. Scammers' "home-equity pensions" have nothing to do with it. They trick elderly people into mortgaging their homes, then use the money to buy investment products they recommend. Some people lose their home without knowing it was mortgaged, and still owe the loan.
- Benefit: The CBIRC's consumer protection bureau has laid out the difference between real and fake versions. The legitimate home-equity pension has only one form: the "reverse mortgage pension insurance for elderly people." An elderly person who fully owns a house legally mortgages it to an insurance company. They keep living in the house, using it, and collecting any rent from it. Selling or disposing of it requires the consent of the mortgagee, the insurer. The elderly person receives a pension on the agreed terms until they die. This product is still in pilot, and few providers offer it. What scammers call "home-equity pension" has "nothing to do with" the government-backed reverse mortgage pension insurance. It is "a means of using a false government policy to promote illegal fundraising." Their method is to trick elderly people into signing house mortgages, then use the borrowed money to buy the investment products they recommend, which are "often Ponzi schemes that pay old investors with new money." The consequence, in the original wording: some participants "do not even know their house has been mortgaged, and end up losing the house while still owing the loan." The notice also gives a yield guide: "If a wealth-management product promises a return above 6%, treat it with suspicion; above 8% is dangerous; at 10% or more, be prepared to lose your entire principal. 'Principal-guaranteed high returns' is financial fraud." The same notice asks you to "be cautious during contract signing and never sign a blank contract" (nationwide, June 2021).
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: 中国银保监会消费者权益保护局 (2021). 关于警惕「投资养老」「以房养老」金融诈骗的风险提示（2021 年第三期）. <https://dfjrjgj.hunan.gov.cn/dfjrjgj/yhlj/202106/t20210608_19450856.html>（湖南省地方金融监督管理局转载）；民政部、中国银保监会 (2021). 关于养老领域非法集资的风险提示（第四种表现形式：以宣称「以房养老」为名非法集资）. <https://www.mca.gov.cn/n152/n164/c36171/content.html>
- Notes: China only: The grade is B because these claims come from regulatory risk warnings and practical judgment, not research data. The 6%, 8% and 10% lines are reference points given by regulators, not legal standards. The CBIRC has been merged into the National Financial Regulatory Administration, and the original notice page no longer opens, so this cites a reprint on a provincial financial regulator's site. Losing money on investments is secondary. Once the house is mortgaged and the elderly person still owes the loan, the loss far exceeds the principal. For the general rules on signing and blank contracts, see item 17 of section 8. For how to stop payment after being scammed, see item 2 of section 8.

### 7. If an elderly relative at home is bedridden for long periods or severely disabled, apply for long-term care insurance at the medical insurance office where they are insured. It is not only for the elderly.
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Submit one application to the medical insurance office where the person is insured. Then have one disability-level assessment. If the person passes the first assessment, the fund pays the assessment fee, so you do not pay it yourself.
- Plain language: Long-term care insurance pays for care services, not usually cash. If someone at home has been severely disabled for more than six months, apply at the medical insurance office where they are insured. There is no age limit, and bed and meal fees are not covered.
- Benefit: Eligible long-term care service costs have no deductible. A deductible is an amount you must pay yourself before reimbursement starts, and here you pay none. For people insured under the employee scheme, the fund reimburses about 70%. Under the scheme for non-employed urban and rural residents, it reimburses about 50%. The annual maximum payment limit may not exceed 50% of the previous year's average per-capita disposable income of urban and rural residents in the local pooling area. To qualify, a person must have paid premiums as required, have a disability that generally lasts 6 months or more, and have been certified after applying and passing an assessment. In the first stage, the scheme covers severe disability first. The rules set no age limit. A person under 18 who does not work is covered under a parent's insurance and, if eligible, receives benefits at the urban and rural residents' rate. The fund pays mainly care institutions and caregivers, and in principle does not pay cash directly to disabled people. Costs that are not care, such as institutional bed fees and meal fees, are not paid by the fund.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国家医保局等八部门 (2026). 关于印发《加快建立长期护理保险制度实施方案》的通知. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202603/content_7063915.htm>：保障对象为「按规定参保缴费且失能状态长期持续（一般为6个月以上），经申请通过评估认定的失能人员」，「制度起步阶段保障重度失能人员」；「待遇享受不设起付标准。符合规定的长期护理服务费用，按未就业城乡居民参保政策参保的，基金支付比例为50%左右；按单位职工参保政策参保的，基金支付比例为70%左右，退休人员享受单位职工参保待遇」；「参保人员基金年度最高支付限额不超过统筹地区上年度城乡居民人均可支配收入的50%」；「除新生儿等特殊群体外，对未在当地长期护理保险制度启动时初次参保，以及中断缴费后再次参保的情形，应制定待遇享受等待期……待遇享受固定等待期原则上按照6个月设置」；「对机构床位费、膳食费等非护理服务费用以及应由医疗保险支付的医疗费用，基金不予支付」；「原则上不直接向失能人员发放现金」；另见中共中央办公厅、国务院办公厅《关于加快建立长期护理保险制度的意见》. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2026/issue_12666/202604/content_7065119.html>
- Notes: China only: Disability must be long-term and continuous (generally 6 months or more). A few weeks in hospital does not meet the requirement to apply. If the local scheme started after the person's first enrolment, or the person re-enrolled after premiums lapsed, there is generally a waiting period of 6 months before benefits start. Bed and meal fees are not care costs, and long-term care insurance does not pay them. Medical costs that medical insurance should pay are not covered either. If someone already receives the work-injury living-care allowance, they do not receive it twice. This benefit and the care allowance under the disability two-subsidy programme are separate applications and separate funds, and they do not conflict; see section 7, item 8 (two-subsidy programme). Home-based and community-based care get a higher reimbursement rate, so the person does not necessarily have to move into an institution. The scheme is rolling out in batches, and disability assessment standards differ between areas. First call 12393 (the national medical insurance service hotline) or the local medical insurance office to ask whether the scheme has started locally and how to book an assessment. If the assessment is not passed, do not give up. The person can apply again when the disability becomes more severe.

### 8. Someone at home is bedridden for long periods: treat pressure sores as the main enemy. Use an electric pressure-relief mattress, turn them on a schedule, and check bony pressure points every day.
<!-- tags: money=low time=high will=high gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: A household electric alternating-pressure mattress costs from a few hundred yuan (RMB). Turning a person takes only a few minutes. The hard part is that you must turn them every two or three hours, including at night, which one person cannot keep up with alone.
- Plain language: Older people who already had pressure sores before hip-fracture surgery are more likely to die within 30 days. An electric air mattress cuts new pressure sores by about 60%. Check the tailbone, heels, hips and shoulders every day, and see a nurse or doctor the same day if you find redness that does not fade when pressed.
- Benefit: The data come from 19,520 hip-fracture surgery patients in the US NSQIP registry, 2016 to 2019. Patients who already had pressure sores before surgery were more likely to die within 30 days. After adjusting for other factors, the link still held (OR 1.2, meaning about 21% higher odds; P=0.004). These patients also had more of other problems: deep vein thrombosis (OR 1.59), pneumonia (OR 1.39) and unplanned readmission (OR 1.43). Their average hospital stay was 0.4 days longer. In a follow-up of 324 hospitalised older people (mean age 86), median follow-up was 18 months. Pressure sores alone predicted long-term death (HR 1.500, 95% CI 1.059 to 2.126, P=0.020). Predicting independently means that, after other factors are excluded, pressure sores still predict death on their own. A network meta-analysis pooled 65 randomised trials comparing electric active-airflow support surfaces with standard hospital bed support surfaces. Electric active-airflow surfaces, a category that includes electric air mattresses, were linked to fewer pressure sores (RR 0.42, 95% CI 0.29 to 0.63; moderate certainty). Electric hybrid airflow surfaces had RR 0.22 (0.07 to 0.66). A Cochrane review of 11 trials with 4,462 people (2026 update) compared turning every 2 hours with turning every 4 hours. The difference was unclear (RR 1.05, 95% CI 0.79 to 1.39; very low certainty). Comparisons between other turning intervals were also highly uncertain. In a trial of 1,226 ICU patients, wearable sensors gave real-time reminders with visual alerts, which got 2-hour turning actually done. Pressure sores were less frequent than with usual care (RR 0.28, 95% CI 0.10 to 0.75; moderate certainty). In a trial of 116 people, the prone (face-down) group had more pressure sores than the supine (on-the-back) group (RR 4.55, 95% CI 2.31 to 8.98).
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Porter SB, Pla R, Chow JH, et al. (2022). Preoperative Pressure Ulcers, Mortality, and Complications in Older Hip Fracture Surgery Patients. JAAOS Global Research & Reviews, 6(11). <https://doi.org/10.5435/JAAOSGlobal-D-22-00117>；Ottaviani S, Rondanina E, Longo E, 等 (2026). Impact of pressure ulcers and frailty on long-term mortality: a prospective cohort study of hospitalized older adults. European Geriatric Medicine, 17(2), 665-672. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s41999-025-01331-8>；Shi C, Dumville JC, Cullum N (2018). Support surfaces for pressure ulcer prevention: A network meta-analysis. PLoS ONE, 13(2), e0192707. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0192707>；Latimer SL, Chaboyer WP, Probst S, 等 (2026). Repositioning for pressure injury prevention in adults. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 6, CD009958. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009958.pub4>
- Notes: B is given for three reasons. First, the two mortality studies only show that people with pressure sores do worse later. That does not mean that preventing sores would lower mortality. Second, the randomised trials on turning intervals are very unreliable. Third, the mattress evidence is stronger, but those studies compared hospital bed mattresses, so applying them to home mattresses is extrapolation. Checking the tailbone, heels, hips and shoulders every day for redness that does not fade under pressure is standard nursing practice; there is no separate study of it. For care services that long-term care insurance can cover, see item 7 of this section. If a leg suddenly swells, treat it as a possible deep vein thrombosis; see section 13, item 11. For bed rest after a fall or serious injury, see section 1, item 34.

### 9. If cataracts already affect an older person's ability to get around, arrange an eye exam and surgery. If both eyes are affected, do not delay the second eye too long.
<!-- tags: money=high time=mid will=no gain=mid scope=health who=elderly,60plus -->
- Cost: First take the older person to an eye clinic for an exam, and let a doctor decide whether surgery is needed. Each eye needs one operation. Many hospitals do it as day surgery, so the person can go home the same day. If both eyes have cataracts, they must be done in two separate operations. How much you pay yourself depends on the type of artificial lens (intraocular lens) chosen and on how local medical insurance reimburses it, so ask before surgery.
- Plain language: For women over 70, surgery on the first eye soon can reduce falls over the next year, by about a third. It does not clearly reduce the number of people who fall at all. People who had cataract surgery also had fewer hip fractures within a year. Surgery on the second eye showed no clear difference in falls.
- Benefit: One randomised trial enrolled 306 women over 70 with cataracts. Half had first-eye surgery within about 4 weeks; the other half waited 12 months as usual. Over one year, those who had early surgery fell about 34% less often. The fall-rate ratio was 0.66 (95% CI 0.45 to 0.96). Among those who fell at least once, 49% in the surgery group and 45% in the waiting group did so, so there was no reduction. Among those who fell two or more times, 18% of the surgery group and 25% of the waiting group did so. The surgery group had 4 fractures (3%) and the waiting group had 12 (8%). The Cochrane review included this trial and concluded that first-eye surgery in women reduced falls, but second-eye surgery did not. A trial of second-eye surgery enrolled 239 women over 70. The surgery group fell 32% less often, but the difference may be chance (fall-rate ratio 0.68, 95% CI 0.39 to 1.19). In a 5% sample of US Medicare, 1,113,640 people over 65 with cataracts (2002 to 2009) were included. This study only tracked records and had no control group. People who had surgery had about 16% lower odds of hip fracture within a year (OR 0.84, 95% CI 0.81 to 0.87). The absolute difference was 0.20 percentage points, about 2 fewer cases per 1,000 people.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Harwood RH, Foss AJ, Osborn F, Gregson RM, Zaman A, Masud T (2005). Falls and health status in elderly women following first eye cataract surgery: a randomised controlled trial. - Notes: China only: Disputed: pooling the two randomised trials, the number of people who fell did not clearly decrease (OR 0.81, 95% CI 0.55 to 1.17). In a whole-population hospital record study in Western Australia, hospital admissions for fall-related injuries rose by 27% in the year after first-eye surgery. In the period between the two operations, such admissions were 2.14 times the pre-operative level. This kind of before-and-after comparison cannot separate the effect of surgery from the effect of ageing. Another Australian follow-up study found that falls dropped most after second-eye surgery. So if both eyes have cataracts, do not delay the second too long. Between the two operations, take extra care when walking and using stairs. The Cochrane review also included a trial of helping older people with vision problems; falls there actually increased by about 57%. Surgery addresses seeing clearly, but preventing falls also depends on balance and leg strength; see section 1, item 13 (balance and leg strength). For whether bones can withstand a fall, see section 1, item 39 (bone density test) and section 1, item 40 (medication once osteoporosis is diagnosed). The national eye health plan states that cataract surgery is now widely available at county level, so you do not necessarily need to go to a big-city hospital. The beneficiaries are mainly your parents and grandparents.

### 10. When an older person walks a dog, hold the leash in the palm rather than wrapped around the hand, shorten it, and train the dog not to lunge forward.
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=some gain=low scope=health who=elderly,60plus -->
- Cost: Holding and shortening the leash costs nothing. Walk with the older person once and show them. Training the dog not to surge forward takes time. You can teach it yourself or hire a dog trainer. Hiring costs money, and prices vary widely by area. The hard part is that older people are used to retractable leashes and wrapping the cord around the wrist, so they must change that habit.
- Plain language: Falls while walking dogs among Americans over 65 rose sharply from 2004 to 2017. A hand surgery clinic found that about three-quarters of leash-related hand injuries happened when the leash was wrapped around the hand and the dog pulled. So hold the leash in your palm, keep it short, and train the dog not to lunge.
- Benefit: US emergency-department surveillance data, scaled to the national level, estimated the number of fall fractures among people over 65 while walking leashed dogs. In 2004 there were about 1,671; in 2017, about 4,396. The increase was statistically significant. Of those fractured, 78.6% were women and 28.7% needed hospital admission. By single body site, the hip was the most common at 17.3%. By broad category, the upper limb was the most common at 52.1%. Another nationwide US emergency-department dataset covered 2001 to 2018. Leash-related injuries were estimated at about 360,000 cases (exact figure 356,746). Of these, 193,483 were from being pulled by a dog, and 136,767 from tripping or being entangled in the leash. The rate per million people rose from 25.4 to 105.5. A hand surgery clinic treated 443 patients injured by dog leashes over five years. About three-quarters (75.9%; the abstract gives 337 people) were hand or finger injuries from the leash wrapping around the hand when the dog pulled. About one in five (21.6%; 96 people) needed surgery. The US CDC estimated that in 2001 to 2006, about 86,629 fall-related injuries per year involved cats or dogs. Of these, 88.0% involved dogs. Among dog-related falls, 26% happened while walking the dog, and 21.2% were caused by the dog knocking the person over or pulling them down.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Pirruccio K, Yoon YM, Ahn J (2019). Fractures in Elderly Americans Associated With Walking Leashed Dogs. JAMA Surgery, 154(5), 458-459. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamasurg.2019.0061>；Forrester MB (2020). Dog leash-related injuries treated at emergency departments. American Journal of Emergency Medicine, 38(9), 1782-1786. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajem.2020.05.082>；Plusch K, Givner D, Wiafe B, Lutsky K, Beredjiklian P (2026). Hand and Wrist Dog-Leash Injuries in the Outpatient Setting: A Review of 443 Cases. Hand, 21(4), 582-586. <https://doi.org/10.1177/15589447241299128>；CDC (2009). Nonfatal fall-related injuries associated with dogs and cats — United States, 2001-2006. MMWR, 58(11), 277-281. <https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/mm5811a1.htm>；Rosa R, Buckley RE (2024). Leash-related injuries associated with dog walking: an understudied risk for dog owners? Journal of the American Veterinary Medical Association, 262(7), 973-978. <https://doi.org/10.2460/javma.23.11.0608>
- Notes: The advice to hold the leash in the palm, not wrapped around the hand, comes from the doctors at that hand surgery clinic. They state that this protects the fingers and wrist, but it does not prevent being pulled over when a dog lunges. They also advise shortening the leash: a short leash stops the dog from building up speed, so a pull is weaker; a long leash can catch on objects and cause tripping. A retractable leash extended fully is a long leash. However, no study has specifically compared retractable and standard leashes for fall risk, and a 2024 review notes there is no data on which leash type was in use during injuries. Training the dog not to pull forward was recommended by the US CDC and by the authors of the fracture study. The fracture study also suggests that older people considering a dog choose a smaller one. No study has calculated how much these measures reduce falls, so the size of the benefit is rated small on judgement. For whether bones can withstand a fall, see section 1, item 39 (bone density test) and section 1, item 40 (medication once osteoporosis is diagnosed). For balance practice, see section 1, item 13 (balance and leg strength). For whether dog walking counts as exercise, see section 2, item 44 (dog walking counted in walking volume). The beneficiaries are mainly your parents and grandparents.

### 11. Before sending an older relative to a care home, check that it is registered with the civil affairs department and sign a written agreement. Deposits may be at most 12 months of bed fees and must go into a dedicated bank account.
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=money who=elderly -->
- Cost: No cost. Before signing, call the county-level civil affairs department, or ask the home to show its registration receipt. Then check the agreement, fee schedule and payment account. Half a day is enough. The hard part is that when care home beds are scarce, families hesitate to challenge the contract terms.
- Plain language: A proper care home must be registered and sign a written agreement with you. Deposits can be at most 12 months of bed fees, and advance service fees at most 12 months. Hold off paying if a home exceeds these limits, asks you to pay into a private account, or gives only a receipt instead of an invoice.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Civil Affairs' Measures for the Administration of Elderly Care Institutions require a home to file for registration with the civil affairs department within 10 working days of admitting residents. Complete applications receive a filing receipt. Homes must sign a service agreement with the resident or their representative, stating the care level, fee standards, refund terms and liability for breach. A home that does not sign an agreement is ordered to correct the problem and given a warning, and in serious cases fined up to 30,000 yuan (RMB). A 2024 guideline from seven departments, including the Ministry of Civil Affairs, the National Development and Reform Commission and the Ministry of Public Security, sets caps on advance fees. Advance service fees may be collected for at most 12 months. The deposit may be at most 12 times this resident's monthly bed fee. Deposits may only be used for refunds, for urgent medical costs the home has advanced for the resident, for offsetting arrears, and for compensation. State-run homes and state-built, privately operated homes may not collect membership fees. Homes that are not yet built, or not yet allowed to admit residents, may not collect membership fees either. Deposits and membership fees must all be held in a dedicated bank account under third-party custody, and homes may not collect money into personal accounts. Homes must issue an invoice, not a receipt. If a resident cancels before moving in, the home must promptly refund the advance money. If the resident has moved in, after deducting what has been used, the remainder is in principle refunded in full through the original payment channel. This document took effect on 1 October 2024; provinces may set stricter rules. A 2025 guideline on custody of advance fees requires that, once a home applies for a refund, the custody bank refunds the full amount through the original channel the same day, and at the latest the next day.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 民政部 (2020). 养老机构管理办法（民政部令第 66 号，第十、十二、十六、四十六条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2020-09/19/content_5544761.htm>；民政部、国家发展改革委、公安部等七部门 (2024). 关于加强养老机构预收费监管的指导意见（民发〔2024〕19 号，第四、五、六、八、九条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202405/content_6950308.htm>；民政部办公厅、金融监管总局办公厅 (2025). 关于印发《养老机构预收费存管工作指引》的通知（民办发〔2025〕9 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202511/content_7049045.htm>；国家市场监督管理总局. 合同示范文本库（各省养老机构服务合同示范文本，如甘肃省 2024 版）. <https://htsfwb.samr.gov.cn/View?id=8377c6f9-b7f8-4cb4-a218-0e5b65de5cce>
- Notes: China only: Provinces may set lower deposit caps and shorter advance periods, so check your province's rules before signing. You can use the model service-contract template issued by the provincial civil affairs and market regulation departments; the State Administration for Market Regulation's contract template library has them. Registration happens after admission, so a newly opened home may still be processing it; ask when it will file. ### 12. If your parents are hospitalised and you are an only child, check your province's rules for caregiver leave. If you are entitled to it, submit a written request to your employer with the hospital admission certificate.
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=money who=elderly -->
- Cost: No cost. Search the national legal database for your province's two regulations, which takes about ten to fifteen minutes. Then submit a written request to your employer with the admission certificate. The hard part is that the employer may refuse, and people often hesitate to ask for leave for fear of upsetting a manager.
- Plain language: Some provinces give only children leave to care for hospitalised parents, and the number of days differs. Henan gives at least 20 days a year, Fujian up to 10 days, and Guangdong up to 15 days for hospital stays. If your employer refuses or docks your pay, Fujian and Guangdong allow you to go to the labour bureau.
- Benefit: Provinces set caregiver leave in different local regulations. Henan's Regulation on the Protection of the Rights and Interests of the Elderly says that when a parent who holds an only-child parent certificate is hospitalised for treatment, the child's employer must give caregiver leave. Leave totals at least 20 days a year, and the period counts as working time. Fujian's Regulation on the Protection of the Rights and Interests of the Elderly says that when an only child's parent aged 60 or over is ill and hospitalised, the employer must give the child care time. Care time totals no more than 10 days a year, and pay and benefits stay unchanged. If the employer does not pay wages and benefits during that time, the human resources and social security department or another relevant authority orders payment within a set deadline. Guangdong's Population and Family Planning Regulation covers Guangdong-registered couples who voluntarily had only one child during the period when the state encouraged one child per couple. For parents aged 60 or over, the employer gives the child 5 days of care leave a year. For hospital treatment, the total is no more than 15 days a year. If an employer does not provide the leave, the county-level or higher human resources department orders correction. Henan's regulation has no chapter on legal liability, and it does not say what happens if an employer refuses caregiver leave.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: 河南省人大常委会 (2018). 河南省老年人权益保障条例（第二十九条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c90e5ba65c68cf70165d0eed8020053>；福建省人大 (2017). 福建省老年人权益保障条例（第二十七条、第七十条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=4028abcc61277793016128072d0848f4>；广东省人大常委会 (2021). 广东省人口与计划生育条例（2021 年第九次修正，第三十六条第五项、第四十五条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff8081817fb1209d017fb5c92e623844>
- Notes: China only: To check your province's rules, search the national legal database (flk.npc.gov.cn) for "[province name] + Regulation on the Protection of the Rights and Interests of the Elderly" and "[province name] + Population and Family Planning Regulation". Read both, and use only the version marked as in force. If the province has no rule, search the city's regulations. The provincial rules apply only to only children, so most cases with siblings are not covered by the text. If your employer refuses leave or docks your pay, first call 12333 (the national labour and social security hotline) or go to the labour inspection office of the human resources bureau to file a complaint, free of charge. Henan's text says "at least" 20 days, while Fujian and Guangdong say "at most", so in the latter two provinces an employer giving less is not a violation. This is rated B because the number of days and the conditions are set by each province. The beneficiary is your parent, but the benefit actually goes to you. The three provinces' figures are based on regulations as of October 2026, and they may be revised.

### 13. If an older person's memory is getting worse and affecting daily life, take them to a memory clinic or neurology department to rule out treatable causes first, such as depression, medication, and poor hearing or vision.
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no gain=low scope=health who=elderly,60plus -->
- Cost: A memory clinic or neurology visit, with cognitive tests, blood tests and urine tests, takes half a day to a full day. Fees follow local price standards, and medical insurance reimburses them according to the rules. The hard part is that the older person may not admit to being confused and may refuse to go.
- Plain language: Worsening memory does not always mean dementia. First check for treatable causes, such as depression, wrong medication, or poor hearing and vision. Dementia that truly improves is rare. But checking early lets the person decide now who will handle money and medical care later, while they can still make those choices.
- Benefit: The UK's NICE dementia guideline (NG97) requires that when dementia is suspected, the clinician takes a history, asking both the person and family members who know them well. A physical examination, blood tests and urine tests should rule out reversible causes before cognitive tests are done. The guideline names four things to check first: delirium (sudden episodes of confusion), depression, poor vision or hearing, and medicines with a high anticholinergic burden. If dementia is still suspected after these are excluded, refer the person to a specialist service such as a memory clinic for diagnosis. A 2003 meta-analysis pooled 39 studies in which 5,620 people had dementia diagnosed. Alzheimer's disease accounted for 56.3% and vascular dementia for 20.3%. Causes that could be reversed accounted for 9%, and dementia that truly improved only 0.6%: partial improvement 0.29% and full improvement 0.31%. The same NICE guideline recommends discussing future planning with patients and families early. Topics include authorising someone to manage health and money, and the person's wishes for future care. China's 2024 national action plan from 15 departments requires cognitive screening for people aged 65 and over, and encourages hospitals to set up memory clinics.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (2018). Dementia: assessment, management and support for people living with dementia and their carers (NG97, recommendations 1.1.12, 1.2.1, 1.2.2, 1.2.6). <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng97/chapter/Recommendations>；Clarfield AM (2003). The decreasing prevalence of reversible dementias: an updated meta-analysis. Archives of Internal Medicine, 163(18), 2219-2229. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archinte.163.18.2219>；国家卫生健康委等 15 部门 (2024). 应对老年期痴呆国家行动计划（2024—2030 年）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202501/content_6996231.htm>
- Notes: China only: The benefit is rated small because few cases are truly reversible, and no study shows that early checking extends life. It is rated B because the checking method comes from a guideline, and the figures only show the proportion that are reversible. The meta-analysis included studies up to 2002, so today's proportion may be lower. If a medicine is causing confusion, do not stop it on your own; bring the medicine box to a doctor. If someone becomes slow and unsteady for weeks after a head injury, that is a different matter; see section 13, item 10 (head CT after a head injury). For hearing loss and hearing aids, see section 33, item 17 (hearing aids). While the person is still clear-headed, arrange item 1 (a written designation of a future guardian) and item 2 (a will). If the person can no longer manage their own affairs, apply to a court for a declaration of incapacity; see section 33, item 18 (applying to a court for a declaration). The beneficiaries are mainly your parents and grandparents.
