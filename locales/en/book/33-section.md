# 33. How to Live After Disability

This section covers the days after a disability has already happened. How to prevent disabling injuries is covered in sections 1 and 13 and is not repeated here.

The four kinds of measure are kept separate and not converted into one another. Acute emergencies and complications are measured in deaths. Subsidies, benefits and lawsuits are measured in money. Schooling and paperwork are measured in time. Psychiatric hospitalization, legal capacity and guardianship are measured in personal liberty.

The World Health Organization's March 2023 fact sheet on disability gives three figures. About 1.3 billion people, or 16% of the world's population, have significant functional difficulties. That is roughly one in every six people. Some people with disabilities die up to 20 years earlier than people without disabilities. People with disabilities have twice the risk of depression, asthma, diabetes, stroke, obesity or poor oral health. The fact sheet says these health inequalities come from unfair conditions that people with disabilities face: discrimination, poverty, being unable to go to school, being unable to find work, and barriers within the health system itself. So the entries in this section are half about the body and half about paperwork and rights.

Five matters are covered elsewhere, so this section only points to them. For how to get a disability certificate, and how the seven categories and grades one to four are assessed, see section 24, item 10 (disability certificate). For when to get a formal disability assessment, see section 24, item 9 (disability assessment). For how to claim the two disability allowances, see section 7, item 8 (two allowances). For the work-injury ability assessment and how the disability grade converts into money, see section 19, item 14 (ability assessment). For how to apply for long-term care insurance for severe disability, see section 17, item 7 (long-term care insurance). That item sits in the section about "families with elderly members", but this benefit has no age limit, so young people can claim it too.

Most of the beneficiaries of this section are you and your family, which places them in the top two tiers. Where other people are involved, the notes say so separately.

The entries in this section are grouped by theme below. Item numbers are in brackets.

**Body and rehabilitation**: sudden severe headache after a spinal cord injury (item 1), switch to a pressure-relieving wheelchair cushion (item 5), do not buy therapies that claim to cure paralysis (item 6), rehabilitation at a proper institution (item 16), hearing aids for hearing loss (item 17), do not leave a urinary catheter in for long periods after a spinal cord injury (item 21).

**Benefits and subsidies**: ask the disabled persons' federation about every benefit at once (item 7), rehabilitation assistance for disabled children (item 8), subsidies for ramps, handrails and bathroom modifications (item 9), reduction in personal income tax (item 11).

**Jobs and defending your rights**: state that you hold a disability certificate when job-hunting (item 10); if you are refused a job because of disability, you can sue (item 20).

**School and travel**: take a guide dog into public places (item 12), reasonable accommodation for the national college entrance exam, the gaokao (item 13), schools may not refuse disabled children (item 14), people who have lost both legs can still get a driving license (item 15).

**Mental health and carers**: suicide risk in the first ten years after disability (item 2), carers looking after their own health (item 4).

**Psychiatric treatment and guardianship**: voluntary admission is the principle for psychiatric hospitalization (item 3), legal capacity has to be determined through an application to the court (item 18), guardians are appointed in statutory order (item 19).

### 1. After a spinal cord injury, sudden severe headache or sweating: sit the person upright, loosen tight clothing, and call 120 (China's ambulance number) at the same time
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. Three actions that take a few dozen seconds.
- Plain language: After a spinal cord injury, the body's warning wiring is cut off partway. A full bladder or tight clothing can make blood pressure spike sharply, and the person may not feel it. The warning signs are a sudden severe headache and sweating. When this happens, sit the person upright, loosen tight clothing, check whether the bladder is full, and call 120 at the same time.
- Benefit: This episode is called autonomic dysreflexia. A systematic review searched four databases (Medline, CINAHL, EMBASE and PsycINFO) and included 31 studies, 6 of which were randomized controlled trials. The review states that the non-drug response during an episode has three steps: sit the patient upright, loosen tight clothing, and remove the triggering stimulus. These three steps rest on clinical consensus and physiological data, which makes this level 5 evidence. The review's main text states that these episodes are most common with injuries at thoracic segment 6 or above, and that the most common trigger is irritation of the bladder or bowel. Stimulation below the level of injury causes a large discharge of sympathetic nerve activity and a sharp rise in blood pressure, while sensation below the injury level is partly or fully lost. Symptoms include severe headache, heavy sweating above the level of injury, and sometimes no symptoms at all. If untreated, it can lead to brain hemorrhage, seizures or even death. No study has specifically measured the effect of sitting up during an episode, but when people with spinal cord injury move from lying flat to sitting up or tilting, their resting blood pressure falls noticeably. Blood-pressure-lowering drugs are used only when blood pressure stays high. Prazosin has level 1 evidence, and nifedipine and prostaglandin E2 have level 2 evidence. The review also states that prevention strategies focus mainly on the two common triggers, urogenital problems and surgery, and most are supported only by level 4 and level 5 evidence.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Krassioukov A, Warburton DE, Teasell R, Eng JJ; Spinal Cord Injury Rehabilitation Evidence Research Team (2009). A systematic review of the management of autonomic dysreflexia after spinal cord injury. Archives of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation, 90(4):682-695. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apmr.2008.10.017>
- Notes: B is assigned because the three on-site steps have no randomized controlled trials behind them, only consensus and physiological reasoning. The review itself says most of these measures are supported only by uncontrolled studies. Drug treatment is the doctor's responsibility, so do not give blood-pressure medicine at home yourself. Lying the person flat would raise blood pressure further, so sit them up. Family members and carers should also know these three steps. Do not put up with the symptoms as an ordinary headache.

### 2. The first ten years after a disabling spinal cord injury are a high-risk period for suicide; do not treat it as something that will sort itself out with "cheer up"
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. One phone call, or one clinic appointment.
- Plain language: Long-term follow-up at two UK spinal cord injury centers found that suicide rates in this group are five times those of the general population. More than 90% of these suicides happen in the first ten years after the injury. People whose injury was itself caused by a suicide attempt have an even higher risk.
- Benefit: The study followed 2,304 patients with traumatic spinal cord injury who were newly admitted between 1991 and 2010, survived the first year after injury, and still had neurological deficits at discharge. Follow-up ran to the end of 2014. Of these patients, 63 (2.7%) had a suicide attempt as the cause of injury. By the end of follow-up, 533 people had died, 4.2% of them by suicide, and 91% of suicides occurred in the first ten years after injury. The age-standardized suicide mortality rate was 62.5 per 100,000 per year (95% CI 36.4–88.6), five times the 2014 suicide rate of 12.2 per 100,000 in the general population of England and Wales. For the group injured through a suicide attempt, compared with other causes of injury, the odds ratio for death was 4.32 (P<0.001), and the odds ratio for suicide was 9.46 (P<0.001).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Savic G, DeVivo MJ, Frankel HL, Jamous MA, Soni BM, Charlifue S (2018). Suicide and traumatic spinal cord injury - a cohort study. Spinal Cord, 56(1):2-6. <https://doi.org/10.1038/sc.2017.98>
- Notes: China only: This study looked only at spinal cord injury. Applying it to blindness, amputation or burns goes beyond the evidence. What it does show is a time window: the risk does not end at discharge, and you need to watch for ten years. If you need someone to talk to, call 12356 (China's national mental health hotline); see section 29, item 11 (12356). Do not stockpile sleeping pills or pesticides at home; see section 1, item 25 (suicidal thoughts). If grief and low mood stay stuck for six months, go to a psychiatry or clinical psychology clinic; see section 29, item 8 (grief lasting six months).

### 3. Psychiatric hospitalization is voluntary in principle; only two situations allow admission without the person's consent
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Remember the two situations so you can name them when needed.
- Plain language: A relative or employer cannot have someone admitted to a psychiatric hospital just by saying so. The law recognizes only two situations, and only after a serious mental disorder has been diagnosed: the person has harmed themselves or is at risk of doing so, or has harmed others or is at risk of doing so. In the first case, admission needs the guardian's consent. If the person or their guardian disagrees in the second case, they can request a second diagnosis within three days, and after that can commission their own medical assessment.
- Benefit: Article 30 of the Mental Health Law states that "inpatient treatment for mental disorders follows the principle of voluntariness." Inpatient treatment should be carried out only when the diagnosis and assessment show the patient has a serious mental disorder and one of the following applies. The first is "having already committed acts that harm oneself, or being at risk of harming oneself." The second is "having already committed acts that endanger the safety of others, or being at risk of endangering the safety of others." Article 31: in the first case, inpatient treatment may be carried out only with the guardian's consent, and if the guardian does not agree, the institution may not admit the patient. Article 32: in the second case, if the patient or guardian objects, they may request a second diagnosis, which must be requested within three days of receiving the diagnostic conclusion and is made by two psychiatrists other than the first diagnosing doctors. If they still object to the second diagnosis, they may independently commission a legally licensed assessment body to carry out a medical assessment of the mental disorder. Article 35: if the second diagnosis or the assessment report shows that the person cannot be determined to have a serious mental disorder, or does not need inpatient treatment, the institution may not admit them. Article 78: anyone who deliberately sends a person without a mental disorder to a medical institution for treatment, presenting them as having one, bears legal liability for compensation (national; in force from 1 May 2013, amended 2018).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 中华人民共和国精神卫生法（2018 年修正，第三十条、第三十一条、第三十二条、第三十五条、第七十八条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf7448a066d>
- Notes: China only: The reverse also applies: not everyone can be left without treatment. Article 28 says that when someone suspected of having a mental disorder has harmed themselves or others, or is at risk of doing so, close relatives, their employer or the local public security authority must immediately act to stop it and send that person to a medical institution for diagnosis. A medical institution that receives such a referral may not refuse to make a diagnosis. So "sending someone for diagnosis" and "admitting someone to stay" are two separate gates: the first is low and the second is high. Article 75 sets out the institution's duties. An institution that uses restraint or isolation in violation of the rules, or diagnoses a person without a mental disorder as having one, faces disciplinary action up to revocation of its practice license. For what to do when someone close to you feels an urge to hurt people, see section 8.

### 4. The person caring long-term for a disabled family member needs someone to watch their own health too
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. Hand off part of the caregiving work, and get a health check-up yourself.
- Plain language: Caregiver burnout is not oversensitivity. One US study followed more than 800 older adults for four years. Among those caring for a disabled spouse who also reported feeling emotionally strained, the risk of death was 63% higher than for people who did not provide care. Caregivers who did not feel strained showed no such increase. So the thing to watch is not caregiving itself, but the long strain that comes with it.
- Benefit: This was a prospective population cohort study conducted in four US communities from 1993 to 1998, with an average follow-up of 4.5 years. The study included 392 caregivers and 427 non-caregivers, aged 66 to 96, all living with a spouse. Over four years, 103 people (12.6%) died. After adjusting for sociodemographic factors, existing disease and subclinical cardiovascular disease, caregivers who reported mental or emotional strain while caregiving had a 63% higher risk of death than non-caregiving controls (RR 1.63, 95% CI 1.00–2.65). Caregivers who did not report strain (RR 1.08, 95% CI 0.61–1.90) and people whose spouse was disabled but who were not caregivers (RR 1.37, 95% CI 0.73–2.58) showed no increase in adjusted mortality.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Schulz R, Beach SR (1999). Caregiving as a risk factor for mortality: the Caregiver Health Effects Study. JAMA, 282(23):2215-2219. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.282.23.2215>
- Notes: China only: Disputed: the lower bound of this study's confidence interval sits right at 1.00, so the possibility of no difference is not fully ruled out. The population was older Americans living with their spouses, and the care recipients were disabled older spouses. Applying this to people caring for a younger disabled family member goes beyond the evidence. What it does show is the direction: the people who struggle most face higher risk. Hand off as much caregiving as you can. For respite and day-care services, ask your local disabled persons' federation and civil affairs bureau. For keeping up your own medication for chronic illness, see section 16, item 1 (take medication as prescribed). The beneficiary is you.

### 5. For long-term wheelchair users, switch to a pressure-relieving seat cushion and check the sit bones and tailbone every day
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=some gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: A pressure-relieving cushion costs several hundred to over 1,000 yuan (RMB). Checking every day takes one minute.
- Plain language: In nursing homes, residents who used pressure-relieving cushions had clearly fewer pressure sores at the sit bones than those on standard segmented foam cushions. When the tailbone was counted as well, however, no difference was found. All participants were first fitted with a well-fitting wheelchair, so the wheelchair itself should also be sized to the person. Do not just buy a generic model.
- Benefit: This randomized clinical trial was run in 12 nursing homes. From 2004 to 2008 it recruited 232 residents aged over 65 who sat in a wheelchair for more than 6 hours a day and had a Braden score of 18 or lower. All participants were first given a well-fitting wheelchair, then randomly assigned to two groups. One group used a skin-protective cushion (air, viscous fluid with foam, or gel with foam). The other used a 7.6 cm thick segmented foam cushion. Follow-up lasted six months or until a pressure ulcer developed. For pressure ulcers at the ischial tuberosity (sit bone), the segmented foam group had 8 cases (6.7%) and the skin-protective cushion group had 1 case (0.9%), P=0.04. Combining ischial and sacrococcygeal pressure ulcers, the segmented foam group had 21 cases (17.6%) and the skin-protective cushion group had 12 cases (10.6%), P=0.14, no significant difference.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Brienza D, Kelsey S, Karg P, et al. (2010). A randomized clinical trial on preventing pressure ulcers with wheelchair seat cushions. Journal of the American Geriatrics Society, 58(12):2308-2314. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1532-5415.2010.03168.x>
- Notes: China only: This is graded B for two reasons. First, the sample is only 232 people, and the main endpoint's P value of 0.04 barely crosses the threshold, while the secondary endpoint showed no difference. Second, the population was older nursing-home residents, so applying the results to younger wheelchair users goes beyond the evidence. In the trial everyone was first fitted with a well-fitting wheelchair, so fit and cushion worked together. Do not just change the cushion. For protection against pressure sores in people who are bedbound long-term, see section 17, item 8 (pressure sores). For how to apply for the basic assistive-device subsidy, see item 7 of this section (ask the county-level disabled persons' federation for the full list).

### 6. Do not buy therapies or devices that claim to cure paralysis, blindness or deafness
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Free, and it saves money. The hard part is that family members are hopeful and cannot be talked out of it.
- Plain language: The advertising law is strict here. Ads for medical treatments, drugs and medical devices may not promise results, state cure or effective rates, or use celebrities to endorse them. So lines like "stand up after three courses" or "many people have recovered" are illegal ads in themselves, and you do not need to study the principle they describe. A product that is not a medical device may not even mention treating illness.
- Benefit: Article 16 of the Advertising Law states that advertisements for medical treatments, drugs and medical devices may not contain the following: "(1) assertions or guarantees of efficacy or safety; (2) statements of cure rates or effective rates; (3) comparisons with the efficacy and safety of other drugs or medical devices, or with other medical institutions; (4) use of advertising spokespersons to recommend or certify." Article 17 states that, apart from advertisements for medical treatments, drugs and medical devices, "any other advertisement is prohibited from involving disease treatment functions, and may not use medical terminology or terms that could make the product easily confused with drugs or medical devices." Article 4 states that advertisements may not contain false or misleading content, may not deceive or mislead consumers, and that the advertiser must be responsible for the truthfulness of the advertising content (national; amended 2021).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 中华人民共和国广告法（2021 年修正，第四条、第十六条、第十七条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff8081817ab231eb017abd6bd860052d>
- Notes: China only: There is only one test: does the ad dare to make absolute promises? A reputable hospital's rehabilitation department will not promise that you will stand up after a few courses of treatment. When you encounter this kind of promotion, report it to 12315 (China's consumer complaint hotline) or to the local market regulation authority. Health supplement sales use the same playbook; see section 6, item 10 (health supplements). If you have already bought and want a refund, follow the rules for online purchases and prepaid consumption; see section 5, item 29 (online purchases follow platform rules). The beneficiaries are you and your family.

### 7. Once you have your disability certificate, ask the county-level disabled persons' federation about everything you can apply for, all in one visit
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. One trip to the county-level disabled persons' federation (the local CDPF office), with a morning of questions. More than one department may be involved.
- Plain language: The disability certificate does not pay out money by itself. It is a key. Several separate benefits hang off it, and each one has to be applied for individually; they will not come to you automatically. On your first visit to the federation, go through every item on the list one by one. That saves more trips than coming back several times.
- Benefit: The certificate unlocks at least six benefits, each with its own legal basis. For the two allowances for disabled people in financial hardship and the care subsidy for people with severe disabilities, see section 7, item 8 (two allowances). For the rehabilitation subsidy for disabled children, see item 8 of this section (rehabilitation assistance). The subsidy for basic assistive devices rests on Article 26 of the Regulations on Disability Prevention and Rehabilitation. For the subsidy for home barrier-free renovation, see item 9 of this section (ramps, handrails and bathroom renovation). For employment quotas and employment services, see item 10 of this section (declaring your certificate when job-hunting). For the personal income tax reduction, see item 11 of this section (income tax reduction). Article 26 of the Regulations on Disability Prevention and Rehabilitation also states that the state provides basic rehabilitation services to poor and severely disabled people in urban and rural areas through key rehabilitation projects, and that "basic assistive devices shall be subsidized in accordance with the relevant national rules." Article 20 requires county-level and higher government departments and the federation to use community resources to set up rehabilitation facilities or buy services, and to carry out community rehabilitation work: rehabilitation guidance, daily-living skills training, rehabilitation nursing, assistive device fitting, information consultation and referral (national; in force from 1 July 2017). Article 21 of the same regulations requires that rehabilitation services begin with a needs assessment, followed by a personalized plan based on the assessment results. The 2016 State Council document states that "places with the means may subsidize basic rehabilitation assistive device fitting for poor and severely disabled people in urban and rural areas." The 2026 three-year action plan issued by the Ministry of Civil Affairs and other departments states that the subsidy system for fitting basic assistive devices for persons with disabilities should be "standardized and strengthened." Work-injury disability follows a different route. The Measures on Work-Injury Insurance Assistive Device Configuration cover prosthetic limbs, orthoses, artificial eyes, dentures and wheelchairs. They require confirmation by the labor ability appraisal committee before fitting. Installation, maintenance and training costs are paid from the work-injury insurance fund as prescribed. Devices fitted without confirmation, or fitted at a non-agreement institution, are not paid for by the handling agency.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2017). 残疾预防和残疾人康复条例（第二十条、第二十一条、第二十六条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2017-02/27/content_5171308.htm>；国务院 (2016). 关于加快发展康复辅助器具产业的若干意见（国发〔2016〕60 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2016-10/27/content_5125001.htm>；民政部等 (2026). 康复辅助器具产业扩能提质三年行动方案（2026-2028年）（民函〔2026〕48 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202607/content_7075249.htm>；人力资源社会保障部、民政部、国家卫生计生委令第 27 号 (2016). 工伤保险辅助器具配置管理办法（第二条、第十五条、第二十四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2016/content_5074060.htm>
- Notes: China only: For the process of getting the certificate itself, see section 24, item 10 (disability certificate). Before buying a wheelchair, prosthesis or hearing aid, first ask what assessment and fitting process the subsidy requires. The work-injury system has explicit rules: confirm first, then fit. Devices you fitted yourself are not reimbursed. For work-injury recognition and ability assessment, see section 19, item 14 (ability assessment). The State Council document says only that places "with the means" may subsidize, so whether a subsidy exists and how large it is gets decided locally. The following points are the author's own experience. When you ask, write down the name of each item, then look up the original document by name on your local federation's website and the government website. Do not rely only on what the counter staff say. Do not expect to finish everything in one visit. Go back a year later, because policies change.

### 8. If your child is under 7 and has a disability or autism, apply to the county-level disabled persons' federation for rehabilitation assistance
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no gain=high scope=money who=infant,child -->
- Cost: Free. Submit one application to the county-level federation, then choose one of the designated institutions.
- Plain language: The state has set up a rehabilitation assistance system for children aged 0 to 6 with disabilities and for children with autism, covering surgery, assistive devices and rehabilitation training. Priority goes to low-income families, but local governments with the funds may relax the age and household-income conditions. Assistance runs to age 6 under the rules, so do not wait until school age to apply. Institutions must be chosen from the federation's list of designated providers.
- Benefit: The State Council's opinion on establishing a rehabilitation assistance system for children with disabilities states that eligible children aged 0 to 6 with visual, hearing, speech, physical or intellectual disabilities, and children with autism, are covered. This includes disabled children from households receiving urban or rural minimum living allowance, disabled children from households registered as poor under the poverty-alleviation records system, disabled children raised in or cared for by child welfare institutions, disabled orphans, children covered by the state's support for extremely needy persons, and disabled children from other financially struggling households. The specific criteria for other financially struggling households are set by county-level and higher local governments. The document also says that "in areas with the means, the upper age limit for rehabilitation assistance may be extended, and the household income requirement may be relaxed." The funding standard is set by county-level and higher local governments based on local fiscal capacity, the number of beneficiaries and the type of disability, with a dynamic adjustment mechanism. Applications go to the county-level disabled persons' federation where the child is registered (hukou) or where the residence permit is issued. Once approved, the guardian freely chooses a designated rehabilitation institution. Designated rehabilitation institutions are selected by the county-level and higher disabled persons' federation together with health, education, civil affairs and other departments, "according to the principle of open and merit-based selection." Costs incurred at designated institutions are settled directly between the finance department and the institution. "Costs of rehabilitation services received at a non-designated institution, after review and approval by the county-level federation, shall be settled by a method the county-level federation agrees with the finance department at the same level." The document requires each provincial government to issue local systems and supporting policies by the end of September 2018. The higher-level basis is Article 26 of the Regulations on Disability Prevention and Rehabilitation, which states that the state establishes a rehabilitation assistance system for disabled children and "gradually makes surgery, assistive device fitting and rehabilitation training free for children aged 0 to 6 with visual, hearing, speech, physical or intellectual disabilities and autism" (national; from 2018).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2018). 关于建立残疾儿童康复救助制度的意见（国发〔2018〕20 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-07/10/content_5305296.htm>；国务院 (2017). 残疾预防和残疾人康复条例（第二十六条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2017-02/27/content_5171308.htm>
- Notes: China only: The project and standards are set by county-level and higher local governments, so first check your province's disabled-children rehabilitation assistance rules on the local federation's website. The federation, together with other departments, selects the designated institutions, and you should ask the federation for the list. The document requires the federation and related departments to strengthen entry and exit supervision of designated institutions, set up regular inspection and comprehensive assessment, and maintain a blacklist system. If you want to go to an institution not on the list, you must first get county-level federation approval, and how costs are settled is decided locally. If you go without approval, the document has no settlement rule for it. The beneficiary is your child, which falls in the second tier. An institution that promises to "fix" the problem should be handled under item 6 of this section (do not buy therapies that claim to cure paralysis).

### 9. Home ramps, handrails and bathroom renovation: you can apply to the county-level government or above for a subsidy
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Applying is free. Most of the renovation work is subsidized, but you may have to pay part of it yourself. You will need to deal with the disabled persons' federation and your street office.
- Plain language: The Barrier-Free Environment Construction Law says that governments at county level and above should support and guide home barrier-free renovation. Eligible households with disabled or elderly members should receive a suitable subsidy. The amount and which items are covered are set locally, so check with your local federation first.
- Benefit: Article 19 of the Barrier-Free Environment Construction Law: "County-level and higher people's governments shall support and guide the barrier-free renovation of homes. Households with disabled persons or elderly persons that meet the conditions shall be given appropriate subsidies." Article 18 states that for existing residential buildings, residential areas, public buildings, public places, transport facilities and urban and rural roads that do not meet barrier-free construction standards, county-level and higher governments shall formulate targeted renovation plans based on actual conditions and carry them out. Article 22: "The state supports existing multi-storey residential buildings in older urban neighborhoods in installing elevators or other barrier-free facilities, to provide convenience for persons with disabilities and elderly persons." The same article requires governments and relevant departments to "give play to the role of community grassroots organizations" in promoting the installation of elevators in existing multi-storey residential buildings. Article 62 states that any organization or individual has the right to make suggestions to the competent government departments on strengthening and improving barrier-free environment construction, and to file complaints or reports about acts that violate this law. The competent departments "shall handle them promptly and reply." Article 63 states that where acts violating this law harm the public interest, the people's procuratorate may issue a procuratorial suggestion or bring public-interest litigation (national; in force from 1 September 2023).
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: 中华人民共和国无障碍环境建设法（第十八条、第十九条、第二十二条、第六十二条、第六十三条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff80818188d7430b0189018493370940>
- Notes: China only: This is graded B because the law says only "appropriate subsidies." The amount and who counts as eligible are left entirely to local governments, with no national figure. Start by searching your local federation's website for "barrier-free renovation for severely disabled households in financial hardship." Installing an elevator in a residential compound is a separate route. Article 22 asks governments and departments to work through community grassroots organizations, so start by asking your street office and community committee. If public facilities do not meet the standard, file a complaint with the competent department under Article 62, which must reply to you.

### 10. When job-hunting, say you hold a disability certificate: hiring you can offset a sum of money for the employer
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. One extra sentence in the interview. The hard part is saying it.
- Plain language: Employers must have disabled staff making up at least 1.5% of their total workforce, with the exact ratio set by each province. If they fall short, they pay a disability employment levy for each missing person, calculated from last year's average wage. Until the end of 2027, that levy is discounted to 50% or 90%, depending on how far the employer falls short of 1%. Employers with 30 or fewer staff do not pay it. So hiring you genuinely saves money, and you should state your certificate clearly in the interview.
- Benefit: Article 8 of the Regulations on Employment of Persons with Disabilities: "The proportion of persons with disabilities that an employer arranges to employ shall not be lower than 1.5% of its total number of employees on the job. The specific ratio shall be set by the provincial, autonomous-region or municipal people's government based on local conditions." Article 9: where the employer does not meet the ratio set by the provincial government, it shall pay the disability employment security levy. Article 8 of the Measures on Collection and Use of the Disability Employment Security Levy gives the formula: "Annual levy payable = (number of employees on the job last year × the employment ratio set by the provincial, autonomous-region or municipal government where the employer is located − actual number of persons with disabilities employed by the employer last year) × average annual wage of employees last year." ### 16. Rehabilitation should be done at a proper facility with a rehabilitation medicine department, at the intensity your therapist sets; more is not better (evidence comes from stroke)
<!-- tags: money=high time=high will=high gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Inpatient rehabilitation costs several hundred yuan a day, and the out-of-pocket share varies by place. The time is counted in months. You must practise every day, which is tedious.
- Plain language: After a stroke, people admitted to an organised stroke unit are more likely to be alive a year later and to live at home. Do not push harder on your own, though. In the first 24 hours after a stroke, starting earlier, more often and at higher intensity led to worse results at three months. Let your rehabilitation team and therapists decide how much to do and when to start.
- Benefit: A Cochrane systematic review included 29 trials with 5,902 participants, comparing organised inpatient stroke unit care with other forms of care. The review describes a stroke unit as one where team members use standard methods to manage common problems after a stroke, providing coordinated multidisciplinary care. At the last follow-up (median 1 year), the odds ratio for a poor outcome (death, disability or needing institutional care) was 0.77 (95% CI 0.69–0.87, credible range); for death it was 0.76 (95% CI 0.66–0.88); and for death or dependence on others it was 0.75 (95% CI 0.66–0.85). The quality of the evidence was moderate. In absolute terms, for every 100 people given stroke unit care, 2 more survive, 6 more live at home, and 6 more are able to care for themselves. On the other hand, the AVERT trial randomly assigned 2,104 patients at 56 acute stroke units in 5 countries, comparing "usual care" with "usual care plus very early high-intensity mobilisation". At three months, a good outcome (modified Rankin Scale 0–2) was reached by 480 people (46%) in the very early group and 525 people (50%) in the usual care group; adjusted odds ratio 0.73 (95% CI 0.59–0.90, P=0.004). Deaths were 88 in the very early group and 72 in the usual care group, with no significant difference (odds ratio 1.34, 95% CI 0.93–1.93, P=0.113).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Langhorne P, Ramachandra S; Stroke Unit Trialists' Collaboration (2020). Organised inpatient (stroke unit) care for stroke: network meta-analysis. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 4:CD000197. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD000197.pub4>；AVERT Trial Collaboration group (2015). Efficacy and safety of very early mobilisation within 24 h of stroke onset (AVERT): a randomised controlled trial. Lancet, 386(9988):46-55. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)60690-0>
- Notes: Disputed: Both pieces of evidence come from stroke. Applying them to other causes of disability, such as spinal cord injury, amputation or burns, is extrapolation. The AVERT result shows that "the earlier and harder, the better" does not hold; it does not show that early movement does no good. In the trial, most people in both groups were out of bed within 24 hours; the difference was in frequency and total amount. When choosing a facility, check whether it has a rehabilitation medicine department and full-time rehabilitation therapists and occupational therapists, and do not judge by the word "rehabilitation" on the sign. This advice is drawn from stroke unit care delivered by multidisciplinary teams. For how to refer between primary-level and tertiary hospitals, and how the threshold payment is calculated, see section 24, item 1 (step-by-step referral). For community rehabilitation and assistive device fitting, see item 7 of this section (ask at the county-level disabled persons' federation for the full picture).

### 17. If your hearing drops, get a hearing aid for the sake of hearing now, not to prevent dementia
<!-- tags: money=high time=mid will=some gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: Hearing aids cost several thousand to tens of thousands of yuan, and most people pay for them out of pocket. You need a fitting, and several rounds of adjustment.
- Plain language: The claim that hearing aids prevent dementia has not been established. A trial of nearly 1,000 people over three years found no overall difference. A difference appeared only among people whose risk of cognitive decline was already higher. So the reason to get a hearing aid is to hear now, to talk with people, and to hear traffic when crossing the road, not to protect your brain decades from now.
- Benefit: The ACHIEVE trial was run at 4 communities in the United States and enrolled 977 people aged 70 to 84 with untreated hearing loss and no obvious cognitive impairment. They were randomly assigned to a hearing intervention group (hearing counselling and hearing aids) or a health education control group, with follow-up every 6 months. The primary endpoint was the change in overall cognitive score over 3 years. In the main analysis combining the two source populations, there was no significant difference between the groups: hearing intervention group −0.200 (95% CI −0.256 to −0.144), control group −0.202 (95% CI −0.258 to −0.145), difference 0.002 (95% CI −0.077 to 0.081), P=0.96. A prespecified sensitivity analysis showed that the effect differed between the two source populations (interaction P=0.010). Participants from the long-running cardiovascular health observational study were older, had more risk factors for cognitive decline, and had lower baseline cognitive scores. Among these 238 people, the hearing intervention group had 48% less cognitive decline over 3 years (difference 0.191, 95% CI 0.022–0.360, P=0.027). Among the 739 newly recruited healthy volunteers, there was no significant difference between the groups (difference −0.061, 95% CI −0.151 to 0.028, P=0.18). The researchers concluded that hearing intervention may reduce 3-year cognitive change in people at higher risk of cognitive decline, but not in people at lower risk. The trial reported no serious adverse events attributed to the study.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Lin FR, Pike JR, Albert MS, et al.; ACHIEVE Collaborative Research Group (2023). Hearing intervention versus health education control to reduce cognitive decline in older adults with hearing loss in the USA (ACHIEVE): a multicentre, randomised controlled trial. Lancet, 402(10404):786-797. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)01406-X>
- Notes: Disputed: The claim that hearing aids cut cognitive decline by almost half comes from a subgroup of 238 people in this trial, not from its main result, and the main result showed no difference between the groups. Keep "getting a hearing aid" and "preventing dementia" apart. The first is worth doing; the second should not be your reason. Hearing-impaired candidates for the gaokao can be exempted from the English listening test; see item 13 of this section (reasonable accommodations). People with hearing impairment who drive must wear a hearing aid; see item 15 of this section (C5).

Article 199: "After accepting the application, the people's court shall, where necessary, arrange an assessment of the citizen whose incapacity is being sought." Article 187: special-procedure cases "shall be concluded within 30 days from the date of filing, or within 30 days after the announcement period expires", and the time limit may be extended with approval from the court president in special circumstances. Article 8 of the Measures for Payment of Litigation Costs: cases heard under the special procedures of the Civil Procedure Law do not pay a case acceptance fee. The court orders an assessment when necessary, and the assessment fee follows the "whoever asserts bears the cost" principle, so it is generally paid by the applicant. If you have not gone through this procedure and are not the statutory representative, and you sign for the person or dispose of their property, then under Article 171 of the Civil Code an act done without authority does not take effect against the person unless they ratify it. A house sold this way may have to be returned. This is the author's inference from the statute. Also remember that limited civil capacity does not mean the person can do nothing. They can do on their own the things suited to their intellectual and mental condition, and purely beneficial things, so do not handle everything on their behalf. For how the guardian is chosen, see item 19 of this section (guardians).

### 19. Adult guardians are appointed in statutory order; if the ward injures someone, the guardian pays
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: No money. Disputes must be settled at the residents' committee, village committee, civil affairs department or court.
- Plain language: When an adult loses legal capacity, guardians are chosen in order: spouse; parents and children; other close relatives; and finally an individual or organisation willing to serve, with the consent of the residents' committee or civil affairs department. Disputes are settled by the residents' or village committee or the civil affairs department, and you may appeal to a court. While still clear-headed, a person can name their future guardian in writing. If the ward injures someone, compensation comes from the ward's own money first, and the guardian covers any shortfall.
- Benefit: Article 28 of the Civil Code: adults with no or limited civil capacity have guardians chosen in the following order from among those with the capacity to be guardians: "(1) spouse; (2) parents and children; (3) other close relatives; (4) other individuals or organisations willing to serve as guardian, provided they have the consent of the residents' committee, village committee or civil affairs department where the ward is domiciled." Article 31: where there is a dispute over who should be guardian, the residents' committee, village committee or civil affairs department where the ward is domiciled shall designate one; if you disagree with the designation, you may apply to the court for a designation, or apply to the court directly. In designating, "the ward's true wishes and the principle of what is most beneficial to the ward" must be respected. Where the ward's rights are unprotected before a designation is made, the residents' committee, village committee, relevant organisations provided by law or the civil affairs department acts as temporary guardian. Article 33: "An adult with full civil capacity may agree in advance with close relatives, or other individuals or organisations willing to serve as guardian, and determine their guardian in writing." Article 1188: "Where a person with no or limited civil capacity causes damage to another, the guardian bears tort liability. Where the guardian has fulfilled their guardianship duties, their liability may be reduced. Where the ward has property, compensation is paid from the ward's own property, and any shortfall is compensated by the guardian." Article 1189: where a guardian entrusts guardianship duties to another person, the guardian remains liable in tort, and the trustee bears corresponding liability if at fault (nationwide; the Civil Code took effect on 1 January 2021).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 中华人民共和国民法典（第二十八条、第三十一条、第三十三条、第一千一百八十八条、第一千一百八十九条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff808081729d1efe01729d50b5c500bf>
- Notes: China only: Article 33 is the voluntary guardianship provision. Naming the person in writing while you are clear-headed settles most later disputes; see section 17, item 1 (voluntary guardianship) for how to write it. Be careful with the compensation rules. ### 20. Rejected, denied promotion or underpaid because of disability? You can sue, but first work out the costs of the process
<!-- tags: money=high time=high will=high gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Lawyer's fees of several thousand to tens of thousands of yuan, paid by you. For ordinary first-instance proceedings, the court must conclude within 6 months, which can be extended. You must preserve the evidence yourself.
- Plain language: The law forbids discrimination against disabled people in hiring, confirmation of employment, promotion, professional title evaluation, pay and social insurance. You can complain to the competent department, or sue in court. But even if you win, the other side will not pay your lawyer's fees. So complain first, apply for legal aid at the same time, and only then consider litigation.
- Benefit: Article 3 of the Law on the Protection of Persons with Disabilities: "Discrimination based on disability is prohibited. Insulting or infringing on persons with disabilities is prohibited." Article 38: "Discrimination against persons with disabilities is prohibited in recruitment, confirmation of employment, promotion, professional title evaluation, labour remuneration, living welfare, rest and leave, social insurance and other aspects of employees." The same article provides that units employing disabled employees shall provide appropriate working conditions and labour protection suited to their characteristics, and renovate workplaces, equipment and living facilities as actual needs require. Article 64: "Where discrimination against persons with disabilities in recruitment or other aspects of employment violates this law, the competent department shall order correction; disabled workers may bring a lawsuit in the people's court in accordance with law." Article 4 of the Regulations on Employment of Persons with Disabilities (State Council, 2007): "Discrimination against persons with disabilities in employment is prohibited." Article 13: employers may not discriminate against disabled employees in promotion, advancement, professional title evaluation, remuneration, social insurance or living welfare. Article 42 of the Legal Aid Law provides that where an applicant has material proof that they belong to a specific group, such as "minors, elderly people or persons with disabilities with no fixed source of living", the check on economic hardship is waived. Article 45 provides that legal aid agencies serving elderly people and persons with disabilities shall provide barrier-free facilities, equipment and services as the actual situation requires (nationwide).

### 21. After spinal cord injury with trouble passing urine, agree on a bladder method with your doctor; do not leave a catheter in for years without checks, and check your kidneys and bladder for life
<!-- tags: money=low time=high will=high gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: Self-catheterisation on a timed schedule means 4 to 6 times a day, each taking a few minutes, and you must keep buying catheters. Check-ups are every one to two years, at urology or a rehabilitation clinic. The difficulty is doing it every day, for decades, without a break.
- Plain language: After spinal cord injury, if you cannot pass urine, you need a method such as catheterisation to empty the bladder. In UK data covering 70 years, just over 10% of deaths were from the genitourinary system. People who keep a catheter in long-term had roughly double the rate of complications compared with people who catheterise on a timed schedule. Whichever method you use, your kidneys and bladder must be checked for life.
- Benefit: Two UK spinal cord injury centres examined 5,483 patients with traumatic spinal cord injury. They were injured between 1943 and 2010, all survived the first year after injury, and were followed up to the end of 2014. In total 2,322 died, and 2,170 of them had a reliable cause of death. The leading causes were respiratory (29.3%), circulatory (26.7%), cancer (13.9%) and genitourinary (11.5%). After adjusting for age, the genitourinary death rate was higher than in the general population; it has fallen over the years but remains high. Another US study reviewed the records of 316 patients with traumatic spinal cord injury, with an average follow-up of 18.3 years after injury, grouped by bladder method. Among those who had complications, the rates were: long-term indwelling urethral catheter 53.5%, clean intermittent catheterisation 27.2%, spontaneous voiding 32.4%, and suprapubic catheter 44.4%. Complications included infection, stones, urethral injury and imaging abnormalities. This was a comparison of medical records, not a randomised trial. The European Association of Urology's 2026 guideline states that urethral indwelling catheters cause many complications and carry a high risk of urinary tract infection, and should be avoided where possible. Suprapubic catheters are similar, though less severe. The guideline also states that long-term follow-up must be lifelong, to check for upper urinary tract damage and the risk of kidney failure. Review intervals are, in most cases, no more than one to two years. People at high risk have an ultrasound about every six months, and a yearly physical check and urine test. Section 3.4.2.e of the guideline states that intermittent catheterisation and indwelling catheters are both standard care for people who cannot empty their bladder, and recommends that doctor and patient decide together. In people with spinal cord injury, quality-of-life scores are worse with intermittent catheterisation than with indwelling catheters, especially for those with more than 4 urinary infections a year. In one observational study, 42.1% of people using intermittent catheterisation had stopped within 12 months, mainly because of inconvenience, leakage and more infections. The guideline says silicone catheters are better because they do not encrust easily, and that latex allergy is more common among people with neurological conditions. Section 3.5.1 lists the most common signs of urinary infection in these patients: fever; new or worsening leakage; worsening spasms; fatigue; cloudier urine with a stronger smell; pain in the kidney area or bladder; and abnormal autonomic reflexes.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Savic G, DeVivo MJ, Frankel HL, Jamous MA, Soni BM, Charlifue S (2017). Causes of death after traumatic spinal cord injury - a 70-year British study. Spinal Cord, 55(10):891-897. <https://doi.org/10.1038/sc.2017.64>；Weld KJ, Dmochowski RR (2000). Effect of bladder management on urological complications in spinal cord injured patients. EAU Guidelines on Neuro-Urology（第 3.3.8、3.4.2.e、3.5.1、3.7 节）. <https://uroweb.org/guidelines/neuro-urology/chapter/the-guideline>
- Notes: Disputed: The same guideline lists intermittent catheterisation and indwelling catheters as standard care and asks doctor and patient to decide together.
