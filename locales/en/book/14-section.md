# 14. Account and information security

Focus: money and personal information. When someone gets into your account, the first thing you usually lose is money. They can also use your account to scam people in your contacts. And your identity can be taken too.

### 1. Turn on two-step verification for your email, payments and social accounts, preferring phone pop-up confirmation over SMS codes
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. Setting it up takes two or three minutes per account, done once.
- Plain language: Two-step verification means that when you log in, you must confirm it is really you, in addition to your password. Tapping to confirm a pop-up on your phone stops most phishing takeovers, more than 94% of them. SMS codes are easier to trick people into giving away. Older methods, such as answering "where did you last log in?" or "what is your backup email?", stop as few as 10% of phishing takeovers.
- Benefit: Google counted 350,000 real account takeover attempts (also called account hijacking). One type of check relies on a device, such as a pop-up on your phone that you tap to confirm, or a security key you plug in. This type blocked "more than 94% of takeover attempts that came from phishing, and 100% of automated takeover attempts." Phishing means tricking you into typing your password on a fake website. Automated means machines trying leaked passwords in bulk. The other type relies on answering questions, such as where you last logged in or what your backup email is. This type "blocked only as little as 10% of phishing takeovers and 73% of automated ones." Both are device-based checks, but SMS codes are weaker than phone pop-ups. Phishers got 4% of SMS codes right, but only 1% of pop-ups. In targeted attacks aimed at one person, SMS blocked 76% and pop-ups blocked 90%.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Doerfler P, Thomas K, Marincenko M, et al. (2019). Evaluating Login Challenges as a Defense Against Account Takeover. The World Wide Web Conference (WWW '19). <https://doi.org/10.1145/3308558.3313481>
- Notes: The same study also found these checks sometimes lock out the real owner. 52% of genuine users failed to log in the first time, but in the end 97% got in. Turn this on for your email first, because most of your other accounts can use email to reset passwords. Only use verification codes yourself. For what you could be liable for if you read out a code for someone else or let them pass face verification for you, see item 45 in section 8 (reading out a verification code).

### 2. Give your email its own password, not used on any other site
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. A password manager stores it, so you don't have to remember it. The hard part is breaking the habit of reusing one password everywhere.
- Plain language: If another site's leaked password is used to log into your email, the attacker gets in. Once your email is taken, every account that uses it to reset passwords goes with it. So give your email its own password, and never use it anywhere else.
- Benefit: Credential stuffing means trying account and password pairs leaked from other sites, one after another. Your email can be tried this way too. Once someone gets into your email, every account that uses it to reset passwords is lost too. The US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) advises: use a different strong password for each account, at least 16 characters long, and store them in a password manager.
- Evidence level: C
- Sources: US CISA. Use Strong Passwords. <https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world/use-strong-passwords>
- Notes: If you can't remember them, use the password manager built into your browser. It remembers a password for each site for you, which is far stronger than reusing one password. Don't keep passwords in WeChat Favorites (WeChat is a Chinese messaging app) or in a notes app.

### 3. Set a screen lock on your phone and a PIN on your SIM card
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. Set the screen lock and the SIM PIN once each.
- Plain language: A SIM card is the small chip in your phone that receives SMS verification codes. If your phone is lost, whoever finds it could pull out the card, put it in another phone, receive your codes, and reset your accounts one by one. A PIN on the SIM means the other phone asks for it at startup, so this route is closed.
- Benefit: If you lose your phone, the fastest route for whoever finds it is to put the SIM card into another phone and receive your verification codes. With those codes, they can reset your accounts one at a time. A SIM with a PIN asks for the code when another phone starts up, so the finder cannot use it to receive codes. In 2020 the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT, the telecom regulator) reported a real case. A user's phone was stolen, and the user did not report the SIM card lost in time, which gave criminals an opening. The criminals logged into a government app using a phone number and code, and got the user's ID number and other information. They then changed the mobile service password. After the user reported the card lost, they tricked China Telecom customer service with a made-up story and got the card unlocked. Finally they exploited a "reset password" loophole on an online lending platform, reset the payment password, and stole loan funds. MIIT used the case to remind users to set a SIM password and report a lost phone at once. On screen locks, the US Federal Communications Commission advises: set a strong password on your phone to limit who can use it. If the phone is lost or stolen, this stops others from running up charges or misusing your personal data.
- Evidence level: C
- Sources: 工业和信息化部 (2020). 工信部提醒！强化个人信息保护意识. 中国政府网转载工信微报，2020-10-22. <https://app.www.gov.cn/govdata/gov/202010/22/464049/article.html>；US FCC. Protect Your Smart Device. <https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/protect-your-mobile-device>
- Notes: The PIN is set under the "SIM card lock" option in the phone settings. The factory default is usually 1234 or 0000. Enter it wrong three times and you need the PUK code from your carrier to unlock it. So write the code down on paper as soon as you set it. We rate this C because both sources are official reminders, plus one case, and no study has measured how much a SIM PIN reduces losses. For what to do first when your phone is lost, see item 4 of this section (steps to take when your phone is lost).

### 4. If your phone is lost, follow this order: suspend the SIM card, lock it remotely, change passwords, report to the police, freeze bank cards
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. The whole sequence takes a dozen or so minutes.
- Plain language: Order matters more than speed. First, suspend the SIM card to cut off your verification codes. Then lock the phone remotely, and change your email and payment passwords from a computer. Report it to the police for a receipt, and freeze your bank card if needed. You can suspend the SIM from someone else's phone.
- Benefit: Order matters more than speed. Step one: suspend the SIM card, so no one can receive your codes. Step two: lock the phone remotely and erase its contents. Step three: change your email and payment passwords from a computer. Step four: report it to the police and get a receipt. Finally, freeze your bank card if needed. The US Federal Communications Commission also advises: lock the phone remotely even if you only think you lost it. If it was stolen, report it to the police right away, giving the model and the IMEI number (the phone's identity number), and tell your carrier immediately.
- Evidence level: C
- Sources: US FCC. Protect Your Smart Device. <https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/protect-your-mobile-device>；步骤顺序是作者经验；补办身份证见第 7 节，冒名贷款见第 8 节关于征信的一条
- Notes: China only: Write down the customer service numbers of the three carriers in advance: China Mobile 10086, China Unicom 10010, China Telecom 10000. Also note the city where you registered your phone number, since customer service will ask. You can still call to suspend the SIM from someone else's phone.

### 5. If unauthorized charges hit your card, freeze it first, then report to the police, then demand that the bank pay out. The bank must prove you made the charge yourself.
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. As soon as you notice odd activity on the card, report it lost or freeze it. Keep the police report, the loss report and the bank's transaction alerts. If the card is still with you, make a small balance check or withdrawal at a nearby ATM, leaving a record that the real card was in your hands at the time. The hard part is holding back from arguing with customer service first. Report the loss first.
- Plain language: If someone uses your card without permission, you must first show evidence, such as a police report and a loss report. If the bank says you made the transaction yourself, the bank has to produce the evidence. If it cannot, it has to pay you. This only works if you report the loss as soon as you notice. If you wait, the extra losses are your responsibility.
- Benefit: Supreme People's Court rules split who must produce evidence. If you say the charge was made with a cloned card or online without your card, you must first provide evidence. Evidence that can help includes a legally effective judgment, where your real card was at the time of the transaction, where the transaction happened, account transaction statements, transaction alerts, police reports, and loss reports. In reverse, if the card-issuing bank or a non-bank payment institution (third-party payment) says you made the transaction yourself or authorized it, they must produce the evidence. If you reported to the bank and it did not verify promptly, or did not provide or preserve transaction records and surveillance video in time, so that evidence cannot be obtained, the bank bears the consequences. Once the claim is established: a debit card holder can ask the issuing bank to pay back the stolen deposit, principal and interest, and compensate losses. A credit card holder can ask for the charged overdraft amount, with interest and penalties, to be returned, and for compensation. If the bank asks you to repay that overdraft, the court will not support it. You can also ask the issuing bank to promptly delete the matching negative credit record (nationwide, effective 25 May 2021).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 最高人民法院 (2021). 关于审理银行卡民事纠纷案件若干问题的规定（第四、五、七、十四、十五条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/304771.html>
- Notes: China only: There are two cases where you bear the responsibility yourself. First, you did not keep your card, password or codes safe and were at fault. You then pay in proportion to your fault. So never tell anyone your password, and never forward a verification code (see item 1, where two-step verification prefers phone pop-up confirmation). Second, you delayed reporting the loss and the losses kept growing. The extra part is yours. So the first step is always to report and freeze, not to argue with customer service. Third-party payment institutions follow the same rules. If their promotional material promises "advance payment" in specific terms, you can ask them to pay first. If you were tricked into transferring the money yourself, a different route applies: see item 2 of section 8 (if you are scammed, call 110, the police line, or 96110, the national anti-fraud hotline, at once to ask for a payment stop).

### 6. Every so often, check your account's logged-in devices and authorized apps, and remove any you no longer use
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. Each check takes a few minutes. The hard part is that nothing reminds you, so you have to remember to look.
- Plain language: "Logged-in devices" are the phones and computers that can still use your account. If you see a device you don't recognise, or an old app still connected to your account, change your password first, then log out of all devices. Also check your email for any auto-forwarding rule you never set up.
- Benefit: The logged-in devices list shows the phones and computers that can still use the account. The authorized apps list shows the third-party software you allowed to log in with the account. Unfamiliar devices and long-unused third-party software are the easiest traces to spot. The UK National Cyber Security Centre lists "logins or login attempts from unfamiliar locations or at unusual times" as signs to check when an account has been hacked. It also says criminals often set up a forwarding rule in your email, copying every email you receive to them, so they can use it to reset your passwords. Its recommended order: first remove any unknown forwarding rules, then change your password, then log all devices and apps out of the account. After logging out, anyone who wants to use your account must enter the new password. The US Federal Trade Commission also advises: after you regain access, log out of all devices and delete any forwarding rules you did not set up.
- Evidence level: C
- Sources: UK NCSC. Recovering a hacked account. <https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/recovering-a-hacked-account>；US FTC. How To Recover Your Hacked Email or Social Media Account. <https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recover-your-hacked-email-or-social-media-account>；两份都是账号出事以后的处理指南，「隔一段时间主动去看」是作者经验
- Notes: China only: WeChat (a Chinese messaging app) and Alipay (a Chinese payment app) both have this setting, as do email, Apple and Android accounts. If you find a device you don't recognise, change your password first, then tap "log out of all devices." This is the order the UK National Cyber Security Centre gives. We rate this C because both sources are official operating guides, and no study has measured how much regular checks reduce losses.

### 7. Don't tap "Agree to all" just to use an app: data the service doesn't need can't be a condition of getting it
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. The hard part is resisting the urge to tap "Agree to all."
- Plain language: An app asks for your information. If the information isn't needed to provide the service, you can refuse, and the app can't use your refusal as a reason to block you. It may collect only what it actually needs. A map app needs your location. A flashlight app doesn't need your contacts.
- Benefit: The law sets two rules. First, a company may not refuse to provide a product or service because you did not consent to processing your data, or because you withdrew consent. The exception is processing that is necessary to provide the product or service. Second, data collection must be limited to the minimum needed for the purpose. You can only be asked for what you actually use.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2021). 个人信息保护法. 中国人大网. <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202108/t20210820_313088.html>：第六条「收集个人信息，应当限于实现处理目的的最小范围，不得过度收集个人信息」；第十六条「个人信息处理者不得以个人不同意处理其个人信息或者撤回同意为由，拒绝提供产品或者服务；处理个人信息属于提供产品或者服务所必需的除外」；第十五条「基于个人同意处理个人信息的，个人有权撤回其同意。个人信息处理者应当提供便捷的撤回同意的方式」
- Notes: China only: The test is whether the data is necessary for this service. A map app needs location; a flashlight app doesn't need contacts. After installing an app, go to the app permissions page in your phone's settings and turn off any permission it doesn't need. When a permission is needed for real, choose "Allow this time only."

### 8. You have the right to view, copy, correct and delete your personal information, and you can sue if refused
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. You only need to complain or sue if the company drags its feet. A lawsuit takes months to start, and you pay your own lawyer, so complaining first is usually cheaper. The hard part is having to keep chasing the company while it stalls.
- Plain language: You can ask a company to show, copy, correct or delete your data. If a service ends, a retention period expires or you withdraw consent, the company should delete it. If it refuses, it must explain why, and you can sue in court. Closing an account is not the same as deleting your data.
- Benefit: Several situations require the company to delete data on its own initiative: the service has ended, the agreed retention period has passed, you have withdrawn consent, or the purpose of collection has been fulfilled. If it doesn't delete the data, you can ask it to. If it refuses to honour these rights, it must give reasons. You can file a lawsuit directly in court.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2021). 个人信息保护法. 中国人大网. <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202108/t20210820_313088.html>：第四十五条「个人有权向个人信息处理者查阅、复制其个人信息……个人请求查阅、复制其个人信息的，个人信息处理者应当及时提供」；第四十六条更正、补充权；第四十七条列了五种应当主动删除的情形，含「（一）处理目的已实现、无法实现或者为实现处理目的不再必要」「（二）个人信息处理者停止提供产品或者服务，或者保存期限已届满」「（三）个人撤回同意」，「个人信息处理者未删除的，个人有权请求删除」；第五十条「个人信息处理者应当建立便捷的个人行使权利的申请受理和处理机制。拒绝个人行使权利的请求的，应当说明理由」「个人可以依法向人民法院提起诉讼」
- Notes: China only: Closing an account and deleting personal information are two separate steps, so after closing, request deletion separately. Before changing or selling an old phone, log out of all accounts and unbind them on the old phone, then do a factory reset. The law gives you a right to deletion after the fact. It cannot take back information that has already leaked.

### 9. You don't have to agree to a face scan: if another method exists, you can't be made to use only your face, and you must be offered an alternative
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. When asked for a face scan, ask one question: "Is there another verification method?" If they say no, ask them to provide one. The hard part is saying it out loud on the spot.
- Plain language: If another method can do the same job, a service may not make your face the only option. If you decline, it must offer something else, such as a card, a password or your ID card. Hotel rooms, public showers, changing rooms and toilets may not have face recognition at all.
- Benefit: The Measures for the Security Management of Face Recognition Technology Applications state: "Where a non-face-recognition method can achieve the same purpose or meet the same business requirements, face recognition may not be the only verification method. Where an individual does not agree to identity verification via face information, a reasonable and convenient alternative must be provided." The Measures also state: "No organisation or individual may mislead, defraud or coerce individuals into accepting face recognition verification by citing business handling, improved service quality or similar reasons." If face information is processed on the basis of consent, the consent must be voluntary, explicit, given with full knowledge, and given separately for this one matter. You may withdraw consent, and processors must provide a convenient way to do so. For minors under 14, consent must come from a parent or other guardian. Face recognition devices in public places must be "necessary for maintaining public safety" and must have prominent signs. No face recognition devices may be installed inside private spaces of public places such as hotel rooms, public bathrooms, public changing rooms and public toilets. Face information must be stored in the face recognition device and may not be transmitted over the internet. The exceptions are where laws or administrative regulations provide otherwise, or where separate consent has been obtained. (Nationwide; in force since 1 June 2025.)
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国家互联网信息办公室、公安部 (2025). 人脸识别技术应用安全管理办法（第 19 号令，第十条、十二条、十三条、十五条，2025 年 6 月 1 日起施行）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-03/21/c_1744174262156096.htm>
- Notes: China only: The most common cases are building access control, rental platforms, gyms and hotels asking you to register your face. When staff say "the system only supports face scanning," read them the rule's exact wording: "where a non-face-recognition method can achieve the same purpose, face recognition may not be the only verification method." Then ask for a card, a password or an ID card. If they still refuse, report it to the local office of the Cyberspace Administration of China, the internet regulator. Some areas, such as certain financial and government services, follow their own rules; follow those where they apply. A face is harder to change than a password if it leaks, so treat it with more care than a password. An organisation that has stored the face information of more than 100,000 people must file with the provincial-level or higher internet regulator within 30 working days. Asking whether it has filed is a useful way to judge whether a company is legitimate. For your right to view, correct and delete your own data, see item 8 of this section. Letting someone else use your face for their own loan is a different matter; see section 8, item 45 (helping someone with a face scan).

### 10. Don't give code, keys or private data to unknown "AI relay stations", especially if you let AI run commands automatically
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=some gain=mid scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Official direct access usually costs more than relay stations, and topping up is less convenient. The catch is that relay stations are cheap, and one gateway gives you access to several models.
- Plain language: A relay station sits between you and the model provider. It can see and change both what you send and what comes back. One study tested more than 400 relay stations. Nine slipped malicious code into their responses, and 17 touched cloud service keys that the researchers had planted on purpose. If AI runs commands automatically, one altered command can install malware on your computer.
- Benefit: China's Ministry of State Security issued a risk warning in June 2026. Some relay stations keep the data users submit on their servers. Some secretly retain it and sell it to other model providers for training. Some have hidden backdoors that plant malicious code on users' devices, steal account keys and cloud credentials, or even install remote-control software. The warning recommends official direct-connection platforms and properly licensed platforms, and advises against platforms of unknown origin, without operating qualifications or security guarantees. When you use one, first remove personal privacy and project data, keep your keys safe, and change them regularly. If you see unusual charges, unexplained account bans or abnormal data, stop using it at once, change your keys, scan for viruses and keep the evidence. A 2026 measurement by researchers at the University of California, Santa Barbara and other institutions bought 28 paid relay stations from Taobao, Xianyu (a second-hand marketplace) and overseas online shops, and collected 400 free relay stations from public communities. One paid and eight free stations injected malicious code into tool calls returned to the user. Tool calls are the commands AI makes your computer run, such as installing software packages. Two more waited for their moment; the paper's example only attacked fully automated sessions. Seventeen used Amazon cloud service keys that the researchers had planted on purpose. One moved money out of the researchers' Ethereum wallet, but the wallet held very little, and the loss was under US$50. The paper's demonstrated tampering was subtle: it changed the install command's "requests" to the similar-looking "reqeusts", which is hard to spot at a glance. (Nationwide)
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: 国家安全部 (2026). 「AI中转站」，风险要防范. <https://www.szzg.gov.cn/2026/xwzx/szkx/202606/t20260608_5331487.htm>（数字中国建设峰会官网转载国家安全部微信公众号）；Liu H, Shou C, Wen H, Chen Y, Fang RJ, Feng Y. (2026). Your Agent Is Mine: Measuring Malicious Intermediary Attacks on the LLM Supply Chain. arXiv:2604.08407. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08407>
- Notes: Rated B because there is only one study, and it is a preprint (a version published before formal peer review). Most of the relay stations tested were free; only 28 were paid, so you cannot work out what share of relay stations have problems. Some media reported the theft as US$500,000, but the paper says under US$50. The size of the benefit is rated medium based on consequences: losses from stolen keys and code vary enormously, and the study gives no amount to work from. If you have already used an unknown relay station, revoke the keys you used with the model provider and generate new ones, and replace your cloud service and code repository keys too. When you go through a relay station, don't let AI run commands fully automatically; check each command before allowing it. The operator of a relay station can face criminal risk; see section 11, item 19 (AI relay stations). The beneficiary is you.

### 11. Check once a year how many phone cards and bank cards are in your name, and cancel any you did not open
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Phone cards are checked through the "Gongxin Weibao" (MIIT Micro Report) WeChat official account, or the "One ID Check" mini-program in Alipay (a payment app). Bank cards are checked through "One-Click Card Check" in the UnionPay app. Each takes a few minutes, and results arrive by SMS within one to two days. The hard part is that nobody reminds you, so you have to remember to check once a year.
- Plain language: Someone may register a phone card in your name using your ID, for fraud, and you may not know. Free official checks now show how many phone cards are in your name, how many app accounts are linked to your phone number, and which UnionPay bank cards you have. If you don't recognise one, ask the operator or bank to verify it and cancel it.
- Benefit: The Anti-Telecom Network Fraud Law states that telecom authorities must "provide convenient channels for users to check the phone cards registered in their names." The People's Bank of China and other departments must "provide convenient channels for customers to check the bank and payment accounts in their names." The nationwide "One ID Check" for mobile phone cards, guided by the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT), went live on 14 September 2021. With your ID card, you can check how many phone cards are in your name and which carrier each belongs to. It was launched because crackdowns on telecom fraud found that some cards used in crimes had been registered with other people's identities, often without the holder knowing, and this can damage their personal credit. Results are sent by SMS from 10699000 within 48 hours. If the number doesn't match, check the details with the carrier. With your authorisation, the carrier can verify, detach, suspend or close the cards. "One ID Check 2.0" was launched in July 2022. With a phone number and the last six digits of your ID number, you can see how many internet accounts the number is linked to. At launch it covered companies including Tencent (owner of WeChat), Alibaba, Baidu, Kuaishou and Douyin (short-video apps), JD.com and Meituan, and it shows counts only. For bank cards, China UnionPay opened "One-Click Card Check" in its app to all provinces and cities in mainland China on 29 June 2022. You choose the bank to check and pass identity verification to submit. A report is generated within 24 hours. Card details in the report are masked, and the report is deleted automatically after 7 days. (Nationwide)
- Evidence level: C
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2022). 反电信网络诈骗法（第十条、第十六条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202209/t20220902_575631.shtml>；人民网 (2021). 工信部推出「一证通查」服务：可查询用户名下电话卡数量. 中国政府网转载，2021-09-15. <http://app.www.gov.cn/govdata/gov/202109/15/475868/article.html>；新华社 (2022). 工信部推出「一证通查2.0」服务. 福建省工业和信息化厅转载，2022-07-22. <https://gxt.fujian.gov.cn/zwgk/xw/jxyw/202207/t20220722_5958108.htm>；中国银联 (2022). 云闪付APP「一键查卡」功能面向境内所有省市开放. <https://cn.unionpay.com/upowhtml/cn/templates/newInfo-nosub/7885004da382485e8bde5a0ba000fdd3/20220629145824.html>
- Notes: China only: "One-Click Card Check" only covers UnionPay cards. UnionPay has said that cards not linked to a phone number at the bank, or cards whose ID has expired, may not appear, and the issuing bank's records decide. So a card not showing up does not mean it doesn't exist. Loans and credit cards opened in your name by fraudsters can show up in your credit report; see section 8, item 9 (credit report). If One ID Check 2.0 shows that a number is linked to many accounts, unbind them before changing or stopping that number; see item 12 of this section (before changing your phone number or dropping an old number). Lending or selling your bank card or phone card to someone else is a different matter; see section 8, item 8 (not lending out your bank or phone cards). This is rated C because all the sources are official service descriptions, and no study has calculated how much loss the checks prevent. The size of the benefit is rated medium based on judgement. The beneficiary is you.

### 12. Before changing your phone number or dropping an old one, move your bank, payment and social accounts to the new number, so the old one doesn't just lapse
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. You have to rebind the phone number on each account, and the number a bank holds on file has to be changed with the bank. If you have many accounts, this can take one to two hours. The hard part is that it's easy to miss obscure accounts.
- Plain language: After a phone number is cancelled, the carrier can reassign it to someone else after at least 90 days. Any account still linked to the number sends its verification codes to the new owner, who could use them to log in to your account. So before switching numbers, move your accounts and your bank's phone number record to the new one.
- Benefit: The Telecommunications Service Standard states that the "minimum freeze period for a mobile phone number is 90 days", meaning the time between cancellation and reactivation. After 90 days, the number may be assigned to a new user. The MIIT's 2025 first-quarter telecom service quality notice reminded users that they can use the "Second-Hand Number Refresh" (二次号码焕新) service in the apps of China's three major carriers. With it, the new holder of a previously used number can unbind internet accounts that the previous holder registered and bound to that number. The MIIT's 2026 second-quarter notice says the service now covers 258 commonly used internet apps, and that unbinding requests have exceeded 850 million. New holders mostly use it only to unbind, which shows that the previous owner's accounts remained linked to the number after it was passed on. In 2024, customer service staff at the three major carriers (China Mobile, China Unicom and China Telecom) told People's Post and Telecommunications News that after a number is cancelled, new users can still log in with SMS verification codes to accounts the old user registered. They warned users to unbind Alipay, WeChat and other apps before changing or cancelling a number, and to update the phone number held by their bank promptly. (Nationwide)
