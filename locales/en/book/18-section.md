# 18. Is Raising a Child Worth It?

This section works out the money and time sums for raising a child. As with section 10, which works out the marriage sums, it only lays the accounts out for you and does not reach a conclusion for you. If you are already pregnant and want to follow the steps one by one, see section 27.

### 1. First, count what you can get: a national childcare subsidy of 3,600 yuan per child per year, paid until age 3; and a special income tax deduction of 2,000 yuan per child per month
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. The subsidy is applied for at the child's household registration (hukou) location, and it takes one trip to finish. The tax deduction is filled in on the "Individual Income Tax" app on your phone, or your employer can fill it in for you.
- Plain language: Children under 3 get a 3,600 yuan childcare subsidy a year, which is not taxed. Babies born before 1 January 2025 get it pro-rated for the months left. Each child also lets parents deduct 2,000 yuan a month from taxable income until full-time education ends. If one parent claims it all, a 3% bracket taxpayer saves 720 yuan a year, and a 10% bracket taxpayer saves 2,400 yuan.
- Benefit: The plan issued by the General Office of the CPC Central Committee and the General Office of the State Council sets this rule: from 1 January 2025, babies and toddlers under 3 receive a subsidy until they turn 3, at a standard of "3,600 yuan per child per year." For children born before 1 January 2025 who are still under 3, the subsidy is pro-rated for the months it is due. A parent or other guardian applies for it. The subsidy is "exempt from personal income tax." When assessing eligibility for welfare such as the minimum living allowance, it is "not counted as household or personal income." The 3,600 yuan is a "national base standard," and "the funding for any local increase is borne by local government finances." The plan also states that "county-level governments and below may not introduce their own childcare subsidy policies or standards." Provincial or municipal governments that add other childcare subsidies or raise the amount must file them with the next higher supervising authority for the record. On tax, the State Council set a deduction for caring for children under 3 in 2022. In 2023 it raised this deduction and the child education deduction together, so that from 1 January 2023 both are 2,000 yuan per child per month. The child education deduction covers the period from age 3 until starting primary school, and also covers full-time education, from primary school through to a doctorate. Parents "may choose for one parent to claim 100% of the standard deduction, or for both parents to each claim 50% of the standard deduction," and the choice cannot be changed for one year once made. Worked example: one child gives a deduction of 2,000 yuan times 12 months, or 24,000 yuan in total. If one parent claims all of it, a person in the 3% bracket pays 720 yuan less tax, and a person in the 10% bracket pays 2,400 yuan less. If each parent claims half, each claims 12,000 yuan; the 3% bracket saves 360 yuan and the 10% bracket saves 1,200 yuan. Tax brackets are based on annual taxable income: up to 36,000 yuan is taxed at 3%, and the part above 36,000 yuan up to 144,000 yuan is taxed at 10%.

- Notes: China only: This is the national base standard. The plan lets provincial and municipal governments raise the amount or introduce other childcare subsidies, but county-level governments and below cannot create their own. Check your province's implementation plan to see whether your area has added anything and how to claim. The three-year total of 10,800 yuan is a small part of the cost of raising a child, so do not treat it as a reason to have or not have a child. A tax deduction you did not claim during the year can still be claimed at the annual reconciliation; see item 2 of section 5 (annual individual income tax reconciliation). People whose annual taxable income is zero save nothing from the deduction. If the deduction moves you across a bracket, the amount saved falls between the two figures calculated for those brackets. If both parents pay tax, having the parent in the higher bracket claim the whole deduction saves the household more.

### 2. Maternity leave is 98 days, and maternity benefits are paid from the maternity insurance fund at the employer's average monthly wage of the previous year; paternity leave for fathers and parental leave for both parents follow the provincial regulations
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Your employer handles the application, so you do not need to go anywhere yourself.
- Plain language: At least 98 days of maternity leave for giving birth, with more for a difficult or multiple birth. A maternity benefit is paid during this time from the maternity insurance fund, or by your employer if you were not enrolled. Paternity leave and parental leave are set by provincial rules. In Beijing, paternity leave is 15 days, and each parent gets 5 working days of parental leave a year until the child turns 3.
- Benefit: The number of maternity leave days is set by the State Council. "Female employees are entitled to 98 days of maternity leave, of which up to 15 days may be taken before the birth; for a difficult birth, maternity leave is extended by 15 days; for multiple births, maternity leave is extended by 15 days for each additional baby." Money received during maternity leave is called maternity benefit. If you are already enrolled in maternity insurance, it is "paid by the maternity insurance fund at the employer's average monthly wage for the previous year." If you are not enrolled, the employer pays you at your pre-leave wage level. Paternity leave and parental leave for both parents are set by each province's population and family planning regulations. Beijing: the woman gets an extra 60 days of extended maternity leave, and the man gets 15 days of paternity leave. Until the child turns 3, each parent is entitled to "five working days of parental leave per year," and pay may not be reduced during leave. Shanghai: the woman gets an extra 60 days of maternity leave, and the male spouse gets 10 days of paternity leave. Until the child turns 3, "each party may take five days of parental leave per year," with pay as for normal attendance. Guangdong: the woman gets 80 days of reward leave, and the man gets 15 days of paternity leave. For children under 3, each parent gets 10 days of parental leave per year, paid as normal. Sichuan, 2025 edition: the woman gets 90 days of extended maternity leave, and the man gets 30 days of care leave. For children under 3, each spouse gets 10 working days of parental leave per year, counted as attendance during leave. In 2024, the General Office of the State Council required localities to ensure that "maternity leave, childbirth reward leave, paternity leave, parental leave and other fertility leave is fully implemented."

- Notes: China only: 98 days is the national minimum. The four provinces above each add 60 to 90 days of childbirth reward leave on top, bringing totals to 158 to 188 days. Other provinces may add more, so check your own province's regulations. Who pays wages during reward leave differs by province. To look up the rules, search the national laws and regulations database (flk.npc.gov.cn) for "[province name] population and family planning regulations," and use the version marked as in force. Paternity leave is called care leave in some provinces, and parental leave is counted in working days in some and calendar days in others. The day counts for the four provinces above reflect the regulations as of October 2026 and may be revised later.

### 3. Know this: an employer may not cut your pay or dismiss you because of pregnancy, childbirth or breastfeeding
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Costs nothing.
- Plain language: If your employer cuts your pay or fires you because you are pregnant, have given birth or are breastfeeding, that is illegal. Until your baby is one, you get one hour a day for breastfeeding, and your employer may not make you work overtime or night shifts. Breaches are fined 1,000 to 5,000 yuan per person. Section 19 explains how to claim compensation.
- Benefit: The State Council sets this rule: "An employer may not reduce a female employee's pay, dismiss her, or terminate her labor or employment contract because of pregnancy, childbirth or breastfeeding." During these three periods, your employer cannot cut your pay, dismiss you, or end your contract. Once a breastfeeding baby is under one year old, you get one hour a day for breastfeeding. Your employer also may not extend your working hours or make you work night shifts. Breaches such as overtime or night shifts during breastfeeding are fined 1,000 yuan or more but less than 5,000 yuan per person. This regulation attaches no fine to pay cuts or dismissals. If you are unlawfully dismissed, you can go to labor arbitration to demand reinstatement or compensation.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2012). 女职工劳动保护特别规定（国令第 619 号，第五条、第九条、第十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2012-05/07/content_6584.htm>
- Notes: China only: If you are unlawfully dismissed, see section 19 for how to claim compensation. When gathering evidence, focus on written notices of job transfer or pay cuts, and keep chat records. Keep both safely.

### 4. Count the time cost of raising a child by whose time is taken up, not by how hard the work feels
<!-- tags: money=0 time=high will=high gain=high scope=time -->
- Cost: Costs no money, but costs time. Until age 3, a child takes up most of the day. The hard part is that it lasts every day; you cannot just grit your teeth for a few days and get through it.
- Plain language: Turn the hours spent looking after the child each day, and the number of times you are woken at night, into time. Multiply by the hourly rate of the person doing the work. The total usually far exceeds the cost of milk powder and diapers. So it is best to agree on the division of work before the baby arrives.
- Benefit: Convert the time spent caring for the child each day, and the number of night wakings, into hours. Multiply by the hourly rate of the person doing the work. The resulting figure is usually far larger than the cost of milk powder and diapers. Caring, cooking and night feeds are unpaid, but they should be recorded as costs in the same way. The method is the same as the one used for housework in section 10. Agree on the division of work first, then decide whether to have a child.
- Evidence level: C
- Sources: 作者经验，无直接文献；口径见第 10 节
- Notes: The numbers do not need to be precise. What matters is that the two people agree on the division of work, looking at the same chart, before the birth, so they do not argue after it. If your employer moves you to another role or cuts your pay when you return to work, see item 23 of section 19 (refusing a job transfer or pay cut) for how to respond.

### 5. Count money in three stages: ages 0 to 3, compulsory education, and after compulsory education
<!-- tags: money=high time=mid will=some gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: There is no fixed amount. It differs greatly between cities and parenting styles. The hard part is to calm down and count the three stages separately, rather than settling on one figure by feel.
- Plain language: Money for ages 0 to 3 goes mainly on how the child is cared for: by you, by grandparents, or by a paid carer. From primary to middle school, the main spending is housing and extracurricular classes. After that, the main spending is the route to higher education. Each stage is spent on very different things, and mixing them together only produces one frightening total.
- Benefit: The main cost for ages 0 to 3 is the care arrangement: whether you look after the child yourself, grandparents do, or a paid carer does. The main cost during compulsory education (primary to middle school) is housing and extracurricular spending. After that, the main cost is the route to higher education. Spending differs entirely across these three stages, and adding them together only produces a frightening total.
- Evidence level: C
- Sources: 作者经验，无直接文献
- Notes: Start by counting the money you can definitely get, such as the childcare subsidy, maternity benefit and medical insurance reimbursement, then see how much is still missing. Do not make decisions based on "raising one child costs X 10,000 yuan" figures circulating online. Those numbers have unclear definitions, so you do not know what they include or what they leave out.

### 6. Keep a separate ledger for having a child for elders, for marriage, and for old-age security
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Costs no money, but takes one serious conversation. The difficulty is talking it through openly instead of avoiding it.
- Plain language: If others want you to have a child, that can be one factor you consider. But the time and money are charged to your own account. "Children for old age" needs its own line, because it depends on another person's financial ability and willingness twenty years from now. Write down the consequences of not having a child too, and compare the two sides.
- Benefit: The approach is the same as in section 10. Others' expectations can be one factor you weigh. But the costs must be recorded in your own account, not theirs. "Children for old age" especially needs its own line, because it assumes another person's financial ability and willingness twenty years from now.
- Evidence level: C
- Sources: 作者经验，无直接文献；同类分析见第 10 节
- Notes: Write down the consequences of not having a child alongside the consequences of having one, and look at them side by side. Recording only one side is not doing the accounts.

### 7. If you plan to have a child a few years later, first count the woman's age: the chance of natural conception around age 40, and the success rate of IVF, both drop noticeably
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Costs no money, but takes a serious conversation. The difficulty is fitting the timing of children together with work and buying a home, rather than simply pushing it back.
- Plain language: The older a woman is, the harder it is to conceive. In her early 40s, her monthly chance is about half that of a woman in her early 30s. Miscarriage also rises, to about one in three at 40 to 44. IVF cannot fully make up for this: at 43 or 44, each embryo transferred has only about a 5% chance of a live birth.
- Benefit: Natural conception: the evidence comes from a US prospective study of women aged 30 to 44 with no history of infertility who had just started trying to conceive. Compared with women aged 30 and 31, the monthly chance of conception was about 53% lower for women aged 40 and 41 (ratio 0.47, 95% CI 0.28–0.78), and about 59% lower for those aged 42 to 44 (0.39, 0.16–0.93). For ages 38 and 39 it was about 30% lower (0.70, 0.48–1.01); this confidence interval just reaches "no difference." Women aged 32 and 33 showed no difference from women aged 30 and 31. Women who had never been pregnant had a lower chance of conception at every age. In a similar Danish study, the chance for women aged 35 to 40 was 0.77 times that of women aged 20 to 24. The effect of the man's age is much smaller: 0.95 for men aged 35 to 39. Miscarriage: Norwegian registry data from 2009 to 2013, covering about 420,000 pregnancies, show the lowest miscarriage rate at ages 25 to 29, at 9.8%. It was 10.8% at 30 to 34, 16.7% at 35 to 39, 32.2% at 40 to 44, and 53.6% at 45 and over. Embryo chromosomes: a US fertility centre tested 15,169 IVF embryos. Chromosomal abnormalities rise steadily with age from 26 onward. Among women aged 26 to 37 undergoing IVF, 2% to 6% had all of their cultured embryos chromosomally abnormal; the figure was 33% at 42 and 53% at 44. IVF success rates come from the UK's Human Fertilisation and Embryology Authority preliminary statistics for 2022. For fresh embryo transfers using the woman's own eggs, the proportion of transferred embryos that led to a live birth was: 35% at 18 to 34; 26% at 35 to 37; 18% at 38 and 39; 10% at 40 to 42; and 5% at 43 and 44. Rules in China: the 2003 Ministry of Health "Technical Specifications for Human Assisted Reproductive Technology" states that "assisted reproductive technology is prohibited for couples and single women who do not comply with national population and family planning laws and regulations." In 2024 the General Office of the State Council required localities to bring suitable assisted reproduction technologies into the scope of medical insurance reimbursement. In July 2025, the National Healthcare Security Administration (the national medical insurance agency) said that 31 provinces and the Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps had fully included assisted reproduction in reimbursement, and that more than 1 million people received benefits in 2024.
