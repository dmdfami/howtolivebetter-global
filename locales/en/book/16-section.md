# 16. Living well after a chronic disease diagnosis

Scope: all-cause mortality and money. After a chronic disease is found, you often still have several decades to live, and this section looks at how to manage those decades.

### 1. Take your medicine exactly as prescribed, and do not stop when you feel better
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=high gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Costs no money, and it saves what you would otherwise spend treating complications later. Taking your pills each day takes only a few minutes. The hard part is taking them every single day.
- Plain language: Those who take their medicine as prescribed are about half as likely to die over the same period as those who take it on and off. Treat this number with caution. Even people who take placebo pills as prescribed die less, so part of the benefit reflects people who already take their health more seriously.
- Benefit: Pooled data from 21 studies with 46,847 people (a meta-analysis) show that, compared with people who take their medicine on and off, those who take it as prescribed are about 44% less likely to die over the same period. The original odds ratio is 0.56, 95% CI 0.50–0.63 (confidence interval). Looking only at treatments that really work, the reduction is about 45% (0.55, 0.49–0.62).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Simpson SH, Eurich DT, Majumdar SR, et al. (2006). A meta-analysis of the association between adherence to drug therapy and mortality. BMJ, 333(7557), 15. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.38875.675486.55>
- Notes: Disputed. This is also the number in this book that most needs to be taken with a grain of salt. In the same pooled analysis, people who took a placebo (fake pill) as prescribed also had lower mortality (0.56, 0.43–0.74). This suggests that much of the benefit comes from people who can keep taking medicine already paying more attention to their health. The author calls this the healthy adherer effect.

### 2. Get outpatient special-condition certification first, then out-of-area registration. Hypertension, diabetes, chemotherapy and radiotherapy, dialysis, and anti-rejection care can then be settled directly while you travel
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Before you travel, complete out-of-area medical care registration once in the national medical insurance app. You can do it on your phone.
- Plain language: For five outpatient conditions (hypertension, diabetes, cancer chemotherapy or radiotherapy, dialysis, and anti-rejection after transplant), out-of-area care usually settles directly through insurance when you are away, so you need not pay up front. Both steps are needed: first certify the condition at home, then register for out-of-area care.
- Benefit: The national medical insurance bureau says this has already been achieved. In its words, five outpatient special-condition treatments can now be settled directly across provinces at county level: hypertension, diabetes, outpatient chemotherapy and radiotherapy for malignant tumors, dialysis for uremia, and anti-rejection treatment after organ transplant. "County-level accessibility" means you can do this at county level. The document adds five more conditions to these five.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国家医保局办公室、财政部办公厅 (2024). 关于稳妥有序扩大跨省直接结算门诊慢特病病种范围的通知（医保办发〔2024〕19 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202409/content_6974467.htm>
- Notes: China only: Both steps are required: first get outpatient special-condition certification in your home area, then register for out-of-area care. How far the five newly added conditions have progressed varies by place, so follow what your local medical insurance office publishes. If the hospital does not stock a negotiated drug you need, see section 24, item 13 (dual-channel designated pharmacies).

### 3. Get check-ups at the intervals your doctor gives, and write every reading in the same notebook
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=some gain=low scope=health -->
- Cost: Each check costs tens to hundreds of yuan (RMB). A trip to the hospital, plus waiting for results, takes some time. The difficulty is that you must go back every so often, and it is easy to keep putting it off.
- Plain language: Chronic disease is tracked by how values change over time, not by any single reading. Measuring at home alone does not, by itself, show that blood pressure has come down. Bringing the readings to your doctor, who adjusts your medicine accordingly, lowers it further. When you change hospitals, a test result marked with the mutual recognition logo usually should not be repeated.
- Benefit: Chronic disease must be judged by how values change over a period, not by one reading. One meta-analysis of hypertension pooled raw data from 7,138 people in 15 randomized trials. Compared with people receiving usual care, those who measured blood pressure at home had an average change in clinic systolic pressure of −3.2 mmHg at 12 months (95% CI −4.9 to −1.6). Self-measurement alone, with no other measures, showed no clear effect: −1.0 mmHg (−3.3 to 1.2; the difference may be due to chance). The group with the most support lowered it by 6.1 mmHg (3.2 to 9.0). Support means doctors, pharmacists, or patients themselves adjusting medicine based on the readings, plus health education and lifestyle guidance. The authors conclude that self-measurement alone cannot lower blood pressure further; it is useful only when combined with adjusting medicine based on the readings. All these figures are for blood pressure; death was not measured directly. When you change hospitals, the measures issued by the National Health Commission and three other bodies state that for existing examination and test results a patient provides, if they meet the mutual recognition conditions and satisfy treatment needs, hospitals and doctors must not repeat them. Reports eligible for mutual recognition carry the "HR" logo, such as "national HR".
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Tucker KL, Sheppard JP, Stevens R, et al. (2017). Self-monitoring of blood pressure in hypertension: A systematic review and individual patient data meta-analysis. PLoS Medicine, 14(9), e1002389. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1002389>；国家卫生健康委、国家医保局、国家中医药局、中央军委后勤保障部卫生局 (2022). 关于印发医疗机构检查检验结果互认管理办法的通知（国卫医发〔2022〕6 号），第十三条、第十七条、第十九条. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-02/19/content_5674575.htm>
- Notes: China only: Rated B only, because the direct evidence covers hypertension alone, and its endpoint is blood pressure, not death. No study was found on whether keeping readings in one notebook improves health outcomes itself. Record at least four things each time: the date, the reading, the medicines you were taking, and the dose. Photographing lab reports with your phone is fine, but keep them all in one place, not scattered. Mutual recognition has exceptions: the hospital may re-test if your condition has changed, if results are changing quickly, before surgery or a blood transfusion, or in emergency rescue. For how to copy and keep medical records and imaging, see section 24, item 5 (keep your own copy of medical records, test reports and imaging).

### 4. Do not stop proper treatment in favor of folk remedies or health supplements
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=high gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Costs no money, and it saves what you would have spent on supplements. Takes no time. The hard part is holding firm when others keep urging you to try them.
- Plain language: Curable cancer patients who relied only on alternative medicine had 2.5 times the risk of death of those given proper treatment. Those who combined it with treatment were more likely to refuse surgery, chemotherapy or radiotherapy, and the extra deaths trace to exactly that refusal. Some herbs weaken anti-rejection and blood-thinning drugs, so ask your doctor first.
- Benefit: Two US cancer registry studies looked at patients with breast, prostate, lung and colorectal cancer that had not spread and could be cured. The first looked at people who used only alternative medicine and had none of surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy or hormone therapy. Compared with matched patients receiving conventional treatment, their risk of death was 2.5 times higher (HR 2.50, 95% CI 1.88–3.27; about 150% higher). For breast cancer it was 5.68 times (3.22–10.04), for lung cancer 2.17 times (1.42–3.32), and for colorectal cancer 4.57 times (1.66–12.61). The second looked at people who used complementary therapy alongside conventional treatment. Their refusal rate was 7.0% vs 0.1% for surgery, 34.1% vs 3.2% for chemotherapy, 53.0% vs 2.3% for radiotherapy, and 33.7% vs 2.8% for hormone therapy. Five-year survival was 82.2% vs 86.6%, and the risk of death was 2.08 times higher (HR 2.08, 1.50–2.90; about 100% higher). After accounting for delays and refusals of treatment, complementary therapy itself was no longer linked to death (HR 1.39, 0.83–2.33). The authors conclude that the extra deaths come from refusing conventional treatment. China's State Administration for Market Regulation requires health-supplement labels to say: "Health supplements are not medicines and cannot replace drug treatment of disease." The US National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH) states that St. John's wort weakens the effect of many drugs, including cyclosporine, which prevents organ-transplant rejection, and the blood thinner warfarin.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Johnson SB, Park HS, Gross CP, Yu JB (2018). Use of Alternative Medicine for Cancer and Its Impact on Survival. Journal of the National Cancer Institute, 110(1), 121-124. <https://doi.org/10.1093/jnci/djx145>；Johnson SB, Park HS, Gross CP, Yu JB (2018). Complementary Medicine, Refusal of Conventional Cancer Therapy, and Survival Among Patients With Curable Cancers. JAMA Oncology, 4(10), 1375-1381. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2018.2487>；国家市场监督管理总局 (2019). 市场监管总局就《保健食品标注警示用语指南》和《保健食品原料目录与保健功能目录管理办法》有关情况举办专题新闻发布会. <https://www.samr.gov.cn/tssps/sjdt/tpxw/art/2023/art_4b658b824b1b4b0ba57c09a56cc93aad.html>；National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (2025). St. John's Wort. <https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/st-johns-wort>
- Notes: Rated B only. Both studies looked only at cancer and followed patients through registry data, with no controlled comparison groups. People who chose alternative medicine may also differ in other ways. For the account of stopping drugs for hypertension and diabetes, see item 1 (take your medicine exactly as prescribed). If you want to try a supplement, tell your doctor first. Whether to buy health products such as supplements, medicated pastes or tonics is covered in section 6, item 10 (health supplements, medicated pastes, tonics).

### 5. For stable chronic conditions, a community clinic can prescribe up to 12 weeks of medicine at once
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=time -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Ask the community health service center whether they can issue a long-term prescription. It takes one sentence.
- Plain language: Stable patients on unchanged medicine can get up to 12 weeks at a community clinic, saving a half-day trip and queue each month. In the same insurance area, reimbursement at primary-level clinics is usually about 10 percentage points higher than at the level above.
- Benefit: You do not need to travel to a large hospital and queue for prescriptions every month. In the same insurance area (also called a pooling area), the reimbursement rate at primary-level institutions is in principle about 10 percentage points higher than at the level above.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国务院办公厅 (2026). 关于加快建设分级诊疗体系的若干措施. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>：「对于符合条件的慢性病患者，基层医疗卫生机构单次可开具不超过12周用药的长期处方。」；同文「原则上统筹地区内医疗卫生机构住院报销比例逐级拉开10个百分点左右的差距」，并要求上级医院在基层开设高血压、糖尿病、慢性阻塞性肺疾病等常见病慢性病门诊；同文「三级医院要聚焦急危重症和疑难复杂疾病，加强转诊会诊和住院服务，逐步酌减常见病复诊和诊断明确、病情稳定的慢性病等普通门诊」，「并将专家团队普通门诊向基层医疗卫生机构延伸」
- Notes: China only: "Eligible" is judged by your doctor, generally for people whose diagnosis is clear and whose treatment plan is stable. The medicine stays the same; what you save is the half-day trip each month and the registration fee. If the clinic does not have your medicine, ask whether they keep a shortage register and offer delivery. The same document also asks tertiary hospitals to gradually reduce general outpatient visits for this kind of stable chronic condition, and to extend specialist team clinics into primary care. So hospitals used only as prescription points will become harder to get appointments at. If the community clinic lacks your medicine, or you need a specialist to adjust treatment, you can book an online follow-up with the hospital that diagnosed you; see section 24, item 14 (online follow-up at internet hospitals).

### 6. Before signing with a community family doctor, ask which services are covered by insurance and which you pay for yourself
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Asking one more question before signing takes about a minute.
- Plain language: The basic service package is covered by insurance as stipulated. You pay in full for any personalized service package. When someone pitches an "upgrade package," first ask whether it is the basic or the personalized package, whether it is filed, and how much it costs per year.
- Benefit: The basic service package is paid by insurance as stipulated. You pay the full cost of any personalized service package, and such a package must be filed with the county-level health authority.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国务院办公厅 (2026). 关于加快建设分级诊疗体系的若干措施. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>：「加强基层门诊付费与签约服务政策联动，基本服务包按规定纳入医保支付；个性化服务包由签约基层医疗卫生机构按程序向县级卫生健康部门备案，费用由个人支付。」
- Notes: China only: The value of signing lies in having someone follow your condition over the long term, not in how many items are bundled. When someone sells you an "upgrade package," ask three questions: is this the basic or the personalized package, is it filed, and how much does it cost per year.

### 7. After a diabetes diagnosis, get an eye exam once, then at the interval your doctor gives. Get your feet checked every year
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: One dilated eye exam costs tens to hundreds of yuan (RMB). Foot checks are usually part of an outpatient visit and cost nothing extra. The difficulty is remembering to get checked when neither your eyes nor your feet feel wrong.
- Plain language: Diabetes can damage the retina and the feet with almost no symptoms, but early detection still allows treatment. Type 2 needs an eye exam at diagnosis; type 1 starts five years after onset. After one or two clear eye checks, with blood sugar on target, eye exams can be every one to two years. Check your feet every year.
- Benefit: Diabetic damage to the eye is called retinopathy, and damage to the feet is called diabetic foot. Both cause almost no symptoms before they become a problem. Caught early, they can still be treated. By the time vision is lost or the foot has ulcerated, it is too late.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 美国糖尿病学会 (2026). 糖尿病诊疗标准 2026·第 12 章 视网膜病变、神经病变与足部护理. Diabetes Care：「People with type 2 diabetes should have an initial dilated and comprehensive eye examination by an ophthalmologist or optometrist at the time of the diabetes diagnosis.」「Adults with type 1 diabetes should have an initial dilated and comprehensive eye examination by an ophthalmologist or optometrist 5 years after the onset of diabetes.」「If there is no evidence of retinopathy from one or more annual eye exams and glycemic indicators are within the goal range, then screening every 1–2 years may be considered.」「Perform a comprehensive foot evaluation at least annually to identify risk factors for ulcers and amputations.」<https://doi.org/10.2337/dc26-S012>
- Notes: Type 2 diabetes is checked at diagnosis; type 1 is checked starting five years after onset. The difference is that type 2 has often been quietly high for several years already. After one or two clear checks with blood sugar on target, the interval can be relaxed to one to two years. If the feeling in your feet has become dull, look at the soles of your feet every day; a mirror helps. Kidney function should also be checked every year; see item 13 (urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio).

### 8. If you have had a kidney stone, drink 2.5–3 litres of water a day and keep salt under 6 grams
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. It means making drinking water a habit. On days when you sweat a lot, drink more. The hard part is remembering every day.
- Plain language: Without treatment, about a quarter of people with a first calcium stone of unknown cause form another within five years. Those who drink enough water: just over one in ten, less than half as many. The UK guideline advises 2.5 to 3 litres of water a day and no more than 6 grams of salt.
- Benefit: A randomised controlled trial ran for 5 years with 199 people who had a first calcium kidney stone with no identifiable cause (also called idiopathic calcium nephrolithiasis). One group drank at least 2 litres of water a day; the other group received no intervention. Over 5 years, stones came back in 12 of 99 people in the water group (12.1%) and in 27 of 100 in the no-intervention group (27.0%), P=0.008. The smaller the P value, the less likely the difference between the two groups is due to chance. Average time between stones: 38.7±13.2 months versus 25.1±16.4 months, P=0.016. At the start, the 24-hour urine volume of stone patients was clearly lower than in people without stones: men 1,057±238 mL versus 1,401±562 mL (P<0.0001), women 990±230 mL versus 1,239±440 mL (P<0.001). NICE guideline NG118, recommendation 1.8.1, advises adults to drink 2.5 to 3 litres of water a day and children and adolescents 1 to 2 litres, depending on age. Add a little fresh lemon juice to the water, avoid fizzy drinks, and keep adult salt intake to no more than 6 grams a day. The same NICE guideline, recommendation 1.7.1, advises that adults who have had a kidney or ureteric stone consider a stone analysis. For recurrent stones where more than half are calcium oxalate, recommendation 1.8.3 suggests considering potassium citrate. NICE found no evidence for 24-hour urine tests and made no recommendation on them. The European Association of Urology (EAU) stone guideline is worded differently: every stone patient should have a reliable stone analysis. People who have had recurrent stones are high risk and should collect 24-hour urine on two occasions for specialised metabolic testing. Its general prevention target is 2.5 to 3 litres of water a day, with a daily urine output of 2 to 2.5 litres.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Borghi L, Meschi T, Amato F, Briganti A, Novarini A, Giannini A (1996). Urinary volume, water and recurrences in idiopathic calcium nephrolithiasis: a 5-year randomized prospective study. The Journal of Urology, 155(3), 839-843. <https://doi.org/10.1016/s0022-5347(01)66321-3>；National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (2019). Renal and ureteric stones: assessment and management. NICE guideline NG118，第 1.7.1、1.8.1、1.8.3 条. <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng118/chapter/Recommendations>；European Association of Urology (2026). EAU Guidelines on Urolithiasis, Chapter 4 Metabolic Evaluation and Recurrence Prevention（第 4.1、4.1.2 节，Table 4.5）. <https://uroweb.org/guidelines/urolithiasis/chapter/metabolic-evaluation-and-recurrence-prevention>（高危结石患者名单见同一指南 Table 3.3：<https://uroweb.org/guidelines/urolithiasis/chapter/guidelines>）
- Notes: Recurrence fell by about 55%, and it costs nothing, so under this book's cost-benefit method it is the best-value item in this section. Track 24-hour urine volume: the European target is 2 to 2.5 litres a day, not how much you drink. People who sweat heavily or work in hot conditions need to drink more. That trial studied people with a first idiopathic calcium stone. Stones come in several types; uric acid, infection-related and cystine stones each need different approaches. Both the UK and European guidelines recommend a stone analysis. For people who have had recurrent stones, the European guideline also calls for 24-hour urine tests before deciding whether to add medication; the UK guideline finds the evidence for this test insufficient. Do not cut out calcium to prevent stones; see section 6, item 21. If the colic is severe, go to hospital; it is not something to flush out with water. Painless blood in the urine has other causes that need checking; see section 1, item 27.

### 9. If gout is confirmed and you have tophi, bone erosion on imaging, or two or more attacks a year, take a urate-lowering drug long term and keep blood urate below 360 µmol/L
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=high gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Allopurinol costs a few to a few dozen yuan a month. At first, check blood urate every few weeks to adjust the dose; once stable, every few months. For the first 3 to 6 months you also take a drug to prevent attacks. The hard part is taking it every day, even when you feel fine.
- Plain language: If you have tophi, bone erosion, or two or more attacks a year, you need long-term urate-lowering medication to bring uric acid below 360 µmol/L. If attacks are rarer, discuss with your doctor whether to take it. When a nurse adjusted doses based on test results, 95% reached the target after two years, versus 30% under usual care. Once you reach the target you must keep taking the drug; diet alone lowers urate only a little.
- Benefit: A randomised trial of 517 adults who had had gout attacks in the previous 12 months. One group received nurse-led care: the nurse explained the disease to patients and adjusted their urate-lowering treatment to reach the target. The other group continued with their usual GP care. At 2 years, the share with blood urate below 360 µmol/L (6 mg/dL) was 95% versus 30%. The chance of reaching the target was 3.18 times that of usual care (RR 3.18, 95% CI 2.42–4.18; the CI is the range within which the true value is likely to lie), P<0.0001. Secondary measures in year 2, including attack frequency, tophi and quality of life, were also better than usual care. Each extra year of life, adjusted for quality (a quality-adjusted life year), cost £5,066. The same trial had a follow-up study: 438 people completed questionnaires, an 82% response rate. Both groups self-reported their attacks over the past 12 months. The target group's median was 0 attacks (the middle half of people fell between 0 and 0), and usual care's median was 1 (0–3), P<0.001. The bracketed range is the interquartile range. The share still taking urate-lowering drugs was 1.19 times higher in the target group (adjusted RR 1.19, 1.09–1.30). The American College of Rheumatology's 2020 gout guideline strongly recommends starting urate-lowering treatment for three groups: people with tophi under the skin, people whose imaging shows gout has eroded bone, and people with two or more attacks a year. For people who have had more than one attack but fewer than two a year, the guideline conditionally recommends starting treatment, a decision to be made jointly by doctor and patient. For people having a first attack, the guideline conditionally recommends against starting. The exceptions are people with stage 3 or higher chronic kidney disease, blood urate above 9 mg/dL, or a history of urinary tract stones; for these people the guideline conditionally recommends treatment. The approach is to monitor blood urate continuously and adjust the dose until the target, below 6 mg/dL, is reached. Allopurinol is the first choice, including for people with stage 3 or higher CKD, with a starting dose of 100 mg per day or less. An anti-inflammatory preventive drug is started at the same time and taken for at least 3 to 6 months. The same guideline cites a case series: patients who stopped their drugs after long-term good control, with blood urate still below 7 mg/dL after stopping. Of them, only 13% (27/211) had no attacks during 5 years of follow-up. On diet: people who limit or avoid alcohol have blood urate 1.6 mg/dL lower than those who do not, and one beer raises blood urate by 0.16 mg/dL. Healthy eating, the Mediterranean diet and the DASH diet have smaller effects. Which urate-lowering drug to choose is disputed. The American guideline strongly recommends allopurinol as the first choice, because it works when the dose is high enough, is well tolerated, safe and cheap. It also conditionally recommends that people with cardiovascular disease who take febuxostat, or who have just had a cardiovascular event, switch to another drug where one is available. This recommendation matches the FDA's boxed warning on febuxostat. One basis is the CARES trial: 6,190 gout patients with cardiovascular disease were randomly assigned to febuxostat or allopurinol. The all-cause death risk in the febuxostat group was 1.22 times that of the allopurinol group (HR 1.22, 95% CI 1.01–1.47, about 22% higher). Cardiovascular death was 1.34 times as high (1.03–1.73, about 34% higher). The European FAST trial reached a different conclusion. It enrolled 6,128 people over 60 with cardiovascular risk factors; febuxostat did not cause more major cardiovascular events than allopurinol (HR 0.85, 0.70–1.03). Deaths were 7.2% in the febuxostat group and 8.6% in the allopurinol group. For people taking drugs that help the kidneys excrete uric acid, the American College of Rheumatology conditionally advises against alkalinising the urine, because there is no proof it works; the certainty of the evidence is very low. The guideline also asks that these people be advised to drink plenty of water. People who already have kidney stones or stage 3 or higher CKD should not use this type of drug. The same guideline notes that the HLA-B\*5801 genotype is most common in people of Han Chinese, Korean and Thai ancestry, at 7.4%, compared with 3.8% in African Americans and 0.7% in White and Hispanic people. Asian and African American people who take allopurinol have three times the risk of allopurinol hypersensitivity syndrome compared with White people. The guideline conditionally recommends testing for HLA-B\*5801 before allopurinol in these two groups; the certainty of the evidence is very low.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Doherty M, Jenkins W, Richardson H, et al. (2018). Efficacy and cost-effectiveness of nurse-led care involving education and engagement of patients and a treat-to-target urate-lowering strategy versus usual care for gout: a randomised controlled trial. Lancet, 392(10156), 1403-1412. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)32158-5>；Abhishek A, Jenkins W, La-Crette J, Fernandes G, Doherty M (2020). Nurse-led care is preferred over GP-led care of gout and improves gout outcomes: results of Nottingham Gout Treatment Trial follow-up study. Rheumatology, 59(3), 575-579. <https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/kez333>；FitzGerald JD, Dalbeth N, Mikuls T, et al. (2020). 2020 American College of Rheumatology Guideline for the Management of Gout. Arthritis Care & Research, 72(6), 744-760：「Strong recommendations included initiation of ULT for all patients with tophaceous gout, radiographic damage due to gout, or frequent gout flares」「For patients who have previously experienced >1 flare but have infrequent flares (<2/year), we conditionally recommend initiating ULT over no ULT」「For patients experiencing their first flare, we conditionally recommend against initiating ULT over no ULT」. <https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.24180>；White WB, Saag KG, Becker MA, et al.; CARES Investigators (2018). Cardiovascular Safety of Febuxostat or Allopurinol in Patients with Gout. New England Journal of Medicine, 378(13), 1200-1210. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1710895>；Mackenzie IS, Ford I, Nuki G, et al.; FAST Study Group (2020). Long-term cardiovascular safety of febuxostat compared with allopurinol in patients with gout (FAST): a multicentre, prospective, randomised, open-label, non-inferiority trial. Lancet, 396(10264), 1745-1757. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(20)32234-0>；中华医学会内分泌学分会 (2020). 中国高尿酸血症与痛风诊疗指南(2019). 中华内分泌代谢杂志, 36(1), 1-13. <https://doi.org/10.3760/cma.j.issn.1000-6699.2020.01.001>
- Notes: China only: Disputed: the drugs used to lower uric acid differ between national guidelines. The American guideline puts allopurinol first and, for people with heart disease, says to avoid febuxostat where possible; but the European FAST trial found no added danger from febuxostat. Benzbromarone, which helps the kidneys excrete uric acid, is still sold in China. The Chinese Medical Association's gout guidelines (2019 edition and the 2024 update) give separate recommendations on drug choice and urine alkalinisation. Whether to add sodium bicarbonate to alkalinise the urine when taking benzbromarone: the American guideline sees no evidence for it, so follow your doctor's advice; drinking water cannot be skipped. **People of Han Chinese ancestry should consider testing for HLA-B\*5801 before taking allopurinol.** It is an inherited, immune-related genotype; one test gives a result that stays the same for life. Carriers make up 7.4% of Han Chinese, Korean and Thai people, but only 0.7% of White and Hispanic people. Asian and African American people who take allopurinol have three times the risk of allopurinol hypersensitivity syndrome compared with White people; in severe cases this condition can strip large areas of skin and can be fatal. The American College of Rheumatology's 2020 guideline suggests that these two groups consider testing before starting. **Starting urate-lowering drugs can trigger attacks at first**, which is why the guideline explicitly requires an anti-inflammatory preventive drug for the first 3 to 6 months. A common failure is "the urate-lowering drug made me hurt more, so I stopped it." When an attack happens, follow your doctor's advice and add an anti-inflammatory drug; do not stop the urate-lowering drug. Target values are written in different ways: <6 mg/dL is about 360 µmol/L, and Chinese lab reports mostly use µmol/L. High uric acid found on a check-up without any attacks is a different matter; see section 6, item 19. For sugary drinks and alcohol, see section 2, items 7 and 19 (avoid sugary drinks, and drink little or no alcohol). Eating large amounts of meat and seafood in one meal, or drinking alcohol, can trigger attacks; see item 10 (at barbecues and gatherings, do not eat large amounts of meat and seafood in one sitting).

### 10. If you have had gout, do not eat large amounts of meat and seafood in one sitting at barbecues or gatherings, and drink less alcohol too
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. At barbecues and meals out, order fewer meat skewers and less seafood, and drink less alcohol. The hard part is restraint at the table.
- Plain language: For people who have had gout, the two days when they eat the most purines may bring an attack nearly 5 times as often as the two days when they eat the least. This holds even for people taking urate-lowering drugs. Drinking two to four drinks the day before raises the chance of an attack by about half. Beer, spirits and wine all count.
- Benefit: One study followed 633 gout patients online for 1 year, comparing the two days before each attack with the same person's two days without an attack (a case-crossover design). Purines are compounds in food that the body turns into uric acid. The total purine intake over two days was split into five groups from lowest to highest; compared with the lowest group, the four higher groups had attack odds of 1.17, 1.38, 2.21 and 4.76 times (OR, trend P<0.001). Counting only animal-source purines: 1.42, 1.34, 1.77 and 2.41 times. Counting only plant-source purines: 1.12, 0.99, 1.32 and 1.39 times. The results were the same when analysed separately by sex, alcohol use, diuretic use, allopurinol, NSAIDs and colchicine. Another study by the same team looked at the alcohol drunk in the 24 hours before attacks in 724 gout patients. Compared with none, drinking more than 1 and up to 2 drinks was associated with 1.36 times the odds (95% CI 1.00–1.88), and more than 2 and up to 4 drinks with 1.51 times (1.09–2.09). Wine, beer and spirits were each linked to attacks. For people without gout, a follow-up study of 47,150 American men tracked for 12 years, which recorded but did not assign groups: the fifth who ate the most meat had about 41% higher gout risk than the fifth who ate the least (RR 1.41, 1.07–1.86). Seafood raised the risk by about 51% (1.51, 1.17–1.95). The fifth who ate the most dairy had about 44% lower risk (0.56, 0.42–0.74). Total purine-rich vegetables and total protein showed no clear link. Among the same men, one extra 12-ounce bottle of beer (about 355 mL) per day raised the risk by about 49% (1.49, 1.32–1.70). One spirit drink a day raised it by about 15% (1.15, 1.04–1.28). Wine showed no statistically clear difference (1.04, 0.88–1.22).
- Evidence level: B

So when someone you know gets gout after barbecues and beer, diet is one trigger, but body constitution accounts for more. The dairy figure is also observational only; no trial has shown that drinking more milk prevents gout. The mortality-based case for eating less processed meat and drinking less alcohol is in section 2, item 18 (eat less processed meat) and section 2, item 19 (drink little or no alcohol).

### 11. If you test positive for hepatitis B surface antigen, get a liver ultrasound and an alpha-fetoprotein test every six months, let your doctor decide whether you need antiviral drugs, and do not stop them on your own once you have started
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=high gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Visit a liver or infectious disease clinic every six months for a blood test and a liver ultrasound; fees vary by region. For those who need antiviral drugs, China's National Healthcare Security Administration says that after volume-based procurement the annual cost of entecavir fell to just over 100 yuan. The hard part is going for checks when you feel fine, and taking the drug long term.
- Plain language: In a Shanghai trial, carriers screened every six months with ultrasound and blood tests had nearly 40% fewer liver cancer deaths. More virus in the blood means more liver cancer later. For badly scarred livers, antiviral drugs roughly halved liver cancer. Most people must take these drugs long term, since stopping often lets the virus rebound.
- Benefit: A randomised trial in Shanghai of 18,816 people aged 35 to 59 who were infected with hepatitis B or had a history of chronic hepatitis. The screening group was invited every 6 months for an alpha-fetoprotein test (a blood marker that often rises when liver cancer is present) and an ultrasound; the control group was not screened. The screening group had 32 liver cancer deaths, the control group 54. Liver cancer mortality was 83.2 versus 131.5 per 100,000 people. The screening group's rate was 0.63 times that of the control group (95% CI 0.41–0.98, the likely range), about 37% lower. Of the liver cancers found in the screening group, 46.5% had surgical removal, versus 7.5% in the control group. A Taiwanese follow-up study of 3,653 people aged 30 to 65 who were surface-antigen positive, followed for an average of 11.4 years, recorded but did not assign groups. The higher the blood virus level at entry, the more liver cancer later: with fewer than 300 copies per mL, there were 108 liver cancers per 100,000 person-years; with more than 1 million copies per mL, there were 1,152. The cumulative liver cancer rates were 1.3% and 14.9% respectively. After adjusting for sex, age, smoking, drinking, liver enzymes and cirrhosis, the pattern of more virus and more cancer still held. An Asian randomised trial of 651 chronic hepatitis B patients with severe liver fibrosis or cirrhosis: one group took lamivudine, the other a placebo, for a median of 32.4 months. Liver cancer occurred in 3.9% versus 7.4%, a risk about half as high (HR 0.49, P=0.047). Disease worsening occurred in 7.8% versus 17.7% (HR 0.45, about 55% lower). The Chinese Medical Association's 2022 guideline states that people with chronic hepatitis B virus infection should have an abdominal ultrasound and an alpha-fetoprotein test every 6 months to screen for liver cancer; high-risk people should have them at least every 3 to 6 months. Carriers who do not need drugs for now should have a blood count, liver function, viral markers, alpha-fetoprotein and an ultrasound every 6 to 12 months. Once they meet the criteria for treatment, treatment should start straight away. People with detectable virus who are over 30 are advised to start antiviral treatment regardless of whether their liver enzymes are raised. The same applies if a family member has had cirrhosis or liver cancer caused by hepatitis B. The oral drugs the guideline recommends are the entecavir and tenofovir class. The guideline states that most people need long-term treatment, and that the rate of virus rebound after stopping is high.

### 12. If you have COPD, have your doctor or pharmacist watch you use your inhaler at each follow-up visit; if you were hospitalised for an acute flare, go to pulmonary rehabilitation after discharge
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Having your inhaler technique checked costs nothing extra: bring your medicine to the follow-up visit and show how you use it, which takes a few minutes. Pulmonary rehabilitation is supervised exercise training, plus instruction on medication and self-management. It involves several sessions over a set programme, and fees vary by area. The hard part is that you feel weak after discharge and must keep going to the sessions.
- Plain language: About three in ten people who use inhalers do the technique completely right, and some errors stop the medicine from reaching the lungs. People who make a key error are roughly twice as likely to be hospitalised or go to the emergency room for a flare. After hospitalisation for a flare, pulmonary rehabilitation can cut the chance of readmission by more than half, and improve quality of life and walking ability. Whether it reduces deaths is not yet clear.
- Benefit: A national Chinese sample survey of 50,991 adults aged 20 and over, all of whom had lung function tests. The COPD prevalence was 8.6% (95% CI 7.5–9.9, the likely range), about 99.9 million people. Among people aged 40 and over it was 13.7%. Among people with COPD, only 12.0% had ever had a lung function test. One analysis pooled 144 studies covering 54,354 patients who used inhalers while watched by trained staff. Technique was fully correct in 31%, acceptable in 41%, and poor in 31%. Comparing the first 20 years with the last 20, there was no improvement. With pressurised spray inhalers, 45% did not coordinate pressing and breathing in; 44% did not inhale fast or deeply enough; and 46% did not hold their breath after inhaling. A French study recorded but did not assign groups: doctors watched 2,935 COPD patients use their inhalers. More than half made an error. Key errors that stop the drug reaching the lungs ranged from 15.4% to 46.9% across different inhaler devices. Among people hospitalised or sent to emergency care for a flare in the past 3 months, 3.3% of those with no errors and 6.9% of those with a key error were in that group (OR 1.86, 1.14–3.04). The summary of the GOLD 2023 report says doctors and patients should be taught correct inhaler use, and checked regularly at follow-up. It also recommends vaccines such as influenza and pneumococcal vaccines, as allowed by local rules. A 2016 Cochrane review pooled 20 randomised trials with 1,477 people, comparing pulmonary rehabilitation after a COPD flare with usual care without rehabilitation. Eight studies with 810 people looked at readmission. Rehabilitation was associated with about 56% lower readmission (OR 0.44, 95% CI 0.21–0.91), with moderate evidence quality, but the results varied widely between studies. Six studies with 670 people looked at death: OR 0.68 (0.28–1.67). The difference may be chance; evidence quality is low. Quality of life improved by more than patients could notice; evidence quality is high. The six-minute walk distance increased by 62 metres on average (38–86 metres); evidence quality is high.

### 13. If you have diabetes, get a urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio and a serum creatinine test every year; if you have high blood pressure too, check for it as well, so chronic kidney disease is caught early

If urine protein is high, taking dapagliflozin, a type of drug, cuts the combined risk of kidney decline, dialysis or death by about 40%, and the risk of death by about 30%.
- Benefit: China's 2018–2019 national chronic disease surveillance tested blood and urine in 176,874 adults. About 8.2% had chronic kidney disease, roughly 82 million people. Among those 2,997 people, only 27% had previously been tested for kidney disease. The KDIGO 2024 kidney guideline says people at risk should have both urine albumin and glomerular filtration rate (how many millilitres of blood the kidneys filter per minute) tested. ### 14. After surgery and chemotherapy for colon cancer, join a supervised exercise programme and keep it up for three years
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=high gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Walking and similar exercise costs nothing. To get a fitness assessment and an exercise prescription, visit a hospital rehabilitation department or an exercise prescription clinic; registration and test fees apply, and a private trainer costs extra. It takes several extra hours of exercise a week, and you must keep it up for three years. Exercisers had more muscle and joint injuries.
- Plain language: After colon cancer surgery and chemotherapy, a three-year supervised exercise programme cut the chance of recurrence, a new cancer or death by about 28%, and the chance of death by about 37%. Eight years on, about 90% of the exercise group were alive, versus about 83% of the control group. Exercisers had more muscle and joint injuries.
- Benefit: A randomised trial in 55 centres (CHALLENGE) enrolled 889 colon cancer patients. All had completed surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy, were at high risk (stage II or III), and had no spread at enrolment. They joined 2 to 6 months after finishing chemotherapy, and did fewer than 150 minutes of exercise a week before joining. 445 people did the three-year exercise programme; 444 received only healthy-living printed materials. In the first 6 months of the programme, participants met an exercise coach every two weeks to talk about sticking with it, and did a session under the coach's supervision.
