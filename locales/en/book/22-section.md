# 22. How to relax: entertainment venues and stress relief

The first half covers which money is wasted in entertainment venues and where the emergency exits are, measured in money and personal freedom. The second half covers how to reduce stress, measured in energy and all-cause mortality. The figures for the two halves are not converted into each other. Items 11 and 12 at the end of this section cover how to stay safe while having fun: do not go into hot springs or steam baths after drinking alcohol, and wear a helmet when skiing or cycling. The measure there is mortality. Section 3 already covered what to do first when your mood is low; here are more specific methods.

### 1. Before entering KTV, a bar or an escape room, check where the emergency exits are; if they are locked or blocked, go somewhere else
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. Look up for 10 seconds when you walk in.
- Plain language: By regulation, entertainment venues must keep evacuation routes and emergency exits clear, with no locks, blockages or obstacles, and signs must stay visible. Internet cafes follow the same rule. If a venue breaks these rules, you can report it. Pick another place if the room is underground, has one exit, or has a cluttered hallway.
- Benefit: This is a legal duty of the venue operator. Failing to meet it is illegal, and you can report it. The regulation states: "During business hours, an entertainment venue must keep its evacuation routes and emergency exits unobstructed. It may not block or lock them, nor place railings or other obstacles that hinder evacuation." It also requires that "clear, visible signs be placed at evacuation routes and emergency exits, and that they must not be blocked or covered." Internet cafes have the same duties: they must not "block or lock doors, windows, safety evacuation routes or emergency exits during business hours," and they are "prohibited from using open flames for lighting and from smoking."
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2006). 娱乐场所管理条例（国令第 458 号，第二十、二十一条）. <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-03/28/content_6297.htm>；国务院 (2002). 互联网上网服务营业场所管理条例（国令第 363 号，第二十一条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2002/content_61788.htm>
- Notes: China only: If any one of the following applies, go somewhere else: the room is underground, it has only one exit, the hallway is full of junk, or the exit sign light is off. In a fire these venues fill with smoke quickly, the corridors are narrow, the crowds are large, and most people have been drinking. You may have only seconds to get out. Under the regulation, the venue must post the reporting hotline on its warning signs.

### 2. Ask for the price list before ordering: venues must display prices clearly and may not charge you after the fact
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. You have to ask for the bill, which can feel awkward in front of others.
- Plain language: Venues must show you a price list and display prices clearly. Most "sky-high drink" disputes happen when no list was shown and the bill followed a verbal quote. Photograph the list, and ask for an itemised bill before you pay. If you dispute the total, call 12315, China's consumer complaint hotline, on the spot.
- Benefit: The regulation states: "Entertainment venues that provide entertainment services or sell goods must clearly display prices and present a price list to consumers. They may not force or deceive consumers into accepting services or buying goods." Venues that break price laws and regulations are penalised by the relevant authorities under the law. The usual "sky-high drink" dispute arises when no price list was shown and the bill was then based on a verbal quote. This last sentence reflects the author's experience; I found no official statistics on it.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2006). 娱乐场所管理条例（国令第 458 号，第二十九、五十三条）. <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-03/28/content_6297.htm>
- Notes: China only: How to do it: when you sit down, ask for a price list, paper or electronic, and photograph it as soon as you get it. After ordering, ask for a confirmed itemised list. Ask the price before ordering any "special" drink or fruit platter the staff recommend. If there is a dispute, call 12315 at once. Do not pay first and then argue: once you have paid, it is much harder to prove what you were told.

### 3. If someone offers you "something" at the venue, leave at once; supplying drugs or letting others use them in your room is not "helping a friend"
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Stand up and walk out. The hard part is leaving in front of the whole table.
- Plain language: Venues may not sell or supply drugs, or let people use them on the premises. Letting someone use drugs in a room you booked or in your home can be a crime. If someone produces unknown powder, pills or vape cartridges and asks you to try them, leave immediately.
- Benefit: The regulation explicitly bans entertainment venues from "selling or supplying drugs, or organising, forcing, instigating, luring, deceiving or harbouring others to take or inject drugs." Staff may not use drugs or help customers do any of the above. Violators have their illegal gains confiscated by the public security authorities and are ordered to suspend operations for three to six months. In serious cases the entertainment licence is revoked. The regulation also requires venues to display anti-drug, anti-gambling and anti-prostitution warning signs in prominent places in lobbies, private rooms and booths, with a reporting phone number.

For individuals, criminal law provides: "Whoever harbours others to smoke or inject drugs shall be sentenced to up to three years' fixed-term imprisonment, criminal detention or public surveillance, and may also be fined." The Supreme People's Court's judicial interpretation sets out when this becomes a crime: harbouring several people at once, repeated harbouring within two years, harbouring minors, and harbouring for profit. Conduct that does not reach the level of a crime is punished under the public security administration punishment law: detention of 10 to 15 days, and possibly a fine of up to RMB 3,000. In less serious cases, detention is up to 5 days or a fine of up to RMB 1,000. Taking or injecting drugs yourself also carries 10 to 15 days' detention and possibly a fine of up to RMB 3,000.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2006). 娱乐场所管理条例（国令第 458 号，第十四、三十、四十二条）. <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-03/28/content_6297.htm>；全国人大 (1997). 刑法（第三百五十四条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>；最高人民法院 (2016). 关于审理毒品犯罪案件适用法律若干问题的解释（法释〔2016〕8 号，第十二条）. <https://www.sc.jcy.gov.cn/zlxx/sfjs/201606/t20160617_1809560.shtml>（四川省人民检察院转载）；全国人大常委会 (2025). 治安管理处罚法（2025 年修订，第八十四、八十五条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>
- Notes: China only: If someone pulls out unknown powder, pills or vape cartridges, leave when they do; do not stay to watch. Your personal risk is greater than the venue's. Taking drugs yourself is already punishable under public security law. Letting a friend "use something" in a room you booked or in your own home could count as harbouring drug users.

### 4. Do not eat sweets or snacks from strangers, do not drink from a glass that has been out of sight, and do not take vape cartridges from others
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Refuse anything strangers hand you. If your drink has been out of your sight, get a new one.
- Plain language: Modern drugs often do not look like drugs. Some vapes laced with synthetic cannabinoids look almost identical to ordinary ones, and dealers hand them out in bars, KTV venues and hotels. Tricking someone into smoking one is illegal. Get a fresh drink if yours has been out of sight.
- Benefit: Modern drugs often do not look like drugs. Synthetic cannabinoids have been controlled as a whole class since 1 July 2021, so the entire category is treated as drugs. Some people mix them into vape liquid to make "high-inducing" e-cigarettes. In a case reported by the Supreme People's Procuratorate, the investigating prosecutor described the liquid as one that "can be turned into any colour or flavour by adding food colouring" and that "looks essentially the same as an ordinary e-cigarette." Sellers bring in others to pitch them in bars, KTV venues and hotels, luring people to try them. In that report, minors made up nearly 40 per cent of the local users in cases handled that year. Etomidate has been listed as a Class II psychotropic drug since 1 October 2023.

Tricking someone into taking drugs is itself a violation: whoever lures, instigates, deceives or forces another person to take or inject drugs faces 10 to 15 days' detention and a fine of RMB 1,000 to 5,000. If it is a crime, the sentence is up to 3 years; in serious cases, 3 to 7 years; for forcing someone, 3 to 10 years; and harsher penalties apply when minors are targeted. People who take or inject drugs themselves face 10 to 15 days' detention and possibly a fine of up to RMB 3,000. In less serious cases, detention is up to 5 days or a fine of up to RMB 1,000. Those who were forced or tricked into taking drugs may receive lighter, reduced or no penalties under the same law. They may also be ordered not to enter entertainment venues for 6 months to 1 year. Addicts are ordered into community-based detox for 3 years. Those who refuse community detox, or who use drugs again during it, are placed in compulsory isolation detox for 2 years, extendable by up to 1 more year (nationwide; public security administration punishment law, since January 2026).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 最高人民检察院 (2021). 江苏苏州吴江区：办案检察官揭秘披着潮流外衣的新型毒品. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202110/t20211011_531831.shtml>；国家药监局、公安部、国家卫生健康委 (2023). 关于调整麻醉药品和精神药品目录的公告（2023 年第 120 号）. <https://yjj.sh.gov.cn/qtgzwj/20230911/3b612c94e35b4b63abba314c972a2593.html>（上海市药监局转载，原文页 nmpa.gov.cn 在本机返回 412）；全国人大常委会 (2025). 治安管理处罚法（2025 年修订，第二十、八十四、八十五条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>；全国人大 (1997). 刑法（第三百五十三条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>；全国人大常委会 (2007). 禁毒法（第三十三、三十八、四十七条）. <http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2520706.html>（广东省人民政府转载）
- Notes: China only: This is not only about entertainment venues; school gates, private gatherings and long-distance coaches are the same. It makes no difference whether what is offered is sweets, drinks or vapes. Being drugged is often followed by robbery, sexual assault and extortion. There are no official public statistics on this; it is an experience-based judgement. Swapping a drink the moment it leaves your sight is far easier than investigating afterwards. For what to do if someone produces unknown powder, pills or vapes at a venue, see item 3 of this section. For carrying things for someone else, see item 29 of section 8.

### 5. Avoid script-murder and escape-room venues located in residential buildings or below the second basement level
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. Ask the address and floor before booking.
- Plain language: A 2022 national notice bans script-murder venues from residential buildings and from basements deeper than the first basement level. Such venues are often dim and narrow, and blocked exits make fire escape hard. Check the floor and address before booking, and choose another venue if it does not comply.
- Benefit: The 2022 notice is specific: script-murder venues "may not be located in residential buildings, or below the first basement floor of a building (not including the first basement floor itself), among other places." The notice also requires venues to carry out the safety production law and fire protection law, and to "regularly carry out self-identification, self-inspection and self-correction of fire risks, improving their ability to organise evacuation and fight early-stage fires in emergencies." The same notice requires age-appropriate warnings for scripts. Venues whose content is unsuitable for minors may not admit them. Outside public holidays, rest days and winter and summer holidays, venues may not offer script-murder activities to minors. At the end of 2021, the fire and culture-tourism authorities of Qingpu District, Shanghai, inspected 13 such venues. Their report noted that these venues are typically dim with narrow passages, and have problems such as flammable decoration and locked or blocked evacuation routes.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 文化和旅游部、公安部、住房和城乡建设部、应急管理部、市场监管总局 (2022). 关于加强剧本娱乐经营场所管理的通知. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-06/27/content_5698021.htm>；上海市青浦区文化和旅游局 (2022-01-10). 关于我区「密室逃脱」类场所消防安全专项检查的情况通报. <https://www.shqp.gov.cn/tv/wlzwgk/ml/yw/20220217/913272.html>
- Notes: China only: The Qingpu report also says some operators make rooms deliberately maze-like for atmosphere, and hide evacuation signs and emergency lights. If something goes wrong, people cannot find an exit quickly. Before booking, the review page will show you the floor and whether the building is residential. If it does not comply, switch venues without hesitation.

### 6. If your mood sinks to the level of depression, go walking or running; the effect of exercise grows with its intensity
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=high gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. About half an hour each time. The hard part is that when you feel low you least want to move, so you have to push yourself.
- Plain language: People with severe depression benefit most from brisk walking or jogging, then yoga, strength training and tai chi or qigong, which differ little from each other. Higher intensity tends to work better. The evidence is low quality, but moving beats not moving, so pick something you can keep doing.
- Benefit: This pools 218 randomised controlled trials, covering 495 arms and 14,170 participants who met clinical criteria for major depression. A Bayesian network meta-analysis compared the exercise types with each other. The comparators were usual care and placebo pills (active control). Effect size is Hedges' g; more negative numbers mean greater improvement in symptoms. Brisk walking or jogging: −0.62 (95% credible interval −0.80 to −0.45, meaning the true value roughly lies in this range). Yoga: −0.55 (−0.73 to −0.36). Strength training: −0.49 (−0.69 to −0.29). Mixed aerobic exercise: −0.43 (−0.61 to −0.24). Tai chi or qigong: −0.42 (−0.65 to −0.21). The source says "the effect of exercise is proportional to the prescribed intensity": higher intensity gives better results. Strength training and yoga had the best acceptability and are the easiest to keep up.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Noetel M, Sanders T, Gallardo-Gómez D, et al. (2024). Effect of exercise for depression: systematic review and network meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials. BMJ, 384, e075847. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-075847>
- Notes: Disputed. The authors themselves note that only 1 of the 218 studies was clean enough not to be easily skewed by study design. By the common grading method, brisk walking or jogging is rated only "low" certainty, and the rest "very low." Exercise also cannot be hidden from participants: people know they are exercising, and the expectation that they will feel better cannot be removed. The direction is still usable: moving beats not moving, do not go too gently, and choose something you can keep up. This expands on section 3's "start moving first when your mood is low," and does not replace standard treatment after a diagnosis is confirmed.

### 7. When anxiety affects your life, a mindfulness stress-reduction course is no worse than a common anti-anxiety drug
<!-- tags: money=low time=high will=high gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: Paying for the course costs some money. The course runs for 8 weeks, with one class a week, and you also practice at home every day. The hard part is practicing every day for 8 weeks in a row.
- Plain language: Adults diagnosed with an anxiety disorder who took an 8-week mindfulness course improved about as much as those on escitalopram. Drug users: 78.6% had at least one side effect, and 10 quit because of them. Course users: 15.4% had side effects, and no one quit. It must be the standard 8-week program, not any meditation app.
- Benefit: This is a US trial run at 3 academic medical centers in cities. It was designed to see whether the course is no worse than medication (a non-inferiority randomized controlled trial). 276 adults diagnosed with an anxiety disorder were randomly split 1:1 into two groups. One group took the 8-week mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR) course. The other took escitalopram (flexible dose of 10 to 20 mg). 208 people completed the full study. At the end, scores on the clinician-rated Clinical Global Impression–Severity scale fell by 1.35 in the mindfulness group and by 1.43 in the drug group. The difference between the groups was −0.07 (95% CI −0.38 to 0.23, meaning the true difference likely falls within this range; P=0.65, meaning the gap cannot be told apart from no difference). The lower end of this range did not cross the pre-set "not clearly worse" line (non-inferiority margin of −0.495). Side effects differed a lot. In the drug group, 78.6% had at least one trial-related adverse event, and 10 people dropped out because of them. In the mindfulness group, it was 15.4%, and no one dropped out because of side effects.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Hoge EA, Bui E, Mete M, Dutton MA, Baker AW, Simon NM (2023). Mindfulness-based stress reduction vs escitalopram for the treatment of adults with anxiety disorders: a randomized clinical trial. JAMA Psychiatry, 80(1), 13-21. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2022.3679>
- Notes: The trial used the standard 8-week MBSR course, and an ordinary meditation app cannot be treated as the same thing. It applies to people who already meet the diagnostic criteria for an anxiety disorder, so you should still see a doctor. If you have been putting off treatment because of worries about drug side effects, there is another option with comparable strength of evidence.

### 8. To calm down on the spot, use a 5-minute "cyclic sigh": inhale in two stages, exhale long
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. Five minutes a day.
- Plain language: Five minutes of daily breathing practice for a month improved mood and slowed breathing more than the same time in mindfulness meditation. The cyclic sigh, which lengthens the exhale, worked best. Breathe in through the nose, take a short extra sip to fill the lungs, then exhale slowly through the mouth. Only one trial supports this, but it is free.
- Benefit: This is a home-based, randomized controlled trial comparing three breathing methods, done for 5 minutes a day, with the same time spent in mindfulness meditation. The trial ran for 1 month. The three breathing methods were: the cyclic sigh (emphasizing a longer exhale), box breathing (equal inhale, hold and exhale), and cyclic hyperventilation with breath-holds (long inhale, short exhale). Pooling each person's data across sessions (mixed-effects model), all the breathing practices beat mindfulness meditation. The cyclic sigh, which focuses on the exhale, did best. The breathing practices improved mood (P<0.05, meaning this difference is unlikely to be chance) and lowered breathing rate (P<0.05).
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Balban MY, Neri E, Kogon MM, et al. (2023). Brief structured respiration practices enhance mood and reduce physiological arousal. Cell Reports Medicine, 4(1), 100895. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100895>
- Notes: Method: inhale through the nose, take a short extra sip to fill the lungs, then slowly exhale fully and long through the mouth. Repeat for 5 minutes. There is only one trial. It was done at home, participants judged the results by filling in questionnaires, and the number of participants was not large. The effect is only slightly better than meditation and does not solve the problem. But it costs nothing, takes only 5 minutes, and can be done anywhere, so it is good value.

### 9. Treat regular time with people as a health expense, not something you only do when you feel bad
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=some gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: It costs some money and time spent meeting people. The hard part is reaching out to people even when you are in a good mood.
- Plain language: People who rarely see others have about a 30% higher chance of dying during the study period. Lonely people have about 25% higher, and people living alone about 30% higher, with a stronger effect under age 65. These findings come from follow-up observation, and poor health may also make people more likely to live alone.
- Benefit: This pools 70 prospective studies, with 3.4 million participants followed for an average of 7 years (a meta-analysis). Social isolation, loneliness and living alone were each linked to higher mortality. After adjusting for confounders such as age and health status, the pooled odds ratios were: social isolation 1.29, loneliness 1.26, living alone 1.32. Converted, that means the chance of dying in the same period is about 30% higher, about 25% higher, and about 30% higher, respectively. The three effects were similar in size, and they were larger in people under 65 than in older people.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Holt-Lunstad J, Smith TB, Baker M, Harris T, Stephenson D (2015). Loneliness and social isolation as risk factors for mortality: a meta-analytic review. Perspectives on Psychological Science, 10(2), 227-237. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691614568352>
- Notes: Disputed. These are associations seen by following populations, not randomized controlled trials, so cause and effect cannot be separated. People in poor health are more likely to live alone and socialize less. The authors treat social relationships as a health factor on a par with smoking and obesity, but no randomized trial proves that more socializing lowers mortality. This does not conflict with section 3, "Reduce the relationships that drain you." That section says to cut relationships that wear you down, while this point is about keeping the few that restore your energy.

### 10. When choosing a home, count nearby green space, but don't expect "going to the park more" to extend your life
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=low scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. Check the greenery around the place while you view homes.
- Plain language: Greener surroundings within 500 meters of your home go with a lower chance of dying, about 4% lower for each 0.1 rise in the vegetation index. This measures the greenery where you live, not how often you visit parks. Green areas often have wealthier residents and cleaner air. When choosing a home, just count nearby green space.
- Benefit: This pools 9 cohort studies that followed people for years (a cohort meta-analysis), covering 8.32 million people in 7 countries. It looks at greenery within 500 meters of home, measured with the normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI, a number that converts ground greenness: bare ground and concrete are near 0, dense vegetation near 1). For each 0.1 increase in NDVI, the pooled risk ratio for all-cause mortality was 0.96 (95% CI 0.94–0.97). That means the chance of dying in the same period is about 4% lower, with the true value likely between 3% and 6% lower. Seven of the 9 studies found that more greenery meant lower mortality, and this pattern is unlikely to be a fluke. The other 2 studies found no association.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Rojas-Rueda D, Nieuwenhuijsen MJ, Gascon M, Perez-Leon D, Mudu P (2019). Green spaces and mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of cohort studies. The Lancet Planetary Health, 3(11), e469-e477. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2542-5196(19)30215-3>
- Notes: Disputed. Results differ widely across the studies (I²=95%; the higher this value, the less the studies agree with one another). The measure covers greenery around where you live, not how often you go to parks. People who live in green areas also tend to be wealthier, breathe cleaner air and exercise more, and these factors cannot be separated from the effect of greenery itself. Do not read this as "going to the park extends your life." What is usable is to count green space when choosing a home, which costs nothing. For NDVI, bare ground and concrete are close to 0 and dense vegetation close to 1, so a change of "0.1" roughly corresponds to moving from a bare block to one with rows of street trees.

### 11. After drinking, don't soak in hot springs or use a sauna, and don't take a hot bath when you get home
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=health -->
- Cost: Free. If you have been drinking, go another day, and just rinse off in a shower when you get home. The hard part is that hot springs and saunas often come as the next stop after dinner, with the whole table going, so you have to say no out loud.
- Plain language: In Finland, about half of the people who died in saunas had been drinking. In Tokyo, about a quarter of autopsied bath-related deaths had blood alcohol above 0.5 mg per milliliter, and alcohol was the top factor in bathtub drownings. Do not bathe after drinking, and do not leave a drunk companion alone in the sauna.
- Benefit: Finland checked each sauna death from 1990 to 2002 one by one, using police and forensic reports. Such deaths happen less than 2 in 100,000 per year. The paper says, "overall, 50% of the people were under the influence of alcohol." The authors say the alcohol risk is growing, and prevention should focus on drinking less and not leaving a drunk person alone in the sauna. Tokyo forensic examiners handled 3,289 bath-related deaths from 2009 to 2011. Among the 550 with autopsies, 140 had blood alcohol above 0.5 mg per milliliter, about a quarter. Another Tokyo autopsy record covered 77 bathtub drownings. Among the contributing factors, alcohol was the most common (25 cases), followed by heart disease (22 cases). A December 2025 reminder from Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency said that in 2024, 7,776 people in Japan drowned in bathtubs, and 7,363 of them were 65 or older. Its accident-prevention key points say to avoid bathing right after eating, after drinking, or after taking medicine. The Chinese Embassy in Japan reminded that "bathing after drinking is inadvisable, because it can easily trigger a heart or vascular emergency." A Shaanxi Provincial Health Commission science article says that soaking in a hot spring after drinking can dilate peripheral blood vessels in the high water temperature, which may cause low blood pressure, low blood sugar, and even fainting.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Kenttämies A, Karkola K (2008). Death in sauna. Journal of Forensic Sciences, 53(3), 724-729. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1556-4029.2008.00703.x>；Suzuki H, Hikiji W, Tanifuji T, Abe N, Fukunaga T (2015). Characteristics of sudden bath-related death investigated by medical examiners in Tokyo, Japan. Journal of Epidemiology, 25(2), 126-132. <https://doi.org/10.2188/jea.JE20140068>；Mishima S, Suzuki H, Fukunaga T, Nishitani Y (2018). Postmortem computed tomography findings in cases of bath-related death: applicability and limitation in forensic practice. Forensic Science International, 282, 195-203. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.forsciint.2017.11.030>；消費者庁 (2025). 年末年始、高齢者の事故に注意しましょう!：「食後すぐの入浴や、飲酒後、医薬品服用後の入浴は避けましょう」. <https://www.caa.go.jp/policies/policy/consumer_safety/caution/caution_084/>；中华人民共和国驻日本国大使馆 (2015). 领事提醒之十七：「空腹、满腹、酒后不宜入浴。酒后入浴易引发心脏或血管系统急病」. <https://jp.china-embassy.gov.cn/chn/jbwzlmnew/lszcnew/LSBHYXZ/LSBHLBZN/201503/t20150327_9475462.htm>；陕西省卫生健康委员会 (2021). 温泉泡对了才养生：「切忌酒后或空腹泡温泉，在高水温的刺激下容易引起外周血管扩张出现低血压、低血糖、甚至晕厥」. <https://sxwjw.shaanxi.gov.cn/ywgz/gbbj/202101/t20210111_2148844.html>
- Notes: Rated B because these studies only looked at people who died. How many people who drank and then bathed ran into trouble was never counted, so we cannot say how many times higher the risk is. Rating the benefit as "medium" is a judgment call: few people die in saunas or bathtubs, but a quarter to half of those who died had been drinking. Bathtub deaths in Japan are mostly among older people, but young people who have been drinking can also get into trouble in a sauna. For the benefits of regular bathing and the reminders about water temperature and duration, see item 36 of section 2 (bathe if you can). ### 12. Wear a helmet when skiing, snowboarding, inline skating or mountain biking
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no gain=high scope=health -->
- Cost: A helmet costs a few hundred yuan. Putting it on and fastening the strap takes a few seconds each time.
- Plain language: Wearing a helmet while skiing or snowboarding lowers the risk of head injury by about 35%, and by 59% in studies that included children under 13. When cycling, it lowers serious head injury by 69% and fatal head injury by 65%. Helmets did not raise the risk of neck injury.
- Benefit: The skiing and snowboarding figures come from a pooled analysis of 12 studies. Most compared people with head injuries against people without, by whether they wore a helmet (case-control studies). Helmet wearers had an odds ratio (OR) of 0.65 (95% CI 0.55–0.79) for head injury, about 35% lower. In studies that included children under 13, the OR was 0.41 (0.27–0.59), about 59% lower. The neck injury OR was 0.89 (0.72–1.09), so there was no increase. The cycling figures come from 40 studies covering more than 64,000 people who fell or crashed. Head injury OR 0.49 (0.42–0.57), about 51% lower. Severe head injury OR 0.31 (0.25–0.37), about 69% lower. Fatal head injury OR 0.35 (0.14–0.88), about 65% lower. Facial injury OR 0.67 (0.56–0.81), about 33% lower. Neck injury OR 0.96 (0.74–1.25), with no association found. The Beijing Municipal Sports Bureau said that after the 2017 revision of Beijing's local standard for ski areas, skiers must wear helmets to enter advanced slopes.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Russell K, Christie J, Hagel BE (2010). The effect of helmets on the risk of head and neck injuries among skiers and snowboarders: a meta-analysis. CMAJ, 182(4), 333-340. <https://doi.org/10.1503/cmaj.091080>；Olivier J, Creighton P (2017). Bicycle injuries and helmet use: a systematic review and meta-analysis. International Journal of Epidemiology, 46(1), 278-292. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyw153>；北京市体育局. 北京市政协十三届四次会议第1276号提案的答复意见：「明确要求滑雪者必须佩戴头盔方能进入高级道滑雪区域」. <https://tyj.beijing.gov.cn/bjsports/zfxxgk_/1421305/jytablgz/11180211/index.html>
- Notes: These figures measure the chance of head injury once you have fallen or crashed. A helmet will not stop you from falling. No pooled studies were found for inline skating or skateboarding, so those activities are inferred from similar falls and head impacts. Make sure the helmet fits your head, fasten the straps snugly, and try on rented helmets first. For motorcycles and e-bikes, see item 2 of section 1 (wear a helmet).
