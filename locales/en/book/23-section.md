# 23. Which skills are worth learning

This section counts money and time. Item 2 also counts mortality. The first six items answer one question: should you keep studying at all? Item 1 starts with what the law rules out. Item 2 works out the link between schooling and lifespan. Item 3 shows where the whole population sits across education levels. Item 4 covers the aid available when you cannot afford school. Item 5 covers the options if you did not get into general senior high school. Item 6 turns "study or work" into one calculation and gives you a method to run it yourself. The last seven items answer another question: once you have decided to learn something, how to choose. Chinese wage statistics by occupation are hard to verify item by item from public official sources. So this section covers only the few points that can be traced to a source: roughly how much one more year of school adds to pay (the return to education), how to check whether a certificate is legitimate, where training subsidies come from, and which skills are harder to replace by automation. This book does not give figures for what a particular job pays per month. Items 14 to 19 answer a third question: once you have decided what to learn, how to learn it in less time. These six items count time, not money. Items 20 to 23 answer a fourth question: if you already work in a professional technical role, where to file for a professional title, how to take the exam, what pitfalls to avoid, and how much the title is worth once awarded. Items 24 and 25 answer whether to go on to a master's after an undergraduate degree, and whether to go on to a doctorate after a master's. No domestic income figure adjusted for individual differences was found, so the research cited is British.

The items in this section are grouped by theme below; item numbers are in brackets.

**Whether to keep studying**: under 16 you cannot take a job (item 1), whether schooling pays off in mortality terms (item 2), the national education structure (item 3), working out the policy aid first when money is short (item 4), the route if you did not get into general senior high school (item 5), and "study or work" as one calculation (item 6).

**What to learn and how to choose**: the return on one more year of schooling (item 7), checking the occupational qualification catalogue before studying for a certificate (item 8), taking training through government subsidy channels (item 9), choosing skills that need hands-on work and on-site judgment (item 10), checking whether the skill has a graded assessment (item 11), checking the local list of in-demand occupations (item 12), and choosing short programs that qualify you for work directly (item 13).

**How to learn with less time**: test yourself after closing the book (item 14), spread study over several days (item 15), do not make highlighting your main method (item 16), mix question types in practice (item 17), do not pick a study method based on whether you are a visual or auditory learner (item 18), and write questions for what you need to memorize and quiz yourself (item 19).

**Professional titles**: where to file for a professional title (item 20), junior and intermediate accounting titles depend on the national unified exam (item 21), do not use agents to have your title assessed for you (item 22), and getting the title does not mean a pay rise (item 23).

**Graduate school**: whether to go on to a master's after an undergraduate degree (item 24), and whether to go on to a doctorate after a master's (item 25).

### 1. Under 16 there is no "going to work" option: an employer that hires you faces a 5,000 yuan monthly fine, so any employer willing to hire you is running illegal child labor
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=freedom -->
- Cost: Nothing to pay. Spend a minute working out how old you are.
- Plain language: A 15- or 16-year-old who just finished junior high is right at this line. Under 16, apart from special arts or sports posts, any employer that hires you is breaking the law and faces a 5,000 yuan monthly fine per child. If you are hurt at such a job, that employer must pay for your treatment.
- Benefit: Compulsory Education Law, Article 11: "Every child who has reached six years of age shall be sent by his or her parents or other legal guardians to school to receive and complete compulsory education." Article 14: "Employers are prohibited from hiring children and adolescents of compulsory-education age." Article 58: a person who, without legitimate reason, does not send a school-age child to school as the law requires may be "criticized and educated and ordered to correct within a time limit" by the local township government or the county-level education administration. Provisions on the Prohibition of Child Labor, Article 2: state organs, social organizations, enterprises and institutions, non-profit private organizations and individually owned businesses "may not hire minors under 16 years old." Article 6: "a fine of 5,000 yuan per child laborer employed per month"; if not corrected by the deadline, the fine is 10,000 yuan per person per month. Article 7: anyone who introduces a child into employment is fined "5,000 yuan for each person introduced." Article 10: if a child laborer falls ill or is injured, the employer "must send them to a medical institution for treatment and bear all medical and living expenses during treatment"; in cases of disability or death, a one-time compensation payment is also due. Article 13 has one exception: arts and sports units, with parental or guardian consent, may hire professional performers and athletes under 16 (national).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2018). 义务教育法（2018 年第二次修正，第十一、十四、五十八条）. <http://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_sjzl/sjzl_zcfg/zcfg_jyfl/202110/t20211029_575949.html>（教育部刊登）；国务院 (2002). 禁止使用童工规定（国务院令第 364 号，第二、六、七、十、十三条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2002/content_61798.htm>
- Notes: China only: The beneficiary is you. Working after 16 is legal. If you want to learn a trade before starting work, run it through the dimensions in item 10 of this section (skills that resist automation) first. Overtime pay, annual leave and work-injury benefits covered in section 19 all depend on having an employment relationship and evidence of it. If a child laborer is injured, under Article 10 of the Provisions on the Prohibition of Child Labor the employer bears all treatment and living costs during treatment, and pays a one-time compensation in cases of disability or death.

### 2. Count "does schooling pay off" in the mortality ledger too: each extra year of education cuts adult death risk by about 1.9%
<!-- tags: money=0 time=high will=high gain=low scope=health -->
- Cost: Nothing to pay in money. The cost is the time and effort of the extra years, and the hard part is staying in school to the end without giving up. For tuition, see item 4 of this section (vocational school fees and grants).
- Plain language: Each extra year of school is linked to an average 1.9% lower risk of adult death. The effect is clearest for ages 18 to 49, where each extra year lowers risk by 2.9%, and only 0.8% above 70. People who stay in school longer often start with better conditions, so how much is due to schooling itself is unclear.
- Benefit: Earlier studies were pooled and reanalyzed together (systematic review and meta-analysis). The search covered January 1980 to May 2023 and included 603 studies, 59 countries, 70 sites and 10,355 observations. Education and adult mortality (deaths from all causes combined) show a dose-response relationship: the more schooling, the greater the reduction. Each extra year of education was linked to an average 1.9% lower death risk (95% uncertainty interval 1.8–2.0, meaning the true value probably falls within this range). For people aged 18 to 49, each extra year lowered risk by 2.9% (2.8–3.0). For people over 70 the reduction was 0.8% (0.6–1.0). No difference was found between men and women. Dividing countries by development level (socio-demographic index) also showed no difference.
- Evidence level: A (disputed)
- Sources: IHME-CHAIN Collaborators (2024). Effects of education on adult mortality: a global systematic review and meta-analysis. The Lancet Public Health, 9(3), e155-e165. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(23)00306-7>
- Notes: Disputed. The authors themselves report publication bias (p<0.0001), meaning studies with favorable results are more likely to be published. Results also vary widely between studies. All included studies are observational follow-ups, not trials that assigned people to groups. Years of schooling are tangled up with family circumstances, region and occupation. The research cannot separate how much of the 1.9% comes from schooling itself and how much from people who stay in school longer tending to start with better conditions. Causation may also run the other way: children with illness or mental health problems are more likely to drop out and more likely to die early, which would make the effect of schooling look larger than it is. So the evidence strongly suggests that more schooling and longer life move together, but it does not mean that going to school will extend your life by 1.9%. The size of the benefit is classed as small. Readers usually face three or four more years of schooling, and at 1.9% per year that adds up to less than 10%, so by this book's own threshold it counts as small. The beneficiary is you.

### 3. Before judging whether degrees are losing value, look at the national education mix: only 15,467 per 100,000 people have a college-level education (2020 census; about 19% in the 2025 sample estimate)
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Nothing to pay. Just look at the figures below.
- Plain language: The 2020 census found about 15% of Chinese people had at least a junior college education. The 2025 sample survey estimates about 19%. In both, junior high was the most common level. People aged 15 and over averaged 9.91 years of schooling, just past junior high, so junior high is common, not rare.
- Benefit: The reference time for the Seventh National Population Census was 1 November 2020. Of the national population, 218,360,767 people had a college-level education (junior college and above), 213,005,258 had high school education (including secondary specialized school), 487,163,489 had junior high education, and 349,658,828 had primary education. Compared with the Sixth National Census in 2010, the number per 100,000 people with a college-level education rose from 8,930 to 15,467; high school rose from 14,032 to 15,088; junior high fell from 38,788 to 34,507; primary fell from 26,779 to 24,767. Average years of schooling for people aged 15 and over rose from 9.08 to 9.91. The illiteracy rate fell from 4.08% to 2.67%. The reference time for the 2025 national 1% population sample survey was 1 November 2025, with a sample of 20.52 million people. Scaled up from the sample, the national population is 1,405.45 million. Of these, 272.33 million have a college-level education (junior college and above), 242.72 million high school (including secondary specialized), 452.83 million junior high and 331.22 million primary. The bulletin gives no figures per 100,000 people. Calculated from the total population, college-level is about 19.4% and junior high about 32.2%, still the largest group.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国家统计局、国务院第七次全国人口普查领导小组办公室 (2021). 第七次全国人口普查公报（第六号）——人口受教育情况. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/tjgb/rkpcgb/qgrkpcgb/202302/t20230206_1902006.html>；国家统计局 (2026). 2025 年全国 1% 人口抽样调查主要数据公报. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202605/t20260522_1963788.html>
- Notes: These figures only show how scarce a degree is across the population. Whether a particular person's schooling pays off has to be worked out with the method in item 6 of this section (study versus work). Note also that census figures cover the whole population, including the elderly and children. They are not the workforce, and they are not your peer group. The bulletin does not break the figures down by age. Going from 2010 to 2020, the number per 100,000 with a college-level education rose by more than 70%, so the rate among young people is probably higher than the national figure. That is an inference. Comparing yourself with the national figure therefore overstates your advantage. What the figures do show is that, across the whole population, a college education is still a minority. The 2025 figure is a sample estimate, while the census counts every household, so the two use different methods. That is why the two sets are presented side by side and the new figures do not replace the old ones. The 19.4% and 32.2% were calculated from the bulletin's headcounts and total population. The beneficiary is you.

### 4. Work out the policy numbers before saying you cannot afford it: public vocational tuition is mostly waived for rural and low-income students, grants are 2,300 yuan, and student loans go up to 20,000 yuan a year
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Nothing to pay. Ask the school's student financial aid office once before term starts, and submit the paperwork once.
- Plain language: Public vocational students who are rural, in urban farming-related majors, or from low-income families are usually tuition-free. Vocational and senior high grants are 2,300 yuan a year from spring 2025. University grants are 3,700 yuan, and loans cover up to 20,000 yuan a year. If money is tight, check these before deciding to quit and work.
- Benefit: Tuition waivers for public secondary vocational schools began in the autumn 2012 term. The original text reads: tuition is waived for "all full-time registered students in years one to three at public secondary vocational schools who are rural (including county towns), in urban farming-related majors, or from families with financial hardship (except performing arts majors)." The aid system was adjusted in 2024. The national grant for undergraduate and junior college students rose from 3,300 yuan to 3,700 yuan per student per year. The national Encouragement Scholarship rose from 5,000 yuan to 6,000 yuan. Both took effect from autumn 2024 (the Encouragement Scholarship from 2024). The national grants for secondary vocational schools and for senior high schools rose from 2,000 yuan to 2,300 yuan per student per year, from spring 2025. National student loans were also adjusted from autumn 2024. The annual loan cap for undergraduate and junior college students rose from "no more than 16,000 yuan" to "no more than 20,000 yuan." For postgraduates it rose from "no more than 20,000 yuan" to "no more than 25,000 yuan." The interest rate is the same-term, same-grade loan prime rate (LPR) minus 70 basis points (0.7 percentage points lower) (national).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 财政部、发展改革委、教育部、人力资源社会保障部 (2012). 关于扩大中等职业教育免学费政策范围 进一步完善国家助学金制度的意见（财教〔2012〕376 号）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2013/content_2332779.htm>；财政部、教育部、人力资源社会保障部有关司局负责人 (2024). 就调整学生资助政策有关问题答记者问. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202410/content_6983672.htm>；财政部、教育部、人民银行、金融监管总局 (2024). 关于调整完善国家助学贷款有关政策的通知（财教〔2024〕188 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202411/content_6984812.htm>
- Notes: China only: The beneficiaries are you and your children. Which groups receive tuition waivers and how the grants are split into tiers are set by each province. Private schools are subsidized at the rate of comparable public-school majors locally, so any gap is still yours to pay. Check the local documents before enrolling. A student loan is a loan, not a grant, and must be repaid after graduation. But it separates "cannot pay the tuition now" from "is this worth it over the next few years," so do not treat a shortage of money as a final answer. Section 7 covers training subsidies and temporary hardship aid during unemployment. If your household has truly lost its income, apply for those first.

### 5. Not getting into a general senior high school does not close the road: vocational schools have linked admissions and separate exams, and public-sector skilled-job recruitment can lower degree requirements
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Nothing to pay. Before you fill in your application choices, check your province's vocational admissions platform once.
- Plain language: The 2022 Vocational Education Law gives vocational education equal standing with general education. Vocational schools can link admissions to vocational colleges, and those colleges must reserve places or hold separate exams for vocational graduates. Employers cannot block vocational graduates from fair hiring. Missing the general high school cutoff means taking another route, not stopping at junior high.
- Benefit: Vocational Education Law (in force 1 May 2022), Article 3: "Vocational education is a type of education of equal importance to general education." Article 37: secondary vocational schools "may, in accordance with state regulations, run linked admissions and training with higher vocational schools in relevant majors." Higher vocational schools "may, in accordance with state regulations, admit students through assessments that combine cultural literacy with vocational skills." Education authorities at provincial level and above must set up a unified vocational admissions platform that publishes school, major and enrollment information and provides search and application services. Article 53: "Vocational school students have equal opportunities with students of comparable general schools in further education, employment and career development." Higher vocational schools, and general universities that offer vocational education, "shall set a corresponding proportion in their enrollment plans or adopt separate examinations to admit vocational school graduates." "Employers may not set application, recruitment or employment conditions that hinder the equal employment and fair competition of vocational school graduates." In public-sector recruitment, posts with a vocational skill-level requirement "may appropriately lower education requirements" (national).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2022). 职业教育法（2022 年修订，第三、三十七、五十三条）. <http://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_sjzl/sjzl_zcfg/zcfg_jyfl/202204/t20220421_620064.html>（教育部刊登）
- Notes: China only: The beneficiaries are you and your children. These are legal routes, not guaranteed admission. Which majors are in linked programs, what share of places is reserved for separate exams, and which subjects the vocational entrance exam covers are set by each province. Check your own province's current vocational admissions platform and enrollment plan before choosing, and do not rely on another province's experience. When choosing a vocational major, run it through the dimensions in item 10 of this section (skills that resist automation): majors that require on-site work in non-standard environments are harder to replace. When choosing a school, check whether it is in your province's current vocational enrollment plan. Verify any certificate it issues using the three steps in item 8 of this section. Avoid any enrollment advertising that promises job placement or guaranteed passing.

### 6. Treat "study or work" as one calculation: compare the three years of wages you would earn by working now with the yearly income gap that more schooling brings over decades
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Nothing to pay. Sit down and work it through once; it takes about half an hour.
- Plain language: Working now gives a few years of early wages, paid only once. Studying on adds a bit more income every year for 30 to 40 years, so compare the two side by side. In urban China, each extra year of schooling was linked to about 4.0% to 10.2% more income, but the data only run to 2001.
- Benefit: This is an estimate for urban China from 1988 to 2001. The amount by which schooling raises income is called the return to education. The original text reads: it "rose sharply from 4.0% per year of schooling in 1988 to 10.2% in 2001." Most of the increase came after 1992. It reflects the growing gap between people who went to university and those who did not (the higher-education wage premium). This is a China-specific scale that can be used to convert "one more year of school" into an annual income difference. For the global average, see item 7 of this section (the global average return to one more year of schooling).
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Zhang J, Zhao Y, Park A, Song X (2005). Economic returns to schooling in urban China, 1988 to 2001. Journal of Comparative Economics, 33(4), 730-752. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jce.2005.05.008>
- Notes: Before running the numbers, take off the tuition waivers and grants in item 4 of this section, and also account for the mortality effect in item 2. The data end in 2001 and cover only urban areas, so they cannot be used directly as today's figures. The 9% global average in item 7 has the same limitation. This gives you a method, not an answer: run the numbers this way and the result will vary from person to person. A course with high tuition, a major and job that do not match, or a long program may come out not worth it. But that only shows that this particular program is poor value. It does not show that schooling is useless. Several factors do not fit into the formula but still matter: the legal education requirements in item 5 of this section (linked admissions and separate exams in vocational schools), resistance to automation in item 10, and whether you can switch industries. The beneficiary is you.

### 7. Remember the baseline first: across the world, one extra year of schooling yields a private return of about 9% a year, the part that lands in your own income
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Nothing to pay. Remember this number and use it as a yardstick for any "learn something" option.
- Plain language: Worldwide, one more year of schooling raises personal income by about 9% a year on average, and returns for women are consistently higher than for men. This figure covers formal schooling and cannot be applied directly to a short course. Use it as a yardstick: for a 30,000-yuan, half-year course, ask whether its income gain matches one more year of school.
- Benefit: A review covering 1950 to 2014, 139 countries and 1,120 estimates found that "the global average private return to one additional year of schooling is 9% per year." The private return is the part that lands in your own income: one more year of school raises average personal income by about 9%. The private return to higher education has been rising. The social return (the part that flows to society as a whole rather than to the individual) remains very high. The average return to schooling for women has consistently been higher than for men.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Psacharopoulos G, Patrinos HA (2018). Returns to investment in education: a decennial review of the global literature. Education Economics, 26(5), 445-458. <https://doi.org/10.1080/09645292.2018.1484426>；世界银行政策研究工作论文 8402. <https://doi.org/10.1596/1813-9450-8402>
- Notes: This is the global average for one more year of formal schooling. It cannot be read as the return from taking a short course, and it is not a figure for China, so treat it only as a rough scale. Its use is to give every training option a yardstick. For a course that costs 30,000 yuan and half a year, ask whether it brings an income gain equal to one year of formal schooling. Note also that the global average hides large differences between majors and between countries.

### 8. Before paying for a certificate, check whether it appears in the National Occupational Qualification Catalogue or on the list of assessment bodies filed with the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security (MOHRSS)
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Nothing to pay. One check, about ten to fifteen minutes.
- Plain language: Some organizations hide behind new-occupation or skill-level labels to run poor training, charge arbitrary fees and issue certificates freely. Before signing up, check three things: is the job in the national catalogue, is the issuer a filed assessment body, and can the certificate number be verified on the official platform.
- Benefit: MOHRSS launched a national special crackdown on "knockoff certificates" in technical and skill-based fields. The notice names the problem: "some organizations and units use new-occupation qualifications, skill-level grades and similar labels to run training, assessment and certification activities at will, charging arbitrary fees and issuing certificates indiscriminately; some organizations and units make false or exaggerated promotional claims, and even impersonate authoritative bodies to run training, assessment and certificate issuance." Measures include joint investigations with the cyberspace, public security and market regulation authorities, and "strict crackdowns on fake assessment bodies and on assessment and certification activities carried out by bodies filed with the HR authorities." Non-compliant promotion on websites and new media is shut down under the law. The notice also asks that "the public be guided to learn how and where to check certificates, and to judge whether a certificate is legal, compliant and of real value."
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 人力资源社会保障部 (2022). 关于开展技术技能类「山寨证书」专项治理工作的通知. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-03/18/content_5679641.htm>
- Notes: China only: Check in this order. First, confirm the occupation is in the National Occupational Qualification Catalogue; if it is not, the certificate is not a national occupational qualification. Second, confirm the issuer is an assessment body filed with MOHRSS. Third, look up the certificate number on the official skill-talent certificate query platform. Do not sign up for any certificate that cannot be found. Certificates labelled "international," "senior" or "registered," or those promising a "guaranteed pass" or income from "registration affiliation," carry the highest risk. For "education-upgrade" classes that sell degrees, see section 31, item 10 (only degrees verifiable on CHSI, the China Higher Education Student Information Network, count).

### 9. Use government-subsidised training first, and don't pay for a commercial course straight away
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Free, or greatly reduced in price. The training itself takes time. The hard part is resisting the urge to pay for a course on the spot; check the subsidy catalogue first.
- Plain language: From 2025 to the end of 2027, the state plans more than 30 million person-times of subsidised training, paid from several funds, including employment subsidy and unemployment insurance funds. The more a trade is short of workers, the bigger the subsidy. If you have a job, ask your employer first. Otherwise, check your local labour agency's subsidy catalogue before choosing a course.
- Benefit: The national scale is set out clearly: from 2025 to the end of 2027, the state will focus on high-end industries, urgently needed sectors and priority job-seeking groups, with more than 30 million person-times of subsidised training. Several funding streams are combined into one package: employment subsidy funds, unemployment insurance funds, industry development funds, east-west cooperation funds and enterprise staff education funds. Subsidy levels depend on how urgently each occupation (trade) is needed and what its training costs, so they differ between trades. Firms are also required to "fully withdraw and use staff education funds as prescribed, with at least 60% used for frontline staff education and training".
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 国务院就业促进和劳动保护工作领导小组 (2025). 关于开展大规模职业技能提升培训行动的指导意见. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202507/content_7032222.htm>
- Notes: China only: Exact eligibility and amounts are set by each province. The entry points are the "training subsidy catalogue" on your local Human Resources and Social Security Bureau website (the local labour and social security agency), and the public training bases. Section 7 covers training subsidies during unemployment (up to 3 times per person) and social insurance subsidies. If you have a job, check your employer's staff education funds first. Check the subsidy catalogue before you choose what to study; that saves far more money than choosing first and then looking for a subsidy.

### 10. When choosing a skill, look first at whether it needs hands-on work and on-site judgement, since these are the hardest to automate
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. Before choosing, run the skills you are considering through this test.
- Plain language: A US estimate put about 47% of jobs at high risk of computerisation; later task-based studies found a much lower share. Machines still struggle with three things: fine manual dexterity, creative thinking and dealing with people. Work done on site, in messy settings, that needs hands-on skill and judgement is harder to replace. Fixed-process, information-handling jobs are most at risk.
- Benefit: The study scored each of 702 US occupations on how likely its work could be handed to a computer, and found about 47% of employment in a high-risk band. What drives the score are three "engineering bottlenecks" that machines cannot yet get past. First, perception and manipulation, including finger and hand dexterity and working in cramped spaces and awkward postures. Second, creative intelligence (the ability to come up with new ideas). Third, social intelligence (dealing with people). So jobs with non-standard environments that need hands-on skill and on-the-spot judgement have consistently lower odds of being replaced, not just in a few occupations. Fixed-process, information-focused jobs carry the highest risk.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: Frey CB, Osborne MA (2017). The future of employment: how susceptible are jobs to computerisation? Technological Forecasting and Social Change, 114, 254-280. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019>
- Notes: Disputed. The study began as an unpublished 2013 working paper that scored whether a whole occupation could be taken over by machines. Later research estimated tasks one by one and found a much lower high-risk share. It also does not say when any of this would happen. When you use it, don't focus on the 47% figure; focus on the three bottlenecks it names. This is why on-site installation, maintenance and nursing resist automation better than pure paperwork. So when choosing a skill, prefer one that takes you on site and into non-standard environments.

### 11. Before you learn a skill, check whether it has a grade rating and whether that grade can be turned into pay
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. One check is enough.
- Plain language: The state is promoting a "new eight-grade skilled worker" system that links pay to skill grade. Strong workers can be graded without meeting seniority rules. This is a policy direction, and how far it is applied varies widely by region and employer. Before learning a skill, check whether it has a national occupational standard and a grade path. Only then can pay rise with grade.
- Benefit: The state is promoting the "new eight-grade skilled worker" occupational skill grading system. The document explicitly requires linking grade to pay, in its words: "strengthen a skills-oriented pay distribution system … guide firms to adopt a pay method that links both job pay to job value and to skill grade". Localities should "step up the number and frequency of senior-grade and higher assessments and appointments". Qualifying firms can run skill grade assessments for their own staff. For outstanding workers, "the requirements of working years, grade, seniority and similar conditions may be set aside, and they may be directly certified at the corresponding skill grade".
- Evidence level: B

### 12. Don't take "high pay online" as proof of a shortage; check the local shortage list and subsidy catalogue

- Plain language: Local subsidies for each trade are set by how urgently workers are needed and how costly the training is, so subsidy amounts show local shortages. Online claims that a trade pays a certain monthly amount are usually job-board listings or self-reports. You mostly see people who earned well, and those figures leave out hours, commuting, occupational illness and seasonal swings.
- Benefit: The subsidy amount is the official price put on which jobs are short. Policy requires a "differentiated subsidy mechanism set by how urgently each occupation (trade) is needed and what its training costs", and each area sets its local list of subsidised training on that basis. So the gap in subsidies for the same trade across cities directly shows how short of workers each city is. - Sources: 国务院就业促进和劳动保护工作领导小组 (2025). 关于开展大规模职业技能提升培训行动的指导意见. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202507/content_7032222.htm>；国家统计局 (2026). 2025年城镇单位就业人员年平均工资情况：「规模以上企业就业人员年平均工资为106080元，其中，中层及以上管理人员210016元，专业技术人员155491元，办事人员和有关人员94936元，社会生产服务和生活服务人员79857元，生产制造及有关人员80739元」. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202605/t20260515_1963707.html>；人力资源社会保障部就业培训技术指导中心 (2023). 2022年第四季度全国招聘大于求职「最缺工」的100个职业排行（中国就业网）. <https://chinajob.mohrss.gov.cn/c/2023-01-30/367442.shtml>

### 13. With the same money and time, choose short programmes that lead straight to a job
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=some gain=mid scope=money -->
- Cost: You pay tuition for a short course, usually not much. It takes up a stretch of time. The hard part is stopping, as you agreed beforehand, when you are halfway through and it has not paid off yet.
- Plain language: Policy favours short, fast courses that lead to work, and micro-majors at vocational colleges. Choose a course by one test: after finishing, is there a specific job you can apply for? Courses lasting a year or more that still need "more experience" afterwards cost far more time than their price suggests. Before you start, set your exit point: stop if you have not found work by a set stage.
- Benefit: Policy wording matches this. It supports "market-based, high-quality vocational skills training by private training institutions, and fast, short-cycle programmes that can lead to employment quickly". It also pushes vocational colleges to "focus on the knowledge and skills the job market urgently needs … and offer micro-majors and vocational ability courses".
- Evidence level: C
- Sources: 国务院就业促进和劳动保护工作领导小组 (2025). 关于开展大规模职业技能提升培训行动的指导意见. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202507/content_7032222.htm>
- Notes: China only: The test is one question: after finishing, is there a specific job you can apply for? A course that runs for a year or more and still needs "accumulated experience" after completion costs far more than its sticker price. The exit condition in section 4 applies here too: before you start, write down how far you will go and stop if you still have not found work at that stage.

### 14. After studying, close the book and test yourself first, rather than rereading
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=time -->
- Cost: Free, and total time does not increase. Use the time you would have spent rereading to close the book and write from memory or do practice questions. The hard part is that the test shows you what you forgot on the spot, which feels worse than rereading.
- Plain language: Closing the book and testing yourself works better than rereading. In classroom studies, adding quizzes gives a moderate boost to results. Among ten common study methods, self-testing ranks in the top tier. After reading a section, write its key points from memory, then check them against the text.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis of 222 independent studies with 48,478 students found that classroom quizzing raises academic results, with a moderate effect size (g = 0.499). The size of the effect depends on several conditions: what the comparison group did instead, whether the quiz format matches the final exam, whether the material fits, and whether corrective feedback is given. It also depends on how many times the quiz is repeated, where and when it is given, how long the intervention lasts, and the study design. A separate review that assessed ten common learning methods rated "practice testing" and "distributed practice" as the top tier (high utility). The reason given: they work across learner ages and ability levels, across many types of test, and in real educational settings.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Yang C, Luo L, Vadillo MA, Yu R, Shanks DR (2021). Testing (quizzing) boosts classroom learning: A systematic and meta-analytic review. Psychological Bulletin, 147(4), 399–435. <https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000309>；Dunlosky J, Rawson KA, Marsh EJ, Nathan MJ, Willingham DT (2013). Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques: Promising Directions From Cognitive and Educational Psychology. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14(1), 4–58. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266>
- Notes: Self-testing is meant to make you pull information out of memory, not to get a score. How to do it: after reading a section, close the book and write its key points from memory, then check them against the text. Feedback that corrects mistakes helps more, so when you check your answers, look closely at what you got wrong. The benefit size is recorded as "large", based on the effect size and the rating. These two sources measure results, not hours spent, so the time-saving threshold was not applied. Self-testing and item 15 (spreading the same time over several days) are the two top-rated items in the same review, so use them together.

### 15. Spread the same study time over several days instead of cramming it into one
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=high scope=time -->
- Cost: Free, and total time stays the same. Only the schedule changes: the same 3 hours becomes three 1-hour sessions over three days. The hard part is starting several days early rather than leaving it to the last night.
- Plain language: The same study time is remembered better when spread over several days than when crammed into one go. A practical rule: the longer you need to remember something, the longer the gap between reviews should be. The research gives no general number of days for that gap, though.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis pooling 184 articles, 317 experiments and 839 distributed-practice effect assessments. It compared two things: studying all at once versus spread over several sessions (the spacing effect), and short versus long gaps between sessions (the lag effect). Its conclusion is that the gap between reviews and how long the material must be remembered together decide retention on the final test. The longer you need to keep something, the longer the review gap that gives the best retention. A separate review rating ten common study methods put distributed practice in the top tier (high utility).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Cepeda NJ, Pashler H, Vul E, Wixted JT, Rohrer D (2006). Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks: A review and quantitative synthesis. Psychological Bulletin, 132(3), 354–380. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354>；Dunlosky J, Rawson KA, Marsh EJ, Nathan MJ, Willingham DT (2013). Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14(1), 4–58. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266>
- Notes: This works best combined with item 14 (closed-book self-testing): spread the self-tests themselves over several days too. Most material in the Cepeda meta-analysis was word-list memory, so no general best day-gap is given for complex content. "The further away the test, the longer the gap" is the rule the source gives; it does not provide a specific schedule. The problem with cramming the night before is not that it does nothing on the spot; it is that you forget quickly.

### 16. Don't treat highlighting, rereading and summarising as your main study methods
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=time -->
- Cost: Free, and it actually saves time. The hard part is that these feel the easiest and give the strongest sense of "I am studying".
- Plain language: Students most often use highlighting and rereading. In a ranking of ten common study methods, both fall in the lowest tier, and so do summarising, keyword mnemonics and imagining pictures for texts. They are not useless, but they only work under narrow conditions. The same time spent on self-testing pays off more.
- Benefit: A review of ten common learning methods rates each one by whether its effect holds across learning conditions, learner characteristics, materials and types of test. Top tier: practice testing and distributed practice. Middle tier: elaborative interrogation, self-explanation and interleaved practice. Lowest tier, five methods: summarising, highlighting (or underlining), keyword mnemonics, imagery for text, and rereading. The review notes that most students say they use rereading and highlighting, but neither reliably improves results. It recommends replacing them with other methods, such as using practice testing in place of rereading.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Dunlosky J, Rawson KA, Marsh EJ, Nathan MJ, Willingham DT (2013). Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques: Promising Directions From Cognitive and Educational Psychology. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14(1), 4–58. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266>
- Notes: Underlining is not banned. You can use it to mark what to self-test later; the problem is treating the underlining itself as the learning. The review also makes clear that the lowest tier does not mean zero: it means the conditions for these methods to work are narrow, and more research is needed. For alternatives, see item 14 (closed-book self-testing) and item 15 (spreading the same time over several days).

### 17. Mix several question types instead of doing twenty of one type in a row
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=time -->
- Cost: Free. Changing from doing a workbook in type order to mixing the types up. The hard part is that mixed practice lowers your accuracy in the moment, and it can feel like going backwards.
- Plain language: Doing one type of problem in a long run feels smooth in the moment. Mixing several types gives better long-term results, with a moderate effect. But the method depends on the material. Visual material, such as recognising an artist's style, benefits most. Maths problems benefit less, and memorising vocabulary is better done in blocks.
- Benefit: A multilevel meta-analysis of 59 studies, 238 effect sizes and 158 samples. Interleaved practice compared with blocked practice has a moderate overall effect (Hedges' g = 0.42). By material, painting is best (g = 0.67), and other visual material also does well. Maths tasks have a smaller effect (g = 0.34). The overall effect is not significant for expository text and taste material. For vocabulary, blocked practice is better (g = −0.39). The regression analysis shows that the benefit of mixing grows when the categories are more similar to each other, the items within each category are more varied, and the material is more complex.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Brunmair M, Richter T (2019). Similarity matters: A meta-analysis of interleaved learning and its moderators. Psychological Bulletin, 145(11), 1029–1052. <https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000209>
- Notes: Item 16 (don't treat highlighting as your main method) cites a review that rated interleaved practice in the middle tier, because there were not yet enough studies at the time. Taken together, mix practice for content where you must recognise which type a problem is and which method fits, such as maths problem types, case studies or applying legal provisions. Use blocked practice for content you need to memorise, such as words and definitions.

### 18. Don't choose study methods based on being a "visual" or "auditory" learner
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=low scope=time -->
- Cost: Free. Stop choosing materials and courses by this label, and stop paying for learning-style tests.
- Plain language: Many people believe in learning styles such as visual or auditory, and think teaching to a style works better. Careful tests of this idea are rare, almost none support it, and a few point the opposite way. People do have preferences, but there is no evidence that matching teaching to them helps, so a style test is not worth paying for.
- Benefit: A review, commissioned to evaluate the evidence on learning styles, says that to prove "teaching by learning style" works, you must first group students by style, then randomly assign each group to different teaching methods, and give everyone the same test. A valid result would show a specific interaction: the method that works best for one style is not the best for another. The review's conclusion is that this interaction was almost never found. There is a large literature on learning styles, but very few studies use a sound experimental design, and some of those directly contradict the matching hypothesis. The authors therefore conclude that current evidence is not enough to support using learning-style assessments in education practice.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Pashler H, McDaniel M, Rohrer D, Bjork R (2008). Learning Styles: Concepts and Evidence. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 9(3), 105–119. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x>
- Notes: The review also notes that this does not mean every version of learning-style theory has been tested and rejected. Many have never been tested at all. What to avoid is paying for style tests and choosing training courses by style. For how to choose training, see item 9 of this section (give priority to government-subsidized training channels) and item 13 (prefer short programs that lead directly to a job).

### 19. Test yourself on what you memorize by how you will use it, not by rereading it word for word
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some gain=mid scope=time -->
- Cost: Free, and not much more time. What changes is the method: swap "reread it word for word" for "quiz yourself on how the material will be used later". The difficulty is first working out how it will be used.
- Plain language: Closing the book and testing yourself helps beyond the exact words you studied: reworded questions, application problems and reasoning problems all benefit, though the effect is modest, on the small side. It helps least when question and answer are reversed, and for material you only read and were never tested on.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis pooled over 40 years of research: 67 published and unpublished articles, 122 experiments, 192 transfer effect sizes, and 10,382 people in total. Compared with restudying without testing, test-enhanced learning did transfer to other situations, with an effect size of d=0.40 (95% CI 0.31 to 0.50). Transfer was strongest when the test used a different question format, when it involved application and reasoning questions, when it involved medical diagnosis questions, and when it used cue words with similar meanings. Transfer was weakest when stimulus and response were reversed, for material seen during initial study but never tested, and for example-type questions. Three moderators strongly affected whether positive transfer appeared: whether the answer format matched between practice and test, whether there was retrieval practice with detailed feedback, and whether the first test was answered correctly. The authors corrected for publication bias with two methods. The moderator effect sizes were barely affected, but the intercept estimate was clearly lowered: when none of the conditions above held, positive transfer often did not appear.
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: Pan SC, Rickard TC (2018). Transfer of test-enhanced learning: meta-analytic review and synthesis. Psychological Bulletin, 144(7), 710-756. <https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000151>
- Notes: "Memorize" mixes two things: going over the material again (rereading, underlining), and actively recalling it with the book closed. The first kind is rated lowest in the review that ranks ten study methods. For that rating, see item 16 of this section (don't treat underlining, rereading and writing summaries as your main method). Do the second kind: recall with the book closed, as in item 14 of this section (self-testing with the book closed), spread over several days, as in item 15 of this section (spread it over several days). To do it, first decide how the material will be used: whether you must pick the answer on paper, or judge it on the spot. Then quiz yourself in that same form. It is also worth quizzing in reverse. This meta-analysis found transfer weakest when stimulus and response are reversed, so both directions need practice.

<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=mid scope=freedom -->

Serious cases that may be criminal are referred to the relevant authorities (nationwide).
- Evidence level: A
- Sources: 人力资源和社会保障部令第 40 号 (2019). 职称评审管理暂行规定. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2019/content_5437137.htm>：第三十九条；人力资源社会保障部 (2024). 职称评审监管暂行办法. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202408/content_6969436.htm>：第五条、第十五条、第十七条、第二十七条；人力资源社会保障部办公厅 (2022). 关于进一步做好职称评审工作的通知. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-12/24/content_5733407.htm>：「未经备案的职称评审委员会，评审结果不纳入全国职称评审信息查询验证系统」；新华社 (2024). 职称评审进入高峰，信息如何查？有何新变化？ <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202410/content_6978917.htm>：「大家可通过www.12333.gov.cn网页、手机12333客户端、人力资源社会保障部门户网站、“人力资源和社会保障部”微信公众号等多种渠道进行登录」；最高人民法院、最高人民检察院 (2019). 关于办理组织考试作弊等刑事案件适用法律若干问题的解释（法释〔2019〕13 号，第一条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/xwfbh/wsfbh/201909/t20190903_430901.shtml>：「国家统一法律职业资格考试、国家教师资格考试、注册会计师全国统一考试、会计专业技术资格考试……等专业技术资格考试」
- Notes: China only: To tell real from fake, look the title up on the national title assessment verification platform. The entrances are the 12333 website and app (the official human resources and social security service platform) and the Ministry's official website. Titles from assessment committees that were not filed with the authorities do not appear in this system. So any offer that promises a "guaranteed pass", says you need not attend, or says the title works even though the certificate cannot be found is almost certainly a problem. The main beneficiary is you, and the size of the stake is judged by "avoiding title revocation and a 3-year integrity record". The accounting professional qualification exam is on the judicial interpretation's list of national exams set by law. Finding someone to take the exam for you, selling answers, or organizing cheating in such an exam is a crime. Merely buying answers and peeking at them yourself is not listed as a crime in the law. It is handled under the exam's disciplinary rules; see item 29 of section 9 (do not hire someone to take the exam for you).

### 23. A title does not mean a raise: first ask whether your employer assesses and appoints within post quotas, or assesses without always appointing
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=low scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. Ask your employer's HR department once.
- Plain language: A title helps decide hiring, evaluation and promotion, but being assessed is not the same as being appointed. Public institutions with post management usually assess within a set number of posts. Other employers may separate assessment from appointment, so a title may not bring a matching post or salary. If you move to another employer or city, the title may need reassessment or confirmation.
- Benefit: Article 2 of Order No. 40: the result of a title assessment is an important basis for hiring, evaluation and promotion of professional and technical staff. The 2016 reform opinion says that public institutions that fully implement post management should generally assess titles within the post structure ratio. Units that do not implement post management, and series or emerging occupations with broad general use, may separate assessment from appointment. When professional staff move across regions or units, titles are reassessed or confirmed under the applicable management authority, unless national rules provide otherwise (nationwide).
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: 人力资源和社会保障部令第 40 号 (2019). 职称评审管理暂行规定. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2019/content_5437137.htm>：第二条、第二十九条；中共中央办公厅、国务院办公厅 (2016). 关于深化职称制度改革的意见. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2017/content_5163445.htm>：「对于全面实行岗位管理、专业技术人才学术技术水平与岗位职责密切相关的事业单位，一般应在岗位结构比例内开展职称评审。对于不实行岗位管理的单位，以及通用性强、广泛分布在各社会组织的职称系列和新兴职业，可采用评聘分开方式。」
- Notes: China only: The evidence level is B because the documents only describe how assessment links to appointment. They give no national figure for how much a title raises pay. What you can do: at public institutions and state-owned enterprises, first ask HR about the unit's vacancies and appointment rules. At private companies, first ask your boss whether the title is recognized and whether it comes with more pay.

### 24. Before a master's after your bachelor's, count tuition and two or three years of lost pay, then check whether your field pays off; if you already work, a part-time program carries the same certificate value as full-time
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. Check one admissions notice and tuition schedule, then run the numbers against the job offers you have. It takes about one to two hours.
- Plain language: A master's costs tuition and two or three years of lost pay, usually the bigger cost. A UK study that adjusted for family background and undergraduate grades found a master's added only about 2% to women's earnings at age 35, and cut men's by about 2%. Results vary a lot by field. If you already work, a part-time program gives the same certificate value, but no national grant.
- Benefit: Start with the cost. A 2013 national opinion says full-time academic master's tuition should generally not exceed RMB 8,000 per year. Full-time professional master's programs follow their existing fee policy and are not capped by this limit, so they often cost much more. All full-time graduate students in the national enrollment plan can get the national graduate grant, which is at least RMB 6,000 per year for master's students, with the exact amount set locally. Now the return. The UK Department for Education's 2020 study used national student and tax records to track people who graduated with bachelor's degrees between the mid-1990s and mid-2000s. It removed differences in family background and undergraduate grades, and looked at income at age 35. The original text reads: "we estimate returns of 2% (women) and -2% (men) for masters". Women with a master's earned about 2% more than those with only a bachelor's, and men earned about 2% less. Differences by field are large. Women with master's degrees in law, economics or management earned about 20% more, while those in arts, English or philosophy earned more than 10% less. For men, most master's fields had clearly negative returns, but law, economics, management and engineering were still clearly positive. People already working can take a part-time master's and study while they work. A 2016 Ministry of Education notice says part-time and full-time graduate students sit the same exam and follow the same training standards, and their degrees have the same legal status and effect. Certificates state the study mode. The national graduate grant goes only to full-time students in the enrollment plan, not to part-time students. A 2019 notice from five ministries says civil service recruitment, public institution hiring and state-owned enterprise recruitment must give equal opportunities to graduates of different study modes, and may not set entry conditions unrelated to the job. The Ministry of Education's 2021 graduate employment notice says recruitment notices from Party and government organs, public institutions and state-owned enterprises may not make the study mode a restrictive condition. The 2022 notice says no campus recruitment activity may do so either.
- Evidence level: B
- Sources: 财政部、国家发展改革委、教育部 (2013). 关于完善研究生教育投入机制的意见（财教〔2013〕19号）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2013/content_2404719.htm>：「原则上，现阶段全日制学术学位研究生学费标准，硕士生每生每年不超过8000元，博士生每生每年不超过1万元。全日制专业学位研究生以及目前已按规定实行收费政策的研究生，暂执行原收费政策」「博士生资助标准不低于每生每年1万元，硕士生资助标准不低于每生每年6000元」；Britton J, Buscha F, Dickson M, van der Erve L, Vignoles A, Walker I, Waltmann B, Zhu Y (2020). That is why the evidence level is B. The calculation method is the same as item 6 of this section (study or work): set the two or three years of lost pay against the extra pay each year after. Some job postings explicitly require a master's, for example universities, research institutes and some state-owned enterprises. The 2025 admissions rules say part-time master's programs in principle admit only people already employed who will return to their original employer after graduation. Tuition, grants and accommodation are set out by the university in its admissions prospectus. On household registration (hukou) and personnel files: none of the national documents found in this guide set a uniform rule, so read the admissions brochure and ask the university before you apply.

### 25. Before a PhD after your master's, only do it if the target job clearly requires one, and count the mental load first
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no gain=high scope=money -->
- Cost: Free. Check the education requirement in the target employer's job postings, then talk with two or three current PhD students.
- Plain language: A UK study found a PhD raised women's pay by about 8% at age 35 and cut men's by about 9%, after allowing for family background and grades. PhD study is also mentally demanding: about 24% of students show clear depressive symptoms, and about 17% show clear anxiety symptoms. So first check whether your target job requires a PhD.
- Benefit: Item 24 of this section (master's) cites the UK Department for Education's 2020 study, which also covers PhDs. The original text reads: "8% (women) and -9% (men) for PhDs". After adjusting for family background and undergraduate grades, a PhD at age 35 went with about 8% more pay for women and about 9% less for men. The study also found that a PhD lowered the chance of unemployment and of earning under 30,000 pounds a year, and lowered the chance of earning over 40,000 pounds a year. Differences by subject are large: maths PhDs have especially low returns, while psychology PhDs are among the highest. The mental load of doctoral study is measured by a 2021 pooled analysis. Across 16 studies with 23,469 PhD students, about 24% had clear depressive symptoms (95% CI 18%–31%, credible interval). Across 9 studies with 15,626 PhD students, about 17% had clear anxiety symptoms (12%–23%). Results varied widely between studies (I² about 98%). Tuition follows the 2013 opinion: full-time academic PhD tuition should generally not exceed RMB 10,000 per year, and the national graduate grant is at least RMB 10,000 per year.
- Evidence level: B

- Notes: China only: Disputed. The depression and anxiety figures simply pool each survey's proportion. They were not compared with peers, and the scales differ.
